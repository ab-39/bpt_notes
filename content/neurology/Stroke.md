### Epidemiology
Stroke is a _very_ common problem:
- 1 in 4 people worldwide will have a stroke in their lifetime
- It is the number 2 cause of death worldwide
- It is the leading cause of disability in adults 
- - -
### Stroke Subtypes
Three(ish)  key variants:
1. Ischaemic stroke (i.e. cerebral infarction)
2. Haemorrhagic stroke (i.e. intracerebral haemorrhage)
3. Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis 
4. Subarachnoid haemorrhage 
- - -
### Diagnostic Process
Key points:
- Typically the _sudden_ onset of a focal deficit- e.g. amaurosis fugax, hemiparesis
  - Note that broader (i.e. not focal) symptoms like confusion, altered conscious state, etc. are often due to another cause - e.g. metabolic 
- Examination helps to localise the lesion
- Imaging - CT stroke protocol: CT brain + [[imaging/CT Perfusion Scan|CT perfusion scan]] + CT cerebral angiogram
  - MRI-B is the most sensitive modality for stroke
    - Even if patients cannot score one on arrival, they’re better in the first few days for identifying stroke 
  - CT angiogram is useful for seeing blocked arteries, or a ‘spot’ sign for vascular malformation

Common conditions that mimic stroke:
- Todd’s paresis
- Migraine
- Functional disorders
- Metabolic disorders/sepsis

### Work-up of stroke
Most high-yield scan = non-contrast CT brain

Key findings:
- Hyperdensity = intracerebral haemorrhagae
  - Deep -> typically hypertensive - affects basal ganglia
  - Peripheral (lobar) -> typically due to amyloid angiopathy - affects elderly
  - Blood in cranial fossae -> arterial bleed (?aneurysm) -> get a CTA
  - Hyperdensity in venous sinus -> venous thrombosis -> get a CTV
- Ischaemic stroke is typically difficult to identify on CT-brain early on, may see:
  - Hyperdense artery -> acute thrombus (better seen on 1mm slices)
  - Clear hypodensities are unlikely until ~4.5hrs have elapsed
  - Subtle loss of grey-white matter differentiation -> irreversible injury

### Treating Stroke
All stroke patients should get treated in a specialised stroke unit, which has meta-analytic evidence for a 3.6x reduction in mortality with a number needed to treat of 28.
