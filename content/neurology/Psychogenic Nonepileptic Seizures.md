# Psychogenic Nonepileptic Seizures
#bpt/neurology #bpt/racp_cls

### Overview
PNES present very similar to epileptic [[seizures]], with motor and sensory changes, but arise from the psyche rather than from electrical activity in the neurones. PNES is much rarer than [[epilepsy]], but, in fact, contributes more to the cost burden of disease. 

PNES arises from the same complex reticulum of early experiences that lead to mood and personality disorders. The mechanism is complex. The diagnosis of PNES is often prolonged (µ: 5y), with epilepsy often being misdiagnosed (although it is important to note that PNES and epilepsy can coexist in a patient). Symptoms are generally non-specific, and video EEG (which tends to have no epileptiform features) and neuropsychiatric assessment are the gold standard. 

#### Epilepsy vs PNES	
| Epilepsy                       | PNES                           |
|--------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Stereotyped (same every time)  | Variable                       |
| Short (<60s)                   | Prolonged (>5m)                |
| Tongue biting (lateral)        | Absent                         |
| Stertorous breathing           | Absent                         |
| Eyes open during tonic phase   | Eyes closed during tonic phase |
| Postictal headache and fatigue | Postictal fatigue              |
- - -
The management of PNES, like any [[functional neurological disorder]], rests on a good therapeutic relationship. There is no evidence-based therapeutic intervention, although the recent _REPROGRAM_ study run at the Alfred suggests that there may be a role for CBT.
