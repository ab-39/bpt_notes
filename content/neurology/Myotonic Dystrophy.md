# Myotonic Dystrophy

### Overview
DM is an autosomal dominant muscular dystrophy that is characterised by a clinical triad of progressive weakness, myotonia (i.e. impaired muscle relaxation), and early cataracts. There are two types of DM:
|                       | Type 1 (DM1)              | Type 2 (DM2)               |
|-----------------------|---------------------------|----------------------------|
| *Prevalence*          | 1/2,500                   | Unknown                    |
| *Gene Mutation*       | CTG repeat in *DMPK* gene | CCTG repeat in *CNBP* gene |
| *Age of Onset*        | Birth to adulthood        | Early to late adulthood    |
| *Pattern of Weakness* | Distal muscles, face      | Proximal muscles           |
| *Myotonia vs Pain*    | Prominent myotonia        | Prominent Pain             |

Both conditions are characterised by _myotonia_, an impairment in the relaxation of muscles:
- Percussion myotonia -> percussion of muscles leads to pronounced contraction with slow relaxation
- Action myotonia -> sustained grip with slow relaxation

Other clinical features include frontal baldness, foot drop, ‘hatchet face’ (thin, sharp face due to wasting of muscles), facial weakness. Cardiac involvement, particularly conduction abnormalities, and respiratory failure are not uncommon. Cases can vary:
- Mild -> cataracts, mild myotonia, diabetes
- Classic -> muscle weakness and wasting, myotonia, cataracts, cardiac conduction abnormalities, diabetes mellitus, hypothyroidism, testicular atrophy, shortened lifespan
- Congenital -> hypotonia and generalised weakness at birth, respiratory insufficiency, intellectual disability, early death

CK is usually mildly elevated, with a myopathic, ‘dive-bomber’ EMG (waxing and waning myotonia). 

Treatment is primarily supportive (e.g. AFO, CPAP, PPM). Mexiletine, a muscular sodium channel blocker, may be useful to aid with myotonia.

### Genetics
DM1 is caused by expansion of CTG trinucleotide repeats in the _DMPK_. Like other diseases caused by repeats, the number of repeats corresponds with disease severity and age at onset:
- Mild = 50-150 repeats
- Classic = 150-1000 repeats
- Childhood onset/congenital = >1000 repeats 

DM1 may display anticipation.
