### Epidural Haematoma
Highly life-threatening arterial bleed, resulting in a rapidly progressive haematoma developing in the potential space between the dura and the skull, associated with a biconvex appearance on CTB. Classically, presents with head trauma followed by a lucid interval, and then rapid neurological degeneration and, if untreated, death. Treatment consists of surgical evacuation, which has a favourable prognosis if carried out promptly.
- - -
### Subdural Haematoma
Acute or chronic in presentation, subdural haematomas are typically due to venous bleeds into the dura and arachnoid matres. This results in a ‘classic’ crescentic appearance on CTB. Acute SDH typically has a poorer prognosis than EDH if requiring surgery, while chronic SDH is an important differential for cognitive decline and possible epileptic focus in the elderly. Note that the density of the SDH on CTB gives clues as to its age.
