### Overview
Think of everything in the nervous system as in a constant state of tension - two sets of forces opposing one another to keep the system in equilibrium.

One principle to help with localisation is to remember that the nerves that supply muscles often come off _before_ the affected muscle.

| *Wrist*   | Radial    | Median           |
| *Fingers* | Radial    | Median/Ulnar     |

#### Radial Nerve
Travels in the spiral groove of the humerus - this is a common site of injury and also helps with localisation: injuries that result in weakness of elbow extension localise to _above_ the spiral groove. 

Atypically, the radial nerve also supplies brachioradialis - a flexor muscle, which is very helpful for localisation.

Finally, in the presence of a radial nerve palsy, when the wrist is dropped, finger abduction _will_ be weak. To test this properly the hand needs to be put in the primary position.

Sensory wise, the radial nerve supplies the posterior aspect of the arm.
