### Overview
A common form of [[dementia]], DLB is closely related to [[Parkinson’s disease]], and arises due to the deposition of abnormal clusters of ⍺-synuclein known as _Lewy bodies_, primarily in the cortex and brainstem.

It is characterised by _fluctuating cognitive deficits_, that are typically insidious in onset and first affecting attention and executive function. Other common features include visual hallucinations and symptoms of [[REM sleep behaviour disorder]]. Spontaneous onset of Parkinsonism within ~1y is characteristic of the condition, but, arbitrarily, if the symptoms occur any earlier than this we term the condition “Parkinson’s disease dementia”.  

> [!NOTE] Early Signs
> DLB is caused by _frontoparietal_ deposition of ⍺-synuclein, and this is reflected in the early symptoms:
> - Visuospatial dysfunction
> - Dysexecutive memory loss 

Patients with DLB are generally _highly_ sensitive to antipsychotics, and these should be avoided. Medications that can be trialled include:
- Cholinesterase inhibitors -> improve fluctuations, apathy, anxiety, hallucinations, and sleep
- If antipsychotics absolutely required -> low dose quetiapine
- L-DOPA - particular utiliity if there are predominant Parkinsonian features
