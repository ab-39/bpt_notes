# Restless Leg Syndrome
#bpt/neurology
- - -
### Overview
Restless leg syndrome is a very common (prev. ~10%) condition that is characterised by four cardinal symptoms:
1. An urge to move the legs caused or accompanied by an unpleasant sensation 
2. Symptoms that begin or worsen with rest
3. Partial or complete relief with movement
4. Worsening during the evening or night

The IRLSSG consensus group question: "When you try to relax in the evening or sleep at night, do you ever have unpleasant, restless feelings your legs that can be relieved by walking or movement?" has 100% sensitivity and 97% specificity for RLS.

The age of onset is in the third decade of life. Iron, particularly in the CNS, is usually low. Although there is a strong genetic component, no clear locus has been identified, but the _PTPRD_, _BTBD9_, and _MEIS1_ genes are implicated. Secondary RLS may be due to pregnancy, anaemia, ferritin deficiency, renal failure, and peripheral neuropathy. Patients often describe the ‘unpleasant sensation’ as a burning paraesthesia. Around 80% of patients experience period leg movements while asleep. In addition to treatment of the cause (in secondary RLS) and supportive treatment (e.g. improving sleep hygiene) dopamine agonists may be of use in management. Additional options include gabapentinoids and opioids. Although pramipexole is a historical treatment for RLS, it is rarely used today due to the the risk of augmentation (worsening of disease after some years of treatment) and impulsive behaviours due to the dopaminergic effect.
