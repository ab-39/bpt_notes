### Overview
Huntington disease is a progressive disorder characterised by motor (chorea), cognitive (dementia) and psychiatric manifestations. It is an autosomal dominant disorder with anticipation that is caused by CAG triplet repeats in the _HTT_ gene on chromosome 4p. 

The mean age of onset is 45y with an average survival from onset of 15y. 

Clinical manifestations include:
- Movement disorder - chorea, bradykinesia, rigidity, dystonia
- Impairment of voluntary motor function
- Cognitive decline
- Psychiatric disturbances

Histologically, the disease is defined by the degeneration of neurons in the caudate, putamen, and cortex, due to intraneuronal inclusions of huntingtin protein.

There is no definitive cure. Predictive testing is thus not recommended for children.

### Genetics 
HD is caused by CAG triplet repeats (i.e. allelic homogeneity) in exon 1 of the _HTT_ gene at chromosome 4p (i.e. locus homogeneity)f:
- ≤26 repeats = normal
- 27-35 repeats = no HD symptoms, but unstable
- HD-causing alleles:
  - 36-39 repeats = reduced penetrance
  - ≥40 repeats = full penetrance
  - ≥60 repeats = juvenile onset

Huntington’s disease displays _anticipation_, that is, there is increased disease severity and reduced age of onset in successive generations, likely due to instability of CAG repeat number during spermatogenesis. Anticipation is more strongly associated with paternal transmission of the allele.
