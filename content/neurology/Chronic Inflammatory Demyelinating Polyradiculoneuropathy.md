# Chronic Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyradiculoneuropathy
#bpt/neurology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
CIDP is a chronic, demyelinating [[Acquired Peripheral Neuropathy]] that usually develops over 6-8 weeks, which can be thought of as a chronic counterpart to [[Guillain-Barré Syndrome]]. The classic presentation is of a proximal weakness due to root involvement, as well as areflexia (in contrast to a length-dependent neuropathy). EMG will show conduction block and slowing. Treatment is typically with pulsed steroids or IV/SCIg, as well as PLEX. CSF in CIDP also shows albuminocytological dissociation, and MRI will show GdE lesions. 

> [!NOTE] Efgartigimod
> Efgartigmod is a recombinant neonatal Fc receptor blocker that prevents the recycling of IgG back into the blood stream, thus blunting the immune response. This is currently being investigated for use in MG and CIDP, and is a promising pathway to reduce use of IVIg.

The _nodopathies_ are closely related to CIDP, with the distinction being that in these conditions, the antibodies are IgG4 (i.e. _non_-complement fixing antibodies) targeted against the nodal and paranodal proteins (e.g. NF155) rather than the myelin itself. 

CIDP is frequently associated with plasma cell disorders such as MGUS or lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma. A CIDP-like phenomenon can occur in combination with organomegaly, endocrinopathy, monoclonal plasma cell disorders, and skin changes as part of the _POEMS_ syndrome.
