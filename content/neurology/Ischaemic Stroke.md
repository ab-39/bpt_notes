### What is an ischaemic stroke?
An ischaemic stroke is a [[stroke]] that is caused by an acute reduction in blood flow and thus oxygenation to a portion of the brain.
- - -
### Definitions in Ischaemic Stroke:
- Umbra = ischaemic core = that part of the brain (typically deep structures) that have no collateral supply, and typically infarct/die quickly
- Penumbra = that part of the brain that has enough collateral supply to stay alive after an ischaemic insult _for some time_, without necessarily functioning normally 
- - -
### Aetiology of Ischaemic Stroke
Most of the clots that result in ischaemic strokes typically arise from:
- Internal carotid atherosclerotic lesions (i.e. atheroembolic phenomena)
- Left atrial thrombi (i.e. cardioembolic phenomena)

This is particularly important as secondary prevention is targeted at aetiology. Every patient who has an ischaemic stroke should get:
- Investigation for arterial pathology:
  - CT angiography (“arch to COW”) is the gold standard, but carotid doppler can also provide information
  - ?atherosclerosis
  - ?dissection
  - ?vasculitis
- Investigation for cardioembolic source:
  - TTE (+/-TOE)
  - ECG/Holter
  - ?AF
  - ?vegetations
  - ?PFO (needs a bubble study)
- Investigation for rare causes:
  - ?thrombophilia
  - ?vasculitis

Further investigations can include CTV, catheter angiography, and delayed MRI-B (particularly useful for angiopathies).

### Reperfusion Therapy
All patients who present with an ischaemic stroke within 4.5h should receive treatment with alteplase:
- This is a _tissue plasminogen activator_ that catalyses the conversion of plasminogen to plasmin, which subsequently degrades fibrinous clots 

Time is brain!
- The [[number needed to treat]] of tPA administration rises rapidly with time, so it is really critical to treat within the first 270 minutes

Selected patients can still benefit from tPA administration in a longer window (up to 9 hours) based on their [[imaging/CT Perfusion Scan|CT perfusion scan]] findings. 
- - -
### Hemicraniectomy
This is a ‘boutique’ procedure for **young** patients (<60y) with **large MCA** strokes. It has a number needed to treat of two(!), and significantly improves the number of patients living independently.
