# Inherited Causes of Cerebellar Ataxia
#bpt/neurology #bpt/genetics
- - -
### Friedreich Ataxia
An autosomal recessive disorder involving GAA trinucleotide repeat expansion that leads to progressive neurodegeneration. Its estimated prevalence in European populations is 1 in 50,000. In 1988 the locus of the genetic defect was mapped to chromosome 9. The *frataxin* gene, encoding a protein of unknown function, was recently identified, and, surprisingly, most of the mutations appeared to be unstable expansions of a GAA repeat in the first intron. It affects multiple spinal cord tracts, causing muscle weakness and impaired coordination of all limbs. A staggering gait in childhood is the resulting main symptom. Other features include skeletal abnormalities, cardiomyopathy, and diabetes.

### Spinocerebellar Ataxia
A group of autosomal dominantly inheritaed ataxias characterised by adult-onset ataxia with a range of additional features that can include pyramidal dysfunction, extrapyramidal symptoms, cognitive dysfunction, and neuropathy. The most common type is SCA3 or _Machado-Joseph disease_ caused by CAG trinucleotide repeats in the _ATXN3_  gene. This condition is most prevalent among Sephardic Jews in the Azores, and areas with emigration from this region (e.g. Brazil).
- - -
### Fragile X-associated Tremor/Ataxia Syndrome
An adult-onset progressive ataxia caused by premutations (i.e. CGG trinucleotide expansion, but to a lesser degree) in the _FMR1_ gene, typically occurring 1-2 generations before the development of full-blown [[fragile X syndrome]]. Characterised by FLAIR hyperintensities in the bilateral middle cerebellar peduncles.
