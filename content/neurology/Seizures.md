### Overview
A _seizure_ is a transient, paroxysmal occurrence of signs or symptoms arising due to abnormal or excessive synchronous electrical activity in the brain. These signs and symptoms can be behavioural, motor, or sensory. Seizures themselves are not a disease.

Seizures can be either _provoked_ or _unprovoked_, and can be divided into _focal_ or _generalised_ depending on whether the affected network affects one or both cerebral hemispheres. Focal seizures are classed according to awareness and motor involvement, while generalised seizures by definition include impaired awareness, and are classed according to motor involvement. 

What historic features are suspicious for seizures?
- Single, paroxysmal episodes
- Recurrent, episodic events
- Unexplained falls
- Recurrent shoulder dislocations
- Bruised/swollen forehead 
- Unexplained lapses in cognition 

Seizures are often succeeded by a period of _Todd’s paresis_:
> a lateralised/localised weakness of the limbs following an acute seizure, mimicking a stroke 

Firstly, a focal seizure will either have consciousness _preserved_ or _impaired_. The _semiology_, or characteristics, of a focal seizure depends on the area of the brain in which it arises:
| Area                  | Focal Seizure Symptoms                                       |
|-----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Frontal lobe          | Focal clonic motor symptoms<br>Hypermotor behaviour (e.g. pedalling, thrusting, etc.) <br> - NB. common in sleep  |
| Mesial temporal lobe  | Autonomic<br>Dysmnesic<br>Déjà vu/jamais vu<br>Gustatory/olfactory |
| Lateral temporal lobe | Auditory<br>Complex visual<br>Dysphasia                      |
| Parietal lobe         | Somatosensory                                                |
| Occipital             | Simple visual                                                |
When accompanied by impairment of awareness, focal seizures typically last 30-180s and have a ‘classic’ postictal phase with confusion and amnesia. 

Generalised seizures, too, fall into some known patterns:
| Pattern                  | Signs                                                        | Postictal Symptoms                              |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Absence                  | Brief behavioural arrest, automatisms; only applicable to a specific syndrome; characterised by a 3Hz spike on EEG | None                                            |
| Myoclonic                | Sudden brief twitching of limbs or axial muscles, usually with preserved consciousness |                                                 |
| Tonic                    | Co-contraction of agonist and antagonist muscles; ‘pure’ tonic seizures typically only occur in Lennox-Gastault syndrome |                                                 |
| Atonic                   | So-called ‘drop attacks’                                     |                                                 |
| Clonic                   | Repetitive, jerking movements                                |                                                 |
| Generalised Tonic-Clonic | An initial tonic phase, followed by clonic movements<br>By definition, awareness is impaired | Amnesia, confusion, incontinence, tongue-biting |
