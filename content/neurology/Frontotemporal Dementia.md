### Overview
FTD encompasses three well-defined syndromes:
- Behavioural-variant frontotemporal dementia -> tends to affect the frontal lobe +/- nondominant temporal lobe
- Semantic primary progressive aphasia -> tends to affect the temporal lobe
- Nonfluent primary progressive aphasia -> tends to affect the temporal lobe
There is, additionally, a _very_ rare variant called _motoric_ FTD, that is characterised by impairments in attention, executive function, and spatial skills with retained memory. 

These are typically tauopathies, traditionally thought to have a slight male preponderance. Onset is typically in the 5th to 7th decade of life. These conditions have a strong association with the motor neurone diseases, including [[amyotrophic lateral sclerosis]].

#### bvFTD
Diagnostic criteria for bvFTD require three of:
- Apathy/inertia
- Lack of empathy
- Disinhibition
- Impaired executive function
- Hyperorality
- Perseverative/compulsive behaviours

Associated with argyrophillic inclusions on histopathology. There is no role for cholinesterase inhibitors in bvFTD as they may _exacerbate_ behavioural symtpoms. The main gene involved in familial FTD is _MAPT_ (not to be confused with ALS-FTD where the gene implicated is C9orf72). 

#### Semantic PPA 
Semantic PPA is defined by impaired single word comprehension and object naming with preserved fluency, repetition, and grammar. 

#### Nonfluent PPA 
Nonfluent PPA is defined by a profound inability to produce words, often accompanied by severe articulatory difficulty.
