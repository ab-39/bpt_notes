### Overview
Limbic encephalitis is one of the most common phenotypes of non-infectious encephalitis, and is characterised by acute to subacute onset of short-term memory impairment, behavioural changes, altered mental status, and seizures. It is most commonly limited to the mesial temporal lobes, only infrequently extending beyond that area. 

It is marked by the presence of neuronal antibodies with specific associations:
- Anti-Hu (ANNA-1 and ANNA-3) - intracellular antigens, associated with [[oncology/Small Cell Lung Cancer|small cell lung cancer]]
- Anto-LUZP4 - intracellular antigen, associated with germ cell tumours
- Anti-Ta/Ma2 - intracellular antigens, associated with [[testicular cancer]] (particularly germ cell tumours) and [[oncology/Lung Cancer|lung cancer]] 
- Anti-AMPAR - extracellular antigen associated with SCLC and thymoma
- Anti-GABAbR - extracellular antigen associated with SCLC
- Anti-mGluR5 - extracellular antigen associated with [[Hodgkin lymphoma]] 
- Anti-Yo - associated with breast cancer and cerebellar signs 

Treatment is with immunotherapy and treatment of the underlying tumour, and (in general) syndromes associated with antibodies against extracellular targets tend to have a more favourable prognosis.
