# Entrapment Neuropathies
#bpt/neurology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Peripheral nerve entrapment, a form of [[Acquired Peripheral Neuropathy]], is a very common problem, associated with trauma, occupational exposure, and pregnancy. The most common form is carpal tunnel syndrome, and other forms include ulnar palsy at the elbow, peroneal entrapment at the fibular head, and Saturday night palsy of the radial nerve. Entrapment is diagnosed on EMG.  

#### Carpal Tunnel Syndrome 
Caused by the entrapment of the median nerve in the flexor retinaculum, this presents with a pain/paraesthesia that is typically worsened by use of the hand and at night. As some fibres of the median nerve travel over the FR, this is typically palm-sparing in terms of sensory loss. In terms of examination findings, the thenar muscles are usually wasted, and Tinel's (pray) and Phalen’s (shake wrist -> improved symptoms) signs are usually positive. Diagnosis is usually with EMG or ultrasound, and treatment is usually with decompression +/- perineural steroids.
