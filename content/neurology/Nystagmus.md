# Nystagmus
#bpt/neurology
- - -
### Overview
The term _nystagmus_ describes a rhythmic, involuntary eye movement that is made up of two phases:
- A _fast_ phase - the direction of the fast phase is how we call the nystagmus - e.g. if the nystagmus is faster 
- beating to the left it is _left-beating_ nystagmus
- A _slow_ phase - the opposite direction to the fast phase, indicative of the actual underlying abnormality

Beyond this, nystagmus can be:
- Unidirectional
- Direction-changing
- Vertical
- Horizontal
- Torsional
- Spontaneous
- Evoked 

#### Central vs Peripheral Nystagmus
Peripheral nystagmus occurs due to a problem in the vestibular system on one side:
- Suppose the right vestibular apparatus is hypofunctional
- There is thus relatively less activity on the right side compared to the left side
- The brain _interprets_ this as movement towards the left
- The vestibuloocular reflex will cause the gaze to deviate slowly to the right
- There will then be a fast leftward corrective saccade to maintain primary gaze

Central nystagmus occurs due to a brainstem or cerebellar issue that makes _holding_ gaze diffult. This leads to a general problem where the eyes drift back to primary gaze slowly, which is followed by a saccade to return to where the gaze is meant to be fixed - hence, direction changing.
