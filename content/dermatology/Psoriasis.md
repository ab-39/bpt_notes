### Overview
Psoriasis is a common chronic inflammatory condition of the skin characterised by pink-red, well-demarcated plaques with silvery-white scales on the extensor surfaces of the limb, the lower back, and scalp. These are frequently precipitated by local trauma (Köbner phenomenon), are often itchy or painful, and frequently bleed if the scales are removed. Nail changes affect up to 50% of patients with psoriasis and include:
- Pitting of the nail plate
- Distal separation of the nail plate - onycholysis
- Subungual hyperkeratosis

Around 40% of patients, particularly those with severe psoriasis, develop [[psoriatic arthritis]]. 

Management is stratified according to disease therapy:
- Topical therapy with vitamin D analogies (typically for chronic plaque psoriasis) - e.g. calcipotriol
- Phototherapy 
- Systemic therapy is typically reserved for cases refractory to the first two lines of treatment, or associated with significant patient distress - e.g. MTX, CsA, acitretin (retinoid)
- Biologics
