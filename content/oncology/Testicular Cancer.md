### Overview
Testicular cancer is a _highly curable_ cancer of young men, even in the presence of relapsed metastatic disease. 
![](Testicular%20Cancer/image.png)

About half of patients will present with a _pure seminoma_, that is characterised by **_not_** producing ⍺-fetoprotein, only β-HCG and LDH. These generally have a low risk of recurrence, but are considered high risk if:
- ≥4cm in size
- Invading the rete testis 
A single dose of high-dose carboplatin has been found to reduce recurrence risk to ~5%. Radiotherapy to local lymphatics is not recommended due to the risk of secondary malignancy.

Slightly less than half of patients present with _non-seminomatous_ testicular cancers, that are characterised by producing ⍺-fetoprotein in addition to β-HCG and LDH.  These have a much higher risk of recurrence, particularly if there is lymphovascular invasion or if the cancer is of the embryonal subtype. Metastasis to the lungs alone is not a poor prognostic sign. Orchiectomy is typically followed by 1-2 cycles of bleomycin/etoposide/platinum chemotherapy, and very occasionall retroperitoneal lymph node dissection. Side effects of BEP include:
- Alopecia
- Pulmonary toxicity (no good screening method) - bleomycin
- Tinnitus
- Peripheral neuropathy
Additionally, there is a high risk of secondary malignancies, particularly acute leukaemias and myelodysplastic syndrome. 

After tretment of a non-seminomatous cancer, AFP is expected to drop rapidly. 

The remaining few percent of patients present with sex-cord stromal tumours:
- Leydig cell tumours (~4%) -> directly secrete androgens (and to a lesser extent oestrogens)
- Sertoli cell tumours (<1%) -> secrete aromatase leading to elevated oestrogen levels
