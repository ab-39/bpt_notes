### Overview
Histologically, renal cell carcinoma encompasses five distinct phenotypes:
1. Clear cell renal carcinoma:
   - ~75% of cases
   - Associated with mutations in the _VHL_ gene
2. Type 1 papillary renal carcinoma:
   - ~5% of cases
   - Associated with mutations in the _MET_ gene
3. Type 2 papillary renal carcinoma:
   - ~10% of cases
   - Associated with mutations in the _FH_ gene
4. Chromophobe renal carcinoma
5. Oncocytoma
   - Both ~5% ea 
   - Associated with mutations in the _BHD_ gene 

Most treatments discussed are for clear cell carcinoma. 
- - -
### Aetiopathogenesis
Mutations in the _VHL_ gene lead to a cascade whereby HIF and ultimately VEGF are activated. Tumours tend to arise from the PCT.

### Prognosis and Treatment
The most commonly utilised prognostic risk score for metastatic clear cell renal carcinom ais the _IMDC_ score, comprising:
- Performance status
- Neutrophil count
- Platelet count (low is better)
- Serum calcium
- Haemoglobin (low is worse)
- Lag time between diagnosis and treatment 

A score above three indicates very poor survival. 

Historically, treatment for mccRCC involved cytokines, predominantly IL-2, which were effective but highly toxic. Now, we have shifted to three main drug classes:
1. TKIs
   - Predominantly multitarget, often against VEGF, AXL, or c-MET, e.g.:
     - Sunitinib - multitarget 
     - Pazopanib - multitarget
     - Cabozantinib - VEGF + c-MET 
     - Axitinib - predominantly VEGF
     - Lenvatinib - VEGF
     - Sorafenib - multitarget (_TARGET_)
2. Immunotherapy, either:
   - PD1 monotherapy - e.g. nivolumab
   - CTLA4 + PD1 combination therapy - e.g. + ipilimumab (_CHECKMATE-214_) 
   - Standard of care - pembrolizumab is the only adjuvant agent to significantly prolong disease free survival for patients with resected ccRCC
3. mTOR inhibition
