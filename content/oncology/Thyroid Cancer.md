### Overview
|                                             | **Papillary** (well-differentiated)                          | **Follicular** (well-differentiated)                         | **Anaplastic** (poorly differentiated)                       | **Medullary** (poorly differentiated)                        |
|---------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| *Epidemiology*                              | Most common, generally ages 25-50y                           | Most common ages 40-60y; 3:1 female predilection             | Mean age at diagnosis of 65y                                 | Most commonly ages 40-50y if sporadic (or FMTC); early decades if MEN  |
| *Genetics/Risk Factors*                     | RET/PTC fusion gene, BRAF mutations; ionising radiation      | RAS, PIK3CA gain of function mutations; PAX8/PPARG fusion gene; iodine deficiency | Generally associated with a history of previous well-differentiated thyroid cancer; any mutation; TP53 | Associated with MEN2A/2B and mutations in the RET gene (also FMTC) |
| *Pathological Features* <br> <br> <br> <br> | Diagnostic features: optically clear nuclei (‘Orphan Annie’), nuclear pseudo-inclusions; papillae with psammoma bodies<br><br>   | Tend to be single nodules that can be well-circumscribed or widely infiltrative; may be dominated by large, eosinophilic cells (oncocytic variant) | Highly variable, anaplastic morphology (pleomorphic giant cells, spindle cells, mixed)<br> | Sporadic: single nodules<br>Familial: multifocal, bilateral nodules with C-cell hyperplasia  |
| *Clinical Features*                         | Usually present as a thyroid nodule, palpable or impalpable have the same risk of being cancer | Tend to present as a solitary, enlarging nodule; lymphatic metastasis is quite rare but some subtypes metastasise aggressively via the blood | Rapidly enlarging bulky neck mass, often with symptoms related to local invasion (e.g. hoarseness). | Neck mass; often get effects from secreted hormones e.g. VIP, ACTH. Calcitonin may be elevated without hypocalcaemia.  |
| *Treatment and Prognosis*                   | Surgery if >1cm +/- radioiodine ablation if BL; excellent prognosis (10y MS >95%) | Surgery if >1cm +/- radioiodine ablation if BL; prognosis depends on stage at diagnosis   | No effective therapies, death usually occurs within 1y. Radiotherapy may occasionally work | Surgery; MEN2B tends to be the most aggressive               |
| *Monitoring*                                | Ultrasound, thyroglobulin                                    | Ultrasound, thyroglobulin                                    |                                                              | Ultrasound, calcitonin                                       |

Other points:
- TSH suppression reduced the risk of recurrence in indviduals with intermediate-to-high risk disease 
- Recurrence is generally treated with surgery or radioactive iodine, or _lenvatinib_ if these cannot be used
