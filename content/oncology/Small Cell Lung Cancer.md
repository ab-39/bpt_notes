# Small Cell Lung Cancer

### What is small cell lung cancer?
Small cell lung cancer is a relatively rare (~15%) subtype of [[oncology/Lung Cancer|lung cancer]] that is characterised by:
- An exceptionally high proliferative rate
- A high concentration of circulating tumour cells
- A strong predilection for early metastasis 
- Poor prognosis 

Histopathologically, SCLC falls into two subtypes:
- Pure - 80% of cases
- Combined with large cell carcinoma or neuroendocrine carcinoma 
- - -
### Epidemiology
SCLC is _very strongly_ associated exposure to tobacco carcinogens. Stage-for-stage, SCLC is more fatal than [[oncology/Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer|non-small cell lung cancer]]. As the rate of smoking is declining, so are the rates of SCLS.
- - -
### Presentation
The primary tumour is typically central. ~2/3rds of presentations with SCLC are with metastatic disease at diagnosis, while the remaining 1/3rd is potentially curable with multimodal therapy. 

Most patients typically present with respiratory symptoms - including cough, dyspnoea, and haemoptysis. Bulky thoracic nodal involvement is common. 
- - -
### Metastases
Metastases is SCLC are common to:
- Contralateral lung
- Brain (~50% of patients)  -> all patients should get a CTB
- Liver
- Adrenals
- Bone
- - -
### Paraneoplastic Syndromes
SCLC is strongly associated with the development of paraneoplastic syndromes, including:
- SIADH
- Cushing syndrome
- LEMS
- Encephalomyelitis
- Sensory neuropathy syndromes
- Dermatomyositis
- Hyperglycaemia
- Hypoglycaemia
- Hypercalcaemia
- Gynaecomastia 
- - -
### Approach to SCLC
SCLC moves quickly, so too must the physician. 

Staging is typically done with the TNM system, although the older Veterans’ Affairs system is also used: this splits patients into limited-stage disease, who can be treated with CRT, and extensive disease, who are treated with systemic chemotherapy.

CT-chest is typically unable to detect SCLC at earlier stages, and so does not improve survival. 
- Smoking cessation is the most effective early intervention for SCLC

Treatment - tends to be very chemotherapy sensitive:
- localised disease -> chemoradiotherapy (+/- prophylactic cranial radiotherapy to reduce cerebral metastases) 
- metastatic disease -> chemotherapy + immunotherapy (+/- consolidation chest radiotherapy for patients who respond well) 
  - standard chemotherapy is cisplatin-etoposide + atezolizumab; but relapse rates are very high
