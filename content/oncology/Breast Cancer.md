### Epidemiology
Breast cancer is the most commonly diagnosed new cancer worldwide, with an approximate worldwide annual incidence of 2.3 million cases.

Mortality has significantly improved in the last fifty years since the widespread introduction of screening programs and imrovements in treatment in stage I-III breast cancer.

### Diagnosis
“Triple test”:
- Physical examination
- Imaging - mammography or ultrasound 
- Biopsy

### Phenotypes
Disease phenotype informs treatment, and is defined primarily in terms of cell-surface receptors:
|                                              | HR+ve/HER2-ve<br>“Luminal A-like”                            | HER2+ve                                             | Triple -ve                                                   |
|----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| *Oestrogen/progesterone receptor expression* | ≥1% of tumour cells                                          | Any                                                 | <1% of tumour cells                                          |
| *Ki67 index*                                 | Low                                                          | High                                                | High                                                         |
| *HER2-amplification*                         | Negative                                                     | Positive                                            | Negative                                                     |
| *Genetic association*                        | *BRCA2*                                                      | *TP53*                                              | *BRCA1*                                                      |
| *Proportion of new cases*                    | ~2/3rds                                                      | ~20%                                                | ~15%                                                         |
| *Risk of disease recurrence*                 | Good prognosis but late recurrences (primarily to bone) can occur | Previously poor, now excellent due to new therapies | Poorest prognosis but late recurrences are uncommon (liver and lung) |
| *Effective treatment*                        | Endocrine therapy +/- chemotherapy                           | Immunotherapy + chemotherapy +/- endocrine therapy  | Chemotherapy +/- immunotherapy                               |

#### Nodal Status
Nodal status is another important determinant of treatment requirements. In general:
- LN+ve -> higher risk of recurrence and death -> (neo)adjuvant chemotherapy is recommended
- LN-ve -> lower risk of recurrence and death -> benefit of (neo)adjuvant chemotherapy is unclear 

#### Intrinsic Subtypes
Gene expression patterns can be used to identify further intrinsic subtypes of the three main varieties of breast cancer, guiding adjuvant therapy:
- HR+ve -> luminal A and luminal B
- HER2+ve -> HER2-enriched
- Triple-ve -> basal-like

Tools such as OncotypeDX, a 21 gene expression assay, can be used to provide informationon both genotypes and recurrence scores. Higher recurrence scores are associated with benefit from chemotherapy.

### Risk Factors 
Non-modifiable:
- Age
- Family history
- Genetics
- Early menarche
- Late menopause
- Exposure to radiation
- Breast density

Modifiable:
- Parity (fewer is worse)
- Age at first child (older is worse)
- Not breastfeeding
- Using HRT (small RR)
- Using hormonal birth control methods
- Processed foods
- Lack of exercise
- Obesity 

Genetics:
- _BRCA1/2_ - 50% of genetic predisposition, about 6% of breast cancers	
  - Note ~0.5% of the population carries a _BRCA_ variant
  - Autosomal dominant transmission
  - Highly penetrant - ~70% lifetime risk of breast cancer in women, ~4% in men
- _PALB2_ - second highest risk 
  - High absolute risk (~50%) of breast cancer, less strong association with pancreatic and ovarian cancer 
- _CHEK2_
- _PTEN_
- _ATM_
- _TP53_
- Lynch syndrome

#### Who should be tested for _BRCA_ mutations?
Consider:
- Individuals diagnosed with breast cancer ≤40y
- Bilateral breast cancer or ≥2 synchronous or asynchronous breast cancers with first cancer diagnosed at age ≤50y
- Triple-negative pathology diagnosed ≤60y or at any age if the individual would potentially be eligible for adjuvant therapy with a PARP inhibitor
- HR+/HER2- breast cancer and ≥1 high risk feature:
  - Grade 3+
  - Lymph node positive
  - Size >20mm
- Male breast cancer diagnosed at any age
- High grade epithelial ovarian cancer

### Screening
In Australia: 
- Funded self-initiated mammography from 40-49
- Invitations to mammography q2y from 50-74y
- Funded self-initiated mammography from 75y onwards
Mammography is less effective in women with dense breasts, which includes all pre-menopausal women. Ultrasonography is recommended in these women. 

For patients with pathogenic variants:
~Breast~:
- MRI +/- USS from age 25-30y
- MRI + mammogram from age 40-60y
- Mammogram alone from age 60y

~Ovarian~:
- No evidence of benefit for screening with CA125 **or** ultrasound

~Other cancers~:
- Males with _BRCA2_ -> annual PSA from age 40y

~Intervention~:
- Bilateral prophylactic mastectomy - most benefit at age <40y
- Risk reducing BSO once faimly complete or over age 35/40y (_BRCA1/2_)

### Treatment
#### Curative vs Non-Curative Intent
Stage I-III disease = disease that is _resectable_ or potentially resectable:
- No distant metastasis 
- _May_ be nodal metastasis 
- Main treatment is surgery +/- neoadjuvant +/- adjuvant chemoradiotherapy 
- This is generally followed by long term hormone blockade 

Stage IV disease = unresectable disease (i.e. usually metastatic) -> palliative approach:
- _May_ consider aggressive treatment in some patients:
  - HER2+ve disease 
  - Few metastases (‘oligometastatic disease’)

#### Early Breast Cancer
Early breast cancer -> surgery:
- Breast conserving (e.g. wide local excision) **or**
- Mastectomy +/-
- Nodal biopsy -> clearance if indicated 
  - Complication of clearance is lymphoedema 

Neoadjuvant therapy is indicated in the following patient groups:
- Triple negative - evidence for benefit of achieving pathological complete response 
  - Tend to use platinum based chemotherapy + pembrolizumab
- HER2+ve - particularly with high risk features (e.g. large tumour, nodal involvement)
- HR+ve/HER2-ve - downstaging and downsizing tumour to facilitate breast conserving surgery

NACTx is useful for:
- Refining prognostic estimates - achievement of pathological complete response suggests excellent prognosis
- Conversion of a tumour from unresectable to potentially resectable
- Refining understanding of a given patient’s cancer biology 

Adjuvant therapies include:
- Chemotherapy +/- anti-HER2 therapy or immunotherapy
  - Often anthracycline and taxane based regimens - tend to be used in triple negative breast cancer as the high Ki67 makes these sensitive to conventional chemotherapy
    - Commonly capecitabine +/- pembrolizumab
  - Dose-_escalation_ strategies are not associated with mortality benefit, while dose-_dense_ strategies are 
  - For HER2+ve early breast cancer treated with adjuvant chemotherapy-trastuzumab-pertuzumab and then surgery, adjuvant treatment is determined by pathologic complete response:
    - pCR achieved -> one year adjuvant trastuzumab-pertuzumab
    - pCR **not** achieved -> switch to trastuzumab-emtansine (TDM1) for 5% improvement in OS
- Radiotherapy
- Endocrine therapy +/- CD4/6 inhibitors (-ciclibs)
- Bisphosphonates - significant reduction in clinical fractures and bone recurrences, with a small signal for mortality benefit
  - General regimen is six-monthly zoledronate for 3y
- Fertility preservation
  - Offer ovarian suppression during systemic chemotherapy

#### HER2-positive Breast Cancer
The _APHINITY_ trial found that adjuvant _trastuzumab_, an anti-ERBB2/HER2 monoclonal antibody significantly improves outcomes for HER2+ve tumours, and is associated with significantly improved survival. Although the optimal duration of treatment is unclear, it suggests that there is no benefit to courses longer than twelve months, while courses under twelve months are potentially inferior. Additionally, adding additional HER2 blockade (e.g. with TKIs such as lapatinib or other antibodies such as pertuzumab) also shows no benefit. 

Newer drugs such as **_pertuzumab_** bind to both HER2 and HER3 during the process of heterodimerisation to block downstream activation. 

As such, current guidelines recommend 52 weeks of adjuvant trastuzumab. There can be a role for additional paclitaxel-based chemotherapy, allowing for conventional anthracycline-based chemotherapy (and its concomitant cardiotoxicity) to be avoided. 

The _KATHERINE_ trial, additionally, found that trastuzumab-based antibody-drug conjugates do improve survival.

#### Endocrine Therapies
Adjuvant endocrine therapies are associated with a reduction in recurrence risk and mortality. They fall into two main drug classes:
|                          | Tamoxifen                                                    | Aromatase Inhibitors<br>e.g. letrozole, exemastane           |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| *Mechanism of Action*    | Selective oestrogen receptor modulator<br>Antagonist of oestrogen at the breast<br>Agonist of oestrogen at bone and uterus | Inihibition of oestrogen biosynthesis                        |
| *Common Toxicities*      | Hot flushes<br>Arthralgias (less)<br>Vaginal atrophy (less)  | Hot flushes<br>Arthralgias (more)<br>Vaginal atrophy (more)  |
| *Significant Toxicities* | VTE<br>Endometrial cancer<br>Fulvestrant: hepatotoxicity     | Accelerated bone loss<br>Paradoxical increase in oestrogen levels (pre-menopausal women) |
| *Clinical Utility*       | Pre- and post-menopausal                                     | Post-menopausal only (or premenopausal if ovarian suppression) |
| *Note*                   | Prodrug - metabolism and thus effect reduced by CYP2D6 inhibitors (e.g. SSRIs particularly paroxetine) | Slightly superior to tamoxifen - generally preferred in moderate-high risk patients |

General duration is:
- Tamoxifen -> 10y
- Aromatase inhibitors -> 5y
Can switch between the two if needed (e.g. 5y tamoxifen + 5y AIs)

#### Metastatic Breast Cancer
Metastatic (stage IV) breast cancer is generally considered incurable, and treatment follows a palliative approach. In general, if a patient presents with metastatic recurrence, there should be rebiopsy due to the high rates of discordance in the hormone receptor profile. Treatment approach depends on receptor profile:
- Hormone receptor positive disease:
  - Endocrine therapy +/- CDK4/6 inhibitors
  - Chemotherapy - particularly for bulky or rapidly progressive disease
  - Bisphosphonates
- HER2+ve disease:
  - Doublet anti-HER2 therapy with trastuzumab _and_ pertuzumab
  - Chemotherapy
  - Consideration of antibody-drug conjugates (e.g. trastuzumab-emtansine, trastuzumab-deruxtecan)
- Triple negative disease:
  - Chemotherapy +/- immunotherapy
  - Antibody-drug conjugates (e.g. sacituzumab-govitecan)

Chemotherapy is usually offered in a sequential, single-agent format, as there is significant risk of toxicity without evidence of mortality benefit when using combination treatment. The exceptions are if there is rapid clinical progression, life-threatening visceral metastases, or need for symptom control. Common regimens are based on taxanes or anthracyclines.

#### Novel Targeted Therapies in Breast Cancer
![](Breast%20Cancer/image.png)
Cyclin-dependent kinase 4 and 6 inhibitors are now the mainstay treatment of hormone-receptor positive metastatic breast cancer by inhibiting cell cycle progression in G1. CDK4/6 works in combination with cyclin D1 to stimulate Rb/E2F and encourage progression through the cell cycle (and thus replication) in response to mitogenic signals such as from oestrogen. This needs to be combined with endocrine therapy to more completely inhibit this pathway. Examples of CD4/6 inhibitors include:
- Abemaciclib - the _MONARCH_ 3 trial found improvements in mortality 
- Palbociclib - the _PALOMA-2_ trial found an improvement in progression-free survival but not overall survival 
- Ribociclib - the _MONALEESA-2_ study found statistically significant improvements in 5y mortality
All three agents provide significant progression free survival benefit in combination with endocrine therapy in patietns with HR+ve, HER2-ve metastatic breast cancer, but the preference is for ribociclib or abemaciclib given the improvements in mean overall survival. Side-effects include fatigue, myelosuppression, QT prolongation, and diarrhoea. 

PI3-kinase inhibitors work upstream of CDKs to also attempt to inihibit cell proliferation, as genetic alterations in this pathway tend to be enriched in breast cancer. There are four isoforms of the p110 subunit of PI3K: α, β, γ, and δ. While pan-isoform inhibitors exist, they tend to be highly toxic. Newer drugs, such as _**alpelesib**_ target only the ⍺ isoform, and have been found to significantly improve progression free survival when used in combination with fulvestrant for patients with hormone resistant HR+ve advanced breast cancer **and** the PIK3CA mutation (_SOLAR-1_). Toxicities include, rash, hyperglycaemia, and N/V/D. These are yet to be publicly funded in Australia. 

PARP-inhibitors, such as ***olaparib*** exploit the deficiencies of _BRCA_ positive tumours in homologous repair of DNA. In the absence of the ability to repair double stranded breaks in DNA, cancerous cells rely on base-excision repair. PARP-inhibitors block this process from occurring, leading to accumulated DNA breakages and synthetic lethality:
![](Breast%20Cancer/image%204.png)
These lead to substantial improvements in disease-free survival, particularly for ovarian cancers, but also in breast, pancreatic, and prostate cancer where there is a _BRCA_ mutation. 

[[oncology/Immune Checkpoint Inhibition|Immune checkpoint inhibition]], particularly tumour cell PDL-1 blockade with _**pembrolizumab**_, has demonstrated improvements in progression free survival with patients with advanced triple negative breast cancer, but only in patients with tumours with high expression of PD-L1 as measured by a CPS score >10 (_KEYNOTE-355_). 

There are also three [[oncology/Antibody-Drug Conjugates|antibody-drug conjugates]] approved for use in breast cancer:
- TDM1 - trastuzumab emtansine -> used in HER2+ breast cancer
  - ADRs: thrombocytopenia, peripheral neuropathy
- TDXD - trastuzumab deruxtecan - associated with significant improvement in PFS and OS (_DESTINY_) -> used in patients with metastatic ER+/HER2 low breast cancer
  - Neutropenia, nausea, pneumonitis
  - Pneumonitis can be a severe side effect of TDXD -> regular screening CT chest
- SG - sacituzumab (anti-TROP2) govitecan - significant improvement in PFS and OS in pre-treated patients with metastatic triple negative cancer (_ASCENT_)
  - Neutropenia, diarrhoea
Side-effects include N/V/D, alopecia, myelosuppression, and treatment-induced interstitial lung disease. 

#### Treatment and Pregnancy
The [_POSITIVE_ study](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2212856) found that in women with endocrine-responsive breast cancer, interrupting adjuvant endocrine therapy for up to two years to allow for conception, delivery, and breastfeeding was _not_ associated with worse outcomes.
