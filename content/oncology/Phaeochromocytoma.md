# Phaeochromocytoma
#bpt/endocrinology #bpt/oncology

### Overview
Phaeochromocytoma and paraganglioma are types neuroendocrine tumours either of the adrenal medulla (phaeochromocytoma) or arising elsewhere with similar biochemical effects (paraganglioma). While uncommon, these should be suspected in patients with classical symptoms: palpitations, headaches, and sweating int he presence of hypertension, or those with a history of [[Hereditary Cancer Syndromes#Key Cancer Syndromes|known germline mutations]] (e.g. MEN2, VHL, NF1) or adrenal incidentalomas. 

The key diagnostic test is plasma and urinary metanephrines, breakdown products of adrenaline (due to catechol-O-methyltransferase. While catecholamines are physiologically produced in a pulsatile fashion, PPGLs lead to a steady secretion of catecholamines, leading to relatively stable levels of metanephrines in the blood and urine:
- Urinary/plasma metanephrines >3x ULN is diagnostic of a phaeochromocytoma

Intermediate results could be due to illness or interfering medications, including those with:
- Pharmacological effects - e.g. TCAs, SNRIs, MAOis, L-DOPA, methyldopa, β-blockers, etc.
- Analytical effects - e.g. caffeine, paracetamol, 5-ASA, mesalazine 

> [!WARNING] Caution
> There have been cases of fatalities due to catecholaminergic crisis following dexamethasone suppression testing in phaeochromocytoma.

Further testing can involve a _clonidine suppression test_, which should suppress metanephrine levels within three hours of administration. If metanephrine levels remains:
- ≥ULN
- <40% from pre-test level
This is suggestive of a PPGLs. Predominantly elevated _metanephrines_ suggest an adrenal phaeochromocytoms, while predominantly elevated _normetanephrines_ suggest an extra-adrenal PPGL:![](Phaeochromocytoma/image.png)
Atypically, von Hippel-Lindau disease is associated with relatively _low_ levels of metanephrines, due to a relative deficiency of the enzyme phenylethanolamine-N-methyltransferase, which converts noradrenaline to adrenaline. 

CT adrenal with contrast is generally sufficient or localising a phaeochromocytoma, but a dotatate-PET scan may be required for locating other paragangliomas.

Management is with surgery, or peptide receptor radionuclide therapy (PRRT, which is used in several types of neuroendocrine cancers). It requires somatostatin receptor expression on the tumour to be effective.
