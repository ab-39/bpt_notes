### Chromosomal Instability
Chromosomal instability refers to ongoing chromosome segregation errors throughout consecutive cell divisions, ultimately leading to aneuploidy. This can fall into two categories:
- Numeric - i.e. aneuploidy
- Structural - i.e. micronuclei
![](Genomic%20Instability/image.png)
Around 70% of tumours demonstrate chromosomal abnormalities suggestive of instability.

### Microsatellite Instability
Microsatellites refer to di-, tri-, or tetranucleotide tandem repeats. Microsatellite instability refers to genetic hypermutability that arises as a consequence of impaired mismatch repair. Tumours that have disruptions of the MSI genes are referred to as _mismatch repair deficient_ (dMMR). Thus, while MSI and MMR are interrelated concepts, they are _not_ the same thing:
- Microsatellite instability is tested by genetic analysis looking for repeats -> analysis of the DNA sequence
- Mismatch repair is tested by immunohistochemistry looking for expressed protein -> _loss_ of staining is abnormal

In terms of _how_ mismatch repair is carried out, there are four main proteins that act as two complexes:
- MLH1-PMS2
- MSH2-MSH6
![](Genomic%20Instability/image%202.png)
Microsatellite instability leads to the development of aberrant proteins that can function as neoantigens. This makes immunotherapy _highly_ effective for dMMR tumours -> _pembrolizumab_ is frequently the standard of care.
