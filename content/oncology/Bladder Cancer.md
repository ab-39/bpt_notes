### Overview
The vast majority of bladder cancers in the developed world are _urothelial_ or _transitional cell_ carcinomas, accounting for >90% of cases. Outside the developed world, however, squamous cell carcinoma is the most common due to the major contributory role of _Schistosoma haematobium_. Adenocarcinoma is a much less common phenotype, and is treated like a GI tumour. 

The key landmark for staging is the extent to which bladder cancer invades into muscle:
- Non-muscle invasive = T1 = high risk of superficial recurence, low risk of progression to invasive disease
- Muscle invasive = T2/T3a = poor prognosis
- Extending beyond muscle = T3b/4

Treatment of MIBC focuses on three main modalities:
- TURBT or radical cystectomy 
- Chemotherapy - current regimens are usually GC (platinium + antimetabolite) or else dose-dense MVAC (MTX/vinca/anthracycline/cisplatin)
  - Good evidence now exists for maintenance PDL-1 inhibition (_avelumab_) in patients who have responded to platinum-based CTx (_JAVELIN_) as well as PD-1 inhibition (_pembrolizumab_) in non-responders (_KEYNOTE_)
  - Newer therapies include various classes of antibody drug conjugates and targeted therapiesthat are typically 3rd+ line for metastatic urothelial carcinoma where there has been progression on conventional CTx and immunotherapy:
    - Enfortumab (anti-nectin 4) vedotin (EV)
    - FGFR inhibitors, paticularly mUC originating from the upper renal tract - _erdafitinib_, a pan-FGFR inhibitor
- Radiotherapy 

The recent _EV-302_ study has the potential to be practice-changing, finding significant survival benefit for EV + pembrolizumab as _first line_ for metastatic urothelial cancer, almost doubling the median survival time.
