# Superior Vena Cava Syndrome
#bpt/oncology
- - -
### Overview
Compression of the SVC due to either external compression or direct invasion by a tumour, most commonly due to lung cancer ot lymphoma. Particularly common in small cell lung cancer, where it is the presenting complaint in ~10% of cases. 

Clinical symptoms include:
- Swelling of the face/neck/arm
- Chest pain
- Respiratory symptoms
- Neurologic manifestations

Grading:
1. Asymptomatic
2. Asymptomatic
3. Severe symptoms
4. Life threatening symptoms
5. Death

Aims of treatment = alleviate symptoms +/- treat underlying disease. Options include:
- Glucocorticoids
- Endovenous recanalisation and stenting
  - May require DAPT
- Radiation therapy
- Chemotherapy (if tumour sensitive)
