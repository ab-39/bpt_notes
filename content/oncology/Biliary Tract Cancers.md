### Overview
Biliary tract cancers are a heterogeneous group of cancers that can arise in three main spaces:
- Within the liver = intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma
- Without the liver = extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma
- In the gall bladder = gallbladder carcinoma

These are predominantly adenocarcinomas, and they are quite rare, with an incidence of <2/100,000/yr. The symptoms tend to be fairly nonspecific - jaundice, weight loss, RUQ pain, etc.

Risk factors include:
- [[Primary Biliary Cholangitis]]
- Chronic liver disease
- Viral hepatitis
- Cholelithiasis
- Obesity 
- MetSyn
- Parasite infection - the Mekong river fluke (Opisthorchis viverinii)
- Lynch syndrome
- BAP1 tumour predisposition syndrome

Like pancreatic cancer, CA19-9 has a role in monitoring. Investigations are also similar - ERCP/PTC, CT, MRCP/MRI, etc.

The mainstay of treatment is chemoimmunotherapy as established with TOPAZ1:
- Durvalumab + GEM/cisplatin

There are also emerging targeted small molecule inhibitors:
- FGFR2 - pemigatinib, infigratinib, futibatinib, derazantinib
- IDH1 - ivosidenib
- BRAF - dabrafenib, trametinib
- ERBB - neratinib
