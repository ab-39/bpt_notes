### Overview 
In general, ‘conventional’ [[oncology/Chemotherapeutics|chemotherapeutics]] cause systemic side effects as their mechanism of action targets _any_ rapidly dividing cell.

Hence, we see that the most common side effects are general:
- Fatigue
- Myelosuppression
- GI toxicity:
  - Diarrhoea
  - Nausea
  - Hepatic dysfunction
- Alopecia 
- - -
### Nausea and Chemotherapy
Nausea is a _very_ common side effect of chemotherapy, due to the relative permeability of the blood-brain barrier at the level of the area postrema at the floor of the fourth ventricle (proximate to the medulla):
| Emetogenicity | Drugs                                            | Antiemetics                                           |
|---------------|--------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| Low           | Taxanes, antimetabolites, vinorelbine            | Dexamethasone +/- metoclopramide                      |
| Intermediate  | Most platinum agents, toposiomerase I inhibitors | Dexamethasone +/- ondansetron                         |
| High          | Cisplatin, anthracyclines, alkylating agents     | Dexamethasone + ondansetron + aprepitant + olanzapine |

_Anticipatory_ nausea, however, is largely a conditioning response, and is best treated with behavioural therapy and lorazepam.

### Specific Side Effects 
| Drug Class                  | Drug Example            | Side Effects                                                 |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Antimetabolites             | 5FU, capecitabline      | Cardiac toxicity, including vasospasm; heart failure<br>Hand-foot syndrome: pain, erythema, and blistering of the palms and soles (palmoplantar erythrodysaesthesia); radiation recall dermatitis |
| Topoisomerase I Inhibitors  | Irinotecan              | Acute cholinergic syndrome: transient visual disturbances, dysarthria, diarrhoea<br>UGT1A1*28 (e.g. Gilbert) -> extra diarrhoea |
| Topoisomerase II Inhibitors | Doxorubicin, etoposide  | Cardiac toxicity, heart failure                              |
| Microtubule inhibitors      | Vincristine, paclitaxel | Peripheral neuropathy, nail changes                          |
| Alkylating agents           | Cyclophosphamide        | Haemorrhagic cystitis, pericarditis, highest risk of permanent amenorrhoea |
| Platinum agents             | Cisplatin, carbiplatin  | Ototoxicity, cold sensitivity, peripheral neuropathy         |

### Managing Side Effects
The key aspects to consider when considering chemotherapy toxicities are:
- Severity:
  - Grade I = not impairing ADLs
  - Grade II = impairing instrumental ADLs
  - Grade III = warranting admission
  - Grade IV = life-threatening
  - Grade V = dead 
- Reversibility:
  - Irreversible = platinum neurotoxicity, etc.
  - Reversible = most others
