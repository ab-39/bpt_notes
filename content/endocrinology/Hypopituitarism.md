# Hypopituitarism
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Overview
Causes are multiple, but the pattern is usually predictable (in order from first to last): GH -> LH/FSH -> TSH -> ACTH. 
- - -
### Vascular 
#### Pituitary Apoplexy
Haemorrhage or infarction of the pituitary is almost always associated with an existing tumour. Presents with severe headache, visual disturbance, altered consciousness, and cranial nerve palsies. 

In the acute phase, CT is the most sensitive test, after which MRI is superior. 

The acute deficiency of ACTH may be life threatening, so _replacement steroids must always be given_. In general, management can be conservative, but significant mass effect may require surgery.

#### Carotid Artery Aneurysm
Rare cause of hypopituitarism. 
- - -
### Iatrogenic
#### Post-Irradiation
Hypopituitarism is typically a late complication of pituitary radiotherapy, most commonly presenting with growth hormone deficiency. TSH deficiency is relatively rare. 
- - -
### Inflammatory
#### Primary Hypophysitis
1. Lymphocytic hypophysitis = 70% 
   - Most common in women, particularly perinatally
   - ACTH most common deficiency, GH and prolactin relatively rare
2. Granulomatous = 20%
3. IgG4-related = 4%
4. Xanthomatous = 1%

#### Secondary Hypophysitis
1. [[oncology/Immune Checkpoint Inhibition#Immune Related Adverse Effects|IRAEs]] 
   - Most common with CTLA-4 or combination drugs
   - Tend to see pre-contrast hypointensity of the posterior pituitary, and some post-contrast T1 hyperintensity on MRI 
   - Anti-CTLA4 -> panhypopituitarism
   - Anti-PD1 -> ACTH deficiency
2. Anti-PIT1 antibody syndrome:
   - Paraneoplastic syndrome, commonly from a thymoma, generally affecting GH, TSH, and prolactin
3. Inflammatory or infectious disorders - e.g. sarcoidosis, tuberculosis, etc.
