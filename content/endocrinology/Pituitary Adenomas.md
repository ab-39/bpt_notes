# Pituitary Adenomas
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Overview
A pituitary adenoma is a glandular growth of the pituitary gland. These tend to arise from neuroendocrine cells and have a low malignant potention. These can arise from three main embryological lineages:
1. _PIT1_ -> lactotroph, somatotroph, and thyrotroph cells
2. _TPIT_ lineage -> corticotorph cells
3. _SF1_ lineage -> gonadotroph cells 

There are two fundamental divisions among pituitary adenomas:
- Size: 
  - Microadenoma = <1cm -> hypodense with Gd contrast
    - Cannot cause visual field defects or hypopituitarism
  - Macroadenoma = >1cm
- Functionality:
  - Non-functioning = not secreting hormones
  - Functioning = secreting hormones

There are other lesions that can occur in the region of the pituitary gland:
- Rathke cyst - a remnant of Rathke’s pouch
- Chordomata - locally invasive tumours arising from the remnants of the notochord
- Craniopharyngiomas - common in children
  - Often _BRAF_ V600E positive	
  - ~10% of childhood tumours
- Meningiomas
- Optic gliomas = low-grade asrocytomas
- Hypothalmic masses - e.g. hamartoma, astrocytoma, etc.
- Metastases - usually to posterior pituitary
- Infiltrative diseases
- Lymphocytic hypophysitis
