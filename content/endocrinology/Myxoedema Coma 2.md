# Myxoedema Coma 
#bpt/endocrinology #bpt/fracprac
- - -
In suspected myxedema coma, empirical treatment with intravenous levothyroxine (synthetic T4) is often initiated without waiting for confirmation from blood tests. A loading dose of levothyroxine (200-400 mcg IV) is typically given followed by maintenance doses.
Other important treatment to consider for suspected myxedema coma before emergency blood test results are available includes:
1. Airway Management and Oxygen Support: Ensure the patient has a patent airway and provide supplemental oxygen to maintain adequate oxygenation.
2. Intravenous Fluids: Administer intravenous fluids to support circulation and correct any dehydration or hypotension.
3. Warming Measures: External warming measures, such as warm blankets or heating devices, should be used to help raise the patient’s temperature.
4. Monitoring and Supportive Care: Continuous monitoring of vital signs, including heart rate, blood pressure, and temperature, is crucial. Close monitoring in an intensive care setting is often necessary due to the high risk of complications.
5. Treatment of Associated Conditions: If any other concurrent conditions, such as infection or electrolyte abnormalities, are identified, appropriate treatment should be initiated.
Myxedema coma is a life-threatening condition that requires prompt recognition and immediate intervention. Levothyroxine replacement is the cornerstone of treatment, aiming to restore thyroid hormone levels and metabolic function. However, this should be initiated cautiously, especially in the elderly, as excessive thyroid hormone replacement can lead to adverse effects.
Once the emergency treatment is initiated, further investigations including thyroid function tests (TSH, free T4), adrenal function assessment, and other relevant tests should be performed to confirm the diagnosis and guide ongoing management.
