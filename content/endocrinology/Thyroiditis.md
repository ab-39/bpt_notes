# Thyroiditis

### Overview
_Thyroiditis_ is a state of inflammation of the thyroid that leads to the release of preformed thyroid hormone. Thus, while the patient will have features of _thyrotoxicosis_, while the pattern of uptake on [[imaging/Thyroid Scintigraphy|thyroid scintigraphy]] is that of no uptake. It is characterised by a self-limited, triphasic course that lasts for about three months:
1. Thyrotoxicosis
2. Hypothyroidism
3. Euthyroidism

Antithyroid drugs are typically ineffective. Management of thyrotoxicity is symptomatic with β-blockers. There is no role for levothyroxcine supplementation in the hypothyroid phase.

### Subacute Thyroiditis 
Subacute, or de Quervain’s thyroiditis is usually associated with fever, pain in neck, local thyroid tenderness. It is typically postviral. The characteristic histologic finding is _granulomatous inflammation_. Investigations will reveal both increased inflammatory markers _and_ free T4. Uptake will be absent on scintigraphy. Treatment is with aspirin +/- steroids.

Clues to suggest de Quervain’s thyroiditis(subacute/granulomatous thyroiditis):
* abrupt onset of viral-like illness, with fever,myalgia, malaise,pharyngitis, and accompanying symptoms of thyrotoxicosis, such as palpitations, tremor, and heat intolerance.
* thyroid pain and tenderness.
* very low thyroid uptake on nuclear scan
* high erythrocyte sedimentation rate.
* normal or decreased vascular flow on doppler.

⠀Clues to Infectious, suppurative, or acute thyroiditis:
* pharyngeal abscess is usually present, with dysphagia as a prominent symptom.
* unlike in subacute/granulomatous thyroiditis, there may be skin erythema over the painful thyroid.
* white blood cell count: elevated.
* FNA biopsy: polymorphonuclear neutrophils. Gram stain shows organisms.

### Subacute Painless Thyroiditis
Characterised by a painless goitre and _lymphocytic_ infiltrate on histology. Most usually autoimmune in aetiology, postpartum, or else drug-induced (think: interferons, lithium, interleukins, TKIs, amiodarone). 

The most commonly positive antibodies are those against thyroid peroxidase. Scintigraphy shows no uptake.
