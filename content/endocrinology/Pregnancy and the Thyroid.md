# Pregnancy and the Thyroid
#bpt/endocrinology #bpt/fracprac
- - -
During the first trimester of pregnancy, the thyroid hormone that is most likely to decrease is TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone). This is because of the increase in the hormone human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), which is produced by the placenta and has a structure similar to that of TSH.
HCG can bind to and activate the TSH receptor, leading to increased production of thyroid hormones by the thyroid gland. As a result, the body reduces the production of TSH to avoid overstimulation of the thyroid gland. This decrease in TSH levels can result in lower levels of thyroid hormones, particularly free thyroxine (T4), in the first trimester of pregnancy.
However, the thyroid gland adapts to the increased demand for thyroid hormones, and TSH levels usually return to normal by the end of the first trimester.
