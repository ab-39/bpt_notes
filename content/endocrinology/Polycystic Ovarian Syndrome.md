# Polycystic Ovarian Syndrome
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Rotterdam Criteria
Needs at least two of:
1. Oligo/anovulation
2. Clinical and/or biochemical signs of hyperandrogenism
3. Polycystic ovaries on ultrasound (does not have to be bilateral)
- - -
### Overview
PCOS is a _very_ common syndrome that is a common cause of subfertility. It is closely associated with insulin resistance and the eventual development of [[type 2 diabetes]]. There appears to be a strong genetic association, and an increased risk of endometrial carcinoma due to the unopposed effect of oestrogen. 
- - -
### Pathogenesis
Complex and multifactorial, likely involving:
- Hypothalamus - more rapid pulses of GnRH than normal, leading to:
- Pituitary - increase secretion of LH relative to FSH
- Ovaries - increased androgen synthesis (also stimulated by insulin) and follicular arrest
- - -
### Investigations
Biochemical:
- Testosterone and DHEA (elevated)
- SHBG (reduced)
- LH/FSH ratio (elevated)
- Lipids - elevated TGs and decreased HDL
- HbA1c, OGTT

Imaging:
- Pelvic ultrasound - often >20 cysts 
- - -
### Differentials
Need to rule out:
- [[Congenital adrenal hyperplasia]] - test 17-hydroxyprogesterone + synacthen test
- [[Hyperprolactinaemia]]
- [[Cushing Syndome]] 
- [[Acromegaly]]
- [[Hypothyroidism]]
- Androgen secreting tumours 
- - -
### Treatment
Treatment needs to be patient-focussed and often centres around:
- Management of insulin resistance -> metformin + lifestyle measures
- Management of menstrual disturbance -> can utilise OCP/IUD if not requiring fertility or not contraindicated
- Management of hirsutism:
  - Cosmetic measures
  - Anti-androgens -> spironolactone (in combination with contraception) or cyproterone
- - -
### Management of Fertility
While lifestyle intervention may lead to an improvement in ovulation, women with PCOS often continue to ovulate intermittently, which can lead to long time to conception or fewer children than desired. This is also associated with a higher rate of first trimester miscarriage. Options for treatment include:
- Ovulation induction:
  - Letrozole = aromatase inhibitor -> reduced levels of oestrogen
    - Modestly higher live birth rates when compared with clomiphene 
  - Clomiphene = ER antagonist
- IVF
