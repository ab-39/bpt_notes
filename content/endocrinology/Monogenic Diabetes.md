### Overview
The monogenic types of diabetes are generally characterised by mutations that cause defects in β-cell function, most commonly due to mutations in:
- Glucokinase (GCK MODY/MODY 2)
- Hepatonuclear factor genes:
  - HNF4A MODY/MODY 1
  - HFN1A MODY/MODY 3
  - MNF1B MODY/MODY 5
  
| MODY Type | Gene                             | Characteristics                                              |
|-----------|----------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1         | HNF4⍺                            | Reduced insulin secretory response to glucose, normal renal glucose threshold; sensitive to sulfonylureas. |
| 2         | Glucokinase <br>(glucose sensor) | Non-progressive, mild hyperglycaemia; minimal risk of microvascular disease; diet-controlled. |
| 3         | HNF1⍺                            | Abnormal insulin secretion; low renal glucose threshold -> glycosuria ++; extremely sensitive to sulfonylureas. Associated with liver adenomas. |
| 4         | IPF1                             | Rare. Pancreatic agenesis.                                   |
| 5         | HNF1β                            | Pancreatic atrophy + kidney cysts/impaired function + hypomagnesaemia; treated with insulin. |
| 6         | NDF1                             | Treated with insulin.                                        |

> [!TIP] Mnemonic
> MODY caused by mutations in gluco*CAN*ase **can** be managed with diet alone, beyond that ⍺ > β:
> - HNF4/1⍺ -> OAHGs alone (sulfonylureas)
> - HNF1β -> needs insulin + associated with kidney cysts  

The HNF1A mutations (MODY3) is the group that are sensitive to sulphonylureas, NOT MODY2.

In patients with GCK mutation (MODY2), glucose lowering therapy can actually be stopped once stable glucose is confirmed without glucose lowering therapy. Screening for cardiovascular risk factors should therefore be based on traditional risk profiles as this condition is not associated with micro or macrovascular complications. These patients usually have mild fasting hypergycemia.

Other rare forms of monogenic diabetes include: 
- Maternally inherited diabetes and deafness - mitochondrial
- Wolfram syndrome (‘DIDMOD’) - diabetes inspidius, diabetes mellitus, optic atrophy, and deafness
- Wolcott-Rallison syndrome - neonatal diabetes, skeletal abnormalities, and episodes of liver failure
- Insulin receptor mutations (Rabson-Mendenhall syndrome) - _extremely_ high insulin requirements
- Lipodystrophic syndromes - failure to develop adipose tissue -> ectopic fat deposition in the liver leading to insnulin resistance
