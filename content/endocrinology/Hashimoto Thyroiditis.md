# Hashimoto Thyroiditis
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Epidemiology
- Relatively common - ~4/1000 people/y
- Can affect any age group but peaks in middle age
- Female predilection
- High iodine diet _may_ increase risk
- - -
### Pathogenesis
The predominant genetic risk factors for Hashimoto disease are:
- _CTLA4_ polymorphisms 
- HLA-DR3 and -DR5

Characterised by aggressive destruction of the thyroid gland, predominantly by cytotoxic T-cells or NK-cells. Less commonly, may be antibody-mediated (by TSH-receptor _blocking_ antibodies). 

Anti-TPO (‘microsomal’) antibodies are almost always positive in autoimmune hypothyroidism.
- - -
### Treatment
Replacement with thyroxine. This drug is absorbed in the small intestine, with optimal absorption in the fasted state. 

The half-life is ~7 days.

Thyroxine is titrated to achieve a TSH in the normal range, bearing in mind that this is different in pregnancy.

There is no evidence for combination T4/T3 replacement.
