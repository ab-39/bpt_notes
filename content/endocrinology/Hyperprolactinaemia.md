# Hyperprolactinaemia
#bpt/endocrinology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Unlike most pituitary hormone excesses, which are typically caused by excessive autonomous secretion, prolactin can also be caused by reduced tonic inhibition if the dopamine secreting neurons of the pituitary stalk are compressed by a nonfunctioning pituitary adenoma. This requires some discretion when interpreting serum prolactin levels:
- Microadenoma (<1cm) + prolactin 2-5x ULN -> microprolactinoma
- Macroadenoma (>1cm) + prolactin 2-5x ULN -> stalk compression
- Macroadenoma + prolactin >10x ULN -> macroprolactinoma

Additional causes of hyperprolactinaemia include:
- Drugs - e.g. antipsychotics, risperidone; antiemetics; TCAs; oestrogen; verapamil
- Hypophysitis
- Pregnancy - additionally, prolactinomas may expand during pregnancy 
- Chest wall lesions
- Primary hypothyroidism
- Renal failure (reduced clearance)
- False positive (usually anti-prolactin antibodies -> macroprolactin complex formation, inactive, but unable to be cleared)

Clinical features include:
- Galactorrhoea (50% of women, 20% of men)
- Menstrual irregularity 
- Infertility
- Recurrent miscarriage
- Hirsutism
- Osteopenia
- Loss of libido
- Mass effect

The pathophysiology of the changes in menstruation are related to the fact that prolactin inhibits _kisspeptin_ a hypothalamic peptide, that in turn inhibits GnRH. Additionally, high prolactin leads to increased dopaminergic tone in the pituitary (in an attempt at negative feedback), which itself can impair GnRH pulsatility. 

The mainstay of treatment is the _dopamine agonist_, as this induces suppression of prolactin secretion. D2 receptors in the pituitary gland suppress prolactin release, while D1 receptors are located throughout the CNS and mediate many of the side effects.
- Bromocriptine = nonselective D1/D2-R agonist -> moderately effective, many side effects
- Cabergoline = selective D2R agonist -> highly effective, well tolerated
  - May have a cytocidal effect in some patients, inducing long-term normoprolactinaemia

The main side effects of dopamine agonism include nausea, postural hypotension, and issues with impulse control. Cabergoline can, in rare situations (typically high doses e.g. in [[Parkinson’s disease]]), cause cardiac valvulopathy through a serotonin mediated pathway. When disease is refractory to dopamine agonism, the next line is transsphenoidal surgery.

### Macroprolactinaemia
Macroprolactinaemia - essentially, multimerisation of prolactin - is a common spurious laboratory finding that can mimic hyperprolactinaemia. It is corrected for with a PEG-precipitation assay. 
- - -
### Genetics
Pituitary adenomas are associated with several [[hereditary cancer syndromes]]: in particular, prolactinomas are associated with multiple endocrine neoplasia type 1 (MEN1) syndrome.
