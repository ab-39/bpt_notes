# Amiodarone-Induced Thyroiditis
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Overview
#### Type I AIT
Type I AIT is due to the [[Thyroid Physiology#Jod-Basedow Phenomenon|Jod-Basedow phenomenon]], occurring in the presence of underlying thyroid autonomy. It is typically self-limiting if amiodarone is ceased. Colour Doppler flow is normal in type I AIT, and there is usually normal or increased uptake on scintigraphy. Treatment is with correction of the underlying problem, withdrawal of amiodarone, and antithyroid drugs if needed.

#### Type II AIT
Type II AIT is due to the direct inflammatory effect of iodine on the follicular cells of the thyroid leading to a self-limited thyrotoxicosis that _may_ result in permanent hypothyroidism. Colour Doppler flow within the thyroid is reduced in type II AIT, and there is typically no uptake on scintigraphy. Amiodarone can be continued, and treatment is with prednisolone and β-blockers if needed.
