### Overview

#### Wolff-Chaikoff Effect
The response to an acute load of iodine is a _transient reduction_ in thyroid hormone production due to temporary downregulation of the sodium iodide symporter (which takes up iodine from the circulation). This is the rationale behind the administration of iodine in thyrotoxic crisis.

#### Jod-Basedow Phenomenon
A pathologically _exaggerated_ response to exogenously administered iodine where iodine loading results in additional thyroid hormone production. This occurs in people with impaired thyroid autoregulation (e.g. autoimmune diseases, nodular goitre).

This means that iodine-induced thyrotoxicosis tends to only occur in patients with underlying thyroid disease, usually a nodular goitre. It is transient, and occurs in repsonse to agents such as kelp tablets, iodinated contrast, and drugs containing iodine (e.g. amiodarone). Management is with antithyroid drugs, if needed.

In rarer cases, iodine (or drugs that increase the serum iodide levels, such as amiodarone) can directly cause thyroiditis and release of preformed thyroid hormone. This requires steroids.
