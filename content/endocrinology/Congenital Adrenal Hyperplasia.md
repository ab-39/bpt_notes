# Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Overview
Congenital adrenal hyperplasia is a group of enzyme deficiencies in the adrenal gland that lead to impaired adrenal steroidogenesis and reduced cortisol synthesis:
![](Congenital%20Adrenal%20Hyperplasia/image.png)

The most important of these, implicated in “classical” CAH is _21-hydroxylase_, deficiency of which leads to impaired production of aldosterone and cortisol. This is matched by an increase in 17⍺-hydroxyprogesterone, which is the diagnostic marker for classical CAH. 

![](Congenital%20Adrenal%20Hyperplasia/image%202.png)

Classical CAH can have two phenotypes: salt-wasting (severe deficiency; ~75%) or simple virilising (moderate deficiency; ~25%). In both cases, the female fetus is _virilised_ due to the shunting of progesterone down the testosterone biosynthetic pathway, with the development of amibugous genitalia. Diagnosis is at birth, with elevated levels of 17-hydroxyprogesterone (>300nmol/L)

Non-classical CAH is a “milder” phenotype of CAH, with diagnosis often occurring post-puberty in females due to issues with fertility, irregular period, and/or hirsutism. Here, diagnosis is with a short synacthen test. 17-OH-progesterone levels are moderately elevated (30-300nmol/L). 

Treatment of CAH depends on the phenotype:
- Classical -> glucorticoid +/- mineralocorticoid replacement
- Non-classical -> antiandrogen therapy

There is emerging evidence for suppression of ACTH levels in classical CAH with a drug called _**crinecerfont**_, a corticotorpin releasing hormone antagonist. Suppression of ACTH reduces activation of the adrenals, thus suppressing the drive to produce testosterone. This allows for lower doses of glucocorticoid replacement to be used.
