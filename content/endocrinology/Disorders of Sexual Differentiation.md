# Disorders of Sexual Differentiation
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Overview
The undifferentiated gonad has two possible fates:
1. In the absence of the _SRY_ gene on the Y-chromosome, it will go on to become an ovary and develop Müllerian ducts and ultimately have associated female genitalia
2. In the _presence_ of the _SRY_ gene it will become a testis, where:
   - Leydig cells -> testosterone:
     - -> development of Wolffian ducts and ultimately seminal vesiclces
     - -> converted to 5-DHT by 5-⍺-reductase -> development of male genitalia
   - Sertoli cells -> anti-Müllerian factor -> inhibition of Müllerian ducts

### 46;XY Gonadal Dysgenesis 
A Y-chromosome _without_ the _SRY_ gene will lead to a the development of female anatomy in the presence of a 46;XY karyotype = 46;XY gonadal dysgenesis.
- Patients with this condition will still not have ovaries as this requires two X chromosomes - instead, they have two intra-abdominal _streak gonads_ which can develop into gonadoblastoma
- Clinical symptoms include amenorrhoea and lack of breast growth
- Biochemically, there is normal testosterone, reduced oestradiol, and increased FSH/LH
- Treatment involves:
  - HRT to encourage the development of female secondary sex characteristics
  - Removal of streak gonads due to malignant potential
  - Fertility - IVF and donor oocytes

### Complete Androgen Insensitivity 
Defects of androgen receptors result in female external genitalia but absent Müllerian structures (i.e. upper part of the vagina, uterus, Fallopian tubes) due to the ongoing effect of the anti-Müllerian factor = complete androgen insensitivity syndrome. 
- Presentation is generally with primary amenorrhoea in adolescence, or bilateral inguinal swellings in an infant
  - Breast development is normal due to the aromatisation of testosterone
- Biochemically. testosterone is elevated, as is LH
- The karyotype is 46;XY
- Treatment is with testis removal (due to malignant potential) and HRT
