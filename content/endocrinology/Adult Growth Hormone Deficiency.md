# Adult Growth Hormone Deficiency
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Overview
Lack of growth hormone generally presents with nonspecific symptoms of lethargy, malaise, low energy, etc. It almost always arises secondary to a pituitary tumour, trauma, or some previous treatment.

Phenotypically, adults with GH deficiency tend to present with:
- Increased abdominal adiposity
- Decreased lean body mass
- Decreased bone mineral density
- Elevated total and LDL cholesterdal 
- Decreased muscle strength and aerobic capacity
- Poor quality of life 

The best diagnostic test for GH deficiency is stimulation testing with glucagon:
- GH <3µg/L following administration of glucagon is considered diagnostic of GH deficiency
- A lower cutoff (<1µg/L) is used in obese patients  

The IGF-1 level is useful but not diagnostic, as it may be low-normal in GH deficiency. If, however, there are multiple pituitary hormones deficient _and_ IGF-1 is low, there is no need for a stimulation test.

Treatment is with growth hormone supplementation, which generally reverses the points above. There are some possible interactions with other hormones:
- Increases cortisol -> inactive cortisone -> may need to increase or commence glucocorticoid replacement
- Increases metabolism of T4 -> T3 -> may need to start or increase thyroxine
- Inactivated by oestrogen -> women on OCP require higher doses of GH or reduction of OCP
