### Pituitary
Most common macroadenoma of the pituitary gland, accounting for ~30% of all [[pituitary adenomas]] overall. Although not intrinsically associated with any hormonal derangements, these adenomas can compress the pituitary stalk and remove the tonic inhibition of dopamine on prolactin, leading to elevated prolactin levels. 

Despite being ‘non-functional’, these generally tend to be ‘silent’ gonadotroph tumours (~75%), or else corticotroph tumours (which are more aggressive). 

The clinical presentatino is usually asymptomatic, or less commonly with signs of panhypopituitarism, mass effect, or pituitary apoplexy. 

Completely asymptomatic patients can be followed up, but ~20% of patients will go on to develop symptoms of some sort. Symptoms or significant enlargement of the tumour on subsequent imaging is an indication for transsphenoidal surgery. This is completely successful in 65% of cases, and the remainder of patients either require repeat surgery or radiotherapy (although this itself can result in further hypopituitarism. Dopamine agonists and octreotide rarely have much effect on tumour shrinkage. acro
