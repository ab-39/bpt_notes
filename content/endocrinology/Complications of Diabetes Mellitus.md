### Overview
The [DCCT study](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199309303291401) was one of the first studies investigating the long term complications of diabetes, and yielded the key finding that _an HbA1c above 7%_ (~53mmol/L) is associated with increasing rates of complications:
![](Complications%20of%20Diabetes%20Mellitus/image.png)
The study randomised individuals to intensive versus conservative glycaemic management, with the intensive control group achieving a mean HbA1c of 7% while the conservative group were 9%. They found that the intensive group had:
- A 75% reduction in development of retinopathy
- A 54% reduction in progression of retinopathy
- A 39% reduction in development of microalbuminuria
- A 60% reduction in development of clinical neuropathy
- Reduction in CVD and mortality 
- **_But_** increased hypoglycaemic episodes 

The [EDIC study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2745938/) investigated this further, and found that despite patients’ HbA1c converging after the trial stopped:
![](Complications%20of%20Diabetes%20Mellitus/image%202.png)
patients who had previously been on intensive therapy _continued_ to have superior outcomes with respect to retinopathy, nephropathy, and cardiovascular outcomes. 

Ultimately, the _[STENO 2](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0706245)_ trial found that multifactorial intervention is associated with an almost _50%_ relative risk reduction for all-cause mortality, and a similar reduction in cardiovascular events.

### Diabetic Retinopathy
Retinopathy is the complication of diabetes that tends to emerge the earliest, and is the leading cause of blindness in adults under the age of 75y. The risk of retinopathy correlates with the duration of diabetes and glycaemic control. 

Intensive glucose control can delay onset and slow progression, but there is a risk of transiently worsening retinopathy if BSLs are decreased too quickly. Pregnancy may worsen retinopathy, even to the point of threatening vision.

![](Complications%20of%20Diabetes%20Mellitus/image%203.png)

#### Screening:
- Within 5y of diagnosis of T1DM
- From diagnosis of T2DM 

#### Treatment:
- Tight control of BP with an A2RB or β-blockers, aiming SBP <140mmHg (UKPDS)
- Lower lipids - fenofibrate appears to be particularly useful (FIELD, ACCORD)
- Panretinal photocoagulation - can be used in severe proliferative DR or if vision is threatened; reduces risk of severe visual loss by 50%
- Intravitreal VEGF inhibition (e.g. **_ranibizumab_**) has shown benefit in both proliferative and non-proliferative retinopathy (RESTORE)

### Diabetic Kidney Disease 
Consists both of nephropathy and the cardiovascular effects of diabetes on the kidneys. Generally occurs 10y after onset in patients with T1DM. Affects ~40% of patients with DM, and is th leading indication for dialysis. ==🔴Independent risk factor for MACE==. Screen with urine ACR. 

Diabetic nephropathy is generally mediated by advanced glycation end-products and oxdative stress, and histopathological features include:
- Thickening of the GBM
- Mesangial expansion (-> _Kimmelstiel-Wilson lesions_) 
- Podocyte injury
- Glomerulosclerosis 
- Tubulointerstitial fibrosis 
![](Complications%20of%20Diabetes%20Mellitus/image%204.png)
Key aspect of management is the reduction of hypertension, particularly with RAAS inhibition, as this has an independent effect of proteinuria ([The Effect of Angiotensin-Converting-Enzyme Inhibition on Diabetic Nephropathy](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJM199311113292004)) with _either_ an ACE-inhibitor or an A2RB, but not both (which is associated with no CV or renal benefits, but increased adverse effects - [_ON TARGET_](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0801317)).

Some key diabetes medications have been found to be renoprotective:
- SGLT2-inhibitors:
  - Renoprotective through mechanism of increasing sodium at the macula densa -> activating tubuloglomerular feedback to reduce renal perfusion 
  - Empagliflozin reduces incident/worsening nephropathy, progression to macroalbuminuria, and composite renal outcomes ([*EMPA-REG OUTCOME*](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1515920))
  - Similar effect for dapagliflozin (*[DECLARE TIMI 58](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587%2819%2930180-9/abstract)*)
- GLP1RAs:
  - Renoprotective through inhibition of Na/H exchanger in the proximal tubule -> increases sodium at the macular densa -> activating tubuloglomerular feedback to reduce renal perfusion 
  - Semaglutide reduces the risk of clinically important kidney outcomes and death from cardiovascular causes ([_FLOW_](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347))

### Diabetic Neuropathy
Spectrum can be broad, including:
- Large fibre neuropathy
- Small fibre neuropathy
- Proximal motor neuropathy (diabetoc amyotrophy)
- Acute mononeuropathy
- Multifocal mononeuropathy

The most common phenotype is a distal symmetric mononeuropathy with a ‘glove and stocking’ pattern of sensory loss. 

This is also associated with a loss of protective sensation in the feet, leading to an increased risk of [[diabetic foot infection]] and ulceration.

### Cardiovascular Disease
DIabetes confers a 2-4x fold increased risk of CVD, which increases monotonically with blood sugar. However, unlike microvascular complications, reducing HbA1c has only a modest effect on reducing CV risk and mortality. Interestingly, the [*ACCORD* study](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa0802743) actually found an _increased risk_ of mortality with an aggressive reduction strategy for HbA1c, _without_ improving cardiovascular outcomes. 

Treatment of hypertension has demonstrated significant benefit in reducing cardiovascular events. In those with a history of CV events, aspirin (or another APA) can be used for _secondary_ prevention. Moderate-tohigh-intensity statin treatment lowers LDL by ≥40%, and has an effect on cardiovascular mortality. SGLT2-inhibitors reduce cardiovascular mortality in patients with T2DM as demonstated for empagliflozin by [_EMPA-REG OUTCOME_](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1504720). 

#### Heart Failure 
_Very_ common in the diabetic cohort, and admissions with herat heart failure occur twice as often in patients with DM. Among the antihyperglycaemics, SGLT2is have the best evidence for improving heart failure outcomes, to the point where they are now  guideline-directed therapy for that condition. Multiple trials have demonstrated reductions in death and admissions for patients with heart failure with both reduced and preserved ejection fraction, with and without diabetes:
- [_DAPA-HF_](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1504720)
- [_EMPEROR_-Reduced](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2022190)
- [_EMPEROR_-Preserved](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2107038)
