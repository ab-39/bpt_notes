### Overview
Osteoporosis is common and has significant morbidity defined as a systemic skeletal disease characterised by low bone mass and microarchitectural deterioration of bone tissue, leading to increased bone fragility and susceptibility to fracture. The most common types of fracture are those of the vertebrae and of the hip (later in life). Diagnosis is made by minimal trauma fracture or DEXA/BMD T-scores. 

Osteoporosis:
- T < -2.5 (i.e. less than 2.5 SD in a young population of the same sex)
- **Or** fragility fracture
  - Note: in Australia, you can get access to OP meds with T < -1.5 if on 3/12 steroids (7.5mg pred equivalent)

1SD reduction in T score = 2x fracture risk

The Z score (age **and** sex matched reference) is more useful for guiding who (i.e. the relatively young) needs investigation for secondary bone loss. For patients with Z ≤—2, it is worth considering:
- Hyperparathyroidism
- Vitamin D deficiency
- Coeliac disease
- Multiple myeloma
- Hypogonadism
- Thyrotoxicosis
- Glucocorticoid excess
- Antiseizure drug use 

FRAX risk score calculator -> treatment if:
1. 10y risk of osteoporotic fracture >10% 
**or**
2. 10y risk of hip fracture >3%

The FRAX score includes:
- Age
- Sex
- Weight
- Height
- Previous fracture
- Family history
- Smoking status
- Steroid use
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Secondary OP
- Alcohol use
- Femoral neck BMD T-score

And allows for adjustment with the _trabecular bone score_. This is a textural analysis, related to the bone microarchitecture, that is derived with information from the spine DXA:
- TBS > 1.35 is normal
- 1.2 < TBS < 1.35 is partially degraded 
- TBS < 1.2 is degraded
Bear in mind that TBS is not validated at extremes of BMI

### Treatment
Treatment is generally instituted for:
- Patients with a T-score of ≤—2.5 at the femoral neck, total hip, lumbar spine, or distal raius
- Patients with fractures of the hip or spine _regardless_ of DXA
- Patients with osteopenia at the femoral neck or total hip + 10y fracture risk >3% by FRAX score (or risk of osteoporotic fracture >10%)

[[endocrinology/Treatment of Osteoporosis|Treatment options]]:
1. Bisphosphonates
2. Denosumab
3. (Less commonly) SERMs
4. Bone anabolic agents:
   - Teriparatide
   - Romosozumab 

In general, these treatments should be accompanied by supplemenation of vitamin D +/- calcium if indicated. 

Therapy can be monitored by checking markers of bone turnover:
- CTX (C-terminal telopeptide of type I collagen, a marker of resorption) should be _reduced_ by three months
- P1NP (procollagen type I N-propeptide, a marker of bone formation) should be increased by six months 

> [!TIP] Mnemonic
> **C**TX for osteo**C**last activity
