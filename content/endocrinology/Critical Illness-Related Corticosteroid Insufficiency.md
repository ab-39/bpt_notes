# Critical Illness-Related Corticosteroid Insufficiency
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
When significantly unwell, inflammatory cytokines stimulate the production of CRH in the hypothalamus, leading to increased ACTH and thus cortisol. Additionally, levels of cortisol binding globulin and albumin _decrease_, leading to an overall significant increase in the level of free cortisol. Free cortisol has a high volume of distribution, which means that it is able to diffuse into the tissues and take relatively longer to clear. Additionally, the activity of 5-⍺-reductase in the liver and 11-β-hydroxysteroiddehydrogenase in the kidney decline, leading to reduced metabolism of cortisol. 

On the other hand, there appears to be some reduction in sensitivity of the glucocorticoid receptors peripherally, leading to an overall state where cortisol levels are increased, but cortisol sensitivity is decreased. 

In the long-run, this leads to negative feedback and suppression of ACTH, which can be further suppressed by medications. Prolonged suppression of ACTH can then secondarily impair adrenocortical structure and function.

There is no diagnostic test for CIRCI, as the usual dose used in a synacthen stimulation test is insufficient to account for the increased Vd of cortisol in illness. The diagnosis should be suspected in anyone with a prolonged stays in ICU, and treatment with divided daily doses of relatively high dose hydrocortisone (e.g. 60mg/d -> 40mg + 20mg) is worth trialling.
