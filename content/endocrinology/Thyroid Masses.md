# Thyroid Masses
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Overview
The term _goitre_ refers to swelling of the thyroid gland, which can arise due to multiple causes.
- - -
### Nontoxic Goitre
Nontoxic goitre is often an interplay of genetic and environmental factors. In areas where there is significant iodine deficiency (e.g. the Himalayas), goitre can be _endemic_. Other goitrogens include metabolites of cruciferous vegetables, some industrial pollution, micronutrient deficiencies (vitamin A, selenium, iron).

These goitres are characterised by hyperplasia of the entire gland, but can be followed by somatic mutatinos that cause focal thyroid epithelial proliferation - nodules.
- - -
### Thyroid Nodules
Thyroid nodules can be non-functioning, euthyroid, or hyperfunctioning. Hyperfunctioning or _toxic_ nodules are autonomous, and secrete thyroid hormone independently of TSH. 
- - -
### Autonomous Thyroid Hormone Secretion
Autonomous thyroid hormone secretion generally occurs in two circumstances:
- Toxic multinodular goitre
- Toxic adenoma

These are situations in which thyroid hormone is secreted independently of TSH, usually due to constitutive activation of the TSH receptor or somatic mutatinos in the ⍺-subunit of the G-protein.

Thyroid autonomy progresses slowly, and the course is usual from euthyroidism -> subclinical hyperthyroidism -> T3 hyperthyroidism -> overt hypothyroidism.

Treatment is generally with radioactive iodine as the ‘hot’ nodules tend to take up iodine _very_ rapidly, while the suppression of TSH leads to the rest of the gland being relatively less prone to iodine uptake. This has a moderate (~30%) risk of hypothyroidism at 8y. Large goitres with mass effect may require surgery.
