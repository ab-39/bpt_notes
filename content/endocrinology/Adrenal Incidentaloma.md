# Adrenal Incidentaloma
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Overview
An _adrenal incidentaloma_ is a clinically unapparent adrenal lesion >1cm in diameter that is detected incidentally, usually in 1-6% of other CT scans. Tend to be:
- Non-functioning (75%)
- Functioning - typically producing cortisol (5%)
  - Diagnostic confirmation with the overnight low-dose dexamethasone suppression test, which should suppress cortisol levels to <50nmol/L in the case of a _non-functioning_ adenoma
  - If there is evidence of hypertension or hypokalaemia, the aldosterone-renin ratio should be checked 
- Phaeochromocytoma (~5%)
  - Diagnostic confirmation can be made with elevated plasma metanephrines 
  - Important to rule _out_, as biopsying a PCC can lead to a catecholamine surge
- Primary or metastatic cancer (~5%)
  - Diagnostic confirmation with biopsy _after_ PCC has been ruled out 

Some imaging findings can also point to certain diagnoses:
- Adrenocortical adenoma:
  - Small (<4cm)
  - Smooth margins
  - Homogeneous
  - Attenuation <10 Hounsfield units (i.e. fatty)
  - Rapid washout of contrast
  - Isointense to liver on T2 weighting
  - Not FDG-avid on PET
- Phaeochromocytoma:
  - Often large
  - Smooth margins
  - Heterogeneous
  - Attenuation >10 Hounsfield units
  - Slow washout of contrast
  - Hyperintsense to liver on T2 weighting
  - FDG-avid on PET
- Adrenocortical carcinoma:
  - Large (>6cm)
  - Irregular margins
  - Attenuation >10 Hounsfield units
  - Slow washout of contrast
  - Hyperintsense to liver on T2 weighting
  - FDG-avid on PET 

Thus, in general, suspicious lesions have:
- Diameter >4cm
- Irregular margins
- Density on CT >10 Hounsfield units
- Slow washout of contrast (<60%)
