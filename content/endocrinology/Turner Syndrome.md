### Overview
Turner syndrome arises due to the loss of all or part of an X-chromosome, with 45X being the most common karyotype (50%). This condition affects 1:2,500 live female births, and presents with:
- Short stature
- Supravalvular aortic stenosis, bicuspid aortic valve
- Chronic otitis media
- Osteoporosis
- Streak gonads
- Ovarian insufficiency/primary amenorrhoea (due to rapid loss of oocytes)
- Characteristic facies
- Shortened metacarpals
- Naevi

Biochemically, there is elevated levels of LH and FSH. Management is with:
- Growth hormone -> increase height
- HRT -> induce puberty and maintain until age 50y
- Pregnancy is possible with donor oocytes
