# Primary Hyperaldosteronism
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Overview
Primary hyperaldosteronism, is the most common cause of secondary hypertension, but is often underdiagnosed. The likely prevalence ranges from 6% in those with hypertension to up to 20% in those with resistant hypertension. Compared with essential hypertension, primary hyperaldosteronism is a risk factor for stroke, heart disease, heart failure, atrial fibrillation, and renal disease. 

Patients with severe or early-onset hypertension, those with a strong family history of HTN or CVD, or those with hypokalaemia should be screened for primary hyperaldosteronism, although hypokalaemia is relatively uncommon (25%). Screening is done with an _aldosterone-renin ratio_, preferably before anihypertensive treatment is initiated:
- Sprionolactone and diuretics have the greatest effect on ARR, and need to be withheld for at least six weeks prior to a test
- Β-blockers, ACEi/A2RBs, and dihydropyridines have a lesser effect, and should be stopped for 2-4 weeks
- Non-dihydropyidines, ⍺-blockers, hydralazine, and moxonidine do not affect the test

Although most commonly due to a functioning adenoma in the zona glomerulosa of the adrenal gland unilaterally (a _Conn_ adenoma), there are less common causes:
- Type 1 bilateral adrenal hyperplasia: due to a chimeric gene of CYP11B1 (aldosterone synthetic pathway) and CYP11B2 (cortisol synthetic pathway) that leads to ACTH-dependent aldosterone production in the zona fasciculata
- This can be treated with low dose dexamethasone

Despite being a good screening test, a positive ARR requires confirmation:
- Hypokalaemia, undetectable plasma renin, or plasma aldosterone >550pmol/L are considered diagnostic of PH
- Otherwise, the most common test is the _IV saline test_:
  - Administer 2L of 0.9% sodium chloride over 4h
  - Plasma aldosterone levels >171pmol/L are diagnostic of PH
- In patients unable to tolerate the fluid challenge, the _captopril test_ can alternatively be used:
  - Administer 25-50mg captoptil
  - Normal patient -> captopril reduces plasma aldosterone -> renin increases -> ARR is decreased
  - In PH patients -> aldosterone does not suppress: >305pmol/L is diagnostic of PH

If imaging of the adrenal glands is used and an adenoma is identified, it is important to confirm the diagnosis with adrenal vein sampling.

Treatment is with surgical removal for unilateral disease (usually due to a Conn adenoma), or mineralocorticoid antagonism with or without adjunctive amiloride for bilateral disease.
