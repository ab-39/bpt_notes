### Diagnostic Criteria
The following tests are considered diagnostic of diabetes:
- Fasting plasma glucose  ≥7.0mmol/L
- OGTT ≥11.1mmol/L
- HbA1c ≥6.5% = 48mmol/mol
- Random glucose in the presence of compatible signs and symptoms ≥11.1mmol/L

### Types
In addition to [[Type 1 Diabetes]] and [[Type 2 Diabetes]], it is also important to remember:
- [[endocrinology/Monogenic Diabetes|Monogenic Diabetes]]
- ’Type 3c Diabetes’ - diabetes as a consequence of diseases of the exocrine pancreas (e.g. pancreatitis, trauma, CF, haemochromatosis)
- Diabetes secondary to endocrine disorders - e.g. acromegaly, PPGs, hyperthyroidism, etc.
- Rare forms of immune-mediated diabetes:
  - Insulin autoimmune syndrome
  - Anti-insulin receptor antibodies
  - Stiff Person Syndrome (anti-GAD antibodies)

Some drugs are also diabetogenic:
- Weakly:
  - Thiazides - inhibit insulin secretion
  - Β-blockers - impair insulin release
  - Statins - risk ~2/1,000 person years once treatment is initiated 
- Strongly:
  - Glucocorticoids
  - Immunosuppressants
  - Antipsychotics - independent effect to weight gain
  - Protease inhibitors
  - Pentamadine
  - Nicotinic acid 

There is also a risk of developing _NODAT_ - new onset diabetes after transplantation, which occurs after roughly one in every three-to-five solid organ tarnsplants and is associated with a higher risk of graft failure and mortality. The risk is likely mediated (or at least exacerbated) by immunosuppressive medications:
- Glucocorticoids -> induce insulin resistance and enhance gluconeogenesis
- Calcineurin inhibitors (_especially_ TAC) -> inhibit β-cell function

[[oncology/Immune Checkpoint Inhibition|Immune Checkpoint Inhibition]], particularly with PD-1 inhibitors (e.g. pembrolizumab) or PDL-1 inhibitors (e.g. avelumab), can also cause diabetes as PD-1 is expressed on pancreatic β-cells. This frequently presents with DKA and is irreversible, requiring lifelong insulin treatment.

### Monitoring
The HbA1c gives a three-month ‘rolling average’ of diabetes control, with the target HbA1c of 7% roughly corresponding to am average blood glucose of 8.5mmol/L
- Factors that increase A1c:
  - Things that increase RBC lifespan (e.g. iron deficiency, decreased erythropoiesis)
  - Alcoholism
  - CKD
  - Splenectomy
  - Large doses of aspirin (assay interference)
  - African-American ethnicity
- Factors that decrease A1c:
  - Things that decrease RBC lifespan (e.g. haemolytic anaemia, ESA use)
  - Inegsion of aspirin/vitamin C/vitamin E
  - Haemoglobinopathies
  - CKD
  - Splenomegaly
  - Pregnancy
