## Anti-Resorptives
### Bisphosphonates
The bisphosphonates are derivatives of inorganic pyrophosphate that bind to hydroxyapatite crystals at sites of active bone remodelling. Here, they are taken up by osteoclasts where they inhibit the enzyme farnesyl pyrophosphate, leading to reduced osteoclast function and ultimately apoptosis: 
![](Treatment%20of%20Osteoporosis/image.png)<!-- {"width":278} -->
They tend to have poor bioavailability. 

Maximum reduction in bone resorption is quite quick - within three months, after which bone mineral density begins to increase. This effect lasts for about ~4y, after which it plateaus. The greatest effect is in areas rich in trabecular bone, such as the spine, where RR for fractures is as low as 0.3. There is also good efficacy at the hip, where the RR for fractures is about 0.6. The antifracture effect of alendronate and zoledronic acid persists for 3-5y following discontinuation. 

#### Atypical Fracture
A key risk of bisphosphonates is _atypical femoral fractures_, which usually ocur below the level of the trochanter in low-trauma settings:
![](Treatment%20of%20Osteoporosis/image%202.png)<!-- {"width":218} -->
The risk is ~13/10,000 after 8y of bisphosphonate use, and are often the reason that after ~5y of bisphosphonate treatment, a ‘bisphosphonate holiday’ can be considered, as the risk rapidly reduces after one year. The pathognomic finding on imaging for atypical fractures is the ‘duckbill sign’:
![](Treatment%20of%20Osteoporosis/image%203.png)

#### Osteonecrosis of the Jaw
ONJ refers to exposed bone in the jaw that does not heal within eight weeks. This occurs in patients (most commonly oncology patients) receiving high-dose bisphosphonate therapy at a rate of ~10/10,000 patient-years. Risk factors include poor oral hygiene and dental procedures.

#### Drug Holidays
Drug holidays are carried out to minimise the risk of atypical fractures in patients at whom there is sufficiently low risk of regular fractures to withdraw the drug:
- Aledronate - ~2y holiday after 5y of treatment
- Risedronate - similar to aledronate, holiday ~1y
- Zoledronic acid - ~3y holiday after 6y of treatment 

### Denosumab
Denosumab is a monoclonal antibody to RANKL, which acts similarly to osteoprotegerin, in that it acts as a decoy receptor for RANKL, preventing it from stimluating osteoclastogenesis. It can reduce bone resorption markers (i.e. CTX) to undetectable levels within days of administration. The _FREEDOM_ study found significant reductions in both vertebral and non-vertebral fractures in women over the age of 60y with osteoporosis who were treated with denosumab. 

Unlike bisphosphonates, there is no plateauing of the increase in bone mineral density, but withdrawal of the drg is associated with increased risk of vertebral fractures. Because of this, patients should be ‘covered’ with zoledronic acid or alendronate if denosumab is ceased. 

Risks of denosumab include osteonecrosis of the jaw, atypical femoral fractures, cellulitis, and eczema.

## Bone Anabolic Agents 
### Teriparatide
Teriparatide is a truncated analogue of PTH that exploits the fact that intermittent pulses of PTH appear to be associated with osteoblast differentiation and formation of bone rather than bone resorption. The effect appears to be greatest at the lumbar spine, where fracture rates are reduced by up to 50%. In addition to minor side effects such as headache and nausea, there is a significantly increased risk of osteosarcoma with teriparatide use, and so it is typically only used in courses of 2y duration, after which an antiresorptive agent is reintroduced. 

There is a similar drug, **_abaloparatide_**, that is an analogue of PTHrP, with similar benefits and risks as teriparatide. 

### Romosozumab
Romosozumab is an anti-sclerostin antibody that leads to increased activity of the [[endocrinology/Physiology of Bone#Canonical Wnt Pathway|Wnt pathway]], stimulating osteoblast activity and survival. The _ARCH_ and _FRAME_ trials found significant reductions in vertebral fracture incidence without an increase in cardiovascular events.

### Combination Treatments
Teriparatide plus either of zoledronic acid or denosumab leads to a greater increased in bone mineral density than either agent alone. Teriparatide + ZA is associated with fewer fractures than ZA alone, while teriparatide + denosumab does not appear to further reduce fracture risk. In general, the optimal sequence appears to be anabolic _then_ antiresorptive agent. 

> [!WARNING] 
> Denosumab _followed_ by teriparatide is associated with significant bone _loss_.
