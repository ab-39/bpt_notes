# The Adrenal Glands
#bpt/endocrinology
- - -
### Overview
The adrenal glands sit on top of the kidneys, and comprise four zones:
- The adrenal cortex, from outside to inside made up of:
  - The zona glomerulosa -> secretes aldosterone under the influence of [[physiology/The Renin-Angiotensin System|the renin-angiotensin system]]
  - The zona fasciculata -> secretes glucocorticoids and cortisol under the influence of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis
  - The zona reticularis -> secretes sex hormones under the influence of the HPA axis
- The adrenal medulla, in which the chromaffin cells produce and secrete catecholamines 

The adrenal cortex is responsible for the production of three main classes of hormones:
- Glucocorticoids - produced from a cholesterol precursor through a four step pathway that involves the sequential actions of 17-⍺-hydroxylase and 3-β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (in either order depending on the pathway) to convert pregnenolone into 17-hydroxyprogesterone, and then of 21-hydroxylase (11-deoxycortisol) and 11-β-hydroxylase, which ultimately creates cortisol 
  - Corticosteroids act at type 2 corticosteroid receptors to exert a variety of effects that tend towards muscle catabolism, sodium retention, insulin resistance, gluconeogenesis, and immunosuppression
  - Cortisol is the prototypical glucocorticoid, with a relative potency defined as 1 for both glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid effect 
- Mineralocorticoids - produced from pregnenolone via the sequential action of 3-β-hydroxysteroid (progesterone), 21-hydroxylase (deoxycorticosterone), 11-β-hydroxylase (corticosterone) and finally 18-oxidase to produce aldosterone
  - Mineralocorticoids act at type 1 corticosteroid receptors to effect sodium and potassium balance in the distal tubule of the kidney
  - Aldosterone has roughly 400 times the mineralocorticoid potency of cortisol
- Sex hormones - produced via similar synthetic pathways, with the added action of 17,20-lyase (dehydroepiandrosterone/androstenedione) and then 17-β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase and p450 into testosterone and estradiol
