76F evaluated for OP. Currently on vitamin D + Ca. Drinks milk regularly + exercises 20min/day. Menopause at age 46yo. Nil Hx HRT. UEC, CMP, vit D all normal. DEXA LS T = -1.5, hip = -1.2.

Note: this patient has osteopenia based on her T-scores. Note that having normal vitamin D and Ca in this setting doesn’t necessarily mean they’re normal as she is on replacement. 

A: No indication for strontium as this is a second-line treatment for OP in any case (which this patient doesn’t have). No indication for HRT as this is for women with menopause who have symptoms. Strontium is no longer used due to cardiovascular risk.  Raloxifene is a second line drug. 

Therefore the best plan is to continue calcium and vitamin D, i.e. option A. Should the patient later accrue new risk factors, we can consider initiating a bisphosphonate.

In what age group is the T-score most likely to be similar to the Z-score in regards to bone mineral density?

A: Thirties - bone mineral density generally peaks around 25-30years of age. 
- - -
Bisphosphonates primarily act on which cell?

A: Bisphosphonates are antiresorptive drugs, and therefore primarily act on osteoclasts. They attach to hydroxyapatite binding sites on bone and integrate into the bone matrix.

74F p/w severe jaw pain following dental extraction. Jaw = swollen + disfigured. XR shows extensive changes consistent with osteonecrosis. 

A: bisphosphonates - the absolute risk is low but can occur in inpatients with high uses. Can also occur with denosumab.

A patient was started on bisphosphonates following cessation of denosumab. The reason for this is reduction in: **vertebral fractures**

The FREEDOM trial showed that consolidation therapy after a course of denosumab improves long term bone density and a reduced risk of vertebral fractures.

76F suffers a vertebral fracture due to osteoporosis. She is treated with IV zolendronic acid. In addition to reducing rate of refracture what is the benefit of bisphosphonates?

Decreased mortality (the HORIZON trial).

Which of the following is more likely to be related to corticosteroid-induced OP rather than post-menopausal?

Vertebral fractures - glucocorticoid treatment is associated with increased risk of vertebral fractures. 
- - -
What is the mechanism of action of teriparatide?

It is an analogue of the active fragment of human PTH that acts on osteoblasts as an anabolic agent. Promotes bone formation and increases BMD.  Lifetime maximum duration of 24 months of treatment due to *potential* risk of osteosarcoma. This is all similar to romasozumab.

Diabetic neuropathic arthropathy is most likely to affect which of the following areas?

A: Charcot’s arthropathy affecting the mid foot due to a loss of proprioception and subsequent collapse of the arch.
