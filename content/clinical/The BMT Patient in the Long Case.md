### Overview
Recall that there are two types of BMT:
| Allogeneic                                                   | Autologous                                                   |
|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Genetically different donor                                  | Patient’s own cells                                          |
| Donor can be:<br>Sibling (50-100% haplotype match)<br>HLA-matched unrelated donor (MUD)<br>Permissive mismatched donor<br>Banked cord blood | Not applicable                                               |
| Peripheral blood (mobilised by GCSF)<br>Bone marrow<br>Cord blood | Peripheral blood (mobilised by GCSF)<br>Bone marrow          |
| Transplant for both replacement of the patient’s haematopoietic system as well as graft-vs-tumour effect | Purpose of the transplant is to allow for high dose chemotherapy (i.e. stem cell rescue) |
| Potential for long term remission                            | Often associated with relapse                                |
| Possibility of graft rejection                               | Rejection (and thus graft failure) is very rare              |
| High risk of graft-vs-host disease                           | No GVHD                                                      |
| High transplant related mortality                            | Lower TRM                                                    |
| High risk of infections - bacterial, fungal, viral           | Typically higher risk of bacterial infections                |

Transplantation in either kind replaces diseased marrow with healthy marrow, either to restore normal haematopoietic and/or immune function (e.g. severe immunodeficiencies, aplastic anaemia) or to treat cancer, either by allowing high-dose chemotherapy to be administered with with (AlloSCT) or without (AutoSCT) a graft-vs-tumour effect.

The stem cells for transplant can either be sourced from the peripheral blood (more common) or bone marrow:
| PBSC                  | BM                                               |
|-----------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Rapid engraftment     | Slow engraftment                                 |
| More GVHD             | Less GVHD (fewer T-cells)                        |
| More GVL effect       | Less GVL effect                                  |
| Minimal complications | Highly invasive - 50-100 aspirates for the donor |

### Process
#### Conditioning
Conditioning is the process of delivering high-dose chemoradiotherapy to both provide adequate immunosuppression to prevent rejection of the transplanted graft, and to eradicate the disease for which the transplant is being performed. 

Can incluide:
- Myeloablative conditioning - generally young, fit patients with malignant disorders. Combination chemotherapy with total body irradiation. 
- Reduced intensity conditioning - either for non-malignant conditions or malignant conditions in less fit patients.
- Non-myeloablative conditioning 

#### Engraftment
Engraftment occurs when the stem cells are established in the recipient marrow and begin to produce new donor cells (typically, we consider an absolute neutrophil count >0.5 cells/nL on two consecutive days to represent engraftment). This usually occurs somewhere between day +15 and day +30. 

_May_ be preceded by an engraftment syndrome consisting off fevers, rash, ARDS, and fluid shifts, that is managed with high dose steroids. 

#### Chimerism 
AlloSCT patients are assessed periodically with chimerism studies, which assess the genotypic origin of post-transplant haematopoiesis:
- Chimerism 100% donor -> perfect engraftment
- Mixed chimerism -> a variable ratio of donor and recipient derived cells
- Engraftment failure -> majority or entirely receipient-derived haemtopoiesis

Increasing levels of mixed chimerism tends to predict relapse in patients with haematologic malignancies.

### Graft-verus-Host Disease
GvHD refers to the activation of donor-derived T-cells against the tissues of the recipient. Risk factors include:
- Older age
- Sex mismatch
- HLA disparity
- Intensity of conditioning regimen
- Stem cell source
- Gut microbiota 

Can be:
- Acute = within the first 100d 
- Chronic = after d100 -> tends to manifest similarly to autoimmune diseases -> manage similarly to acute 

#### Organ Involvement in Acute GvHD:
- Skin - 90% of cases, can range from mild rash to SJS/TENS-type syndrome
- Liver - often presents with deranged LFTs +/- high bilirubin -> ==🔴key differential is VOD/SOS== 
- Gut - nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea 
- In theory can affect any organ 

#### Management:
- Mild -> topical steroids, budesonide
- Moderate/severe -> high dose steroids +/- other agents, e.g.:
  - Extracorporeal photophoresis
  - ATG/Campath
  - Ruxolitinib

#### Prophylaxis
Current standard of care is post-transplant cyclophosphamide (PTCy), as well as the usual maintenance immunosuppression.

### Other Post-Transplant Issues
#### CMV
Unlike solid-organ transplant, the highest risk in BMT is when the _donor is negative_ but the _recipient is positive_. Can affect any organ and be difficult to differentiate from GvHD (particularly in the gut). Treat with:
- Valganciclovir
- IV ganciclovir
- Foscarnet
- ANti-CMV CTLs

#### EBV -> similar to CMV + long-term risk of PTLD

#### VOD/SOS
 Veno-occlusive disease (VOD)/ sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS):
* Usually within 21 days post allogeneic SCT.
* Hepatomegaly, ascites, weight gain.
* Risk factors: prior Rx (gemtuzumab) age, pre-existing liver dysfunction, HLA mismatch, TBI, busulfan.
* Prevention with ursodeoxycholic acid.
* Mortality rate >80%.
* Start defibrotide.

#### Prophylaxis 
Bacterial infections most common.
* Lifelong penicillin V or alternative if penicillin allergy.

⠀Viral infections / reactivation.
* Valaciclovir prophylaxis.
* Weekly CMV, patients on prophylactic valganciclovir till D100.
* EBV viral load: risk of post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder (PTLD).
* Other viruses: HHV6, HHV8.

⠀Fungal infections.
* Fungal prophylaxis (posaconazole/voriconazole) on discharge till D100, persistent neutropenia or have active GVHD.

⠀PJP/ Toxoplasmosis prophylaxis.
* Bactrim DS or dapsone + pyrimidine or atovaquone.

HSV/VZV and PJP Prophylaxis should continue for 12 months or for another 2 months from cessation of immune suppression whichever is longer.

### Disease Relapse or Impending Relapse
Risk depends on:
* Measurable residual disease (MRD) status at transplant.
* Poor risk cytogenetics and high risk next-generation sequencing (NGS).
* Myeloablative (MA) versus non-myeloablative (NMA) transplant.

⠀Assessment:
* Day 30 BMAT to assess disease status.
* Chimerism monthly in the first 100 days.

⠀Therapy:
* Immunosuppression withdrawal aiming for increased GVL.
* Donor lymphocyte infusion.
* Post transplant maintenance ie Azathioprine, Flt 3 inhibitor, etc

### Psychosocial Factors
This is a _**MASSIVE**_ aspect of BMT:
* Did they patient feel that they were mentally prepared for the transplant. 
* Physical limitations, mental health.
* Family and social support, financial support 
* Will, advanced care directives etc.
