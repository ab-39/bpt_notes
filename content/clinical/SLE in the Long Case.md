# SLE in the Long Case
#bpt/clinical

### Definition 
SLE is a complex autoimmune condition with variable, multisystem manifestations. The EULAR criteria require the following to hold for a diagnosis of SLE to be made:
- Positive ANA (≥1:80)
- At least one clinical feature of lupus, e.g.:
  - Fevers
  - Leukopenia
  - Autoimmune haemolysis
  - Seizures
  - Cutaneous lupus
  - Pericarditis
  - Joint involvement
  - Lupus nephritis
- A total score >10, where different features carry different amounts of points, the highest points being given for:
  - Positive anti-dsDNA or Smith antibodies
  - Lupus nephritis
  - Lupus arthropathy
  - Pericarditis 
  - Acute cutaneous lupus
  - Seizures
- Note that it is possible to have APLS without having SLE 
  
> [!WARNING] Lupus and Pregnancy
> In a long case of a woman with lupus (or, indeed, any immunological condition), the consideration of pregnancy needs to be _at least_ asked about.

### Symptoms
As lupus can affect multiple organs, the symptoms can be varied:
- General - fatigue (almost always), Raynaud phenomenon, weight loss, fevers
- MSK - arthralgias, inflammatory arthritis, Jaccoud arthropathy, myalgias, RA/SLE overlap syndrome
- Cutaneous - malar rash, photosensitivity, skin rash, alopecia, painless oral/nasal ulcers
- Renal - lupus nephritis
  - Proteinuria >500mg/24h (i.e. spot uPCR >50mg/mmol) -> renal biopsy
- Respiratory - ILD, pleurisy, pleural effusion, pulmonary hypertension, diaphragmatic weakness
- Cardiac - pericarditis, pericardial effusion, myocarditis, valvular regurgitation, Libman-Sacks endocarditis, arrhythmia
- GI - oesophageal dysmotility, relfux, abdominal pain, hepatitis, pancreatitis, serositis
- Haematological - anaemia, lymphadenopathy, ITP
- Neuropsychiatric - cognitive dysfunction (“lupus fog”), mood disorder, anxiety, headaches (common), seizures, psychosis, neuropathies, aseptic meningitis
- Secondary Sjögren syndrome
- Antiphospholipid syndrome

### Principles of Management 
- Aim for remission or low disease activity as measured by the SLEDAI score (low activity = 0, remission <4), as well as preventing flares
- _All_ patients get HCQ @ 5mg/kg:
  - Effective for skin, joints, serositis, and prevention of end-organ complications
  - Prevents renal flares
  - Also has antithrombotic effects
  - Safe during pregnancy
  - Risks - ocular toxicity (can use mepacrine instead), GI upset, rash, aquagenic pruritis
  - Chloroquine is an “upgrade” from HCQ, but is even _more_ eye toxic 
- If major organs are involved -> immunosuppression:
  - Prednisolone
  - Conventional immunosuppressants - e.g. CYC, MMF, AZA, MTX, etc. (often used as steroid sparing agents)
  - Biologics - e.g. rituximab, newer therapies like anifrolumab and belimumab
- Vitamin D for all patients
- Markers of activity:
  - Clinical -> fatigue, alopecia, arthralgias, chest pain
  - Biochemical -> hypocomplementaemia (esp. C3), rising anti-dsDNA titres, cytopenias
***
### Complications of SLE and its treatment
- Cardiovascular disease (direct risk of SLE)
- Malignancy - risk overall reduced by HCQ:
  - NHL
  - Lung cancer
  - Invasive cervical cancer
  - **But** decreased risk of breast, endometrial, and ovarian cancer
- Infection

### Pregnancy and Lupus
Pregnancy is contraindicated in SLE patients if there is:
- Severe pulmonary hypertension
- Severe ILD
- Advanced renal insufficiency
- Advanced heart failure
- Previous severe pre-eclampsia/HELLP despite therapy 

Pregnancy should be deferred if there is:
- Severe disease flare within the last six months
- Active lupus nephritis 
- Stroke within the past six months

Drugs in pregnancy:
- MMF - stop 3-6 months prior to conception
- Safe in pregnancy = HCQ, AZA, prednisolone, TAC/CsA
- Active lupus nephritis/APLS -> aspirin (HCQ actually also helps)

### General Structure in the Long Case
- Diagnosis and context
- Investigations and confirmation of diagnosis
- Progress and flares
- Management since diagnosis
  - Important to get a medication history 
- Current activity and functional status
- Complications of SLE and treatment
  - Is there a prothrombotic state?
- Monitoring and follow up
- Cardiovascular risk
- Effect on QOL (_especially_ for women of childbearing years)

#### Common Issues in the long case
* Management of SLE.  Treatment goals: 
  * long term patient survival, 
  * prevention of organ damage, 
  * prevention of flares, 
  * management of comorbidities
  * optimisation of health-related quality of life
  * management of pregnancy
* Others depending on organ involvement and complications.
* Adherence issues, with diet, appointments, etc.
* Psychosocial issues.
* Complications and monitoring of immune-suppression.
* Cardiovascular risk factors.
