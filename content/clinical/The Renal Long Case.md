# The Renal Long Case
#bpt/clinical
- - -
### Key Points
- Chronic kidney disease is the appropriate name of the entity = defined as abnormalities of kidney structure _or_ function for over three months, with implications for health
  - Once requiring renal replacement therapy or eGFR <15mL/min -> end-stage kidney disease
- Need to know:
  - Aetiology of renal disease
  - eGFR
  - Presence or albuminuria = most important prognostic factor in CKD - measured with albumin:creatinine ratio:
    - Normal = A1 = <3mg/mmol = 30mg/g
    - Microalbuminuria = A2 = <30mg/mmol = 300mg/g
    - Macroalbuminuria A3 = >30mg/mmol = 300mg/g
- Commonest aetiologies of CKD (NB. often multifactorial):
  - Diabetic nephropathy +/- HTN (~40%)
  - Glomerulonephritis (most commonly IgAN)
  - HTN
  - Renovascular disease
  - Autoimmune disease
  - Obstructive uropathy
  - Toxic nephropathy
  - ADPKD

### CKD as a Long Case Issue:
~Confirm the diagnosis~:
- UEC to assess current eGFR/sCr 
- Urine ACR to assess albuminuria
- Additional tests to determine aetiology:
  - HbA1c, renal Dopplers, vasculitis screen, etc.

~Assess severity~: as above

~Set goals~: reduce albuminuria, discuss eventual further options of treatment 

~Treatment plan~

~Involve others~: renal physician, dietitian, PT, transplant MDT, etc.

~Complications~:
- CKD-MBD -> check CMP, DXA etc.
- Hyperparathyroidism -> check PTH (may need phosphate binders)
  - Secondary = high phosphate, low calcium, high PTH
  - Tertiary = high phosphate, high calcium, very high PTH 
  - Dialysis patients may be treated with calcimimetics 
  - Check for a parathyroidectomy scar on examination
- Anaemia -> check FBE (may need darbepoietin) - aim Hb 100-115g/L + correct iron deficiency
- Gout -> uric acid 
- CVD -> CVRFs
- Hyperkalaemia -> UECs (may need binders)
- Malnutrition
- Fluid overload
- HTN (note hypotension is frequently a complication of dialysis requiring antihypertensives to be withheld on non-dialysis days)
- Metabolic acidosis -> worsens bone demineralisation and sarcopenia -> treat with NaHCO3
- Depression
- OSA

### Dialysis
Indications for dialysis:
- Signs/symptoms of uraemia = absolute indication (rare)
- Other common relative indications:
  - Refractory fluid overload
  - Refractory hyperkalaemia
  - Refractory metabolic acidosis
  - Significant malnutrition secondary to nausea/vomiting/anorexia
- Dialysis should be raised with the patient when eGFR <15mL/min, and recommended when eGFR <5mL/min irrespective of symptoms

> [!TIP] Indications for dialysis = AEIOU
> A = acidosis
> E = electrolytes (i.e. hyperkalaemia)
> I = intoxicants
> O = fluid overload
> U = uraemia 

Modalities of dialyisis:
- No mortality benefit between PD and HD - depends on what works for the patient
- ~Peritoneal dialysis~:
  - Daily therapy via Tenckhoff catheter - inserted surgically or radiologically into abdomen and requiring 4-6 weeks to mature 
  - Either manual exchanges (CAPD) or automated overnight machine exchanges (APD)-3-4/day ~ 3 short and 1 long dwell
  - Suitable for relatively independent patients
  - Typically can only be done for a few years before the peritoneal membrane becomes exhausted
  - Relative contraindications:
    - Previous abdominal surgery
    - Uncontrolled abdominal malignancy
    - Concerns re: hygiene, housing, etc. 
  - Complications:
    - PD peritonitis
    - Tenckhoff skin site infection
    - Malnutrition
    - Difficulty with dextrose in bags in patients with diabetes
- ~Haemodialysis~:
  - Usually 3x 4-5h sessions a week - significant time commitment!
  - Usually at a dialysis centre but can happen at home
    - This makes access a significant long case issue in patients on dialysis 
  - Via:
    - AVF - needs surgical creation and takes ~8-12 weeks to mature -> significant source of complications - e.g. thrombus, sepsis, steal phenomena, etc. 
    - Tunnelled central line - suitable for immediate initiation
  - Suitable for patients without significant cardiac disease and able to tolerate the efect on lifestyle
  - Contraindications:
    - Cardiac disease - due to the effects of brisk fluid shifts
    - Comorbidities
  - Complications:
    - Bacteraemia
    - Cardiac disease
    - Hypotension -> needing to use midodrine to get through dialysis is a bad sign
    - Malnutrition -> HD is protein catabolic
    - Dialysis dysequilibrium syndrome
    - Many others…
- Monitoring adequacy of dialysis:
  - Fluid management - interdialytic weight gain
  - Urea reduction ratio (=Kt/V -> should be 1.4 in HD and 1.7/w in PD)
  - Monitoring electrolytes

### Transplantation
- Donor source:
  - Cadaveric (cannot be waitlisted until on dialysis)
  - Living related (may be pre-emptive)
  - Living unrelated (either paired exchange or ABO-incompatible)
- Transplant workup:
  - Need to know: was the patient on dialysis at the time of transplant?
  - Need to know: what was the HLA-matching status?
  - Need to know: what was the donor/recipient CMV status?
- Usual placement = right iliac fossa
- Immunological matching is a complex process, need to factor in:
  - ABO status
  - HLA typing -> development of donor specific antibodies, risk of sensitisation can be increased by certain events:
    - Blood transfusion
    - Previous transplantation
    - Pregnancy
- Absolute contraindications to transplantation:
  - Cardiovascular
    - Severe peripheral arterial disease
    - Severe cerebrovascular disease 
  - Infection
    - Active/incurable infection
  - Malignancy
    - Most malignancies in the past five years
    - Local prostate cancer can be an exception
  - Immunologic/haematologic
    - Incurable bleeding diathesis
  - Social/environmental
    - Documented active and ongoing treatment nonadherence
    - Tobacco/alcohol/drug dependence within the last
    - Significant untreatable psychosocial issues
    - BMI >35
  - Reversible kidney failure
- Relative contraindications to transplantation:
  - Advanced age
  - History of myocardial infarction in the past 3-6 months
  - HFpEF or HFrEF
  - Diabetes with additional significant complications
  - Hypercoagulable states
  - Pulmonary hypertension
  - Urological abnormalities 
  - Hyperparathyroidism
- Immunosuppression:
  - Induction -> very early on, most patients might not be aware
  - Maintenance treatment is usually with _triple_ therapy:
    - Calcineurin inhibitor, e.g.:
      - Tacrolimus -> HTN, tremor, NODAT, alopecia
      - Cyclosporin -> hirsutism, more rejection
    - Antimetabolite, e.g.:
      - Mycophenolate mofetil -> diarrhoea, fewer drug reactions
      - Mycophenolic acid
      - Azathioprine -> safer in pregnancy, but high risk of cytopenias 
    - Prednisolone -> side-effects +++, frequently an issue in its own right 
  - May see _everolimus_ or another mTOR inhibitor substituted (often to lower skin cancer risk or in the case of unacceptable toxicity) **but** carries the risk of proteinuria and cytopenias  
  - Need infection prophylaxis:
    - CMV/VZV/EBV -> (val)ganciclovir
    - PJP -> TMP/SMX 
- Post-transplant care:
  - Generally ICU for 2-3 days followed by an additional week in hospital 
  - Early postoperative complications:
    - Surgical issues -> e.g. vascular, urological complications
    - ATN of donor kidneys:
      - Accounts for 90+% of AKI in the postoperative phase 
        - Cadaveric kidney = 34% of cases
        - Living donor = 9% of cases
  - Late complications:
    - Cardiometabolic disease 
    - Malignancy:
      - PTLD
      - Skin cancers
    - Infection:
      - Regular community-acquired infections
      - CMV
      - BK virus 
- Causes of deterioration in renal function post-transplant:
  - Common:
    - Volume depletion
    - ATN
    - CNI nephrotoxicity
    - Urinary obstruction
    - Infections - e.g. pyelonephritis, BK polyomavirus, CMV
  - Less common -> often recurrent primary disease, e.g.:
    - FSGS
    - Primary membranous nephropathy
    - Diabetic kidney disease
    - IgA nephropathy
    - C3 glomerulonephropathy
  - De novo glomerular disease
- Normal monitoring post-transplant:
  - Close monitoring of UEC, ACR, urinalysis post-discharge 
  - Viral PCR monitoring for CMV and BK
  - Protocol biopsy at 3 and 12 months, or if any earlier concern re: graft function
- Rejection:
  - Hyperacute - immediately post-transplant, rare
  - Acute - days-to-weeks post-transplant:
    - Either antibody-mediated - DSAs targeted at HLAs or T-cell-mediated 
    - Often precipitated by acute infection, change to immunosuppressive medication, and nonadherence to immunosuppression
  - Chronic - over years:
    - Fibrosis and scarring of the donor kidney 
    - Causes include previous episodes of acute rejection and use of calcineurin inhibitors
