### General Approach
1. Position the patient lying at 45’ with the chest adequately exposed 
2. Inspection:
   - Cyanosis -> congenital heart disease
   - Oxygen -> pulmonary hypertension
   - Wasting/cachexia
   - **Scars**:
     - Midline sternotomy:
       - Without radial or saphenous harvest scars -> valve surgery -> listen for a click
       - With radial or saphenous harvest scars -> CAGS
     - Thoracotomy scar -> ?transplant
     - Posterior scars -> ?childhood cardiac surgery
     - Port scars -> mini?minimally invasive surgery
   - **Devices**:
     - Usually upper left chest - may need to palpate if higher BMI
       - 1-2 leads, both thin on CXR -> PPM
       - 3 leads, all thin on CXR -> CRT
       - 2-3 leads, one of them a thick coil on CXR -> ICD +/- PPM
3. Hands:
   - Clubbing -> congenital heart disease 
   - Tar staining -> cigarette smoker
   - Palmar crease pallor -> anaemia 
   - Arachnodactyly -> Marfan syndrome
     - Can then proceed to thumb sign and wrist sign
4. Pulses:
   - Feel both radials bilaterally (if present): radio-radial delay -> coarctation of the aorta
   - Comment on *rate* and *rhythm* 
   - Perform the maneouvre for waterhammer pulse, if present -> AR 
5. Blood pressure:
   - Narrow pulse pressure -> AS, MS (mitral stenosis) 
   - Wide pulse pressure -> AR 
6. Face:
   - Xanthelasma -> hyperlipidaemia
   - Conjunctival pallor -> anaemia 
   - High arched palate -> Marfan syndrome
   - Central cyanosis -> congenital heart disease 
7. Neck:
   - JVP:
     - Elevated, large v-waves -> TR
   - Carotids - feel _both_ one after another:
     - Weak, slow rising -> AS 
     - Collapsing -> AR 
     - Small-volume -> MR 
8. Praecordium:
   - Palpate the apex beat - expect to feel a moderate impulse localised to the fifth intercostal space in the midclavicular line:
     - _Volume-loaded_ = forceful but unsustained, often displaced laterally -> AR, MR
     - Pressure-loaded = forceful and sustained, usually not displaced -> AS 
     - Double or triple impulse -> HCM 
     - May also feel an apical thrill -> mitral murmur
   - Palpate with the palm for a parasternal heave -> pHTN, RV failure 
   - Gently palpate with the fingers for a palpable P2 -> pHTN
   - Palpate with the fingers for an aortic thrill -> AS 
   - Auscultate in the following order, timing against the carotids (murmurs listed based on where they should be loudest):
     - With each cycle, also listen for the character of the heart sounds:
       - Soft S1 -> MR
       - Loud S1 -> MS
       - Soft S2 -> AR
       - Loud S2 -> AS (A2), pHTN (P2)
       - Split S2 - helps to differentiate causes of congenital heart disease:
         - Fixed splitting -> ASD
         - Increased normal splitting -> MR, VSD
         - Paradoxical splitting -> PDA, AS
     - Bell at the apex:
       - Pansystolic murmur -> MR
       - Late systolic murmur with an opening click -> MVP
       - Mid-diastolic rumbling murmur -> MS 
       - Early diastolic, low-pitched heart sound = S3 -> AR, MR 
       - Late diastolic, low-pitched heart sound = S4 -> AS, HCM
     - Swith to diaphragm and listen again at the apex
     - Left lower sternal border:
       - Pansystolic murmur -> TR, possibly VSD (associated thrill)
       - Early diastolic murmur -> AR 
     - Left upper sternal border:
       - Late ejection systolic murmur -> HCM 
       - Ejection systolic murmur -> ASD (associated fixed splitting of S2), PS
     - Right upper sternal border:
       - Ejection systolic murmur -> AS 
     - Carotids _bilaterally_:
       - Technique involves gradually moving up from the aortic region
       - Radiant murmur -> AS
       - Bruit -> carotid stenosis
     - Listen in the axilla if indicated
     - Listen again where the murmur is loudest, and listen for accentuation. Get the patient to:
       - Breathe in -> right-sided murmur
       - Breathe out -> left-sided murmur
       - Valsalva manoeuvre -> HCM (also prolongs MVP)
       - Handgrip -> **softens** HCM (also shortens MVP)
   - Reposition the patient into the left lateral decubitus position:
     - Repalpate
     - Listen again in the apex: mitral murmurs should be accentuated 
     - Perform manoeuvres if relevant
     - Listen in the axilla if indicated
   - Reposition the patient so that they are sitting forward:
     - Repalpate
     - Listen again at the left lower sternal border with the patient breathing out: AR should be accentuated 
     - Perform manoeuvres if relevant 
     - Percuss and listen to the lung bases for coarse crackles
   - Reposition the patient so that they are lying flat (if tolerable):
     - Palpate the liver: pulsatile -> TR 
     - Palpate the shins: oedema -> RV failure 
     - Look at the feet: clubbing -> cyanotic heart disease

### Presentation
Today I performed a cardiovascular examination on Mr/s F, an X-year-old wo/man presenting with Y. The most salient findings include:
- Comment on:
  - Presence of: oxygen, device, scars
  - Auscultation findings: murmur and its characteristics
    - _Grade_ the murmur - the key difference is between grades III and IV:
      - III = easily heard but _no_ palpable thrill
      - IV = palpable thrill 
  - Supportive peripheral findings
  - Evidence of pHTN or heart failure
    - pHTN particularly relevant to congenital heart disease -> indicates development of Eisenmenger physiology 
- Provide a top differential (particularly if it is a simple lesion)
