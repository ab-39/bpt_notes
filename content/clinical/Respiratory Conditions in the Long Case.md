If the primary diagnosis is the ILD rather than the underlying immunological condition, it is better to refer to it as “connective-tissue disease related ILD related to X”. Don’t forget that in addition to the dyspnoea, cough is also a major symptom in ILD. 

> [!TIP] Progression
> One key thing not to forget is to ask the patient _if the disease is progressing_:
> - Are the scans getting worse?
> - Are the PFTs getting worse?
> - Are their symptoms getting worse?
> In particular, it is important to mention the presence or absence of any _exacerbations_.

Indications for a lung transplant in the setting of ILD include:
- 6MWT desaturation <88%
- Use of supplemental O2 at rest or exertion
  - i.e. ILD + home O2 -> need for a transplant
- Pulmonary hypertension

Think about barriers to a lung transplant:
- Uncontrolled malignancy
- Surgical factors
- Other/multiple organ failure
- Obesity (>35, more of a relative contraindication)
- Significant underweight (<16)
- Age >70y *(relative)
- Poorly controlled diabetes
- Psychosocial factors -> this in particular is a significant point to consider in the long case 
- Substance abuse

> [!TIP] Think about _Treatable Traits_
> In respiratory medicine, most symptoms are multifactorial, so management is often about finding the _treatable traits_ that can be fixed and optimising them rather than curing the underlying symptoms. 

#### Sleep Apnoea 
Sleep apnoea is often a “hidden” condition in many medical patients, and can be easily raised as a potential issue in many long case patients. It is, in a sense, part of the metabolic syndrome, and has a significant association with cardiovascular risk. Risk factors are primarily metabolic syndrome and overweight, as well as neck circumference, and treatment is primarily with CPAP, weight loss, and other devices. Importantly, we should also consider if patients are eligible for GLP-1RAs. Complications are primarily related to cardiovascular disease. 

#### Pulmonary Embolus 
Main things to consider in the long case:
- What were the risk factors?
- How was it treated?
- Has pHTN now developed as a complication (CTEPH)? -> group IV pHTN usually warrants lifelong anticoagulation 

#### Bronchiectasis
- Ix: check Ig (if deficient -> IVIg), check Aspergillus serology, do a myeloma screen, sputum culture + AFB (?NTMBs)
- Tx: airway clearance techniques prescribed by a respiratory physiotherapist; mucolytics (e.g. bromhexine, nebulised saline, dornase in CF), Pseudomonas eradication if recurrent exacerbations
  - Typically _don’t_ use inhaled corticosteroids (risk of NTMBs) unless there is a comorbid indication
