# The Abdominal Short Case
#bpt/clinical
- - -
### General Approach
1. Position the patient lying flat with the abdomen adequately exposed
2. Inspection:
   - Obvious abdominal distention -> ?ascites vs mass
   - Jaundice -> chronic liver disease
   - **Scars**:
     - Rutherford-Morrison incision (typically in RLQ) -> renal transplant
     - Hockey stick incision -> liver transplant
     - Other abdominal scars
   - Caput Medusae -> CLD
3. Abdomen:
   - Four quadrant light then deep palpation
   - Liver:
     - Begin in the RLQ and palpate superiorly with the edge of the hand, moving up on expiration
     - Note the texture and quality of the liver edge:
       - Firm and nodular -> cirrhosis, ADPKD/hepatic cysts, metastatic disease 
       - Tender -> hepatitis, right heart failure, Budd-Chiari syndrome, HCC
       - Pulsatile -> TR, HCC, vascular abnormalities 
     - Measure the liver span by percussion:
       - Percuss up from the RLQ until dullness is encountered
       - Either find a dermatologic landmark or ask the patient to mark it with their finger
       - Percuss down in the mid-clavicular line until dullness is encountered
       - Measure the span with a measuring tape - the normal span is ~12cm (although up to 14cm can be normal in men)
   - Spleen:
     - Begin in the RLQ and palpate superomedially with the edge of the hand, moving up on expiration
     - If the spleen is palpable _beyond_ the midline, this suggests that the spleen is _massively_ enlarged (i.e. likely >20cm in span or >1kg in weight)
   - Kidneys:
     - Note the presence of a Rutherford-Morrison incision - palpate over it if needed to feel for a transplanted kidney, noting the presence of tenderness 
     - Palpate bimanually for both native kidneys
     - Auscultate over both renal arteries and any transplant for a bruit -> renal artery stenosis, AVF (e.g. post-kidney biopsy), possibly normal in early stages of a transplant
   - Ascites
     - With the hand in the sagittal plane, percuss from the midline down in the leftward direction until dullness is encountered - hold this point
     - Reposition the patient into the right lateral decubitus position and percuss again:
       - Resonant -> ascites is present (“shifting dullness”)
       - Dull -> ascites is less likely to be present
     - Take this opportunity to re-palpate for the spleen if it was not felt above
     - If the spleen is still not palpable, percuss over Traube’s space:
       - Dull -> mild splenomegaly
       - Resonant -> no splenomegaly 
4. Depending on the findings, proceed down the appropriate path…

### The Renal Short Case
The name of the game is finding evidence of CKD and/or graft dysfunction. It is also imperative to check for side effects of immunosuppressants:
1. Most of this can be done lying at 45’
2. General inspection:
   - Note the presence of Cushingoid facies, thin skin, ecchymoses, etc. -> steroid ADRs
   - Hirsutism -> cyclosporine ADR
3. Hands/Arms:
   - Terry’s nails -> CKD 
   - Palmar crease pallor -> anaemia
   - Excoriations -> uraemic pruritis
   - **Look for a fistula** -> palpate for a _thrill_ and auscultate for a _bruit_, which indicate adequate patency 
     - The presence of a working fistula in a patient with a transplant suggests that the graft is not functioning appropriately 
   - Tremor -> tacrolimus ADR 
   - Proximal myopathy -> steroid ADRs
4. Check for metabolic flap -> uraemia
5. **Check the blood pressure** 
6. Check the JVP
7. Listen to the heart 
8. Check for peripheral oedema 
9. Briefly check for ophthalmoplegias that could suggest a COW aneurysm

### The Hepatologic Short Case
The name of the game is looking for signs of chronic liver disease:
1. Most of this can be done lying at 45’
2. General inspection:
   - Note the presence of jaundice 
3. Hands/Arms:
   - Clubbing 
   - Palmar erythma
   - Dupuytren’s contracture
   - Tremor -> tacrolimus ADR 
   - Proximal myopathy -> steroid ADRs
4. Check for metabolic flap -> ammonaemia 
5. Eyes:
   - Scleral icterus -> CLD 
   - Conjunctival pallor -> anaemia 
6. Neck: check the JVP
7. Chest:
   - Spider naevi -> CLD
   - Gynaecomastia -> CLD 
8. Check for peripheral oedema

### The Haematologic Short Case
The name of the game is assessing for further lymphadenopathy:
1. First assess inguinal lymphadenopathy
   - Also check the large joints of the legs for haemarthrosis 
2. The remainder of the examination can be done sitting up  
3. General inspection:
   - Bruising -> thrombocytopenia, haemophilia
   - Rash -> lymphoma
   - Cyanosis -> polycythaemia 
   - Jaundice -> haemolysis
   - African race -> ?sickle cell disease 
4. Hands/arms:
   - Koilonychia, palmar crease pallor -> anaemia
   - Epitrochlear lymph nodes
   - Axillary lymph nodes
5. Face:
   - Scleral icterus -> jaundice
   - Conjunctival pallor -> anaemia 
   - Gum hypertrophy -> leukaemia
   - Gingival haemorrhage -> thrombocytopenia
6. Head:
   - Cervical lymph nodes (from behind)
7. Palpate the spine for bony tenderness
