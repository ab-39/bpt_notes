### Overview
#### Diagnosis and General Targets:
Diagnosis of diabetes is made with one positive test in symptomatic patients, or two positive tests in asymptomatic patients. The tests can be:
- BSL:
  - Fasting ≥7.0mmol/L
  - Random ≥11.1mmol/L
- oGTT ≥11.1mmol/L (typically after equivocal lab tests)
- HbA1c ≥6.5%

Diabetes is either:
- Type 1 -> autoimmune destruction of islet cells -> often associated with microvascular complications due to hyperglycaemia
- Type 2 -> insulin resistance -> macrovascular complications tend to predominate due to other metabolic syndrome risk factors. Common microvascular complications include:
  - Retinopathy (20%)
  - Neuropathy (9%)
  - Nephropathy (10%)

The general HbA1c target for most patients is ≤7.0% (53mmol/mol), with some exceptions:
- In the _absence_ of CVD, long duration of diabetes, severe hypoglycaemia, or another contraindication -> aim ≤6.5%
- In the presence of impaired hypoglycaemic awareness -> aim ≤8.0%
- In the presence of a life limiting illness, aim for symptomatic control (usu. BSL 8.0-15.0mmol/L)
- In women planning for pregnancy, aim for the tightest possible control without severe hypoglycaemia, preferable ≤6.0%

#### Management
Lifestyle factors:
* Dietary modification
* Regular exercise
* Smoking cessation
* Lifestyle change also halves the progression from impaired glucose tolerance to T2DM
* As complications are often present in T2DM at diagnosis, prevention has an important role in reducing complications
* Lifestyle measures may also reduce other cardiovascular risk factors
  * Moderation of salt intake, weight reduction and exercise reduce blood pressure & cholesterol

Pharmacotherapy:
- First line -> metformin
- Second line options:
  - SGLT2i -> dapagliflozin, empagliflozin
    - Enhance glycosuria
    - Cardioprotective and renoprotective
    - Side effects include urinary tract infections, thrush, genital infections.
    - Possible amputation and atypical fracture risk. 
    - Increased risk diabetic ketoacidosis (even euglycaemic) especially if fasted or with illness. ==🔴Important in setting peri-operative evaluation==. 
  - DPP4i -> alogliptin, linagliptin, sitaglipitin, etc.
  - GLP1RA -> semaglutide, dulaglutide, etc.
    - “Incretins” which enhance beta cell function
    - Slew of cardiac and renal benefits, as well as weight loss
    - Side effects include N+V and constipation, pancreatitis.
    - Hypoglycaemia minimal but possible
    - Rare complication of non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy 
  - Sulfonylureas ==🔴risk of hypoglycaemia== -> gliclazide, glimepiride, glibenclamide, etc.

#### Diabetes in the Perioperative Setting
For the purposes of diabetes:
- “Minor” surgery = day case
- “Major” surgery = requiring admission

Key points:
- Postpone elective surgery, if possible, if glycaemic control is poor (HbA1c ≥ 9%).
- Blood glucose levels should be kept between 5 – 10mmol/l during the peri-operative period. 
- Hypoglycaemia must be avoided.
- Surgery for diabetics should be ideally scheduled in the morning.
- An insulin-glucose infusion is the best way of maintaining euglycaemia post-operatively.
- Traditional “sliding scale insulin” is usually ineffective and a “basal-bolus” insulin regimen is preferable once the patient has resumed eating when post-operative insulin requirements are unknown.  
- Frequent BGL monitoring in peri-operative period – AVOID HYPOGLYCAEMIA.
- Must cease SGLT2 inhibitors at least 72 hours pre-operatively to minimise the risk of euglycaemic ketoacidosis.
- GLP1 analogues should be continued but patients should adopt a clear fluid diet for 24 hours prior to planned procedures.

### Complications of Diabetes
#### Diabetic Nephropathy
Typically occurs in 20-30% of patients with diabetes, defined as:
- Persistent clinically detectable proteinuria
- Hypertension
- Reduced glomerular filtration

First sign is subclinical microalbuminuria -> marker of risk of progression to macroalbuminuria and ESKD + independent risk factor for CVD.
- Usual treatment is with an ACEi/ARB +/- SGLT2i
- Some newer trial data (e.g. [_FLOW_](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347)) for GLP1RAs also

#### Diabetic Retinopathy
Most important risk factors:
- Hyperglycaemia (recall that the HbA1c target of 7.0% comes from the DCCT study finding that the risk of retinopathy increases exponentially
- Hypertension 

Should follow up every 1-2 years with an optometrist. 

Treatments if proliferative:
- Laser photocoagulation
- Anti-VEGF injections

#### Diabetic Foot Disease
Develops as a combination of neuropathy, peripheral arterial disease, and poor wound healing. Patients should be screened at least annually, and follow up with a podiatrist. Daily foot care is of paramount importance. 

The “end stage” of diabetic foot disease is Charcot foot - a neuroarthropathy characterised by _pes planus_. Careful examination of Charcot foot should include assessment of:
- Pulses
- CRT
- Vibration sense
- Monofilament light touch sense
- Reflexes 

#### General Approach to Complications
In the long case, frame as:
- Macrovascular -> stroke, AMI, PVD
- Microvascular -> retinopathy, DKD, neuropathy
- Autonomic -> erectile dysfunction, orthostasis
- Diabetic foot disease -> ulcers, sepsis 
- Hospitalisations -> DKA, HHS
- Hypoglycaemia -> frequency of episodes, awareness
