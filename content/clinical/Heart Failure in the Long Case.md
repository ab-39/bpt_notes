# Heart Failure in the Long Case
#bpt/clinical
- - -
### Overview 
Heart failure is a clnical syndrome consisting of signs and symptoms caused by structural and/or functional cardiac impairment corroborated by elevated natriuretic peptide levels and/or evidence of pulmonary or systemic congestion. Left ventricular function can be:
- Reduced -> ≤40%
- Mildly reduced -> 41-49%
- Preserved -> ≥50%
- Improved -> initially below 40%, now improved >10% to above 40%

> [!TIP] Grading Symptoms
> Symptoms of heart failure are graded with (and in the long case, should always be reported with) the New York Heart Association classification:
> 1. Class I = cardiac disease without functional limitation; no symptoms with ordinary physical activity
> 2. Class II = cardiac disease with slight functional limitation; comfortable at rest but symptomatic with ordinary activity 
> 3. Class III = cardiac disease with marked functional limitation; comfortable at rest but even mild activity results in symptoms
> 4. Class IV = symptomatic at rest

### Aetiology 
HFrEF -> almost always due to ischaemic heart disease and prior myocardial infarction (>50%). Other important causes include:
- Hypertension
- Non-ischaemic dilated cardiomyopathy, e.g.:
  - Alcohol
  - Thyrotoxicosis
  - Viral myocarditis
  - Idiopathic

HFpEF can in a sense be thought of as a part of the metabolic syndrome, going in tandem with:
- HTN
- Diabetes
- Obesity
Other important risk factors are female sex and aortic stenosis. 

Acute exacerbations almost always have some sort of trigger, e.g.:
- ==🔴An episode of ischaemia== - not to be missed!
- Non-adherence to medications or fluid restriction
- Arrhytmias - especially atrial fibrillation
- Acute kidney injury
- Systemic infections
- Anaemia
- Hyperthryoidism
- Drugs - NSAIDs, negative inotropes, caffeine
- Others - e.g. PE, OSA, etc.

> [!NOTE] Taking the History
> Quantify the frequency of exacerbations when taking the history.

#### Features of advanced HFrEF
* ≥ 2 hospital admissions in ≤ 12 months.
* Hypotension.
* Persistent ≥ NYHA 3.
* Recurrent ≥ 2 AICD shocks.
* End organ dysfunction (incl. right heart failure).
* LVEF < 20%.
* High/rising BNP/NT-proBNP.
* Low Serum Na+.
* Down-titration of ACE/ARB, β-blocker (as unable to tolerate).
* Increasing diuretic need.
* Prior or ongoing inotrope need.

### Key Investigations
The triad of key investigations in HF are:
- TTE - ?ejection fraction
- ECG - ?ischaemia
- UEC - ?renal function

BNP is also important for diagnostic purposes.

### Management
Goals:
- Manage fluid overload
- Improve morbidity -> reduce hospital admissions
- Reduce mortality
- Prevent and aim to reverse myocardial damage where possible

Lifestyle measures:
- **MDT** approach
- Regular physical activity tailored to NYHA class
- Management of comorbid OSA
- DASH approach -> sodium <2g/d
- Fluid restriction - usually ~1.5L/d
- Alcohol intake <20g/d 
- Regular vaccinations
- Daily weight -> report weight gain of >2kg in 2 days 
- Limit saturated fat
- Smoking cessation

Particularly in HFpEF, treatment of the underlying risk factors (e.g. diabetes, hypertension, etc.) is important.

Pharmacotherapy is based around the four pillars in HFrEF, all of which have been proven to improve mortality and morbidity:
- ACEi/ARB/ARNI
- Beta-blocker (once euvolaemic)
- MRA (some evidence for reduction of hospitalisations in HFpEF)
- SGLT2 inhibitors (these are the only drugs with high grade evidence at present for mortality benefit in HFpEF) 

The goal is to introduce all of these medications, and increase them to the highest tolerated dose. 

Secondary therapies includeL
- Ivabradine - typically only if LVEF <35% and HR >70bpm despite maximal GDMT
- Hydralazine
- Vericiguat 
- Newer therapies - e.g. omecamtiv mecarbil (myosin activator)
- Nitrates

Diuretics are about maintaining euvolaemia, and are not disease modifying therapy. They should not be used too liberally in HFpEF, as they can over-reduce filling pressures. 

Patients with ferritin <100µg/L should receive iron infusion. 

Device therapy:
- CRT is recommended for:
  - Patients with LVEF <35% and QRS >150ms
  - Patients with LVED <35% and LBBB
- ICD is recommended for secondary prevention, and also for primary prevention in patients with:
  - Recent myocardial infarction and LVEF <30%
  - LVEF <35%

[[Approach to a Heart Transplant|Cardiac transplantation]] may be recommended in patients with end-stage HF with severe symptoms, a poor prognosis, and no remaining alternative treatment options.
