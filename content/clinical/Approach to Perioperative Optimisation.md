# Approach to Perioperative Optimisation
#bpt/clinical
- - -
### Key Question:
Is there a reasonable chance of getting this patient a good postoperative outcome?

Ultimately, risk needs to be evaluated in light of the individual patient and their condition. 
- - -
### Main Issues:
- Cardiovascular:
  - Multiple conditions - AF, AS, HFrEF, CAD, etc. 
  - Predictors of increased risk include:
    - Recent myocardial infarction (<30d)
    - Decompensated HF (NYHA IV)
    - Unstable angina
    - Uncontrolled arrhythmias
    - Severe ventricular heart dysfunction
    - Pulmonary hypertension 
  - In ischaemic heart disease _stability_ is key:
    - How long has it been since their last event?
    - Do they have ongoing symptoms?
    - Have they had an ESE?
    - Remember - 12 months of uninterrupted DAPT is ideal
  - In heart failure, _decompensation_ is the main determinant of suitability for surgery (although even compensated HF still carries a perioperative mortality risk)
  - In aortic stenosis, problems typically arise as the severity approaches moderate to severe
  - Arrhythmias need to be _stable_ for an operation to occur, particularly, uncontrolled tachycardia can carry a significant postoperative risk
  - Cardiac procedures delay surgery:
    - CAGS -> at least three months
    - DES -> usually 6-12 months
    - BMS (uncommon) -> at least 6 weeks
- Respiratory:
  - Obstructive airways disease:
    - PFTs need to be repeated if symptoms have _changed_, not necessarily as a part of perioperative work up - the _functional status_ and exercise tolerance are more important 
    - Usual COPD-X things - pulmonary rehabilitation while awaiting surgery, smoking cessation, guideline-directed therapy, etc. 
    - More detail on smoking cessation:
      - Even stopping smoking a few days prior to surgery is better than not at all, but _six weeks prior_ to surgery is ideal 
      - Ensure that NRT is discussed
  - Pulmonary hypertension:
    - Severity is graded with the mPAP:
      - Mild = 20-40mmHg
      - Moderate >40-55mmHg
      - Severe >55mmHg 
    - Shared decision making is critical here, as mortality and morbidity risks are high! Need to consider factors such as:
      - Can we make this procedure minimally invasive?
      - Can we modify the anaesthesia? e.g. nerve block, regional, etc.
  - Obstructive sleep apnoea:
    - Commonly missed, commonly underdiagnosed
    - See obesity? See metabolic syndrome? -> think OSA
    - OSA = need for increased airway support during operation
    - Closely linked with obesity hypoventilation syndrome
- Stroke:
  - Delay elective surgery for at least six, but preferably nine months
- Diabetes mellitus:
  - Euglycaemia reduces the risk of perioperative complications
  - Pereoperative target in general is to aim for HbA1c <8%,
