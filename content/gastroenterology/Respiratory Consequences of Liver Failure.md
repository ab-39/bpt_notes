### Hepatopulmonary Syndrome
Hepatopulmonary syndrome arises as a result of intrapulmonary vascular dilatation (up to 100µm in size!) due to an imbalance of systemic vasoconstrictors and vasodilators in liver disease. This leads to rapid transit of blood through the pulmonary vasculature, causing a functional [[right-to-left shunt]] and subsequent hypoxia. The classic clinical finding in this situation is the platypnoea-orthodeoxia syndrome due to the exacerbating role of gravity, as well as clubbing. Investigations reveal an elevated [[respiratory/Alveolar-Arterial Gradient|alveolar-arterial gradient]] on ABG, and positive bubble sign on echocardiography. Symptomatic treatment can be with supplemental oxygen, while definitive treatment is with liver transplantation.

### Portopulmonary Hypertension
The pathophysiology of portopulmonary hypertension is poorly understood, but the result is of concomitant [[portal hypertension]] and group I [[pulmonary hypertension]] due to vascular remodelling of the pulmonary artery. Clinically, this presents with hypoxia, dyspnoea, and right heart failure. Diagnosis is with echocardiography or Swann-Ganz catheterisation. Treatment for PAH (e.g. PDEV inhibitors, endothelin antagonists) can be helpful, but definitive treatment is with liver transplantation.
