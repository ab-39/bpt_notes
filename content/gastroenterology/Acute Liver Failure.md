# Acute Liver Failure
#bpt/gastroenterology
- - -
### Overview
Acute liver failure is a triad of:
1. Acute liver injury
2. [[Portal Hypertension#Hepatic Encephalopathy|Hepatic Encephalopathy]]
3. [[haematology/Haemostatic Abnormalities in Liver Disease|Prolonged INR]] (usually >1.5)
Generally occurring in those without a history of liver disease. Most often, this is due to viruses or medications, but there are numerous agents and conditions that can provoke acute liver failure.

> [!TIP] Fun Fact
> The toxin of _Amanita phalloides_ is one of the most recently newsworthy causes of acute liver failure. 

Acute liver failure requires prompt discussion with a liver transplant centre, particularly if the patient meets the _King’s College Criteria_:
- If paracetamol is the causative agent:
  - Arterial pH <7.30 **or**
  - All three of:
    - Severe encephalopathy **and** 
    - PT >100s **and** 
    - sCr >300µol/L

> [!TIP] Mnemonic
> **A** **P**ara**C**eta**M**ol - the King’s College criteria are:
> - Acidosis (sufficient alone), or all of
> - PT/INR prolongation
> - Creatinine elevated
> - Mental status changes 

- If paracetamol is _not_ the causative agent:
  - PT >100s **or**
  - Any three of:
    - Age <10y or >40y
    - Bilirubin >300mmol/L
    - Jaundice >7 days prior to onset of encephalopathy
    - PT >50sau
