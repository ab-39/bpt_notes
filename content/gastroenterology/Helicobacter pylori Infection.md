# _Helicobacter pylori_ Infection
#bpt/gastroenterology
- - -
### Who To Test?
Consider testing for _H. pylori_ in patients:
- <60y presenting with uninvestigated dyspepsia _without_ alarm features
- Symptomatic of dyspepsia with a history of endoscopy
- With an active history of [[peptic ulcer disease]], MALT, or endoscopically-resected gastric cancer
- With a high risk of gastric cancer (e.g. East Asians or strong family history)
- When NSAID therapy is going to be initiated or has been used for prolonged periods
- Part
- Adults with ITP
- Unexplained [[iron deficiency anaemia]] after standard workup

#### Testing 
Urease breath test:
- Radiolabelled urea
- Metabolised to carbon by bacterial urease
- Detected on breath

Direct biopsy:
- Rapid urease test unreliable if active PPI use
- - -
### Treatment 
First line treatment for _H. pylori_ in Australia is generally with fourteen days of triple therapy:
- Amoxicillin (can utilise metronidazole if allergic)
- Clarithromycin
- PPI 

If there is concern for antibiotic resistance in the community (particularly >15% for clarithromycin or metronidazole), the latest American guidelines suggest _optimised bismuth quadruple therapy_:
- Triple therapy + metronidazole **or**
- Bismuth + metronidazole + tetracycline + PPI

Treatment should be followed with test of cure _at least 4 weeks_ after completion of treatment - typically with a breath test or faecal antigen test.
