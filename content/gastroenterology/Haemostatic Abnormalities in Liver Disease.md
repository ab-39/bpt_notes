# Haemostatic Abnormalities in Liver Disease 
#bpt/gastroenterology #bpt/haematology
- - -
### Overview
While the INR is frequently prolonged in patients with [[cirrhosis]], this is not truly representative of the extent to which the haemostatic system is deranged. This is because hepatic dysfunction affects the synthesis of both _procoagulant_ factors (i.e. I, II, VII, IX, X, and XI) as well as anticoagulant factors (i.e. protein C, protein S, antithrombin, and fibrinolytic factors). Moreover, platelets are reduced in number and function. This means that patients with cirrhosis can be at increased risk of both significant bleeding and thrombosis. 

> [!NOTE] Prolonged INR
> Factor VII has the shortest half-life in the serum of any of the clotting and anticoagulant factors, and hence the INR is the first test to become deranged.

Thromboelastography and ROTEM are more useful tests to assess the bleeding/clotting situation of the patient compared to the INR.

### Management
In general, cryoprecipitate is the best option to correct haemostatic abnormalities in cirrhotic patients with low or dysfunctional fibrinogen. While also useful, FFP runs the risk of fluid overload. Platelets should be transfused to maintain a count >50. hp
