# Haemochromatosis
#bpt/gastroenterology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### What is haemochromatosis?
Hereditary haemochromatosis is an autosomal recessive disorder leading to hepcidin deficiency and subsequently increased iron absorption from the GI tract, ultimately leading to iron overload and end organ damage - affecting the heart, pancreas (-> diabetes), liver, and skin. Classic exam finding is a tan. 

> [!NOTE] Haemochromatosis Arthropathy
> The key radiographic features of chondrocalcinosis, joint space narrowing, subchondral sclerosis, and hook-like osteophytes, particularly in the second and third MCP joints are characteristic of the arthropathy associated with hemochromatosis. These findings are distinct from those typically seen in other arthritic conditions like rheumatoid arthritis or gout. Haemochromatosis is strongly associated with pseudogout. 

- - -
### Epidemiology
Prevalence of ~0.5% in the Australian population. Slight male preponderance for clinically significant iron overload. 
- - -
### Pathophysiology
HH is caused by biallelic mutations in the _HFE_ gene, with two mutations being particularly common:
- C282Y (85% of cases)
- H63D - less severe phenotype 

Even with a homozygous C282Y/C282Y _HFE_ mutation, the penetrance is quite low (~30%) -> the majority of patients will not develop iron overload. 
- - -
### Investigations
The most important marker of iron load is the serum ferritin:
- Ferritin <1000µg/L -> low risk of cirrhosis
- Ferritin >1000µg/L *and* transaminitis *and* thrombocytopenia -> highly suggestive (80% PPV) of cirrhosis 

It is important to investigate specifically for liver (liver biopsy to quantify) and heart involvement. 
- - -
### Management 
Therapeutic venesection is indicated in HH if:
- Serum ferritin >400µg/L
- Evidence of tissue injury - e.g. reduced LVEF, transaminitis
- Increased tissue iron (MRI or biopsy)

Longitudinal studies have provided strong evidence for venesection in reducing mortality and morbidity. The aim is to restore ferritin to a normal range. If ferritin <300µg/L, monitoring is also an appropriate strategy. Iron chelation therapy is typically avoided.  

Extrahepatic manifsetations vary in their responsiveness to venesection:
- Improve with venesection -> cardiomyopathy, hypogonadism
- _Do not_ improve with venesection -> diabetes, arthropathy
