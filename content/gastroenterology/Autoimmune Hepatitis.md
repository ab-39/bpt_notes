# Autoimmune Hepatitis 
#bpt/gastroenterology
- - -
### Overview
Autoimmune hepatitis is a progressive, inflammatory disease of the liver that is characterised by rapidly progressive disease and significant mortality in the absence of intervention. There is a strong association with other autoimmune conditions. There are broadly two types:
- Type I -> anti-smooth muscle antibodies (ASMA), anti-nuclear antibodies
- Type II -> anti-liver/kidney microsomal (LKM1) antibodies (= CYP 2D6) 
The _most_ specific antibody for AIH, however, is the antibody against soluble liver antigen, anti-SLA ([Autoimmune Hepatitis: Serum Autoantibodies in Clinical Practice](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9464171/)). 

Diagnosis is based on published criteria ([Autoimmune hepatitis: Diagnostic criteria and serological testing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6567853/)), which weight factors such as the presence of autoantibodies, roughly equal elevation of both hepatic and cholestatic LFTs, elevated IgG, histology, and the absence of alternative (e.g. viral, drugs, alcohol) causes of liver injury.

Treatment is generally not indicated in asymptomatic patients with normal biochemistry. If initiated, treatment generally consists of prednisolone monotherapy, to which AZA can be eventually added. Budesonide is an alternative steroid in patients without cirrhosis.
