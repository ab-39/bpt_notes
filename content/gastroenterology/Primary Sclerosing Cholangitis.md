# Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis
#bpt/gastroenterology
- - -
### Overview 
PSC is a chronic cholestatic liver disease characterised by fibrosing inflammatory desctruction of both intra- and extrahepatic bile ducts. It is associated with [[inflammatory bowel disease]], particularly [[ulcerative colitis]], in 75% of cases, and is often associated with a positive MPO-[[ANCA]]. There are really no disease-modifying therapies for PSC, and median survival (without a liver transplant) is 12y. PSC is one of the strongest risk factors for colon cancer, and patients should receive annual screening. 
- - -
### Clinical Features
- Often asymptomatic 
- Pruritis
- Jaundice
- Cholangitis

#### Complications
- Cholangiocarcinoma
- Gallbladder cancer - ~10% of cases -> screen
- [[Cirrhosis]] and portal hypertension
- Deficiency of fat soluble vitamins
- Colon cancer -> screen **annually**
  - One of the strongest risk factors for colorectal cancer 
- Metabolic bone disease
- Cholangitis
- - -
### Diagnosis
MRCP is generally the test of choice. The classic finding is a ‘beaded’ appearance of the bile ducts. 

Histology shows fibrous obliteration of small bile ducts, with concentric replacement by connective tissue in an "onion skin" pattern:

### Treatment
Unlike in [[Primary Biliary Cholangitis|PBC]], ursodeoxycholic acid is not of benefit in PSC. If stricturing occurs, ERCP can be used to relieve this.

The only curative treatment is liver transplant, but of these patients approximately 20% will relapse.
