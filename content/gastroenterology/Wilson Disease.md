# Wilson Disease
#bpt/gastroenterology

### Overview
Wilson disease, or _hepatolenticular degeneration_, is a rare, autosomal recessive impairment of intracellular copper transporter ATP7B, which leads to impaired incorporation of copper into caeruloplasmin (its transport protein) as well as biliary excretion. Low serum caeruloplasmin is highly suggestive of the disease.

> [!NOTE] Caeruloplasmin
> Dietary copper far exceeds the requirements of the body, and so, is transported to the liver bound to albumin. Here, the amount required for daily functioning (~0.75mg/d) will complex with a glycoprotein called _apocaeruloplasmin_ to form caeruloplasmin, while the remainder will be excreted in the bile. Caeruloplasmin then functions as a metallochaperone for copper, preventing its being deposited in the tissues.

Clinical manifestations tend to involve the liver and the nervous system (particularly the basal ganglia, hence the alternative name):
- Chronic liver disease
- Tremor
- Dysarthria
- Involuntary movements
- Dementia
- Kayser-Fleischer rings (_highly specific_, particularly for neurological involvement)
- Psychiatric symptoms: psychosis, depression, anxiety

The best diagnostic test is with 24h urinary copper excretion, which is generally >100µg/d. If this increases in response to a challenge with 500mg penicillamine to >1600µg, this is diagnostic of Wilson disease. The serum copper and caeruloplasmin are generally low, while hepatic copper on liver biopsy is generally high (>250µg/g). Genetic testing is generally of limited utility due to Wilson disease being highly polygenic.  

Treatment is with lifelong **_penicillamine_**, or else with trientene or tetrathiomolybdate. Oral zinc interferes with copper absorption. High copper foods should be avoided. 
- - -
Diagnosis: the ratios of alkaline phosphatase to bilirubin (<4) and aspartate aminotransferase to alanine aminotransferase (>2.2) are diagnostic markers that are highly (100%) specific and sensitive for Wilson’s disease in adults, according to the Acute Liver Failure Study Group.
