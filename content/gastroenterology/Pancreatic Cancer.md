### Overview
Pancreatic cancer is an aggressive malignancy that is becoming increasingly incident. By 2030, in fact, ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is predicted to be the second leading cause of cancer related death. 

Pancreatic cancers can disrupt the endocrine and exocrine functions of the cancer. There are four key varieties of pancreatic cancer:
- PDAC - 90% - the vast majority of which (70%) originate from the head of the pancreas
- Neuroendocrine 
- Lymphoma 
- Sarcoma
- - -
### Clinical Presentation
The ‘classic’ presentation of PDAC is of Courvoisier’s famous law - painless jaundice. The other symptoms that can develop tend to be fairly nonspecific - epigastric/back pain, fatigue, anorexia, weight loss, malabsorption, etc. 

Often, these vague presentations lead to late presentation, and in combination with the naturally aggressive biology and low immunogenicity of pancreatic cancers, this leads to the high fatality of these tumours. Another feature that contributes to this is the _desmoplastic stroma_ of pancreatic cancer, the matrix of fibroblasts and immune cells that surround the tumour, and make it harder for treatments to penetrate.

### Treatment Paradigms 
![](Pancreatic%20Cancer/image.png)

In general, only local cancers that are not locally invasive or circumferentially encased (i.e. not locally advanced) are amenable to surgical resection. Some borderline cases may be candidates for neoadjuvant therapy prior to surgical resection.

Pancreatic tumours are difficult to surgically resect. In particular, the proximity of the celiac trunk and SMA can make local invasion a massive problem. The most well-known surgical approach is the pancreaticoduodenectomy - Whipple’s procedure. This involves the removal of the pancreas, bile duct, and associated territory of the duodenum, with a subsequent anastomosis to the distal part of the stomach. Nowadays, however, surgeons tend to preserve the pylorus. Distal pancreas tumours can be excised by themselves.

Following surgery, most patients will proceed to adjuvant chemotherapy, e.g.:
- 5FU
- Gemcitabine (GEM)
- Paclitaxel-GEM 
- FOLFIRINOX - 5FU + oxaliplatin + irinotecan (good overall survival, but high risk of infection so often avoided if patient has a metal biliary stent)

While there are few targeted options for patients with pancreatic cancer, the POLO study in 2019 found that olaparib (PARP inhibition) is effective in patients with a gBRCA mutation.
