### Acute Pancreatitis
Acute pancreatitis refers to the syndrome of panceratic inflammation initiated by acute injury. There are several precipitating causes.

> [!TIP] Mnemonic = _I GET SMASHED_
> I = idiopathic
> G = gallstones 
> E = ethanol
> T = trauma
> S = steroids
> M = mumps
> A = autoimmune
> S = scorpions/semaglutide
> H = hypercalcaemia/hypertriglyceridaemia
> E = ERCP
> D = drugs/medications

Clinical features are often vague, and can range from mild upper abdominal pain to severe pain, shock, multiorgan failure, and bleeding into the periumbilicus and flanks (Cullen and Grey-Turner’s signs respectively). Severity can be graded as follows:
- Mild = no organ failure or complications
- Moderate = <48h of organ failure with local or systemic complications
- Severe = persistent (>48h) organ failure

Serum amylase is an extremely sensitive test _if_ it is elevated >3x ULN, but can also be raised by gastroduodenal perforation. As such, imaging of the pancreas is also recommended. 

The complications of pancreatitis include:
- Fluid collections
- Pseudocysts (i.e. not lined by epithelium) - mature, walled-off fluid collections outisde the pancreas without necrosis or solid material
- Necrotic collections - adjacent to or involving the pancreas, not walled-off and involving liquid and solid components

Aggressive volume repletion with additional supportive measures is the mainstay of treatment, and antibiotics are only indicated if infection is suspected.

### Chronic Pancreatitis
Chronic pancreatitis is a state of progressive fibroinflammatory damage to the pancreas that results in structural and functional damage to both the exocrine and endocrine glands. This can be asymptomatic over long periods, and often without elevations in the amylase. The diagnosis 

Most cases of chronic pancreatitis are due to alcohol or autoimmune attack. 

Faecal elastase is the most sensitive and specific test for chronic pancreatitis. 

Management involves correction of exocrine and endocrine insufficiency.
