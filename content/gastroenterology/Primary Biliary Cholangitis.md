# Primary Biliary Cholangitis
#bpt/gastroenterology
***
### Overview 
PBC is a chronic cholestatic disease of the intralobular bile ducts. It is an autoimmune disease, likely mediated by T-cells, that is associated with the **anti-mitochondrial** antibody, and less so the ANA. This is a necrotising process, ultimately leading to bile duct destruction and cirrhosis. 
![](Primary%20Biliary%20Cholangitis/image.png)
***
### Common Associations
- Sjögren syndrome 
- Middle age 
- Female sex 
- AMA
- IgM (raised)
***
### Diagnostic Criteria
2 of:
- ALP >1.5 ULN
- AMA titre of 1:40 or stronger
  - 95% sensitive and 98% specific 
- Histologic evidence of PBC: nonsuppurative destruction of small intralobular bile ducts 
***
### Common Symptoms
- Skin hyperpigmentation 
- Pruritis
- Fatigue
***
### Treatment 
**_Ursodeoxycholic acid_** - improves LFTs and mortality (cannot be taken at the same time as cholestyramine). Above a level of about 15mg/kg/day there is an increased risk of stroke and AMI.

An emerging treatment is peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor activation with a drug known as **_seladelpar_** (also: elafibranor), which the _RESPONSE_ trial found to be superior than placebo in improving symptoms and biochemical markers in patients with PBC ([A Phase 3 Trial of Seladelpar in Primary Biliary Cholangitis](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2312100)). 

The only definitive treatment is [[liver transplantation]]. 
***
### Differentials 
Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis:
- MPO-ANCA
- Elevated IgG
- Strong association with [[ulcerative colitis]] 
- Large ducts with an “onion skin” appearance 

Autoimmune Hepatitis:
- ANA
- Anti-SMA
- Anti-SLAP
- Anti-LKM
