### Overview
Eosinophilic oesophagitis is a Th2-cell-mediated condition that presents with [[dysphagia]] and food impaction. It is common in young males, and is strongly associated with atopic disorders. There is an overlap with [[gastro-oesophageal reflux disease]]. Diagnosis requires endoscopy which typically reveals exudates, rings, and furrows:
![](Eosinophilic%20Oesophagitis/image.png)and biopsy (showing eosinophilic infiltrates), with all three of:
- Symptoms related to oesophageal dysfunction
- Biopsy: ≥15 eosinophils per high powered field **or** ≥60 per sqmm
- Exclusion of other diagnoses associated with oesophageal eosinophilia
![](Eosinophilic%20Oesophagitis/image%202.png)

Management centres around acid suppression therapy, with a potential role for topical steroids (e.g. swallowed fluticasone or budesonide). There may also be a role for diet changes (typically a six-food elimination diet for common allergens, followed by graded reintroduction) due to the link with allergy. Dupilumab (IL4 and IL13 antagonist) is an emerging therapy. There is a high rate of recurrence and risk of stricturing. Dilatation carries a risk of severe mucosal tearing.
