# Oesophageal Spasm
#bpt/gastroenterology #bpt/racp_cls

### Overview
Distal oesophageal spasm is characterised by synchronous, uncoordinated, contractions of the smooth muscle of the distal oesophagus, probably due to a loss of inhibitory inhibition. Although distinguished from [[achalasia]] by the presence of relaxation of the sphincter, spasm may proceed to type 3 achalasia with time. Symptoms include variable dysphagia, regurgitation, and chest pain. Diagnosis is typically made with barium swallow showing a ‘corkscrew oesophagus’ due to tertiary contractions. Gastroscopy and manometry are also useful, particularly in differentiating this from achalasia. First-line treatment is typically with dietary modification, typically involving soft foods eaten slowly. Acid suppression is also useful. CCBs may be useful. Endoscopic botox, dilatation, and myotomy can also be considered.
