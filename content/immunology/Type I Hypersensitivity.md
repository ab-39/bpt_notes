# Type I Hypersensitivity 
#bpt/immunology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Type I [[hypersensitivity reactions]] are caused by IgE-mediated crosslinking of mast cells and basophils, leading to degranulation of these cells and release of their inflammatory compounds including histamines and cytokines, causing a systemic inflammatory response. The typical clinical presentation is with urticaria, angioedema, bronchospasm, and anaphylaxis. The typical timeframe is within an hour of exposure to the offending antigen. Treatment is with intramuscular adrenaline.

#### Specific Symptoms:
- Urticaria - also called ‘wheal’: a sharply circumscribed, superficial, central swelling, typically surrounded by erythema; usually transient (picture on left) 
- Angioedema - a sudden erythematous, deep swelling in the lower dermis and subcutis; can take up to 72h to resolve (picture on right )
![](Type%20I%20Hypersensitivity/image.png)

Note that while urticaria, angioedema, and anaphylaxis almost always indicate an IgE-mediated reaction, there are certain drugs (most notably iodinated contrast and vancomycin) that can directly cause mast cells to degranulate leading to a similar presentation. 
![](Type%20I%20Hypersensitivity/image%203.png)
A key clinical aspect of type I hypersensitivity is the possibility of a _late phase response_ due to circulating cytokine 6-10 hours after the initial exposure.

### Diagnosis 
Diagnosis can typically be made clinically, however the skin prick and intradermal challenge tests may be used for a limited group of IgE-mediated drug allergic reactions. Where the reaction is either not IgE-mediated or the specific epitope remains unknown, skin testing is not validated. 

Tryptase is tested at the time of symptoms and also 24h after resolution of symptoms:
- Elevated event-tryptase with normal 24h-tryptase -> diagnosis of anaphylaxis confirmed
- Elevated event-tryptase **and** 24h-tryptase -> suspicious for mast cell disorders 

For allergies against some β-lactam antibiotics (-oyls and cefaclor), as well as chlorhexidine, pholcodeine, and suxamethonium, there are specific serum antibodies. These tend to be more specific but less sensitive than skin tests, and hence are often used in conjunction with them.
