# Human Leucocyte Antigens
#bpt/immunology

### Overview
The major histocompatibility complex is a set of highly conserved mammalian proteins that are essential for the recognition of foreign antigen. In humans, these proteins are referred to as the _human leucocyte antigens_ or HLA. 

MHC plays an important role in preventing the adaptive immune system from becoming activated unless there has been presentation of antigen through one of the MHC molecules, a phenomenon referred to as *MHC restriction*. In general, HLAs belonging to the MHC I class are expressed on all nucleated cells and interact with TCRs on CD8+ T-cells, while HLAs belonging to the MHC II class are only expressed on antigen-presenting cells and interact with TCRs on CD4+ T-cells.
![](Human%20Leucocyte%20Antigens/image.png)

HLA molecules are encoded by genes on chromosome 6p:
![](Human%20Leucocyte%20Antigens/image%202.png)
Note the presence of a ‘class III’ MHC domain. This encodes proteins such as:
- C2 and C4 (complement)
- TNF⍺ 
- Heat shock protein 70

HLA genes are codominantly expressed, meaning that HLAs from _both_ parents are expressed.

There are several differences betwen class I and class II MHC molecules:
|             | MHC I                                                      | MHC II                                          |
|-------------|------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| *Structure* | Single transmembrane domain:<br>⍺-chain + β2 microglobulin | Two transmembrane domains:<br>⍺-chain + β-chain |
Structurally, T-cell receptors resemble MHC class II molecules.

### HLA Polymorphisms
HLA molecules are _highly_ polymorphic, with polymorphisms playing a major role in autoimmune disease and drug sensitivities.
![](Human%20Leucocyte%20Antigens/image%203.png)
In terms of inheritance, HLA genes do not follow standard Mendelian patterns, but rather follow _linkage dysequilibrium_.
