### Overview
The primary immunodeficiencies are a group of over 400 disorders characterised by one of:
- Abnormal immune response to infection
- Immune response to self (i.e. autoimmune disease)
- Spontaneous inflammation in the absence of insult (i.e. autoinflammatory disease)

### Classifying PIDs:
The IUIS committee classifies the primary immunodeficiencies as follows:
1. **Combined** immunodeficiencies
2. [[Well-Defined Syndromes with Immunodeficiency]]
3. Predominantly **antibody** defects - overwhelmingly the most common 
4. [[Defects of immune dysregulation]]
5. [[Phagocytic defects]]
6. [[Defects in innate immunity]]
7. **Autoinflammatory** disorders
8. **Complement** deficiencies
9. **Bone marrow** failure
10. **Phenocopies** of PID

### Epidemiology
Although these are a diverse group of conditions, the overall prevalence of _clinically significant_ PID is estimated at 1:1000 to 1:5000. Overall, it is likely that PIDs remain under diagnosed, and associated with increased morbidity and mortality.

### Diagnosing Immunodeficiencies
The clinician’s index of suspicion for an immunodeficiency should be raised if a patient presents with:
- Severe infections in the absence of other factors
  - Complicated or prolonged pneumonia
  - Meningococcal disease 
  - Sepsis
  - Osteomyelitis
- Unusual infections
- Persistent infections
  - Extensive warts
  - Chronic diarrhoea
  - Chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis 
- Recurrent infections
  - Sinopulmonary infectoins
  - Herpes infections - cutaneous or invasive
  - Warts (HPV)
- Malignancy
- Autoimmune disease
- Not gaining weight
  -> _SUPeRMAN_ 

Once we think a patient has a primary immunodeficiency, we need to uncover which part of the immune system is defective:
- B-cells (antibodies) -> bacterial infections
- T-cells (combined) -> fungal/viral/HPV infections
- Phagocytes -> _Staphylococcus_/fingal
- Natural killer cells (haemophagocytosis)
- Complement -> encapsulated organisms (also think spleen)
