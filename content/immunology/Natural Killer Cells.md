### Overview
The most hardcore cells of the immune system, natural killer cells, are critically important to the immune response against viral pathogens. Despite being innate immune cells, they derive from lymphoid progenitors - hence their alternative name: _large granular lymphocytes_. 

Recall that when viruses infect host cells, host cells will try and ‘signal’ this by presenting viral proteins to CD8+ T-lymphocytes through MHCI (HLA-A, B, and C). To try and evade destruction by CTLs, viruses trigger _downregulation_ of MHCI.

MHCI, however, also functions as the _inhibitor_ of NK-cells when it is presenting self peptides. Thus, when viruses downregulate MHCI, this triggers a response by NK-cells.

NK-cells can also be activated by other mechanisms:
- Activating/inhibiting receptors on the cell surface (e.g. FAS)
- Antibody-binding to FcγRIIIa (which also confers a degree of specificity)
