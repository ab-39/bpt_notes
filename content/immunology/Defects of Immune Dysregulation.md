### Autoimmune Lymphoproliferative Syndrome
ALPS is characterised by lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, autoimmune haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, and an increased risk of lymphoma. Flow cytometry reveals increased double negative ⍺β T-cells in the peripheral blood.
- - -
### Immune Dysregulation Polyendocrinopathy-Enteropathy X-Linked Disorder
IPEX is caused by a defect in the _FOXP3_ gene, leading to a deficiency in CD4+/CD25+ regulatory T-cells. This leads to a severe and early onset of autoimmune enteropathy, type I diabetes, eczema, and hypothyroidism. Treatment involves immunosuppression, rituximab, and bone marrow transplant. 

> [!TIP] Historical Point
> The 2025 Nobel Prize in Medicine went to Mary Brunkow for her work on _FOXP3_.

### Autoimmune Polyendocrinopathy/Candidiasis/Ectodermal Dysplasia Syndrome 
APECED (or autoimmune polyglandular syndrome) is caused by a deficit in the _AIRE_ gene, which is an autoimmune regulator controlling the expression of self antigens. This is associated with hypoparathyroidism, Addison disease, chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis, and other autoantibodies. 

~APS1~:
- Rare (~15% of cases)
- Characterised by: adrenal insufficiency, hypoparathyroidism, chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis, ovarian insufficiency
- Due to _AIRE_ gene mutations

~APS2~:
- Less rare (~85% of cases)
- Characterised by adrenal insufficiency with either autoimmune thyroid disease or T1DM
- Ovarian insufficiency may be present

### [[Haemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis]]
HLH is caused by defective natural killer and T-cell cytotoxicity, including impaired activation-induced apopotosis, that is often triggered by viruses (especially EBV and CMV) or malignancy.

Its diagnostic criteria include:
- Clinically: hepatosplenomegaly, ferritinaemia, cytopenias
- Laboratory: high LDH, triglycerides, and bilirubin
- Specific: haemophagocytosis on microscopy, elevated soluble CD25/IL-2R, NK function testing

There is an association of familial syndromes.
