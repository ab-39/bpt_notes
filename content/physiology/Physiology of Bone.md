### Osteoblasts![](Physiology%20of%20Bone/image.png)
The osteoblast derives from mesenchymal marrow cells, and expresses ALP as well as receptors for PTH and vitamin D. It lays down bone matrix (osteoid) which is then mineralised by the deposition of hydroxyapatite. Osteoid consists of:
- Type 1 collagedn
- Osteocalcin
- Osteopontin
Mineralisation of this osteoid matrix requires the presence of alkaline phosphatase. 
- - -
### Osteocytes
Once incorporated into bone, osteoblasts become osteocytes. These develop cytoplasmic processes than enable contact with other osteocytes, and act as sensors of mechanical load. These secrete sclerostin, a tonic inhibitor of bone formation, that is suppressed in response to mechanical stress. They also secrete RANK ligand (RANK-L), which stimulates osteoclastogenesis; [[endocrinology/FGF23|FGF 23]]; and osteoprotegerin (a decoy receptor for RANKL that is upregulated by oestrogen). 
> Two key anti-osteoporotic agents:
> - Romosozumab - anti-sclerostin -> promote bone formation
> - Denosumab - anti-RANKL -> inhibit osteoclast formation by blocking RANK on preosteoclasts

### Osteoclasts
The osteoclast is a multinucleated giant cell that derives from monocyte precursors and is specialised for bone resorption. Its formation and activation is stimulated by osteoblasts. Osteoclasts pump protons generated by carbonic anhydrase into the bone to dissolve the mineral and release calcium and phosphate. The collagen-rich osteoid matrix is then degraded by local proteases.

### Bone
Bone is largely comprised of two types:
- Corticol bone in the long bone
- Cancellous bone in the vertebrae

This is under a constant process of remodelling taht replaces the whole skeleton approximately once every ten years. This process occurs at discrete individual sites called _bone remodelling units_:
![](Physiology%20of%20Bone/image%202.png)
1. Recruitment of osteoclasts -> resorption of an area of mineralised bone
2. Osteoclast apoptosis -> osteoblast recruitment
3. Deposition of osteoid in the resorption cavity -> new mineral bone deposition

This process takes approximately four months - three weeks for resorption and then three months for remineralisation.

### Canonical _Wnt_ Pathway 
Wnt is a glycoprotein that binds to the LRP5/6 receptor that, when costimulated by frizzled, will stimulate a downstream protein called dishevelled. Dishevelled is able to inhibit glycogen synthase kinase 3β, which allows β-catenin to translocate to the nucleus where it alters transcription factors leading to two key outcomes:
- Inhibition of osteoblast apoptosis
- Stimulation of osteoblast differentiation

This pathway is inhibited by _sclerostin_, which is secreted by the osteoblasts.
