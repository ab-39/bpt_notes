### Overview
Angiotensinogen is a 32-aa ⍺2-globulin produced in the liver. Renin, when released by the juxtaglomerular apparatus, cleaves angiotensinogen into a decapeptide: _angiotensin I_. Angiotensin-converting enzyme, found in the lungs, is then able to cleave this into an octapeptide: _angiotensin II_. 

Angiotensin II has two main effects:
- Vascular smooth muscular constriction -> increased afterload
  - Note that in the kidneys, ATII preferentially constricts the efferent arteriole to increase GFR
- Stimulation of aldosterone secretion -> fluid and sodium retention -> increased preload

In combination, these work to increase blood pressure.

Note that this is all independent of the hypothalamus and pituitary. 
- - -
### RAAS Pharmacotherapy
ACE-inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers play a renal-protective role by antagonising the effect of angiotensin II, thus dilating the efferent arteriole. This leads to reduced pressure in the glomerulus, which in the long run leads to reduced filtration and reduced proteinuria, but also explains the acute reduction in GFR often associated with these medications.
