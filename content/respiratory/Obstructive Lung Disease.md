### What is obstructive lung disease?
Obstructive lung disease implies there is some factor that impedes the flow of air through the airways. Typically, we think about [[respiratory/Asthma|asthma]], [[COPD]], and [[bronchiectasis]] being in this category.
- - -
### Obstructive [[Spirometry and Pulmonary Function Testing|Spirometry]]
The hallmarks of obstructive lung disease on spirometry are:
- Reduced FEV1
- Preserved FVC (although note that this can drop off in severe disease) 
- Reduced FEV1/FVC ratio

The characteristic sign of this on the flow-volume loop is ‘scalloping’ of the expiratory limb:

#### Bronchodilator Reversibility
If there is an improvement in FEV1 of 10% (old system: 12% and/or 200mL) 15 minutes after administration of bronchodilators, this is deemed ‘bronchodilator reversibility’, and is typically most characteristic of asthma. 

#### Bronchoprovocation
Agents that provoke bronchoconstriction either directly (e.g. histamine, metacholine) or indirectly (e.g. hypertonic saline, adenosine) can be used in bronchoprovocation testing to further assess for asthma, where the dose required to reduce FEV1 by 20% is compared to reference values. This is not a first-line test, but is a reliably sensitive test for asthma. 
- - -
### Grading obstructive deficits
This is done with the FEV1 Z-score:
- < -1.65 -> mild
- < -2.5 -> moderate
- < -4.0 -> severe
