### Malignant Pleural Effusion
The management of malignant pleural effusion is fundamentally a palliative procedure, with an emphasis on symptom improvement and hospital avoidance. The main consideration, then, is patient preference. Other important features to consider when deciding to treat include: prognosis, underlying cancer type, and **expansivity of the lung**. 

We need to know if the lung is non-expandable, because if it isn’t (a “trapped lung”), talc pleurodesis is unlikely to be of any benefit.
- The best way to assess for this is with a post-drainage CXR (although not right after aspiration) 
- Although non-expandable lung affects management, it does not affect response to treatment

### Talc Pleurodesis 
Talc pleurodesis can be carried out two ways:
1. Slurry through a chest drain
2. Poudrage with VATS

Note that having an indwelling chest catheter is the way to go in terms of minimising time in hospital. Once the catheter is in, frequency of drainage is largely a decision to be made in terms of patient preference and convenience.
