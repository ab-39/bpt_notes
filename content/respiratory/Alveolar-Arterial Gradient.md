# Alveolar-Arterial Gradient
#bpt/respiratory #bpt/physiology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### What is the alveolar-arterial gradient?
The A-a gradient is the difference in partial pressures of oxygen in the artery and the alveolus. 
- - -
### Deriving the A-a gradient
The derivation of the A-a gradient is as follows:
$A-a$ 
$= P_AO_2 — P_aO_2$
$= [FiO_2 * (P_{atm} — P_{H_2O}) — 1.25 * P_aCO_2] — P_aO_2$
$= [FiO_2 * 713 - 1.25 * PaCO_2] - PaO_2$
  
When we account for the fact that room air is 0.21 oxygen, and account for atmospheric and water pressures in a standard body at room temperature, this simplifies to:
> $A-a = (150 — 1.25 * P_aCO_2) — P_aO_2$

The ‘normal’ value for the A-a gradient is `~ Age /4 + 4` 
- - -
### What does it mean?
There are five main reasons why the arterial oxygen tension might be reduced:
- Hypoventilation:
  - The arterial CO2 tension is inversely proportional to the alveolar minute ventilation -> as ventilation decreases, PaCO2 _will_ increase 
- Diffusion limitation
  - Under normal circumstances, perfusion is the limiting factor deciding how oxygen and CO2 diffuse across the alveolar-capillary interface 
  - When diffusion is limited, the time in capillary will not be sufficient for gas transfer to occur
- Shunt
  - Defined as the passage of blood from the pulmonary to the systemic circulation without comig into contact with ventilated alveoli
  - Can be intrapulmonary (e.g. unventilated lung, hepatopulmonary syndrome)
  - Or cardiac (e.g. Eisenmenger physiology, some congenital heart defects)
- V/Q mismatch
  - Although V/Q mismatch is a physiologic mechanism, it is also the most frequent pathological cause of hypoxia, particularly in COPD
  - In general, both ventilation and perfusion are greatest at the base of the lung
- Reduced FiO2 

A normal A-a gradient is suggestive that there is a purely ventilatory problem leading to a patient’s hypoxia or hypercapnia, and the parenchymal lung disease is _unlikely_ to be the cause. 
- Normal -> hypoventilation
- Elevated -> V/Q mismatch, [[right-to-left shunt]]
