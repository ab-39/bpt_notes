### Initial approach to the patient with pleural effusion
The three most common causes of pleural effusion are:
1. Malignancy
2. Heart Failure
3. Infection

As such, when approaching a patient with a pleural effusion, history and examination targeted at signs and symptoms of these three conditions is a good place to start.

Also consider [[drugs that cause pleural effusion]], as well as procedures, and fluid elsewhere in the body (e.g. ascites -> hepatic hydrothorax).

### Thoracocentesis 
Drainage of a pleural effusion is both a diagnostic and therapeutic procedure. Most patients should receive one, with the possible exception of those with overt heart failure. 

Contraindications to pleurocentesis include:
- Insufficient pleural fluid (-> 2’ PTx) - typically need >1cm of fluid on USS
- Skin infection at puncture side
- Severe bleeding diathesis (INR >2 or plt. <50) 

Complications have been decreasing since the introduction of USS, but include:
- pain at puncture site
- breathlessness/cough
- bleeding
- pneumothorax (ex vacuo, i.e. to do with inelastic lung)
- empyema
- SSTI
- spleen/liver puncture
- re-expansion pulmonic oedema
