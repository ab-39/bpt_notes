### What is restrictive lung disease?
A group of lung conditions characterized by an impaired ability of the lungs to expand. Causes include disorders that affect the lung parenchyma (e.g., interstitial lung disease, pneumonia, pulmonary oedema), pleura, and pleural cavity, deformities of the thorax, and respiratory muscle weakness. Typically causes ↓ vital capacity, ↓ total lung capacity, normal/↓ FEV1 (due to ↓ in vital capacity), and a normal or ↑ FEV1:FVC ratio.

### Restrictive [[Spirometry and Pulmonary Function Testing|Spirometry]]
Note that unlike in obstructive lung disease, spirometry is only ever ‘suggestive’ of restrictive lung disease - the gold standard is measurement of lung volumes. 

Characteristic spirometry findings are:
- Preserved FEV1 (can drop off as disease progresses) 
- Reduced FVC
- _Increased_ or preserved FEV1/FVC (tends to increase as severity progresses)
- Reduced PEFR
- Reduced DLCO
- Reduced VA 
  - Which means that the KCO can actually appear normal when calculated despite the physiology actually being deranged 

In the flow-volume loop, the shape tends to be preserved although the amplitude shrinks.
