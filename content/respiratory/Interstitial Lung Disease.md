### What is idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis?
Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis:
- A chronic, inexorably progressive fibrosing lung disease of unknown cause
- Requires the exclusion of other known causes of pulmonary fibrosis
- Characterised by radiologic (or histologic) usual interstitial pneumonia 
- Warrants discussion at an MDM, surgical biopsy is usually not mandatory

It is one of many types of interstitial lung disease (ILD), a group of rare pulmonary disorders characterised by inflammatoin and progressive scarring of the lungs. 

The ‘classic’ examination findings for IPF are clubbing and bibasal end-inspiratory fine crackles, whereas ‘squawks’ on auscultation are less suggestive. 

> [!TIP] Mnemonic
> There are multiple conditions under the aegis of ILD, which can be classified by their location in the lung:
> ~Upper lobe predominant - “SCHART”~
> - Silicosis/sarcoidosis
> - Coal workers pneumoconiosis
> - Histiocytosis
> - Ankylosing spondylitis
> - Radiation 
> - Tuberculosis
> 
> ~Lower lobe predominant - “RASIO”~
> - Rheumatoid arthritis
> - Asbestosis
> - Scleroderma
> - Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis
> - Other

### Pathophysiology
‘Complex and poorly understood’ ([Mikolasch et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6297625/)), but likely relates to dysfunction of epithelial cells and fibroblasts in the lung interstitium.

### Diagnostic Features of ILD
The radiologic features that constitute the presence of pulmonary fibrosis include:
1. Reticulation - small, fine, white lines in the subpleural regions of the lung
2. Traction bronchiectasis within areas of fibrosis
3. Honeycombing - the formation of arcades of cysts, the end stage 
4. Architectural distortion - unusual airways
5. Loss of volume - the end result of fibrosis as the lungs shrink

> [!NOTE] UIP
> UIP _cannot_ have ground glass changes.

And for specific interstitial conditions:
| Radiologic Label                                             | Features                                                     |

### Differentials
Among others, consider pulmonary lymphangioleiomyomatosis. This is a diffuse, bilateral cystic disease that is associated with the tuberous sclerosis complex in women. The mainstay of management is rapamycin.

### Basic Management Principles of ILD
| Treatment Principles                        | Conditions                                                   |
|---------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Avoid exposure +/- immunosuppression        | Pneumoconiosis<br>Bird-fancier’s lung<br>Langerhans cell histiocytosis<br>Smoking-related ILD |
| Observe +/- immunosuppression               | Sarcoidosis<br>CTD-ILD<br>COP<br>NSIP                        |
| Antifibrotics (nintendanib and pirfenidone) | Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis                                |
| Supportive care/no known treatment          | IPPFE                                                        |

New evidence suggests that nintendanib is effect for many phenotypes of progressive ILD with a UIP pattern, and perhaps even outside IPF (e.g. in systemic sclerosis, etc.) - [_INBUILD_](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1908681).

IPF used to be treated with immunosuppression, but the [_PANTHER_](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1113354) study in 2012 showed that this was actually associated with excess mortality. Now, the only use of immunosuppressants is corticosteroids during acute exacerbations. Instead, the mainstays of IPF treatment are nintendanib and pirfenidone which slow the rate of progression of decline in FVC and improve survival, but do not reverse the fibrosis. 
| Nintendanib                                                  | Pirfenidone                                                  |
|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Small-molecule Y-kinase inhibitor (PDGF, VEGF, FGF)          | Reduces fibroblast activation through TGF-β                  |
| Slows rate of disease progression (measured through FVC) but does not improve symptoms | Slows rate of disease progression (measured through FVC) but does not improve symptoms |
| Reduces incidence of exacerbations                           |                                                              |
| Improves survival                                            | Improves survival                                            |
| ADEs: diarrhoea, weight loss, possible cardiovascular (clotting) and bleeding risk | ADEs: nausea, photosensitivity rash<br>                      |
| Key trials: INPULSIS ([Efficacy and Safety of Nintedanib in Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1402584)), INBUILD | Key trials: ASCEND ([A Phase 3 Trial of Pirfenidone in Patients with Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1402582)), CAPACITY (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21571362/) |

### Risk factors for IPF
- Older age (60+) - rarely can occur in young patients, familial 
- Male gender
- Smoking Hx
- GORD
- Family history of ILD
- Absence of an alternative cause of ILD

### Genetics of IPF
There is an association between IPF and the **_telomeropathy syndrome_**, other signs of which include:
- Premature greying of hair 
- Aplastic anaemia
- Macrocytosis
- Thrombocytopenia
- Opportunistic infections
- Premature onset emphysema 
- Cryptogenic cirrhosis
- Osteoporosis/AVN
- Epithelial and haematological malignancies

This is autosomal dominant syndrome with ‘genetic anticipation’ (i.e. the phenotype gets worse over time). It is associated with worse outcomes post-transplant, and worse survival. The diagnosis should be considered in patients with young-onset UIP.

### Other forms of ILD
#### Connective Tissue Disease and ILD
Essentially any connective tissue disease can manifest with an ILD:
- RA -> UIP
- Systemic sclerosis -> NSIP (associated with anti-topoisomerase) 
- Idiopathic inflammatory myopathies -> organising pneumonia 
- Sjögren syndrome -> lymphoid interstitial pneumonia 

In general, management of CTD-ILD involves immunosuppression. It is important to carry out an autoimmune screen, too. 

#### Hypersensitivity Pneumonitis 
Hypersensitivity pneumonitis arises from exposure to an antigen, typically related to birds or mould. Hence, its management centres on exposure avoidance, with immunosuppression, and if fibrotic, nintendanib. Importantly, if fibrotic, the disease will resemble a UIP pattern. 
- The presence of gas trapping and/or ground glass on CT can help to differentiate this from IPF
- Lymphocyte-predominant on BAL
- Many forms exist - farmer’s lung, Finnish sauna worker’s lung, maple bark disease, bird fancier’s lung, bagassosis (exposure to mouldy molasses), Japanese summer house lung, etc. etc. 

#### Smoking related ILD
Typically one of:
- RB-ILD
- DIP
- Langerhans cell histiocytosis 

Mainstay of management is smoking cessation

#### Sarcoidosis
Sarcoidosis can be a differential for anything! It has two key principles of management:
- Prevent life threatening organ dysfunction
- Improve quality of life 

#### HIV
Associated with LIP.
