### What is COPD?
COPD is a heterogeneous lung condition consisting of **chronic respiratory symptoms** (dyspnoea, cough, sputum) due to abnormalities of both **the airways** (bronchitis) and **the alveoli** (emphysema) that together cause progressive, persistent airflow obstruction.

On spirometry, we see persistent airflow limitation that _is not fully reversible_ with bronchodilators, i.e. post-bronchodilator:
> `FEV1/FVC < 0.7 (or < LLN)`

COPD is not diagnosed on clinical or radiologic findings alone - it needs spirometry! 
- FEV1/FVC < 0.7
- Increased FRC and RV due to gas trapping
- Reduced DLCO due to impaired gas transfer
- - -
### Pathophysiology
Results from a destructive cascade of inflammation and oxidative stress leading to alveolar wall destruction, which in turn causes parenchymal destruction and narrowing of the peripheral airways. Ultimately, this results in airflow obstruction, gas trapping, and impaired gas transfer. The distribution of disease is typically apical with centrilobular involvement.

### Risk Factors for COPD
1. Tobacco smoking - ~50% of global risk 
- Air pollution
- Asthma
- Occupational exposure
- Low maximally attained lung growth
- Recurrent childhood respiratory infections
- Low SES
- Dietary factors
- Older age

### Staging
Staging occurs on two axes according to the GOLD criteria:
1. FEV1 as a percentage of predicted:
   1. 1 = ≥80% (mild)
   2. 2 = 50-79% (moderate)
   3. 3 = 30-49% (severe)
   4. 4 = <30%
2. Symptoms (mMRC score where 2 = walks slower than age-matched controls) and exacerbations:
   1. A = mMRC <2 **and** 0-1 exacerbations 
   2. B = mMRC ≥2 **and** 0-1 exacerbations 
   3. E = ≥2 exacerbations or ≥1 admission

The BODE index predicts the risk of all-cause mortality among patients with COPD:
- BMI
- Obstruction - i.e. FEV1
- Dyspnoea - mMRC scale
  - mMRC 0 = minimal
  - mMRC 1 = dyspnoea with exertion
  - mMRC 2 = slower than age matched controls on the level
  - mMRC 3 = ~100m exercise tolerance
  - mMRC 4 = breathless with dressing/undressing
- Exercise capacity - 6MWT

### Management of COPD
#### Non-Pharmacological Interventions:
- Reduce risk factors - smoking cessation is #1
- Optimise function
- Refer to pulmonary rehabilitation
  - Program of exercise and education
  - Reduces: symptoms, mood issues, admissions
  - Improves: exercise capacity, QOL, muscle function, sense of control
- Optimise treatment of comorbidities 

#### Pharmacological Interventions are introduced in a stepwise fashion:
- Start with a reliever - SABA (e.g. salbutamol) or SAMA (e.g. ipratropium) 
- Add a preventer - LAMA (e.g. tiotropium), LABA (e.g. formoterol), or combination (e.g. umeclidinium/vilanterol, tiotropium/olodaterol)
  - There is evidence that combination LAMA/LABAs are significantly better than single agents alone
  - Note that LAMA/LABA provides a superior reduction in the rate of exacerbations when compared with LABA/ICS ([_FLAME_](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa1516385?url_ver=Z39.88-2003&rfr_id=ori:rid:crossref.org&rfr_dat=cr_pub%20%200www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov))
- If blood eosinophils are >300/µL or GOLD E -> add an ICS 
  - Can consider triple therapy (e.g. Trelegy, Trimbow) at this point
  - These are much more effective in reducing mortality than LAMA/LABA alone in frequent exacerbators 
  - ICS can increase rates of pneumonia, and repeated episodes of pneumonia is a relative contraindication to ICS use 
  - In patients with severe COPD, withdrawal of ICS in a tapered fashion is non-inferior to continuation of ICS with respect to exacerbations, but may be associated with a mild reduction in FEV1 of unclear significance ([_WISDOM_](http://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1407154))

#### Surgery
- Lung volume reduction surgery (LVRS) improves survival in patients with severe _upper_ lobe emphysema and low post-rehabilitation exercise capacity 
- Bronchoscopic insertion of endobronchial valves can be used to deflate nonfunctional, emphysematous areas of lung

#### Lung Transplantation
Referral for lung transplantation can be considered in patients for whom there are several of the following factors:
- Progressive symptoms despite maximal non-pharmacological, pharmacological, and oxygen therapy
- Contraindication to LVRS or endobronchial intervention
- BODE index 5-6 
- PaCO2 >50 and/or PaO2 <60mmHg
- GOLD 4 airflow restriction

#### Oxygen Therapy 
Typically indicated for non-smoking, stable patients who have:
- PaO2 ≤55mmHg on room air
- PaO2 55-59mmhg with evidence of pulmonary hypertension or polycythaemia 

Note that for patients with moderate hypoxia (defined as SpO2 >88% at rest or desaturating to 80% on exercise), there is no hospitalisation or mortality benefit to long-term oxygen ([A Randomized Trial of Long-Term Oxygen for COPD with Moderate Desaturation](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1604344)). 

In patients with _severe_ hypoxia (i.e. meeting criteria), evidence from older trials suggests that the daily duration of oxygen is probably somewhere around 16h/d to achieve mortality benefit ([_NOTT_](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6776858/), [Long term domiciliary oxygen therapy in chronic hypoxic cor pulmonale complicating chronic bronchitis and emphysema. Report of the Medical Research Council Working Party - PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6110912/)).

### Preventing Deterioration
Smoking cessation and vaccination prevent deterioration
- Pneumonia + influenza vaccination

### Develop a Care Plan 
This typically involves an MDT developing a COPD action plan, which encompasses:
- Baseline medications
- Plan for an exacerbation

These have good evidence for reducing ED visits and hospital admissions.

### What is a COPD exacerbation?
A COPD exacerbation is an event characterised by:
- Increased dyspnoea and/or
- Cough, sputum, which
- Occurs in <14 days

There are four main phenotypes:
- Bacterial-predominant
- Viral-predominant
- Eosinophil-predominant -> the eosinophil count can be used to guide the use of steroids in the management of exacerbations ([viz.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22447964/))
- Pauci-inflammatory 

~70% of these are infective exacerbations, typically bacterial or viral. 
It is important to consider other differentials e.g. CCF, PE

### Managing Exacerbations:
Medications:
- Bronchodilators - SABA +/- SAMA 
- Oral steroids - typically prednisolone 50mg PO daily for 5 days
- Antibiotics - if suspected IECOPD

Other treatments:
- Oxygen therapy - aim SpO2 88-92%
- NIV with BPAP - shown to reduce LOS, progression to intubation, and mortality with a number needed to treat of 11/approximately _halving_ the mortality rate ([viz.](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD004104_non-invasive-ventilation-people-respiratory-failure-due-exacerbation-chronic-obstructive-pulmonary))
  - BPAP is indicated if there is either:
    - Respiratory acidosis/T2RF 
    - Severe dyspnoea with fatigue 
- Physiotherapy
- Smoking cessation 

The _DECAF_ score can be used to predict mortality:
- Dyspnoea
- Eosinopenia
- Consolidation
- Acidaemia
- (Atrial) Fibrillation

### Palliative Treatments
To relieve breathlessness:
- Low-dose long-acting morphine (e.g. MS Contin) - up to 30mg/d (mixed evidence)
- Benzodiazepines (no evidence)
- Oxygen (no evidence)
