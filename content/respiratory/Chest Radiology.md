### CT Chest
Average slice = 5mm
- Use 1.25mm slices for [[Interstitial Lung Disease]] and other interstitial diseases

Nodules = any mass <3cm, can be solid (if visible in all [[CT windows]]) or subsoil. Guidelines: 
- <6mm in a low risk  -> no follow up
- solid and >6mm -> 6-12m scan, then 2yrs, then stop
- subsolid and >6mm -> 3-6m scan, then 5yrs (annual), then stop

Contrast is typically most useful for detecting [[pulmonary embolism]]. In addition to a filling defect in the pulmonary vasculature, we may also expect to see the interventricular septum bowing into the left ventricle (i.e. right heart strain).

‘Ground glass’

‘Tree in bud’ changes refer to fluid/blood/pus within the bronchial lumen, and associated inflammation, it is a nonspecific sign:
![](Chest%20Radiology/image%203.png)

‘Mosaic attenuation’ refers to a patchwork pattern of hyperlucency and density in the lung, depending on what is normal lung, it can represent:
- obstructive airways disease
- occlusive vascular disease
- parenchymal disease
  ![](Chest%20Radiology/image%204.png)<!-- {"width":358} -->

‘Crazy paving’ sign refers to a pattern of ground-glass opacity with superimposed interlobular septal thickening and interlobular reticular thickening:
![](Chest%20Radiology/image%205.png)<!-- {"width":346} -->
It typically points to:
- ARDS
- interstitial pneumonia
- pulmonary haemorrhage
- pulmonary infection

### Types of findings:
| Condition           | Signs                                                        |
|---------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Consolidation       | Amorphous density<br>Air bronchograms<br>No volume loss      |
| Interstitial oedema | Upper zone venous diversion<br>Intersitial markings - e.g. Kerley B lines<br>Alveolar opacities<br>Pleural effusion - note may also follow drainage of an effusion (post-expansion pulmonary oedema) |
| Atelectasis         | Volume loss<br>Displacement of interlobar fissures<br>Opacification of affected lobe (airlessness)<br>Can mimic consolidation if incomplete  |
| Pleural Effusion    | Simple - one concave margin, dependent<br>Complex - many concave margins, static |
| Pneumothorax        | Absence of lung markings<br>Pleural edge<br>Tension - mediastinal shift away from PTx, flattening of diaphragm |
| Pleural Thicekning  | Nodular thickening of the pleura (unilateral - ?mesothelioma) |
| Abscess             | Rounded structure with air-fluid level                       |
| Bronchiectasis      | Dilated bronchi close to pleural edge<br>Lack of tapering<br>Large size relative to adjacent artery = signet ring sign (dilated airway with adjacent vessel) |
| Emphysema           | Hyperexpanded lungs<br>Air spaces <br>Flattened diaphragms   |

### Differentials for ‘White Out’
A completely ‘whited out’ hemithorax only has a limited number of causes, and these can be differentiated based on the volume in the hemithorax, which will be reflected by the position of the trachea and mediastinum:
- Volume loss -> displacement towards white out = pneumonectomy, collapse
- No change -> no displacement = consolidation, oedema, pleural mass
- Volume gain -> displacement away from white out = pleural effusion, pulmonary mass
