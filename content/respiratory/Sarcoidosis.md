#  Sarcoidosis
#bpt/respiratory #bpt/rheumatology
- - -
### Overview
Sarcoidosis is a multisystem granulomatous disorder of unknown aetiology characterised by noncaseating granulomas that form in organs. The most characteristic presentation is of cough, dyspnoea, and fatigue, accompanied by hilar lymphadenopathy and reticular lung opacities on CXR, although any organ can be affected. A common skin association is erythema nodosum or erythema multiforme, and a common neurological manifestation is a CNVII palsy (i.e. Bell’s palsy) paired with anterior uveitis. The most common cardiac manfestation is heart block. 

The pathophysiology of sarcoidosis is poorly understood, but is known to involve T-lymphocytes and tumour necrosis factor, and to result in the formation of noncaseating granulomas. The identification of these granulomas on biopsy is the definitive test, although serum angiotensin converting enzyme levels are greater than double the upper limit of normal has a good (>90%) specificity. A CD4/CD8 ratio in BAL fluid of >3.5 is also characteristic. Note that sarcoidosis is associated with hypercalcaemia in ~10% of patients due to the effects of macrophages on calcitriol. Naturally, this will lead to a suppressed PTH level. 

Sarcoidosis is radiologically staged as follows:
1. Stage I = bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy 
2. Stage II = Stage I + parenchymal involvement
3. Stage III = parenchymal involvement without lymphadenopathy 
4. Stage IV = pulmonary fibrosis 

For most people, sarcoidosis either enters remission or follows an indolent course. Patients with extensive pulmonary involvement, however, are likely to progress to [[Interstitial Lung Disease|progressive fibrosis]], [[pulmonary hypertension]], and ultimately respiratory failure. Glucocorticoids are the mainstay of treatment:
- Mild disease:
  - 20mg/d pred. equivalent
  - Or, inhaled corticosteroids
- Moderate-severe disease:
  - MTX
  - 20mg/d pred. equivalent - longer course 

In asymptomatic patients who meet the following criteria, treatment is not warranted:
* ﻿﻿Stage IV radiographic disease (severe reticular disease with volume loss and/or traction bronchiectasis)
* ﻿﻿Moderately or severely reduced vital capacity or diffusing capacity
* ﻿﻿Exertional hypoxaemia

The triad of hilar lymphadenopathy, erythema nodosum, and a migratory polyarthralgia is known as “Löfgren Syndrome” and is typically self-resolving, perhaps requiring some NSAIDs at most.
