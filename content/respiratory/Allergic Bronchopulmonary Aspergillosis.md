# Allergic Bronchopulmonary Aspergillosis
#bpt/respiratory
- - -
### Overview
ABPA is a cause of [[bronchiectasis]] that is characterised by a complex hypersensitivity reaction to colonisation of the airway by _Aspergillus fumigatus_. Although rare in the general population, it is highly prevalent in patients with [[respiratory/Asthma|asthma]] (1-2%) and [[respiratory/Cystic Fibrosis|cystic fibrosis]] (2-9%). 

Patients typically present with recurrent exacerbations of their asthma, expectoration of mucus plugs, fever, and possible haemoptysis. Patients may also present with coincident allergic aspergillus rhinosinusitis.

Although there is no single test to diagnose ABPA, indicative results include:
- FBE: eosinophils >0.5
- IgE >1000 iu/mL
- IgG antibodies or precipitating IgG antibodies (precipitins) to _Aspergillus_
- Sputum MCS: eosinophils, Charcot-Leyden crystals, growth of _Aspergillus_
- HRCT (gold standard): bronchiectasis, particularly in the upper and middle lobes and central bronchi
- Fleeting infiltrates on chest X-ray

Note that serum galactomannan is not considered useful in the diagnosis of ABPA. 

Treatment of ABPA typically involves systemic glucocorticoids and antifungals (typically a later generation azole, e.g. itraconazole).
