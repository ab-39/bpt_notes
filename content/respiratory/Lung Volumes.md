### Definitions
![](Lung%20Volumes/image.png)
~Tidal Volume~
The amount of air that moves in and out of the lungs with each respiratory cycle. Usually around 400-500mL in healthy subjects. Along with the respiratory rate, this is the major determinant of minute ventilation.

~Inspiratory Reserve Volume~
The additional volume of air able to be inhaled after a normal breath. Together with the tidal volume gives the inspiratory capacity. 

~Expiratory Reserve Volume~
The additional volume of air able to be exhaled after a normal tidal breath.

~Residual Volume~
The air that remains in the lungs _after_ maximum forceful expiration. This is the air volume that cannot be expelled, and which keeps the alveoli open (hence, PEEP increases the residual volume). 

~Functional Residual Capacity~
The sum of the expiratory reserve volume and the residual volume. 

~Vital Capacity~
The volume of air occupying the lungs at the different phases of the respiratory cycle, minus the residual volume (i.e., the maximum volume of air able to be exhaled after full inspiration).

~Total Lung Capacity~
The volume of air in the lungs upon maximum effort of inspiration, equivalent to sum of the vital capacity and the residual volume (i.e. VC + RV). Usually around 6L in healthy subjects.

### Lung Volume Testing 
This is usually carried out by _body plethysmography_ - i.e. where the patient is seated in an enclosed space where the volumes and pressures are known, and then made to breathe into a tube so the changes in volume and pressure can be measured.

Alternatively, helium dilution or nitrogen washout can be used to measure volumes using other gasses as surogate markers. These methods to not measure poorly ventilated parts of the lung.
