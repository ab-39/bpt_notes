### What is narcolepsy?
Narcolepsy is a _central disorder of hypersomnolence_ caused by autoimmune destruction of the neurones that create hypocretin (orexin), leading to a dysregulation of arousal and the transition between waking and sleep, with two subtypes:
1. Type 1:
   - Excessive daytime sleepiness for at least three months
   - One of:
     - Definite cataplexy and a positive mean sleep latency test 
     - Low CSF hypocretin
     - REM sleep latency <20 minutes (‘SOREM’) 
2. Type 2:
   - Excessive daytime sleepiness for at least three months
   - Cataplexy absent 
   - Positive MSLT
   - Exclusion of other causes of sleepiness

Cataplexy = sudden, transient episodes of muscle weakness with conscious awareness, usually triggered by emotions.

Patients may also experience _hypnopompic hallucinations_ - very vivid hallucinations prior to falling asleep. 

This is a rare condition, and the most commonly associated haplotypes are HLA-DQB1*0602 and DR3.

### Treatment of Narcolepsy
- Stimulants - e.g. dexamphetamines, modafinil
- SSRIs/SNRIs for cataplexy
- Rarely: sodium oxybate, gamma-hydroxybutyrate (GHB)

Note that modafinil treats narcolepsy but _not_ cataplexy, while methylphenidate, GHB, and amphetamines treat both. Drugs such as venlafaxine and clomipramine are specifically anti-cataplectic.

Emerging treatments include **_pitolisant_**, an H3 agonist.

### Mean Sleep Latency Test
Needs to follow a diagnostic PSG (exclude other causes of sleepiness) and ensure the patient is well rested.

Take a sequence of 20 minute naps separated by two hours, and assess sleep latency. 
> A mean sleep latency <8 minutes associated with 2 sleep onset REM periods strongly suggests a diagnosis of narcolepsy
