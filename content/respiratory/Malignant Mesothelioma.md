# Malignant Mesothelioma

### What is malignant mesothelioma?
Malignant mesothelioma is an invasive cancer arising from the mesothelial cells of the pleural and peritoneal cavities, and occasionally the tunica vaginalis of the scrotum. 
- - -
### Epidemiology
Malignant mesothelioma is almost exclusively caused by the inhalation of asbestos, and is incredibly prevalent in Western Australia due to the mining of crocidolite. There is typically a 30-40 year latency between exposure and development of mesothelioma. 

Note the possibility for legal compensation through the courts. 
- - -
### Approach to Mesothelioma
Unfortunately, there is no secondary prevention or cure for mesothelioma - breathing in asbestos confers risk. Additionally, most medical treatments are highly toxic and poorly effective, while radical surgery is also not useful.

The presence of asbestos pleural plaques, however, does not appear to be an additional risk factor for multiple myeloma, and patients with plaques do not need additional follow up. 
- - -
### Presentation 
Mesothelioma typically causes an insidious onset of:
- chest pain (<- invasion)
- breathlessness (<- effusion -> ascites, if in abdomen)
- loss of weight

Typical radiological signs include pleural thickening and nodularity. 
- - -
### Diagnosis
Diagnosis is typically based on biopsy or cytology, leading to sub typing:
- epithelioid - most common
- sarcomatoid - worst prognosis  **but** more sensitive to immunotherapy
- biphasic
- - -
### Prognosis
Poor:
- Epithelioid tends to have the best prognosis
| **Histologic subtype** | **Features** | **Prognosis** |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| **Epithelioid** | Most common (~60–70%) | Best prognosis; median survival ~12–24 months |
| **Sarcomatoid** | Spindle-shaped cells | Worst prognosis; median survival ~4–8 months |
| **Biphasic (mixed)** | Both epithelioid and sarcomatoid | Intermediate prognosis; depends on % sarcomatoid component |

Median survival time in 9-12months, worse if the peritoneum is involved. 
- - -
### Management
Multidisciplinary! Some key strategies include:
- palliative care input on pain and dyspnoea
- pleural aspiration/drainage and pleurodesis/indwelling catheter for effusions
- limited role for surgical debulking - no survival benefit but may be high risk
- limited scope for radiotherapy as MM involves the entire pleura - it may be used for localised pain or obstruction

First line treatment now is either:
- Non-epithelioid -> Ipilimumab + nivolumab, or
- Epithelioid -> platinum based chemotherapy (e.g. cisplatin + permextred) which may improve survival by ~3 months in 40% of patients

Benefit is generally best in sarcomatoid variant, for which immunotherapy is definitively first line.
