### What is lung transplantation?
Lung transplantation is a life-saving treatment for patients with end-stage respiratory failure not responding to other medical and/or surgical therapies. It should be considered for patients who are likely to:
- Die within 2 years from their lung condition
- Survive the procedure
- Survive for at least 5 years after their transplant 

In terms of single vs double lung transplantation:
- Single -> COPD, ILD
- Bilateral -> CF, bronchiectasis, pulmonary hypertension

### Indications for referral for transplantation by condition
#### [[respiratory/Cystic Fibrosis|Cystic Fibrosis]]
- Chronic type 1 or type 2 respiratory failure
- NIV dependence 
- Frequent admissions to hospital (especially with infections or haemoptysis)
- pHTN
- Rapid decline  

#### [[COPD]]
- BODE index ≥7
- Very severe airflow obstruction (FEV1 < 9,2
- Frequent exacerbations
- Hypercarbic respiratory failure

Note that although transplantation does improve survival in COPD, this is typically only for patients with severe disease (i.e. FEV1 < 0.2 or 6MWT < 300m).

#### [[Interstitial Lung Disease]]
Early referral is critical! Patient needs a certain amount of physiological reserve to benefit from transplantation. Signs to consider referral for assessment include:
- 10% decline in FVC over 6 months
- 15% decline in DLCO over 6 months
- SpO2 <88% on 6MWT
- ET <250m or decline in distance of >50m in 6 months
- pHTN
- Worsening CT findings 

Moreover, need evaluation of signs of causes of disease:
- GORD -> scleroderma
- haematological/liver/renal effects -> telomere associated ILD
- connective tissue manifestations
- age
- frailty

### Other considerations
#### [[Pulmonary Hypertension]]
Patients are stratified according to risk for pulmonary hypertension. This is important as high-risk patients are more likely to die while awaiting transplantation, have poor survival, and develop chronic lung allograft dysfunction.

### Types of Lung Transplantation
- Single lung
  - May allow use of a donor organ that was otherwise unusable (e.g. trauma affected one lung only)
  - Can have ongoing problems with remaining native lung
  - Higher mortality than double lung transplant
- Double lung
  - Performed as a bilateral sequential single lung transplant (abbrv. BSSLTx) with two separate anastomoses with the main bronchi 
- Heart-Lung
  - Usually for patients with congenital heart disease (especially Eisenmenger syndrome)
- Heart-Lung-Liver

### Post-Transplant Medical Management
Generally requires a combination of:
- Calcineurin inhibition - TAC/CsA
- Cell cycle inhibitors - MMF/AZA
- Prednisolone
The immunosuppression required for a lung transplant is generally higher than that for a renal transplant, and pausing/stopping immunosuppression for side effects (e.g. nephrotoxicity) can be tricky. 

Prophylaxis:
- CMV
- EBV
- PJP prophylaxis 
- Azithromycin - may have prophylactic effect against chronic allograft dysfunction

### Complications of Lung Transplantation
Lung transplantation is the solid organ transplant with the highest mortality rates: ~80% in the first year and as low as 50% after 5y. 

- Hyperacute
  - Donor-recipient mismatch - rare with matching these days
- Early
  - Bleeding
  - Primary graft dysfunction (especially if pHTN or RHF) -> early ECMO
  - Pleural and other surgical complications
  - AKI
- Intermediate
  - Acute cellular rejection
  - Acute antibody mediated rejection
  - Airway complications - dehiscence or stenosis
  - Pulmonary vein stenosis
  - PE
  - Infections (systemic)
  - Metabolic 
- Late
  - Chronic lung allograft dysfunction 
    - Bronchiolitis obliterans
    - Restrictive allograft syndrome
  - Post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder
    - Malignant complication if EBV+ transplant in naive donor
  - Malignancy 2’ immunosuppressive therapy
  - Primary disease recurrence (rare)
  - Hypertension
  - Renal impairment 
  - Post-transplant diabetes
