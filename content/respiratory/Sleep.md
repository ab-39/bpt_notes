### Sleep Architecture
The normal brain function and activity during sleep is termed _sleep architecture_, that are elucidated through findings on EEG, EOG (ocular), and chin EMG. These are:
1. REM sleep
   - ~20% of sleep, in the latter half of the night
   - Characterised by atonia on chin EMG, and tonic/phasic eye movements on the EOG
   - Importantly, disordered in [[narcolepsy]] and [[REM sleep behaviour disorder]]  
2. NREM sleep 
   - Vast majority of sleep (~80%)
   - Further divided into N1, N2, and N3 sleep, corresponding to depth

Sleep architecture is pictorially represented in the **hypnogram**.

### Sleep Studies
Sleep studies consist of a polysomnogram (EEG + EOG + EMG + ECG + airflow + respiratory effort). There are two types of sleep studies:
- Attended (in a sleep lab) - the gold standard (type 1)
- Unattended (at home) - contraindicated if neuromuscular weakness, hypoventilation, intellectual impairment, seizures, or parasomnias (type 2)

While most studies have ≥7 channels, there are also ‘limited channel’ (type 3 and type 4) studies.

### Important Sleep Definitions:
| Term                               | Definition                                                   |
|------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Apnea                              | Cessation of airflow for at least 10s<br>Can be either central, obstructive, or mixed |
| Hypopnea                           | A partial apnoea - a reduction in airflow by at least 30% for at least 10s<br>Needs signs of arousal on EEG or desaturation of 4% |
| AHI (Apnoea-Hypopnoea Index)       | Events per hour -> marker of severity:<br>Normal = ≤5/h<br>Mild = 5-15/h<br>Moderate = 15-30/h<br>Severe = ≥30/h |
| Respiratory Effort Related Arousal | Events of increased respiratory effort for ≥10s with evidence of EEG arousal, but not meeting criteria for hypnopnea  |
| Respiratory Disturbance Index      | AHI + RERAs                                                  |
| Sleep Hypoventilation              | Increases in transcutaneous CO2 to >55mmHg for >10min (or a >10mmHg increase in CO2 to >50mmHg for >10min) |
| Cheyne-Stokes Respiration          | Cyclical, recurrent apneas/hypopneas alternating with a respiratory phase demonstrating a crescendo-decrescendo pattern of flow (typically >40s cycles)<br>Found in heart failure and nuerological disorders |

### Key Sleep Breathing Disorders
1. [[Obstructive Sleep Apnea]] - upper airway obstruction with ongoing respiratory effort
2. [[Central Sleep Apnea]] - reduction of airflow due to reduced respiratory effort
3. Sleep Hypoventilation/[[Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome]] - abnormal increase in PaCO2 with sleep
