# Atrial Flutter
#bpt/cardiology
- - -
### Overview
Atrial flutter (AFL) typically arises due to a macro re-entrant circuit firing in a counterclockwise fashion around the tricuspid annulus. This leads to a characteristic ‘sawtooth’ appearance on ECG with positive flutter waves in V1:
![](Atrial%20Flutter/image.png)
The atrial rate is around 300bpm, and depending on how this is conducted, the ventricular rate will be in the range of 150-350bpm. The reason for this difference is that the AV node is often refractory when the impulses arrive, and so they are only conducted in a 2:1, 3:1 etc. fashion. 

Similarly to atrial fibrillation, AFL can cause a tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathy. As such, it is a class I indication for catheter ablation.

> [!WARNING] 
> Flecainide can slow conduction within the flutter circuit without blocking the AV node. If it slows the rate of flutter to <300bpm, this will allow the AV node to complete its refractory period, and 1:1 conduction will follow.
