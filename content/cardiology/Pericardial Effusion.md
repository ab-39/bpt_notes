# Pericardial Effusion
#bpt/cardiology
- - -
### Overview
Cardiac tamponade is a medical emergency and occurs when abnormal amounts of fluid accumulate in the pericardial sac compressing the heart and limiting the ability its ability to fill and pump effectively. 

In tamponade, the accumulation of fluid in the pericardial sac increases the **intrapericardial pressure**. This pressure restricts the heart's ability to fill during diastole, especially affecting the right side of the heart first, but eventually impacting both ventricles. This results in decreased stroke volume and cardiac output, contributing to hypotension. 

**Intrapericardial pressure**: In tamponade, the intrapericardial pressure is **increased**, not decreased. The fluid within the pericardial sac exerts pressure on the heart, impairing its ability to expand and fill.

**Jugular venous pressure (JVP)**: Jugular venous pressure is typically **increased** in tamponade due to impaired venous return from the right side of the heart. The increased pericardial pressure limits right ventricular filling, causing blood to back up in the systemic veins.

**Left atrial pressure**: Left atrial pressure can be **increased** in tamponade, as the restriction in ventricular filling leads to a backup of pressure that affects both the right and left sides of the heart. 

**Left ventricular end diastolic volume :** This is **decreased** in tamponade. The increased pericardial pressure impairs ventricular filling, and particularly in the left ventricle, where the restrictive effect from the pericardium reduces the volume of blood that can enter the ventricle during diastole.
