### Overview 
Since its invention in Rouen by Prof. Alain Cribier in 2002, TAVI has emerged as the dominant strategy for treating patients across all risk categories for aortic valvulopathies.

In general TAVI is favoured over SAVR when:
- Severely calcified (‘porcelain’) aorta
- Age >75y
- EuroSCORE II >4*
- Fraily
- Restricted mobility
- Previous cardiac surgery
- Sequelae of chest radiation

On the other hand, SAVR is favoured as first line when:
- Low coronary ostium - note that this can be overcome by the BASILICA procedure, which involves perforating the wall of the TAVI to allow for access for the coronary arteries 
- Shallow coronary sinus
- Unfavourable femoral access
- Extensive CAD
- Endocarditis
- Aortic aneurysm

### Valve Failure
Valve-in-valve TAVI can be used after failure of an initial (often surgical) valve. This is treated with TAVI _followed by_* balloon dilatation to fracture the ring of the initial valve. 

*doing the BAV _after_ the TAVI is critical to prevent the development of significant AR. 

There is a small risk of coronary obstruction with a VIV TAVI, as the displacement of the old surgical valve may cause occlusion of a coronary sinus. This is prevented with a procedure called BASILICA, where an electrified guidewire is used to cut open the surgical valve leaflet to allow coronary blood flow.
