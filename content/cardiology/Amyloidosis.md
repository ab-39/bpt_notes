### Overview
Amyloidosis is a systemic disorder of protein folding, in which proteins and their fragments are deposited in the extracellular space as insoluble fibrils that cause organ dysfunction.

There are three main groups of amyloidosis:
- AL amyloidosis - associated with immunoglobulin light chains - _severe_: prognosis as low as 6m
- AA amyloidosis - reactive to chronic inflammation
- ATTR amyloidosis - autosomal dominant disorder of transerythtin, which can either deposit in nerves or cardiac muscle
  - There is an entity known as _wild type_ ATTR amyloidosisi that can occur in the elderly
- - -
### Renal Amyloidosis
Renal amyloidosis is a common secondary cause of [[nephrotic syndrome]]. It can either arise due to immunoglobulin light-chains (AL), which are typically monoclonal in nature (κ or λ), and secreted in excess due to an underlying lymphoproliferative disorder (e.g. multiple myeloma, Waldenström macroglobulinaemia), systemic inflammation (AA), or congenital causes (ATTR, transerythrin-associated). Although the light-chains themselves are seen in the urine (as Bence-Jones proteins), the damage to the nephron leads to albuminuria and subsequent nephrotic syndrome. It is most common in patients above 40 years of age.

### Cardiac Amyloidosis
Cardiac amyloidosis is most commonly associated with transerythrin amyloid, and is characterised by the deposition of amyloid proteins in the myocardium, leading to left ventricular stiffness, oxidative stress, and ultimately HFpEF. Associated with impaired myocardial energetics as measured through the phosphokinase:ATP ratio. 

#### Wild-Type vs Mutant ATTR
Wild type tends to be a disease of the elderly (hence ‘senile ATTR amyloidosis’), and predominantly leads to:
- Cardiomyopathy
- Conduction block
- Arrhythmia
- Carpal tunnel syndrome
- Vitreous opacities 
- Rupture of biceps tender
- Spinal stenosis
- Peripheral neropathy
- MGUS

Mutant ATTR is an autosomal dominant condition that tends to either have a cardiac or peripheral neurological phenotype, and involve:
- Cardiomyopathy
- Conduction block
- Arrythmia
- CNS - dementia, ataxia, seizures, paresis, stroke-like symptoms
- Ocular - vitreous opacities, glaucoma
- Carpal tunnel syndrome
- Nephropathy
- Autonomic neuropathy
- GI manifestations
- Peripheral sensorimotor neuropathy 

#### Diagnosis
Gold standard diagnosis of ATTR cardiac amyloidosis is with endomyocardial biopsy, showing amyloid proteins displaying apple-green birefringence with Congo red staining.

In the absence of a cardiac biopsy, diagnosis is made with an _extracardiac_ biopsy displaying the same findings, as well as typical cardiac imaging findings. Clinical diagnosis can be made if a clonal process is excluded, there is evidence of cardiac uptake on bone tracer scintigraphy (relative to uptake in the ribs), and typical cardiac imaging findings.

‘Typical cardiac imaging findings’ generally refers to echocardiography or else cMRI. Key findings include:
- Increased LV wall thickness (>12mm)
- Restrictive phenotype - small chambers and enlarged atria
- Reduced global longitudinal strain with apical sparing
- ≥Grade 2 diastolic dysfunctoin
- Pericardial effusion
- AV valve thickening

#### Staging
- Stage I: NT-proBNP <3000ng/L and eGFR >45
- Stage III: NT-proBNP >3000ng/L and eGFR <45
- Stage IV: NT-proBNP >10,000ng/L (poor survival)

#### Treatment
Specific treatment strategies for ATTR amyloidosis centre around three principles:
1. Suppression of TTR synthesis with [[nucleic acid therapies]] (typically siRNAs or CRISPR-Cas9) -> inotersen, patirisan, eplontersen, etc.
2. Stablisation of TTR to prevent infiltration -> e.g. tafamidis - improves mortality and slows (but does not eliminate) progressive morbidity 
3. Fibril disruptors -> doxycycline + TUDCA

Multiple trials have found that SGLT2-inhibitors are associated with reduced mortality and hospitalisations in ATTR cardiac amyloidosis. 

In addition to this, nonspecific supportive therapies can be used to improve symptoms and prevent complications:
- HF -> diuresis (tend to avoid neurohormonal blockade)
- AF -> rate control (avoid CCBs and digoxin)
- Anticoagulation
- Conduction abnormalities -> PPM

Serum TTR is the most commonly used biomarker to measure treatment response.
