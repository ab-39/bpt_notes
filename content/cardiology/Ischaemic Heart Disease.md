### Risk Factors
~Non-Modifiable~:
- Age
- Male sex
- Family history of vascular illness or early sudden death
- [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]]

~Modifiable~:
- High cholesterol
- [[renal/Hypertension|Hypertension]]
- Smoking
- [[Type 2 Diabetes]]/insulin resistance
- Physical inactivity
- Obesity

~Biochemical/Genetic~:
- Fibrinogen
- CRP
- tPA/plasminogen activator-inhibitor 1
- Homocysteine
- ApoE4 allele
- ACE polymorphism
- Angiotensinogen gene
- [[Lipoprotein A]]
- Remnant-like lipoprotein particles

### Investigation of Chronic Coronary Syndromes
Investigation is stratified by risk:
![](Ischaemic%20Heart%20Disease/image.png)
In patients with suspected chronic coronary syndrome, and a low-to-moderate risk of obstructive CAD (<50% risk), CTCA is recommended as the first line investigation. The exception is for stable patients over the age of 65y with LBBB, who should receive functional imaging:
- LBBB is also a contraindication for stress MIBI, along with severe asthma

### Treatment of Chronic Coronary Syndromes
Multiple studies (e.g. _COURAGE_, _ISCHAEMIA_, and most importantly _ORBITA_) and meta-analyses have found that PCI has no advantage over medical management in stable angina. 
- In any case: <70% stenosis is unlikely to warrant a stent 

> [!TIP] Mnemonic
> Symptoms -> stents

Indications for revascularisation of stable CAD - if ongoing symptoms despite GDMT -> can revascularise with PCI.
- New trial: _[ORBITA-2](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2310610)_ - upfront PCI showed reduction in angina symptoms compared to placebo
- Use FFR <0.8 to guide ongoing treatment
- Multivessel disease + diabetes or LVEF <35% -> CAGS is superior

### PCI vs CAGS
This question is answered with the SYNTAX score, of which anatomic complexity is essentially the main determinant (unless the patient has proximal left main disease).

### Treatment of Acute Coronary Syndrome
Patients with acute STEMI should receive reperfusion within 90 minutes:
- If it is feasible to get to a PCI centre in this time -> PCI
- Otherwise -> fibrinolysis (TNK)

> [!NOTE] STEMI Equivalents
> Posterior myocardial infarction
> - ST depression ≥ 0.5 mm in leads V1–V4
> - ST elevations ≥ 0.5 mm in leads V7–V9
> Left main-vessel occlusion or three-vessel disease 
> - ST depression ≥ 1 mm in ≥ 6 leads 
> - Combined with ST elevation in leads aVR and/or V1
> LBBB or RBBB with strong clinical suspicion for MI
> 
> Also look for other signs that can indicate new/impending ischaemia:
> - New conduction block -> ?RCA occlusion
> - Wellen syndrome -> ?LAD occlusion
> - De Winter T-waves 

Patients with acute NSTEMI can be stratified depending on risk factors to see if they warrant early angiography:
- Confirmed diagnosis of NSTEMI based on troponin-based algorithms
- Dynamic ST-segment or T-wave changes
- Transient ST-segment elevation
- [GRACE score](https://www.mdcalc.com/calc/1099/grace-acs-risk-mortality-calculator) >140
  - Age
  - Heart rate
  - Systolic BP
  - Creatinine
  - Cardiac arrest at admission
  - ST-segment deviation on ECG
  - Abnormal cardiac enzymes
  - Killip class (essentially signs of LV failure)

After treatment for an acute coronary syndrome, patients should be commenced on treatment with:
- High-dose statin therapy
- Dual antiplatelet therapy 
- Antianginal therapy
- Β-blockade depending on ejection fraction:
  - LVEF ≥50% -> no mortality benefit from β-blockade (*[REDUCE-AMI](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2401479)*)
  - LVEF <50% -> mortality benefit (*[BHAT](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/370103)*)

> [!IMPORTANT] DDx
> Key differentials for _nonatherosclerotic_ ischaemic heart disease include:
> - Fibromuscular dysplasia - a nonatherosclerotic, noninflammatory condition that leads to multiple stenoses of the arteries, particularly in the kidneys, heart and brain. Predominantly a disease of younger women. Exact aetiology unknown. Characterised by a ‘string of beads’ appearance on imaging.
> - Spontaneous coronary artery dissection - nontraumatic, noniatrogenic separation of the coronary arterial wall, leading to intramural haematoma formation and vessel occlusion. More common in women and patients under 50y. 

The major coronary arteries, which are distributed over the epicardial surface of the heart, occasionally have a segment that is intramyocardial. The muscle overlying the intramyocardial segment of an epicardial coronary artery (most often the left anterior descending artery) is referred to as a "myocardial bridge."
