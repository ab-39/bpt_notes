### Overview
Disorders of the cardiac valves are common, and associated with significant mortality and morbidity. The demographic features of the patient are associated with particular syndromes, of relevance:
- [[Congenital heart disease]] patients -> pulmonary stenosis, [[cardiology/Aortic Stenosis|aortic stenosis]]
- Young/remote/Indigenous patients -> [[cardiology/Rheumatic Heart Disease|rheumatic heart disease]] -> mitral stenosis
- Elderly -> degenerative -> aortic stenosis, [[mitral regurgitation]], [[tricuspid regurgitation]], [[aortic regurgitation]]

The lynchpins of diagnosis are physical examination and echocardiography, and severe valvular lesions warrant intervention. 

Note that some patients will have multi-valve pathology, that can result in a severe phenotype without meeting echocardiographic parameters for severity.

### Continuous Wave Doppler
MS and AR have a similar waveform but MS is seen BEFORE QRS while AR is seen AFTER QRS.
MR and AS have a similar waveform but in MR wave goes up, AS wave goes down and again MR is seen BEFORE QRS and AS is seen AFTER QRS.
