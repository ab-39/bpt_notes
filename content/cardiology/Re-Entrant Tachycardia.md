# Re-Entrant Tachycardia
#bpt/cardiology
- - -
### Overview
[[Supraventricular tachycardia]] that arises due to the presence of a re-entrant circuit leads to a short RP tachycardia that can be divided into two groups:
- AV **_nodal_** re-entrant tachycardia (AVNRT) - where the re-entrant circiut is located _within_ the AV node
  - Typically, anterograde conduction is down the slow limb of the loop, while retrograde conduction is down the fast limb
  - Due to the ‘compact’ nature of the loop, the atria and the ventricles end up activating in parallel leading to a _very_ short RP (<80ms)
- AV re-entrant tachycardia (AVRT) - due to the existence of an accessory pathway, with the most notable example being the [[Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome]] 
  - Note that the AV node is still a component of the loop
  - As the re-entrant loop is much larger than in AVNRT, the atria and ventricles have time to contract sequentially, leading to a moderately short RP (>80ms)

Management:
- Reassurance + monitoring
- Drug therapy - usually a β-blocker, CCB, or flecainide
- EP studies have a class I recommendation for first line treatment if patient amenable - >95% cure rate with very low rates of complicatoins
