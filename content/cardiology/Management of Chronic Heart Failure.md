### Overview
Disease modifying therapy of chronic heart failure is well established around three pillars:
1. Inhibition of angiotensin
2. Beta-blockade
3. Antagonism of mineralocorticoids

Together, triple therapy with these three classes of drugs leads to a 60-70% relative risk reduction for all cause mortality in HFrEF. Now, there is also excellent evidence for the role of _SGLT2 inhibitors_ as also contributing to mortality benefit. Management with the new ‘four pillars’ has very compelling evidence for reduction in mortality and readmission rates.

### HFrEF
In patients with LVEF <40%, rapid introduction of the following drug classes is strongly recommended once the diagnosis of heart failure is made:
1. ACEi/A2RB/ARNI
   - Reduce mortality and decrease readmission rates
   - A2RBs have the smallest effect on mortality
2. Βeta-blocker - specficially: bisoprolol, carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, nebivolol
   - Evidence from _COPERNICUS_, _MERIT-HF_, _CIBIS-II_, and _COMET_ 
   - Best if not started acutely during an episode of [[acute decompensated heart failure]] 
3. MRA
   - Evidence from _RALES_ and _EMPHASIS-HF_ 
   - Reduce mortality
   - ADRs: hyperkalaemia, gynaecomastia (inhibit testosterone binding to breast tissue)

In general, these drugs exert independent, additive effects. While it is advisable to aim for the target doses used in trials for individual drugs, this should not be at the detriment of instituting all drug classes for HF. Treatment should not be stopped unless a reversible cause is identified and corrected. 

Another important aspect of treatment is that patients with ferritin <100μg/L or ferritin <300μg/L with Tsat <20% should receive iron repletion, and, in fact, the treatment effect is greatest in patients with low Tsat. _IRONOUT-HF_ found that oral iron is not effective.

#### ARNI
Now, the A2RB comes in combination with the _neprolysin inhibitor_ salcubitril, a prodrug, which prevents the breakdown of endogenous natriuretic peptides (such as the BNP). This causes vasorelaxation, reduced BP, reduced sympathetic tone, and reduced activation of the RAAS. Salcubitril cannot be combined with ACE-inhibitors due to an unacceptably high risk of angioedema. The _PARADIGM-HF_ trial established superiority of ARNI cf. enalapril (which was itself the ACE inhibitor with the best evidence from _CONSENSUS_ and _SOLVD_) in terms of CV death or hospitalisation with heart failure. 

ARNIs are recommended (after a 36h washout) if patients continue to have reduced LVEF despite maximal treatment with and ACEi and β-blocker. Previously, the _ATLAS_ study suggested that increasing the dose of an ACEi alone is typically not effective at improving outcomes. Subsequent studies found that addition of a different class of drug or switching to an ARNI tended to be more effective. 

#### SGLT2i
Approximately 90% of glucose (daily load 180g) is reabsorbed in the proximal tubule by SGLT2, with the remainder being reabsorbed in the loop of Henle by SGLT1. Inhibition of SGLT2, thus, leads to a profound glycosuresis and attendant natriuresis, with beneficial effects on both diabetes and cardiovascular events. The cardiovascular benefit of SGLT2i began to emerge as signals in the safety studies for their use in diabetes, and then went on to be proved by later landmark trials, including:
- _DAPA-HF_ - in patients with LVEF <40%, dapagliflozin reduced cardiovascular deaths and heart failure hospitalisations when compared to placebo
- _EMPEROR_-reduced - in patients with LVEF <40%, with or without diabetes, empagliflozin reduced cardiovascular deaths and heart failure hospitalisations when compared to placebo

In general, patients who are congested should be commenced on an SGLT2i early and a β-blocker when euvolaemic, while patients who are euvolaemic should get a β-blocker first and an SGLT2i in the first ~4w.

#### HFmrEF
The _CHARM_ and _TOPCAT_ studies seem to suggest that for candesartan and spironolactone, respectively, there is evidence for treatment in the ‘moderately-reduced’ ejection fraction range. 

#### Ivabradine
Ivabradine, the sinus node _Ifunny_ inhibitor, was tested for use in patients with LVEF ≤35% in the _SHIFT_ study, where it was found to reduce the composite of heart failure mortality and hospitalisation at 2y when compared to placebo. The caveat is that to be prescribed ivabradine, the patient must have a heart rate >70bpm in sinus rhythm.

#### Vericiguat
Vericiguat is a guanylate cyclase stimulator that enhances the cGMP pathway, leading to increased vasodilatation both due to and independently of nitrous oxide. The _VICTORIA_ study found that in patients with recent exacerbations of heart failure, vericiguat reduced cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalisation.

#### Omecamtiv mecarbil
A new drug, omecamtiv mecarbil, binds to cardiac myosin and increases the number of mysin heads available to bind to actin and initiate powerful contraction at the beginning of systole, thus directly increasing systolic function. Unlike traditional inotropes, this does not deplete intracellular calcium, which improves its long term safety profile. The _GALACTIC-HF_ trial found that, compared to placebo, increased time to first heart failure event or cardiovascular death. Omecamtiv mecarbil does _not_ reduce blood pressure.

#### Other Treatments
Hydralazine and nitrates:
- May imrpove mortality and hospitalisation in black patients of African descent with HFrEF on maximal therapy

Digoxin:
- Blocks the Na/K-ATPase ni the heart -> increased intracellular calcium -> improved contractility 
- Risk of toxicity -> treat with digifab
- Some drugs increase serum levels:
  - Cardiac drugs: amiodarone, verapamil
  - Other: clarithromycin, erythromycin, azoles, ciclosporin
  - No effect: spironolactone
- May reduce hospitalisations in patients with HFrEF in sinus rhythm on maximal therapy
- Increases exercise tolerance 
- Does not improve mortality 

N-3 PUFA:
- May reduce mortality and hospitalisation in patients with HFrEF on maximal therapy

CRT/ICD:
- In patients with LVEF <35% despite GDMT and LBBB with QRS duration ≥150ms, CRT has a class I recommendation as it is associated with improved symptoms and reduced morbidity and mortality 	
  - There is a weaker, class IIa recommendation for CRT-D in:
    - Non-LBBB IVCD with QRS ≥150ms
    - Or, LBBB with QRS of 130-149ms
- In patients with LVEF <35% despite ≥3 months of GDMT and QRS <130ms, ICD has a class I recommendation in ischaemic HF and a class IIa recommendation in non-ischaemic HF due to reductions in mortality 

Exercise treatment:
- Increases exercise tolerance, symptoms, and quality of life 
- Reduces hospitalisations

### HFpEF
Until recently, the aims of treatment for HFpEF were to improve quality of life and reduce hospitalisation, as no therapies had demonstrated disease modifying effect. 

Like in HFrEF, diuretics are usually required to manage congestion, and comorbidities such as diabetes, hypertension, and AF need to be identified and managed.

Until a few years ago, the best evidence for HFpEF management came from studies such as _PEP-CHF_ and _TOPCAT_, which showed that perindopril and MRAs, respectively, decreased hospitalisations, although without any effect on overall mortality. Similarly, _PARAGON-HF_ found that ARNIs compared to valsartan alone had a small, nonsignificant benefit in reducing hospitalisations and mortality. 

The revolution in HFpEF management came with the advent of the SGLT2-inhibitor, and two key studies:
1. _EMPEROR_-Preserved - in patients with LVEF >40%, with or without diabetes, **empagliflozin** reduces the composite of cardiovascular death and hospitalisation (primarily hospitalisation) when compared to placebo
2. _DELIVER_ - in patients with LVEF >40%, **dapagliflozin** reduces the composite of cardiovascular death and hospitalisation when compared to placebo

More recently, _FINEARTS-HF_ has found a reduction in mortality in HFpEF with finerenone.
- Finerenone is a _non-steroidal_ MRA that has lower affinity for testosterone receptors and inihibits testosterone biosynthesis less -> fewer antiandrogenic side effects (e.g. gynaecomastia)

#### GLP-1 Inhibition
Like the SGLT2 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor antagonists emerged as treatments for diabetes. Given the association between obesity and heart failure, particularly HFpEF (recall: it is a metabolic syndrome), GLP-1RAs have also been evaluated in this setting. The _STEP-HFpEF_ study, which found a significant reduction in time to first HF event or CV death in patients with HFpEF. More recently, the _SUMMIT_ study has found that tirzepatide, a combined GLP-1 and GIP inhibitor, reduced death from cardiovascular causes or worsening heart failure events when compared to placebo. 

#### Exercise
The evidence for exercise in preventing and treating HFpEF is _excellent_.
