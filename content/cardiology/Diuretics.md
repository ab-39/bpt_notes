### Overview
The unifying mechanistic principle of diuretics is:
> _Inhibition of tubular sodium reabsorption to encourage solute and this fluid loss_

Diuretics (broadly) fall into two classes:
- Potassium-wasting diuretics:
  - The loss of fluid leads to intravascular volume contraction, and thus activates the renin-angiotensin system
  - This leads to a secondary hyperaoldosteronism to encourage sodium reabsorption in the collecting duct
  - Consequently, this leads to wasting of potassium and hydrogen ions
  - Finally resulting in _hypokalaemia and a so-called ‘contraction’ metabolic alkalosis_ (note that the exception to this is carbonic anhydrase inhibitors)
- Potassium-sparing diuretics:
  - These directly antagonise the action of aldosterone
  - This leads to retention of K+ and H+
  - Causing hyperkalaemia and metabolic acidosis

### Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors
Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors such as acetazolamide act by inhibiting the conversion of carbonic acid into water and CO2 in the tubular lumen, and the reverse in the tubular cells. This leads to reduced functioning of the Na+/H+ pump, causing retention of Na+ in the urine, and consequent solute and fluid loss. 

Additionally, this leads to the loss of HCO3- in the urine, mimicking a type II/proximal renal tubular acidosis.
- - -
### Loop Diuretics
Loop diuretics block NKCC2  in the thick ascending limb of the Loop of Henle. This leads to a large retention of solutes, primarily sodium, and fluid in the tubule for subsequent loss in the urine. 

However, to compensate for this, the distal convoluted tubule will _increase_ its reabsorption of sodium. 

Overall, loop diuretics can cause a reduction in serum sodium and potassium levels. This also impairs the electrochemical gradient for paracellular diffusion of Ca2+, leading to hypocalcaemia as an additional side effect. 
- - -
### Thiazide Diuretics
Thiazide diuretics inhibit NCCT in the distal convoluted tubule. This leads to retention of sodium and chloride in the urine, and consequent volume loss. 

In contrast to loop diuretics, thiazides lead to an increase in activity in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle, which can cause an increase in the reabsorption of calcium and subsequent hypercalcaemia.
