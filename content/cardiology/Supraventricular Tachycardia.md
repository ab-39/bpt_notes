# Supraventricular Tachycardia
#bpt/cardiology
- - -
### Overview
Any tachyarrhythmia that rises _above_ the level of the ventricles is described as a supraventricular tachycardia. Typically, this causes a _narrow_ complex (i.e. QRS <120ms) tachycardia, although if there is an intraventricular conduction delay the QRS complexes can appear _aberrant_. SVTs arise via one of three mechanisms:
- Enhanced automaticity
- Reentry
- Triggered activity

The supraventricular tachycardias encompass:
- Distinctive morphology:
  - [[Atrial fibrillation]] (AF)
  - Atrial flutter (AFL)
  - Multifocal atrial tachycardia (MAT)
  - Fascicular VT
- Short RP (i.e. RP < PR):
  - AV-nodal re-entrant tachycardia (AVNRT)
  - AV-reentrant tachycardia (AVRT)
  - Focal atrial tachycardia (AT) with first-degree heart block
- Long RP (i.e. RP > PR):
  - Sinus tachycardia
  - Focal atrial tachycardia (AT)
  - Atypical AVNRT
  - PJRT 

For SVTs other than AF/AFL, treatment begins with blocking the AV node, either through:
- Valsalva manoeuvre
- Carotid massage
- Adenosine (contraindicated in severe asthma due to bronchospasm)
