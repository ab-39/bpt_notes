### Overview
Drug transporter proteins tend to be expressed highly in the kidney, where they are responsible for excreting drugs and in the liver, where they are responible for secreting drugs into the biliary canaliculi. They are also found in the gut epithelium and This group of proteins include:
- Organic anion transporter (OAT)
- Organic cation transporter (OCT)
- Oganic anion transporting polypeptide (OATP)
- p-Glycoprotein (MDR1/ABCB1)
- Mutidrug resistance-associated protein (MRP)
- Peptide cotransporters (PEPT)

### Drug Interactions Involving Transporters
p-Glycoprotein tends to be the most clinically relevant:
- Digoxin clearance is reduced by several drugs, including amiodarone, verapamil, quinidine, ritonavir, and ranolazine
- Other common substrates for MDR1/p-Gp include:
  - CsA
  - Fluoroquinolones
  - HIV protease inhibitors
  - Lignocaine
  - Quinidine - also inhibits p-Gp in the blood-brain barrier -> additional drug interactions (e.g. loperamide can penetrate the CNS and cause sedation in combination with quinidine)
  - Ranitidine

Other interactions include:
- OATP: statin clearance is decreased by gemfibrozil, CsA, rifampicin, and lopinavir/r (bosentan is also affected)
- OCT: multiple drugs, most importantly metformin, can have their clearance decreased by cimetidine
- OAT: blocked by probenecid, which can decrease clearance of multiple antivirals as well as frusemide (can be utilised therapeutically)
