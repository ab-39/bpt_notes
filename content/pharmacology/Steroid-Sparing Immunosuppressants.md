# Steroid-Sparing Immunosuppressants
#bpt/pharmacology
- - -
### Overview
- Azathioprine (6-mercaptopurine prodrug)
  - Antimetabolite for purine synthesis -> impaired nucleic acid synthesis -> T and B-cell inhibition
  - Prodrug for 6-MP, which itself is activated by HGPRT into the active metabolite, 6-TGN
  - PO, dose 2-3mg/kg/day
  - Effective after 4-8 months 
  - ADRs: myelosuppression, toxic hepatitis, pancreatitis, risk of hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma
  - Caution: screen for thiopurine methytransferase to identify patients at risk of myelosuppression (in contrast, if TPMT levels are too high, leading to rapid metabolism, allopurinol or febuxostat can be added to increase the amount of active, available 6-mercaptopurine)
  - Monitor FBE and LFT
  - Dose reduce for patients on allopurinol
    - Note that in the presence of allopurinol, two things happen:
      - More 6-MP is shunted to the HGPRT pathway (as this is the highest affinity pathway)
      - Allopurinol creates the formation of (other) metabolites such as thioxanthine that directly inhibit TPMY
    - Hence, 6-TGN levels will increase while 6-MMP levls will actually decrease 
    - Risk of pancreatitis

- Mycophenolate mofetil (monophosphate dehydrogenase inhibitor)
  - Block purine synthesis 
  - PO, 2-3g/kg given as two doses 12h apart
  - Effective after 1-2 months
  - ADRs: nausea, diarrhoea, leucopenia, anaemia
  - Monitor FBE 
- Rituximab (CD20 monoclonal antibody)
