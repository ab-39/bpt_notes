# Cytokines as Therapeutic Targets
#bpt/immunology #bpt/pharmacology #bpt/racp_cls #bpt/antibodies
- - -
### Overview 
[[Cytokines]] are a key target for therapies that aim to suppress or modulate the immune response. Drugs that fall in this category include glucocorticoids, pentoxifylline, and thalidomide. 

Immunomodulatory therapies tend to favour several strategies:
- Monoclonal antibody mediated immunoneutralisation
- Competitive inhibition of cytokine receptors
- Cytokine attenuation 
- Cytokine gene therapy
- - -
### Glucocorticoids 
Corticosteroids target transcription factors that are responsible for the induction of transcription of inflammatory cytokines.

Glucocorticoids attenuate the expression of endothelial cell adhesion molecules mediated by TNFα and IL1β, and reduce the number of circulating T-cells and inhibit the production of IL-2. Glucocorticoids also cause a shift in TH cell differentiation towards [[Less Inflammatory Cytokines|TH2 cells]] with an attenuation of monocyte IL-12 production, leading to a reduction in TH1 response. 
- - -
### The Role of TNFα
Monoclonal antibodies targeting TNFα are a particularly important class of immunomodulatory therapy, used effectively in the treatment of rheumatoid arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, and psoriasis, and now [[inflammatory bowel disease]] as well. 

There is [strong evidence](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/136/18/2018/461707/Hepatosplenic-T-cell-lymphoma-a-rare-but) that TNF-directed therapies are associated with the development of hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma and other malignancies.
