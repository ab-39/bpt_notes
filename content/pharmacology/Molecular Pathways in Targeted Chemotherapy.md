### Overview
Targeted therapies aim to stem cancer growth by countering the effects of key mutations, often those involving important signalling pathways such as the Janus kinases, or the RAS/RAF/MEK/ERK pathway. This can be complicated if there is high-grade intratumoural heterogeneity, in the event that multiple mutations have arisen within a tumour. 

Every signalling pathway fundamentally consists of:
- Ligands
- Transmembrane proteins
- Intracellular tyrosine kinases

Monoclonal antibodies target the first two components of these pathways, while TKIs target the intracellular proteins.
![](Molecular%20Pathways%20in%20Targeted%20Chemotherapy/image.png)
There are basically two broad pathways that are targeted with these drugs:
- Angiogenesis - VEGF, FGFR, PDGFR
- The Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor pathway, which encompasses:
  - HER2 (=ERBB2)
  - PI3-AKT-mTOR pathway
  - RAS-RAF-MEK-ERK pathway

### Adverse Reactions
In general, there are some ADRs that are common across classes of small molecule inhibitor:
- Hepatotoxicity
- Pulmonary toxicity
- Cardiotoxicity
- Fatigue
- Diarrhoea
- Mucusitis 

Some more specific side effects include:
- EGFR pathway -> dermatologic toxicity (acneiform rash)
- VEGF pathway -> hypertension, impaired wound healing, GI perforation, proteinuria (especially mAbs)

### ERBB1/EGFR 
EGFR/ERBB1 was initially identified in patients with lung NSCLC who did not smoke. Now, multiple generations of tyrosine kinase inhibitors target this pathway.

Simply, EGFR lies upstream of all three main signalling pathways:
- PI3K -> AKT -> MTOR
- RAS -> RAF -> MEK -> ERK
- JAK -> STAT 

Three generations of TKIs target EGFR directly:
1. 1st generation = **erlotinib**, gefitinib
   - Reversible binding to EGFR
   - Inactive against T790M mutation
2. 2nd generation = afatinib, dacomitinib 
   - Irreversible binding to all ERBB receptors
   - Inactive against T790M mutation 
3. 3rd generation = **osimertinib**
   - Irreversible binding to EGFR
   - Now first line for EGFR exon 19del or exon 21 L858R mutations
   - Specific to T790M mutation 

Toxicities include:
- Common: acneiform rash, diarrhoea, fatigue
- Uncommon: transaminitis, ocular changes, alopecia, nail changes, pulmonary toxicity 

There are also some monoclonal antibodies that target EGFR, which are used in advanced RAS wild-type colorectal cancer and some head and neck SCC. These are **cetuximab** and **panitumumab**. On binding to EGFR, these block ligand-induced dimerisation and signalling. Toxicities include acneiform rash (on-target effect), diarrhoea, stomatitis, and hypomagnesaemia.

### ERBB2/HER2
HER2/ERBB2 lies upstream of the PI3K/AKT/MTOR and RAS/RAF/MEK/ERK pathways, and is overexpressed in ~20% of breast cancers (TP53 related),  less commonly in gastric/GOJ cancers. It confers a more aggressive phenotype. 

_Monoclonal antibodies_ - **trastuzumab** and **pertuzumab** - can bind to and block signalling via HER2. These are generally well tolerated, but can cause cardiac toxicity/heart failure, although this tends to be reversible. Pertuzumab causes diarrhoea. 

**Lapatinib** is a TKI blocking HER2 _and_ EGFR, with side-effects including nausea/vomiting/diarrhoea, mucositis, fatigue, rash, and hepatotoxicity. 

Trastuzumab can also be used as part of antibody-drug conjugates: 
- TDM1 (plus emtansine) - ADRs include thrombocytopenia, transaminitis, and pneumonitis
- TDXD (plus deruxtecan) - highly effective in HER2 positive breast, gastric, lung, and colon cancer; can cross the blood brain barrier; ADRs include nausea, alopecia, cytopenias, fatigue, diarrhoea, and pneumonitis

### MAP Kinase Pathway 
The most famous of the mitogen-activated protein kinase pathways is RAS -> RAF -> MEK -> ERK. 

In the absence of RAS mutations, cancers (particularly colorectal cancer) can be effectively treated with EGFR inhibition, as this ‘shuts off’ the pathway upstream. 

_RAF_ mutations, particularly V600E, can also be targeted by treatments such as **dabrafenib**. 

There are also emerging treatments such as **trametinib** (MEK) and **ulixertinib** (ERK). 

Toxicities associated with BRAF and MEK inhibition include anorexia, pyrexia, rash, asthenia, fatigue, diarrhoea, and hypertension.

### PIK3CA-MTOR Pathway
Used in ER-positive (i.e. BRCA2) advanced breast cancers:
- PI3K -> **alpelisib**
  - Used in tumours with PI3KCA mutations
  - ADRs: hyperglycaemia, diarrhoea, nausea, rash
- AKT -> **capivasertib**
  - Used in tumours with PIK3CA, AKT1, or PTEN mutations 
  - ADRs: hyperglycaemia, diarrhoea, maculopapular rash
- mTOR -> **everolimus** 
  - Used irrespective of mutation status
  - ADRs: stomatitis, rash, fatigue, diarrhoea, infections, nausea, haematological toxicity, pneumonitis

### Cell-Cycle Pathway 
In normal cells, cyclin-dependent kinases 4 and 6 (CDK4/6) and cyclin D1 phosphorylate and inactivate the tumour suppressor Rb. In cancer cells, particularly ER+ (BRCA2) breast cancers, there are higher rates of cyclin D1/CDK4/6 activation, leading to over-inhibition of Rb. CDK4/6 inhibition, e.g. **ribociclib**, helps to re-establish Rb in its tumour suppressing role, causing cell cycle arrest in the G1 phase. 

These drugs are relatively well tolerated with side effects including anaemia, neutropenia, LFT derangements, nausea, rash, pruritis, and diarrhoea.

### PARP Inhibitors 
Poly-ADP ribose polymerase (or PARP) is a protein that is involved in the repair of single-strand breaks in DNA, while homologous recombination (involving the *BRCA* genes) repairs double stranded breaks in DNA.

*BRCA*-mutated cancers have limited ability to repair double stranded breaks in DNA, and hence they are more vulnerable to PARP inhibition. Hence, in the presence of _BRCA_ mutations, PARP inhibitors exhibit synthetic lethality while relatively sparing normal cells that are able to utilise homologous recombination. 

Drugs in this class include **olaparib**, and side effects include nausea, diarrhoea, fatigue, and myelosuppression.

### VEGF and Multi-Kinase Inhibitors
Treatments directed at VEGF tend to also affect collateral growth pathways, and the side-effects depend  on which pathway has been affected:
- Sorafenib - used in HCC, thyroid cancer, clear cell RCC
- Sunitinib, axitinib (+ PDGFR)- used in clear cell RCC
- Lenvatinib (+ FGFR) - HCC
- Bevacizumab (VEGF specific) - CRC, gliomas

ADRs include:
- VEGF related: HT proteinuria, VTE, PRES, poor wound healing 
- GI: mucositis, diarrhoea 
- Cutaneous: rash, hand-foot syndrome
- Misc.: transaminitis (esp. pazopanib), hypothyroidism, CCF
- - -
### FGFR Inhibitors
FGFR alterations are present in ~15% of lower renal tract UCCs and ~35% of upper tract cancers. _**Erdafitinib**_, a pan-FGFR TKI has good evidence for overall survival benefit as third-line treatment when compared to conventional chemotherapy, but is not yet PBS approved. Toxicities include:
- Fatigue
- Hyperphosphataemia
- Nail changes
- GI side effects
- Ocular side effect - e.g. dry eyes, reversible central serous retinopathy
