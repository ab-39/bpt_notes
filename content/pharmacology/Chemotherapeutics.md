### Overview
Cancer cells are, by their nature, cells with genetic variants that make them prone to rapid division. Chemotherapeutics, then, can target either processes within the cell cycle, or specific genetic/protein targets.

#### The Cell Cycle:
- S-phase:
  - When DNA replication occurs, creating two identical sets of chromosomes 
    - DNA unwinds from histones (e.g. HDAC inhibitors)
  - CTx that act at this stage typically affect either:
    - DNA replication (e.g. 5-FU, gemcitabine)
    - Synthesis of nucleotides essential for DNA synthesis (e.g. methotrexate)
  - This is where the _antimetabolites_ and _topoisomerase I_ inhibitors take effect
- G1-phase:
  - The period of time before the S-phase and following mitosis
  - This is where the cell ‘regulates’ progression to DNA replication 
  - Very few CTx act ‘on’ this phase, but rather aim to stop progression to the S-phase 
    - Some _hormonal therapies_ and _glucocorticoids_ 
    - _CDK4_ and _6_ inhibitors 
- G2-phase:
  - The period of time _after_ the S-phase and before mitosis 
  - This time involves DNA repair and preparation for mitosis
  - CTx active in this phase include:
    - Those that disrupt DNA repair
    - Topoisomerase II inhibitors, anthracyclines
- G0-phase
  - This refers to cells that have exited the cell cycle - they are neither replicating their DNA (S), nor dividing (M)
  - No CTx directly affects this phase, but some hormonal, targeted, and immunotherapies can targets cells that are in G0
- M-phase
  - Mitosis: the phase of cell division
  - Involves multiples proteins, but critically microtubules, which are involved in the segregation of chromosomes from metaphase to anaphase 
    - Vinca alkaloids
    - Taxanes
    - Eribulin

### Antimetabolites
#### Methotrexate
Inhibits dihydrofolate reductase to reduce the production of thymidine and purines.
#### 5-Fluorouracil
Gets incorporated into nucleic acids, disrupting synthesis and function; also inhibits thymidylate synthase to reduce thymidine production. Capecitabine is a prodrug for 5-FU. 5-FU is degraded by DPD, and patients who are deficient in DPD can experience severe toxicity from 5-FU and prodrugs.
#### Gemcitabine, Fludarabine, etc.
Incorporates into DNA and inhibits DNA polymerase, leading to cell apoptosis. 
- - -
### Topoisomerase I Inhibitors
#### Irinotecan
Inhibits topoisomerase I, leading to increased torsional strain during DNA replication. This causes strands of DNA to break during replication, leading to cell death.
#### Topotecan
Also a topoisomerase I inhibitor. 

Poorly tolerated by patients with UGT1A1*28 -> _extra_ diarrhoea 
- - -
### Hormonal Therapies
#### Tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors (e.g. anastrozole, letrozole)
These reduce oestrogen levels, thus reducing growth signals that push cells from G1 to S-phase.

#### Androgen axis inhibitors
- GnRH agonists - e.g. goserelin
- Peripheral androgen blockade - e.g. bicalutamide
- Arrenal androgen synthesis inhibitors - e.g. abiraterone
- - -
### Glucocorticoids
These can induce apoptosis in the G1 phase by altering gene expression and inhibiting cell growth. 
- - -
### CDK Inhibitors
Cyclin dependent kinases are crucial for cell-cycle progression.
#### CDK4/6 inhibitors (e.g. palbociclib, ribociclib, abemaciclib)
Inhibit cyclin dependent kinases 4 and 6, which are critical for progression from G1 to S-phase, arresting cancer cells in G1-phase. 
- - -
### Bleomycin
Induces DNA strand breaks, particularly when cells are actively repairing DNA in G2.
- - -
### Topoisomerase II Inhibitors
#### Etoposide
Stabilises the DNA-topoisomerase II complex, preventing DNA unwinding This causes DNA breaks that prevent progression to mitosis.
#### Anthracyclines (e.g. doxorubicin) 
Disrupt topoisomerase II function to cause DNA breaks and arrest in G2. Can work in multiple phases, but most effective in G2 via DNA intercalation. 
- - -
### Microtubule Disruptors
#### Vinca alkaloids - e.g. vincristine, vinblastine
Bind to tubulin, the protein that makes up microtubules, to inhibit their formation. This prevents cells from progressing to anaphase, and causes cell cycle arrest in metaphase. Prominent neurotoxicity and myelosuppression.
#### Taxanes - e.g. paclitaxel, docetaxel
Stabilise microtubules, preventing depolymerisation, and causing them to form rigid, nonfunctional polymers that disrupt the spindle.
#### Erbulin
A microtubule inhibitor that blocks their growth, leading to mitotic block and apoptosis. 
- - -
### DNA Crosslinkers 
#### Alkylating agents - e.g. cyclophosphamide, ifosfamide, chlorambucil, melphalan
Act across the cell cycle by causing crosslinking of NDA, interfering with regular function and replication.

> [!TIP] Historical Point
> Drugs such as alkylating agents are derived from nitrogen mustard, the agent used as mustard gas during the First World War.

#### Platinum-based agents - e.g. cisplatin, carboplatin, oxaliplatin
Act across the cell cycle by cross linking DNA, preventing strands from separating for replication or repair.
