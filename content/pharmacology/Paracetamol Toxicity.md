# Paracetamol Toxicity
#bpt/pharmacology
- - -
### Overview
In therapeutic doses, paracetamol is glucoronidated and sulfated in the liver, and only a very small amount is metabolised by the cytochrome system into a highly toxic intermediate compound - *N*-acetyl-p-benzoquinoneimine, or NAPQI, which is immediately conjugated with glutathione (a sulfhydryl donor) and subsequently excreted.

Large doses of paracetamol (>10g or 200mg/kg) can quickly saturate the sulfation pathway and then deplete the hepatic stores of glutathione. This leads to accumulation of NAPQI and severe hepatic and renal injury if left untreated. 

The dose-risk relationship of immediate-release paracetamol is quantified using the Rummock-Mathew nomogram. 

N-acetylcysteine is a _highly_ effective antidote that functions as a sulfhydryl donor. If administered within eight hours of paracetamol ingestion, it can reduce the risk of liver injury to almost zero. There is also a role for activated charcoal, if:
- The patient presents within two hours of a paracetamol IR overdose, or
- The patient presents within four hours of a massive (>30g) paracetamol IR overdose, or
- The patient presents within four hours of a paracetamol SR overdose 

Liver transplantation is recommended in cases of [[acute liver failure]] and has the potential to be life saving.
