### Comparison of AEDs
Almost all AEDs cause ataxia and sedation as mild ADEs. There is a general association between anticonvulsants and suicidality.

Bad combinations:
- Phenytoin + carbamazepine
- Phenobarbital + valproic acid 
- Rufinamide + valproic acid 
- Levetiracetam + brivaracetam 
- Na+ blockers + Na+ blockers 

Best combination is probably lamotrigine and valproate. 

Gabapentin exhibits saturable absorption. Phenytoin exhibits saturable metabolism. Carbamazepine induces CYP3A4 for which it is also a substrate.

### Practical Points
- Only three drugs can be loaded:
  - Phenytoin
  - Levetiracetam
  - Valproate
- Every other drug - start low and go slow to minimise CNS side effects
- Try and maintain the lowest possible dose for seizure freedom, usually around:
  - Levetiracetam 1g/d
  - Sodium valproate 600-1000mg/d
  - Carbamazepine 400mg/d 
- Measuring levels is not helpful for TDM, but is helpful for:
  - Establishing _individual_ therapeutic levels
  - Diagnosing toxicity
  - Assessing compliance
- A trial of withdrawal can be considered if the patient remains seizure free after 2y, with a risk of recurrence of around ~30%, exacerbated by:
  - Long duration of epilepsy before remission
  - Short seizure free interval before AEDs
  - Late age of onset 
  - History of febrile seizures
  - Developmental delay
  - Abnormal EEG prior to cessation
- Enzyme-inducing AEDs have a strong association with osteoporosis, probably in the setting of altered vitamin D metabolism

### Antiepileptic Drugs, Women, and Pregnancy 
In general, hormones can affect the seizure threshold:
- Oestrogen -> neuroexcitatory
- Progesterone -> neuroinhibitory 

This balance can lead to a phenomenon of _catamenial_ epilepsy, where seizure frequency increases during certain phases of the menstrual cycle. 

#### AEDs and Contraception
This relationship is frequently bidirectional:
- AEDs induce hepatic enzymes -> metabolise OCP -> reduce efficacy
- Hormonal contraceptives -> reduce efficacy of some AEDS

In general, the AEDs that most affect hormonal contraception are:
- Carbamazepine
- Phenytoin
- Phenobarbital
- Primidone
- Topiramate

_Lamotrigine_ is the AED for which levels are most affected by hormonal contraception, increasing the risk of breakthrough seizures.

The IUD is the preferred option for contraception if needed in a female patient with epilepsy.

#### AEDs and Pregnancy
Maternal and fetal outcomes are worse if a woman goes through pregnancy with uncontrolled epilepsy, particularly GTCS. The [MONEAD](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2008663) study found that most women with epilepsy need _significant_ changes in their AED dose to remain seizure free throughout pregnancy and the postpartum period. That being said, AEDs are risky drugs,  with as high as a 10% chance of major fetal anomalies. It thus comes down to a balance of risk and benefit based on patient and drug characteristics. Some of the key drugs that are problematic in pregnancy are:
| Drug              | Teratogenic Effects                                          | Comments                                                     |
|-------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Sodium valproate  | Neural tube defects<br>Craniofacial anomalies<br>Hypospadias<br>Neurodevelopmental impairment | Highest risk<br>Dose-dependent risk<br>Note that removing valproate _after_ conception can _double_ the risk of seizures  |
| Carbamazepine     | Neural tube defects<br>Cleft palate<br>Cardiac malformations | Generally safer than valproate but not risk free<br>Can be used in pregnancy if needed |
| Topiramate        | Neurodevelopmental disorders<br>Microcephaly<br>IUGR         | ~10% risk of major congenital anomalies -> contraindicated in pregnancy |
| Phenytoin         | Fetal hydantoin syndrome - IUGR, <br>craniofacial anomalies, nail hypoplasia<br>Cardiac defects<br>Urogenital anomalies |                                                              |
| Phenobarbital     | Cardiac defects and oral clefts<br>Neurodevelopmental impairment |                                                              |

In general, lamotrigine and levetiracetam are considered the safest AEDs for use in pregnancy, with a risk of 2-3% each of congenital anomalies. Caveats:
- Lamotrigine glucoronidation increases significantly due to rising oestrogen levels -> dose increase (sometimes as high as threefold)
- Levetiracetam clearance increases ~50% in pregnancy -> dose increase
- Carbamazepine pharmacokinetics generally not changed
