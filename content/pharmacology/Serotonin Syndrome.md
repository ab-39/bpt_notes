# Serotonin Syndrome
#bpt/pharmacology
- - -
### Overview
The serotonin syndrome is caused by an excess of synaptic serotonin,  most often as a consequence of prescription medication. Clinical signs and symptoms include:
- Mental status changes
- Agitation
- Myoclonus
- Hyperreflexia
- Diaphoresis
- Shivering
- Tremor
- Diarrhoea
- Incoordination
- Fever

The drugs most implicated in the development of serotonin syndrome are SSRIs, TCAs, and a few analgesics such as tramadol and pethidine (the TGA also suggests morphine although this is controversial). Other less common drugs include:
- Monoamine oxidase inhibitors - e.g.  moclobemide
- Partial serotonin agonists - e.g. LSD, buspirone
- Drugs that promote serotonin release - e.g. amphetamines
- Lithium
- Migraine agents - e.g. riztriptan
- Serotonin precursors - e.g. tryptophan
