### Overview
IL-2 is a growth factor for activated T-cells. CD28-dependent co-stimulation of activated T-cells, inducing expression of the high-affinity IL-2 receptor. _Basiliximab_, a drug used in the induction of immunosuppression following [[renal transplantation]], binds to and blocks the IL-2Rc on activated T-cells, acting as a lymphodepleting agent. Other indications for basiliximab include the prevention of rejection in other solid organ transplantation, treatment of refractory acute graft-versus-host disease, and uveitis. 

Fomerly, IL-2 was used (presumably due to its ability to induce T-cell activation) to treat some types of cancers (e.g. [[melanoma]]).
