# Femoral Fracture
#bpt/geriatrics
- - -
### Overview of Fracture Types
| **Feature** | **Intracapsular (Subcapital / Transcervical)** | **Extracapsular (Intertrochanteric / Subtrochanteric)** |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| **Location** | Within joint capsule | Outside joint capsule |
| **Blood supply risk** | High (retinacular vessels at risk) | Lower |
| **Treatment** | Arthroplasty (hemi/total hip) if displaced; screw fixation if undisplaced | Dynamic Hip Screw (DHS) or intramedullary nail |
| **Healing mechanism** | Primary bone healing; less callus | Secondary bone healing; callus formation |
| **Risk of AVN / non-union** | High | Low |
| **Post-op mobilisation** | Early (prosthesis stable) | Slower (requires fracture healing) |
- - -
### Overview of Surgical Approaches
| **Feature** | **Intertrochanteric fracture (→ DHS)** | **Subcapital fracture (→ Hemiarthroplasty)** |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| **Location** | Between greater & lesser trochanters (extracapsular) | Just below the femoral head (intracapsular) |
| **Surgical principle** | Fixation – relies on fracture healing | Replacement – no fracture healing required |
| **Post-op stability** | Depends on fracture union | Immediate mechanical stability |
| **Pain source** | Ongoing due to fracture site | Minimal – pain source removed |
| **Mobilisation** | Slower – pain and healing limit weight bearing | Faster – early full weight bearing possible |
| **Main reason for slower recovery** | Ongoing fracture pain during healing | — |
