### Overview
A fall is an event where an individual comes to rest on the ground or other lower level _without_ known loss of consciousness (thus, this excludes falls secondary to alternative events such as seizure or stroke). 

Falls are common! In terms of risk factors:
- #1 = previous falls (OR = 6.7)
- Lower extremity weakness
- Age
- Female sex
- Balance problems
- Polypharmacy - the more drugs, the higher the risk of falls
  - [Medications associated with falls](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/485251) include:
    - Antidepressants (OR = 1.68)
    - Antipsychotics (OR = 1.59)
    - Benzodiazepines (OR = 1.57)
    - Sedatives and hypnotics (OR = 1.47)
    - Antihypertensives (OR = 1.24)
    - Diuretics (OR = 1.07)
    - β-Blockers (OR = 1.01)
    - Narcotics (OR = 0.96)
- Psychotropic drug use
- Medical comorbidities
- Orthostatic hypotension
- Dizziness
- Anaemia

While the term ‘mechanical fall’ is often used, in reality, there are almost always underlying intrinsic risk factors that have contributed to the event. 

A falls assessment should include:
- Postural BP
- Vision
- Hearing
- Feet and footwear
- Leg strength
- Gait - e.g. TUG
- Balance
- Cognition

The best intervention for preventing falls in the community is participation in a certified exercise program ([viz.](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2678103)). These have been associated with reductions in falls, injurious falls, and fractures **but not** mortality.
