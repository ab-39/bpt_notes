# Normal Ageing
#bpt/geriatrics
- - -
### Overview
From [UpToDate](https://www.uptodate.com/contents/normal-aging):
#### Haematological
The haematopoietic system tends to be able to maintain adequate function throughout the lifespan in the absence of additional insults, but the ability to compensate for said insults does decrease. 

Total circulating white cell counts should not change, but the function of these cells can become relatively impaired.

#### Renal
Kidney mass decreases by ~30% between the ages of 50-80y, with diffuse sclerosis of glomeruli taking place. Renal blood flow decreases, affecting the cortex significantly more than the medulla, and so too does creatinine clearance. Hydroxylation of vitamin D appears to decrease, while production of EPO does not.

#### Cardiovascular
The changes to the right side of the heart tend to be modest. On the left:
- Left atrium enlarges
- Left ventricle stiffens
- AV and MV thicken and calcify

Resting heart rate tends not to change, while the maximum heart rate decreases (“220 — Age”). Resting LVEF does not decline with normal ageing, but maximal LVEF attained during exercise can. 

Postural hypotension is common in the elderly, owing to impaired baroreceptor activity. Systolic blood pressure tends to rise, this increased resistance results in left ventricular hypertrophy

#### Respiratory
Effect of age on pulmonary function tests:
- FVC decreases
- FEV1 decreases
- RV increases
- TLC remains the same

Overall, there is more anatomic dead space. 

#### Neurocognitive
Aspects of memory that tend to be preserved:
- Procedural 
- Primary
- Semantic

Aspects of memory that tend to decline with normal ageing:
- Episodic 
- Executive
- Working memory 
- Attention (tends to be less uniform)

#### Endocrine
Increased HbA1c.

#### Hepatic 
Liver mass and blood flow decline with age leading to a reduction in first pass metabolism, and thus increased bioavailability of drugs.

#### GI
Normal aging reduces the propulsive capacity of the colon, however gastric and small intestinal motility are generally unaffected.
