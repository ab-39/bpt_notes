### Sarcopenia
Age-related changes in muscle include:
- Loss of fast-twitch fibres
- Loss of cross-sectional area
- Anabolic resistance
- Increased adiposity
- Reduction in velocity shortening

Sarcopenia is defined as a triad of:
- Low muscle strength -> probable sarcopenia
- Low muscle quantity or quality -> definite sarcopenia
- Low physical performance -> severe sarcopenia

Sarceponia can also go hand-in-and with cachexia and malnutrition:
- Cachexia = loss of muscle +/- fat in the setting of underlying illness (e.g. cancer, COPD, etc.)
- Malnutrition = two or more of:
  - Weight loss
  - Insufficient energy intake
  - Muscle mass loss
  - Muscle function loss
  - SC fat loss
  - Fluid accumulation

#### The SARC-F Screen:
- Strength
- Assistance in walking
- Rise from a chair
- Climb stairs
- Falls

#### Management
- Exercise
- Nutrition - aim 1.2g/kg/d of protein
There is no compelling evidence for pharmacological interventions

### Frailty
The Fried criteria can be used to quantify frailty, and diagnose frailty if more than three of the following are present:
- Decreased grip strength - weakness
- Unintentional weight loss - weight loss
- Low energy - fatigue/exhaustion
- Low physical activity 
- Slow walking speed - slowness

The most comprehensive inventory to assess frailty is the scale developed by the great Prof Kenneth Rockwood, which provides a quantification of frailty:
- 0-0.19 = non-frail
- 0.2-0.29 = pre-frail
- 0.3-0.49 = frail
- ≥0.5 = severely frail
