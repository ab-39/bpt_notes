### Overview
Haemophilia is caused by X-linked deficiencies of either factor VIII (haemophilia A) or factor IX (haemophilia B). This leads to a characteristic phenotype with spontaneous bleeding, usually into the joints or muscles _often in the absence of trauma or haemostatic challenges_. Haemophilia is graded:
- Severe = <1% of factor activity -> spontaneous bleeding into joints and muscles
- Moderate = 1-5% of factor activity -> occasional spontaneous bleeding; prolonged severe bleeding with minor trauma or surgery
- Mild = 5-40% of factor activity -> severe bleeding with major trauma or surgery; spontaneous bleeding is rare 

Recall that the role of factor VIII is to complex with factor IX to bring it to factor X as part of the intrinsic tenase complex. ***Emicizumab*** is a new, bispecific antibody that links IXa to X, allowing it to activate and resume normal clotting. It has no structural homologyto factor VIII, and so is not affected by the presence of factor VIII inhibitors. Emicizumab impairs measurement of the aPTT. 

Recurrent bleeding into the joints can lead to a chronic arthropathy.

The alternative is:
- Factor concentrate: VIII for haemophilia A; IX for haemophilia B
  - There is strong evidence from trials that regular prophylactic factor transfusions prevent bleeds 
- ddAVP -> trigger release of vWF from Weibel-Palade bodies 

Haemophilia C refers to deficiency of factor XI, but here, there is no correlation between factor levels and clinical bleeding severity.

#### Haemophilia in Women
Can occur due to:
