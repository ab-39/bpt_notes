# Essential Thrombocytosis
#bpt/haematology
- - -
### Overview
Most common mutations:
- JAK2
- CALR
- MPL

- - -
### Treatment
Aspirin is only indicated in ET if:
- Age >60y
- CVD risk factors
- _JAK2_ V617F positive

i.e. in the majority of young patients, aspirin is _not_ indicated

The optimal dose of aspirin for ET is likely 100mg BD. Aspirin therapy is _contraindicated_ if there is extreme thrombocytosis (>1500), acquired von Willebrand syndrome, or low-risk _CALR_-positive ET. aVWS can occur due to accelerated degradation of HMW vWF multimers, resulting in impaired vWF function and bleeding risk. Laboratory findings in AvWS include a prolonged aPTT, abnormal ristocetin cofactor activity, and failure to correct with mixing studies due to the qualitative defect in VWF, not a factor deficiency or inhibitor. 

Cytoreduction is indicated for:
- High-risk patients: prior thrombosis **or** >60y with _JAK2_ mutation
- CVD risk factors
- Platelets >1500
- Uncontrolled myeloproliferation
- Uncontrolled systemic symptoms

First-line cytoreduction is with hydroxyurea or interferon. Second-line options include anagrelide or busulfan. 
- - -
### Survival
Poor if:
- Age >60y at diagnosis
- Leukocytosis >11
- Thrombosis history
- Male gender
- Elevated LDH
- Various mutations - _SH2B3, SF3B1, U2AF1, TP53, IDH2, EZH2_
