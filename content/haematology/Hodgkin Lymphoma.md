# Hodgkin Lymphoma
#bpt/haematology
- - -
### Overview
Hodgkin lymphoma represents approximately 10% of all lymphomas, and is characterised by the presence of morphologically distinctive, CD30+ Reed-Sternberg cells. The disease course is often more predictable than non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and involves arising in a single node/chain of nodes, with subsequent spread to contiguous tissues. The WHO recognises five subtypes of HL:
1. Nodular sclerosing (most common)
2. Mixed cellularity 
3. Lymphocyte-rich
4. Lymphocyte-deplete
5. Lymphocyte predominant (very rare)

### Epidemiology
Bimodal distribution: peak in young adults and then again over the age of 55y. High rates among siblings, particularly monozygotic twins. 
- - -
### Clinical Presentation
Like non-Hodgkin lymphoma, the presentation is commonly with a mobile, rubbery lymphadenopathy, typically above the diaphragm. Less commonly, the symptoms may relate to mass effect from bulky lymph nodes. The ‘classic’ systemic symptoms of lymphoma are the B-type symptoms:
- Weight loss (>10% of BW)
- Fever - although a classic association, the cyclical Pel-Ebstein fevers are actually quite rare
- Night sweats
- - -
### Immunophenotype
Despite being a B-cell lymphoma, CD20-positivity is usually absent. Instead, Hodgkin lymphoma usually expresses:
- CD15
- CD30
- TARC
- MUM1
- - -
### Pathogenesis
The majority of cancerous cells are derived from germinal centre or post-germinal centre B-cells, but typically do not resemble their normal cellular counterparts morphologically or immunophenotypically.

### Treatment
There are two main protocols used in the treatment of Hodgkin lymphoma:
- ABVD = doxorubicin/bleomycin/vinblastine/dacarbazine
  - High cure rates:
    - Up to 95% in early stage disease (+ radiotherapy)
    - Up to 70% in advanced disease
- Escalated BEACOPP = bleomycin/etoposide/doxorubicin/cyclophosphamide/vincristine/procarbazine/prednisolone
  - Cures more, but much more intensive
  - Generally not used in older patients
  - More infertility, menopause, secondary MDS/AML, etc.

The decision of which protocol to follow is generally based on performance status, staging PET, and the presence of unfavourable factors:
- Early stage + no unfavourable factors -> ABVD
- Early stage + unfavourable factors -> ABVD or EscBEACOPP
- Advanced stage -> ABVD or EscBEACOPP

Usually, after completing two cycles of either treatment, the patient will undergo an interim PET to guide further treatment.

Unfavourable factors include:
- Bulky mediastrinum
- ≥4 nodal areas involved
- B-symptoms
- Age >50y
- Elevated ESR

There is new evidence from the [_ECHELON_ trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1708984) that a regimen consisting of doxorubicin/brentuximab-vedotin/vinblastine/dacarbazine (A+AVD) provides almost a year longer PFS when compared to ABVD, but remains prohibitively expensive, and is limited to the relapsed setting. Moreover, there is emerging evidence for nivolumab+AVD improving PFS as well.
