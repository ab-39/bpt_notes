# Paroxysmal Nocturnal Haemoglobinuria
#bpt/haematology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
PNH is caused by acquired somatic mutations in the _PIGA_ gene in multipotent haemotopoietic stem cells. This leads to an inability to synthesise the GPI anchor that attaches proteins (importantly, CD55 and CD59) to the red cell surface. Because of this, there is an absence of complement inhibitors on the cell surface, leading to unopposed complement activation and complement mediated haemolysis

This haemolysis occurs in both the intravascular and extravascular spaces, and the intravascular component is characterised by an increase in free serum and urine haemoglobin. 

Additional features of the disease can include aplastic anaemia, atypical thrombi, and smooth muscle dystonia.

Diagnosis is with flow cytometry looking for CD55 and CD59, which are GPI-linked proteins. 

Like aytpical [[haemolytic uraemic syndrome]], PNH can be treated with C5-inhibitors such as eculizumab. Another novel treatment option is to use [Pegcetacoplan](https://www.uptodate.com/contents/pegcetacoplan-drug-information?search=haematology&topicRef=7163&source=see_link) which is a pegylated peptide that targets the proximal complement protein, C3, and can inhibit BOTH intravascular and extravascular hemolysis. Whereas [ravulizumab](https://www.uptodate.com/contents/ravulizumab-drug-information?search=haematology&topicRef=7163&source=see_link) and [eculizumab,](https://www.uptodate.com/contents/eculizumab-drug-information?search=haematology&topicRef=7163&source=see_link)targets C5, and therefore affects only intravascular hemolysis.
