# Sézary Syndrome
#bpt/haematology
- - -
### Overview
* One of the two major subtypes of cutaneous T-cell lymphoma (along with mycosis fungoides), characterised by the presence of CD4+ T-cells that home to the skin
* This homing is driven by the adhesion molecule *cutaneous lymphocyte antigen* that is expressed on tumour cells, as well as the chemokine receptors CCR4 and CCR10
* Rare: incidence of approx. 0.8-0.9 cases per million persons per year (US SEER, Norwegian Registry), does not appear to be a familial/hereditary component
- - -
### Clinical Features
Skin:
* Widespread erythema (top right), accompanied by palmar and plantar hyperkeratosis
* Often *intensely* itchy
* In mycosis fungoides, often follow a progression from premycotic patches → plaques (top left image) → tumour
* Alopecia is common

⠀Extracutaneous - more common in SS than MF:
* SS characterised by leukaemia of Sézary cells, characterised by a *cerebriform* nucleus 
* Lymphadenopathy is common, while visceral involvement is typically less common

⠀Diagnosis of SS typically requires widespread erythroderma (>80% BSA), confirmatory biopsy, and evidence of leukaemic dissemination.
- - -
### Management
Topical therapies - e.g. topical corticosteroids, bexarotene gel, nitrogen mustard gel, etc. often used in early stage disease.
Systemic therapies:
* For patients *without* visceral involvement (IVa) → extracorporeal photophoresis (ECP) or mogamulizumab (an anti-CCR4 antibody, ADRs include myositis and rash, *MAVORIC*)
* For patients *with* visceral involvement (IVb) → cytotoxic chemotherapy (e.g. doxorubicin, gemcitabine), with the possibility of curative alloHCST in patients who respond well to initial therapy or have high risk disease (e.g. aggressive, multiple relapses)

⠀Generally indolent with a good survival (mOS = 4y), however there is a risk of relapse and of transformation to aggressive T-cell lymphoma.
