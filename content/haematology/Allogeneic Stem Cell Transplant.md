### Overview
Allogeneic stem cell transplantation (ASCT) refers to treatment of a patient (the transplant recipient) with a preparative (conditioning) regimen, followed by an infusion of haematopoietic stem/progenitor donor-derived cells. The specific terminology used to describe the transplant depends on the relationship of the donor to the host:
- _Syngeneic_ -> from an identical twin
- _Matched donor_ -> from an individual matched at HLA-A, -B, and -DR, who can be:
  - Related - usually a sibling
  - Unrelated - MUD 
- _Haploidentical donor_ -> from an individual mismatched at HLA-A, -B, and -DR, typically a parent
- _Umbilical cord blood_ 

The extent of HLA mismatching, particularly at the important loci A (+C), B, and DR increase the risk of post-transplant graft-vs-host disease. Pre-treatment with ATG decreases the incidence of chronic GVHD at one year after transplantation, without adversely affecting rates of disease relapse, serious infection, or overall survival.

Unlike [[autologous stem cell transplantation]], where the primary benefit arises from the administration of high-chemotherapy, ASCT has a few additional benefits:
- _Graft-vs-tumour_ effect - the allogeneic immune response to eradicate residual disease to prevent relapse (e.g. in AML, ALL)
- Replacement of the defective marrow system (e.g. in MDS, aplastic anaemia)
- Replacement of the defective immune system (e.g. in SCID)
- Allowing for the administration of high-dose immunosuppression

Extensive trial data supports the survival benefit from ASCT.

### Indications
Indications include:
- AML
- ALL
- Myelodysplasia
- Severe aplastic anaemia
- Immunodeficiency - SCID
- NHL
- Myeloma
- CML
- Haematological disorders - e.g. thalassaemia, SCD
- Autoimmune disease

### Complications
Phase I = pre-engraftment
- Host immune system = neutropenia
  - Gram-negative bacteria
  - Gram-positive bacteria
  - Candida
  - Aspergillus 
  - HSV
  - Heart failure
  - Acute GvHD - most commonly skin (#1), gut, and liver:
    - Grade A = skin alone, <25% BSA
    - Grade B = skin >25% BSA + mild gut or liver involvement
    - Grade C = significant involvement of any organ system
    - Grade D = stage IV involvement of any organ system 

Phase II = post-engraftment 
- Host immune system = impaired cellular immunity
  - Gram-positive bateria
  - Multiple vital infections
  - Hepatic SOS
  - Diffuse alveolar haemorrhage
  - COP
  - Acute GvHD

Phase III = late post-engraftment
- Host immune sustem = impaired cellular and humoral immunity
  - Encapsulated bacteria
  - Nocardia
  - Aspergillus
  - Pneumocystis
  - HSV
  - CMV
  - PTLD
  - COP
  - Chronic GvHD
