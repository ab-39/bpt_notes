# Polycythaemia Vera
#bpt/haematology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Diagnosis
#### Major Criteria
1. Haemoglobin and haematocrit:
   - Hb >165g/L or Hct >49% (males) 
   - Hb >160g/L or Hct >48% (females)
2. Bone marrow biopsy - particular utility when the patient may have comorbid iron deficiency ‘masking’ their PRV: 
   - Hypercellularity
   - Panmyelosis - i.e. _all_ lineages are increased
   - Pleomorphic megakaryocytes 
3. Mutations:
   - _JAK2_ V617F
   - _JAK2_ exon 12

#### Minor Criterion
= subnormal EPO level 
- - -
### Complications
#### Thrombotic Complications
Tend to be more common, arising due to:
- Hyperviscosity - headache, blurred vision, plethora
- Thrombosis of the large vessels - stroke, AMI, DVT, PE, splanchnic thrombosis, etc.
- Thrombosis of the small vessels - cyanosis, erythromelalgia, ulceration or gangrene in the fingers and toes 

#### Bleeding Complications
Tend to be less common, affecting ~10% of patients:
- GI bleeding
- Epistaxis
- Bruising
- Gum bleeding
- Bleeding during procedures 
- Acquired von Willebrand syndrome
Highest risk in PMF
- - -
### Survival
Poorer prognosis if:
- Advanced age
- WCC >10.5
- History of thrombosis
- Abnormal karyotype
- _SRSF2_ mutation
- - -
### Treatment
All patients should receive venesection to target haematocrit <0.45 (_CYTO-PV_ study) and low-dose aspirin once daily. The goal of treatment is to induce iron deficiency. Treatment after this is based on risk stratification:
- Low-risk = no thrombosis history **and** age ≤60y
  - Venesection + aspirin
  - Consider BD aspirin if persistent microvascular symptoms, cardiovascular risk factors, HTN, or leukocytosis
- High-risk = thrombosis history **or** age >60y
  - Cytoreduction with hydroxyurea or interferon
    - Interferon tends to be highly effective but not curative, and is often poorly tolerated (and can cause liver and thyroid dysfunction)
    - Second-line: 
      - Busulfan (elderly)
      - Ruxolitinib (off-label) - _RESPONSE_ trial found superior control of haematocrit, achieving haematological response, and improvement in symptoms when compared to hydroxyurea 
  - BD aspirin if arterial thrombosis 
  - VKA/DOAC if venous thrombosis - level II data 

Other indications for cytoreduction include:
- Poor tolerance of phlebotomy
- Plts >1500
- WCC >15
- Uncontrolled myeloproliferation - e.g. increasing splenectomy
- Uncontrolled PV-related synmptoms 

Risk factors for thrombosis in PRV include:
- Prior thrombosis
- Age >60y
- Leucocyte count >15
- CVD risk factors 
- HTN -> ACE-inhibitors treatment of choice, may reduce Hb
- Dyslipidaemia -> statins
