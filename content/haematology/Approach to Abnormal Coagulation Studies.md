# Approach to Abnormal Coagulation Studies
#bpt/haematology
- - -
### Overview

> [!WARNING] Is there a thrombus?
> If there are abnormal coagulation studies (prolonged PT/aPTT) **_and_** thrombosis present, this is likely pointing in the direction of critical differentials:
> - DIC
> - Antiphospholipid syndrome
> - Heparin induced thrombocytopenia
> -> call a haematologist urgently!

#### Step #1
Take a history - the cause of deranged coagulation studies is most often apparent from the history - e.g. liver disease, being on anticoagulants, etc.

If the cause is _not_ apparent, proceed to:

#### Step #2 
Assess which arm of the cascade is disordered:
| PT and aPTT                | Only PT                   | Only aPTT                 |
|----------------------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| Proceed to a thrombin time | Proceed to mixing studies | Proceed to mixing studies |

#### Step #3
What is the thrombin time?
- TT prolonged -> test for disorders of fibrinogen
- TT normal -> likely a disorder of the common pathway

> [!NOTE] Mixing Studies
> Mixing studies involve a 50/50 mix of the patient’s plasma with control plasma. The way the tests are calibrated, it only requires 50% of factor activity to achieve normal PT/aPTT results. This means there are two possible outcomes:
> - Abnormality corrects with mixing -> factor deficiency
> - Abnormality does _not_ correct with mixing -> presence of inhibitors 

Prolonged PT -> mixing studies:
- Not corrected -> reduced FVII activity -> FVII inhibitor present
- Corrected -> reduced FVII activity -> FVII deficiency

Prolonged aPTT -> mixing studies:
- Corrected -> problem with one of:
  - Factor VIII (haemophilia A)
  - Factor IX (haemophilia B)
  - Factor XI
  - Factor XII
  - Von Willebrand Factor 
- Not corrected -> first ensure no heparin, if not -> inhibitor present:
  - Inhibition after prolonged incubation -> Factor VIII (time-dependent inhibitor)
  - Inhibition after short incubation (<5min) -> Factor VIII, IX, or XI
  - Antiphospholipid antibodies
