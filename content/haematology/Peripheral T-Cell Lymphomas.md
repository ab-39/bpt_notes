# Peripheral T-Cell Lymphomas
#bpt/haematology
- - -
### Overview
- A very heterogeneous group of disorders - >15 subtypes despite accounting for ~10% of all lymphomas
  - Tend to have diverse clinical presentations compared with B-cell lymphomas - e.g. cutaneous (mycosis fungoides/[[Sézary Syndrome]]), hepatosplenomegaly, eosinophilia, etc.)
- Tend to be aggressive malignancies of mature (CD3+) T-cells
  - Derive from post-thymic T-cells -> do _not_ express TDT or CD1
  - Precursor T-neoplasm = T-lymphoblastic lymphoma
- Share some immunophenotypic and functional characteristics with CD3- NK-cell neoplasms, hence often grouped together
- Poor prognosis compared to DL-BCL
- In general, prognosis is quite poor, with a 5y mOS of just 32%. 
  - The exception is anaplastic large cell lymphoma (either ALK+ or ALK-)
- Treatment:
  - CHOP is the standard treatment, despite not being that effective
  - If CD30+ (particularly ALCL) -> brentuximab-vedotin:
    - Anti-CD30 mAb + MMAE (tubulin toxin) -> internalised -> cell cycle arrest between G2 and M phase 
    - High risk of peripheral neuropathies
