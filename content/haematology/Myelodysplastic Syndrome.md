### Overview
The myelodysplastic syndromes are a group of disorders characterised by:
- Low blood counts - cytopenias of multiple lineages 
- Dysplasia - i.e. abnormal looking blood cells
- Ineffective haematopoiesis 
- A risk of progression to AML
They are associated with a shortened life span, which is determined using the IPSS score, a composite of:
- % blasts
- Karyotype
- Cytopenias
High risk patients have an expected survival of less than one year. 

Note that chronic monomyelocytic leukaemia is actually an MDS/MPN overlap syndrome. 

Analogous to MGUS, ‘clonal haematopoiesis of indeterminate potential’ (or CHIP) is the precursor state to MDS. The most commonly implicated genes in CHIP are _DNMT3A_, _TET2_, _ASXL1_ . The rate of progression to MDS or AML is about 1% per year, and CHIP itself is associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality, typically from cardiovascular causes. 

Diagnosis is similar to [[acute myeloid leukaemia]] and requires a bone marrow biopsy. The key questions are:
- Which cell lineage/s are dysplastic?
- How much dysplasia is present within each lineage?
- What is the percentage of blasts? (If >20%, this is AML)
- What are the cytogenetics and molecular markers?

It is important to remember that there are other causes of cytopenias and dysplasia:
- B12/Folate deficiency 
- Copper deficiency (often due to excess zinc)
- Alcohol
- Heavy metals
- Medications - TAC, MYC, TMP-SMX, SVP, GCSF
- Infections - parvovirus B19

Key mutations include:
- del5q
- _SF3B1_ - ring sideroblasts
- _TP53_ - poor prognosis 

Management depends on the patient’s IPSS risk status:
- Low risk - often an elderly population;  prioritise QoL with supportive treatments: RBCs, platelets, TXA; iron chelation if overloaded
- High risk - active treatment: bone marrow transplant (in younger patients, curative), azacitidine 
- Targeted treatments:
  - del5q - lenalidomide
  - SF3B1 - luspatercept 

Specific syndromes:
- del5q syndrome:
  - Characterised by anaemia, thrombocythaemia, atypical marrow megakaryocytic, and an indolent clinical course 
  - Note the single eccentric nucleus in the megakaryocyte ![](Myelodysplastic%20Syndrome/image.png)
  - Responsive to lenalidomide
