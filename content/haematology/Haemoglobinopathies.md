### Overview
The haemoglobinopathies are characterised by quantiative or qualitative defects in the production of haemoglobin, leading to a typically [[microcytic anaemia]]. 
- Quantitative disorders -> ⍺ thalassaemia, β thalassaemia
- Qualitative disoders -> HbS, HbE, HbD, etc.

These tend to be inherited in an autosomal recessive fashion, affecting 7% of the world population. 

“Normal” haemoglobin usually consists of a majority of HbA (ααββ) with a minority of HbF (ααγγ):

### ⍺-Globin
The genes encoding ⍺-globin are situated on chromosome 16, and each chromosome has _two_ copies of the gene on it, making inheritance more complex. 
- - -
### β-Globin
The gene encoding β-globin is on chromosome 11.

### Diagnostic Methods
FBE:
- MCV and MCH are generally low even in carriers
- This is combined with an elevated RCC (disproportionate microcytosis, as opposed to IDA where there is both microcytosis and few RBCs)
- Reticulocyte count
- Blood film

Ordering Fe studies to exclude [[iron deficiency anaemia]] is also important at this step.

Haemoglobins:
- β-trait -> HbA2 elevated
- ⍺-trait -> HbH sometimes elevated
- Sickle cell trait -> HbS elevated, abnormal bands on EPG
- Other variants (e.g. HbE) -> variant detected

Measurement of haemoglobins - any two of:
- Electrophoresis (EPG/CE)
- Chromatography (HPLC)

[[Genetic testing]] can also be useful here - e.g. MLPA.
