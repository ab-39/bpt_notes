# Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma
#bpt/haematology
- - -
### Overview
The non-Hodgkin lymphomas comprise the vast majority of lymphomas, accounting for approximately 90% of cases. The term ‘non-Hodgkin lymphoma’ encompasses diverse disease entities, including the B-cell lymphomas (~90% of NHL):
- Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma
- [[Follicular lymphoma]]
- Burkitt lymphoma
- Marginal-zone lymphoma
- Mantle-cell lymphoma
- Lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma
- Small lymphocytic lymphoma (the lymphoma equivalent of chronic lymphocytic leukaemia)

And the T-cell lymphomas (~10% of NHL):
- Mycosis fungoides/[[Sézary Syndrome|Sézary syndrome]]
- Peripheral T-cell lymphomas
- NK/T-cell lymphoma

Together, DLBCL and follicular lymphoma account for approximately half of all cases of lymphoma. 
- - -
### Clinical Presentation
Like Hodgkin lymphoma, the presentation is commonly with a mobile, rubbery lymphadenopathy. Less commonly, the symptoms may relate to mass effect from bulky lymph nodes. The ‘classic’ systemic symptoms of lymphoma are the B-type symptoms:
- Weight loss (>10% of BW)
- Fever 
- Night sweats
- - -
### Classification
Tends to be on the basis of aggression and cure rate:
- Indolent/low grade NHL -> incurable
  - e.g. follocular, MALT, marginal zone
- Aggressive/intermediate NHL -> >50% cure rate
  - e.g. DLBCL, most peripheral T-cell lymphomas
  - Mantle cell halfway between indolent and aggressive as it is difficult to cure but has a more aggressive disease course
- Highly aggressive/high grade NHL -> >70% cure 
  - e.g. Burkitt lymphoma, T-lymphoblastic lymphoma

### Staging
#### Traditional
The Ann Arbor method stages disease according to how easily it can be targeted by radiotherapy, i.e.:
- Stage I = confined to a single group of nodes
- Stage II = within multiple groups of nodes on the same side of the diaphragm
- Stage III = on both sides of the diaphragm
- Stage IV = disseminated extranodal involvement

#### Current
The Lugano staging criteria is sufficiently sensitive to obviate the need for a bone marrow biopsy in patients with DLBCL (and Hodgkin lymphoma) uses PET-CT to stage lymphoma. It is scored using the Deauville score:
- Stage I = no uptake
- Stage II = less uptake than the mediastinum
- Stage III = less uptake than the liver, but more than the mediastinum
- Stage IV = more uptake than the liver
- Stage V = markedly more uptake than the liver +/- new lesions
- X = uptake unlikely to be related to lymphoma

### Treatment
Regimens for B-cell lymphomas generally consist of:
1. Anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody, e.g.:
   - Rituximab
   - Obinutuzumab
**plus**:
2. Chemotherapy, one of:
   - Bendamustine
   - CVP = cyclophosphamide/vincristine/prednisolone
   - CHOP = CVP + doxorubicin
