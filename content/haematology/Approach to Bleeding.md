### Overview
In patients with a congenital or acquired bleeding disorder, the _pattern_ of bleeding can give clues to the underlying aetiology
|                                          | Platelet-type                                                | Clotting Factor-type                            |
|------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| *Site*                                   | Skin, mucous membranes<br>(e.g. epistaxis, gums, vaginal,<br>GIT, dental extractions, etc.) | Deep in soft tissues:<br>**Joints** and muscles |
| *Skin cuts lead to:*                     | Prolonged bleeding                                           | Minimal bleeding                                |
| *Petechiae*                              | Common                                                       | Rare                                            |
| *Ecchymoses*                             | Small, superficial                                           | Large, deep<br>Often haematomas                 |
| *Haemarthroses and*<br>*muscle bleeding* | Extremely rare                                               | Common                                          |
| *Bleeding after*<br>*surgery*            | Immediate and usually mild                                   | Delayed (1-3 days) and<br>often severe          |

Abnormalities on clotting tests are usually not enough on their own to predict significant bleeding after surgery. What is useful, however, is a closely taken history. Validated tools such as the *[HEMSTOP](https://practical-haemostasis.com/Clinical%20Prediction%20Scores/Formulae%20code%20and%20formulae/Formulae/Bleeding-Risk-Assessment-Score/HEMSTOP.html)* score can be used to streamline this process.
