### DOACs
![](Anticoagulant%20Reversal/image.png)
Step #1 = resuscitate appropriately

Step #2 = determine time of last DOAC dose. In combination with the half life, this will help to determine the extent to which there is still circulating drug:
| DOAC        | Tmax   | Half Life (normal CrCl) | Half Life (CrCl <50mL/min) | Clearance |
|-------------|--------|-------------------------|----------------------------|-----------|
| Apixaban    | 3h     | 8-12h                   | 8-12h                      | 25% renal |
| Rivaroxaban | 2.5-4h | 7-17h                   | 17-20h                     | 33% renal |
| Dabigatran  | 2h     | 7-11h                   | 7-11h                      | 80% renal |
Note that this is in contrast to warfarin that is predominantly cleared hepatically. 

Step #3 = routine coagulation studies as these may be prolonged by DOACs. Additionally, need to order the ***specific Anti-Xa assay*** for the given drug:
| DOAC        | Effect on Routine Coagulation Studies                        | Specific Test                |
|-------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Apixaban    | aPTT and PT usually normal<br>dRVVT prolonged                | Apixaban anti-Xa             |
| Rivaroxaban | PT may be prolonged<br>aPTT usually normal<br>dRVVT prolonged | Rivaroxaban anti-Xa          |
| Dabigatran  | aPTT prolonged<br>PT usually normal<br>TT prolonged<br>dRVVT prolonged  | Dilue TT<br>Dabigatran level |

Subsequent interventions depend on the extent of the bleeding:
- Mild bleeding -> local haemostatic measures; delay or discontinue DOAC
- Clinically significant bleeding (Hb drop >20g/L) **but** haemodynamically stable:
  - Intervention as required (e.g. embolisation)
  - Activated charcoal if DOAC ingested <2h
  - Local haemostatic support
  - Transfusion support
- Clinically significant bleeding and haemodynamically unstable:
  - As above
  - Intervention as required
  - Consider specific reversal agent if available
  - If no drug specific agent available:
    - Four-factor concentrate 25-50iu/kg **or** 2000iu, or
    - FEIBA 25-50iu/kg

#### Nonspecific Reversal Agents
##### Prothrombin Complex Concentrate
Four-factor concentrate containing factors II, VII, IX, and X, as well as small quantities of proteins C and S. High-level evidence for successful reversal of DOACs. 

#### Ciraparantag
Claimed to reverse multiple anticoagulants, PER977 is a small synthetic cationic molecule that non-covalently binds hydrogen bonds. Passed phase I studies, but remains in phase II. 

#### Specific Reversal Agents
##### Dabigatran = Idarucizumab
Idarucizumab is generally only indicated if the patient is undergoing uncontrollable bleeding or requires emergency surgery with a prolonged thrombin time (or clinical evidence of intracranial bleeding). Administration of idarucizumab should be followed by repeat tests, and consideration of another dose at 24h if required. The median time-to-haemostasis after administration of idarucizumab is ~2.5h despite the fact that plasma levels drop almost instantaneously. This highlights the importance of ongoing supportive management and local haemostatic measures. ~5% of patients will develop a thrombosis within 30d of reversal. 

##### Apixaban/Rivaroxaban = Andexanet Alfa
Andexanet alfa is a ‘decoy’ molecule for factor Xa inhibitors with some key structural changes (S419A in particular) that prevent it from subsequently activating prothrombin. It also cannot be incorporated into the prothrombinase complex. Can be administered at a low dose (for low-dose DOAC) or a high-dose (for regular dose DOAC). This leads to a time-limited reversal of DOACs that lasts for ~2h after completion of the infusion.
