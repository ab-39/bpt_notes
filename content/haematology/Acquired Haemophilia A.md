# Acquired Haemophilia A
#bpt/haematology
- - -
### Overview
Acquired haemophilia A is the development of a clotting factor deficiency that was not present at birth, usually due to an autoantibody against factor VIII. This presents with a significant bleeding phenotype similar to haemophilia: usually into skin, muscles, soft tissues, and mucous membranes.

In the lab, acquired haemophilia A is characterised by:
- Normal platelet count
- Normal PT
- Prolonged aPTT which does not correct with mixing
- Low levels of FVIII
- Presence of inhibitors (quantify using _Bethesda units_ -> the amount of plasma required to counteract the inhibitor)

Treatment is twofold:
1. Treat the bleeding:
   1. Recombinant activated factor VII
   2. Activated prothrombin complex concentrates (e.g. FEIBA = factor VIII inhibitor bypassing activity)
2. Treat the immune response:
   1. Cyclophosphamide
   2. Rituximab
   3. IVIg
