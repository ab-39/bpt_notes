### Overview
From ChatGPT:
| **Bias**                               | **Definition / Mechanism**                                   | **Clinical Example**                                         | **Notes from Studies**                                       |
|:--------------------------------------:|:------------------------------------------------------------:|:------------------------------------------------------------:|:------------------------------------------------------------:|
| **Framing Effect**                     | Decisions influenced by how information is presented         | Same lab results interpreted differently depending on wording | Tversky & Kahneman: classic framing effect in risk/benefit choices |
| **Confirmation Bias**                  | Seeking or interpreting information to confirm prior beliefs | Ordering only tests that support your initial diagnosis      | Croskerry 2003: major contributor to diagnostic error        |
| **Availability Bias**                  | Decisions influenced by what comes to mind easily or recent experiences | Diagnosing common disease without considering rare ones      | Mamede 2010: recent patient cases skew diagnostic decisions  |
| **Premature Closure**                  | Accepting first plausible diagnosis and stopping further reasoning | Concluding pneumonia based on CXR without considering PE     | Graber 2005: up to 30–50% of diagnostic errors involve premature closure |
| **Attribution Bias**                   | Judging patient behavior based on stereotypes or personal beliefs | Blaming patient for non-adherence or lifestyle               | Can affect empathy and treatment decisions                   |
| **Bandwagon Effect**                   | Following decisions because others do                        | Prescribing antibiotics because colleagues do                | Seen in guideline adoption studies; can reinforce non-evidence-based practice |
| **Anchoring Bias**                     | Relying too heavily on first piece of information            | Initial vital signs lead to diagnosis even after conflicting labs | Croskerry 2003: strong contributor to diagnostic error       |
| **Overconfidence Bias**                | Overestimating accuracy of one’s knowledge or diagnosis      | Not seeking second opinion for uncertain diagnosis           | Linked to increased diagnostic error risk                    |
| **Search Satisfying / Detection Bias** | Stopping search after finding an abnormality                 | Finding a cyst on imaging, missing coexisting tumor          | Radiology and pathology studies highlight this               |
| **Outcome Bias**                       | Judging decision quality based on outcome rather than process | Thinking treatment was correct because patient improved      | Studied in surgical and clinical decision-making             |
| **Hindsight Bias**                     | Overestimating predictability after outcome is known         | “It was obvious it was a PE after seeing the CT”             | Common in morbidity & mortality reviews                      |
| **Representativeness Bias**            | Matching patient to a typical disease pattern, ignoring base rates | Assuming young patient can’t have MI                         | Studies in internal medicine show misdiagnosis of atypical presentations |
| Performance bias:                      | Systematic differences in care provided to participants in different groups, apart from the intervention itself. |                                                              |                                                              |
