# Nucleic Acid Therapies
#bpt/genetics #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Nucleic acid therapies fall into two main categories:
1. Therapies that modify or silence endogenous genetic information using complementary base-pairing, e.g.:
   - ASO Gapmers
   - siRNA therapies
   - ASO steric blockers
2. Therapies that supply exogenous information, e.g.:
   - mRNA vaccines
   - mRNA cancer vaccines
   - mRNA therapeutics
   - Immunostimulation
- - -
### Antisense Oligonucleotide Gapmers
These are small sections of DNA flanked by segments of RNA that are able to bind to mRNA in the target cell nucleus. These are then recognised by RNAse H and degraded. An example of this in treatment is _tofersen_, a intrathecal _SOD1_ ASO gapmer that is used in the treatment of [[amyotrophic lateral sclerosis]]. Approximately 2% of MND cases are caused by gain of functino mutations in _SOD1_, that lead to accumulations of toxic protein, and treatment with tofersen has been found to improve biomarkers and slow functional decline scores. 
- - -
### Short Interfering RNA 
These are small sections of dsRNA that silence endogenous gain of function mutations. Once delivered into the cytoplasm via lipid nanoparticles, these are incorporated into _RNA-induced silencing complexes_, where the guide-strand is able to bind to the target mRNA and mark it for cleavage by the RISC. One example of siRNA is _inclisiran_, a treatment for familial hypercholesterolaemia that binds and degrades the mRNA that is translated into PCSK9, thus inhibiting degradation of LDL-receptors, allowing them to be recycled and remove more LDL-C from the bloodstream. Another example is _patisiran_ in the treatment of TTR [[renal/Amyloidosis|amyloidosis]]. 
- - -
### Antisense Oligonucleotide Steric Blockers
Unlike ASO gapmers, ASO steric blockers do not trigger cleavage, but simply block the relevant mRNA from being translated into protein. Alternatively, these can also encourage or inhibit the splicing of specific exons when mRNA is in the process of being refined prior to translation.

The best established example of this is _nusinersen_ for spinal muscular atrophy. Nusinersen is used to prevent the splicing of exon 7 from the _SMN2_ gene that makes it (essentially) a less effective variant of _SMN1_ (the deficient gene in SMA). 

Steric blockers can have the opposite effect in the treatment of Duchenne muscular dystrophy, one of the [[neurology/Dystrophinopathies|dystrophinopathies]]. Here, DMD is caused by out of frame deletions in exon 51 of the _dytrophin_ gene that cause premature stop codons that inhibit production of dystrophin. _Eteplirsen_ is able to bind to this site in exon 51 and force the entire exon to be excluded. This causes the production of a shortened but still functional variant of dystrophin, which can slow the decline of muscle function in DMD.

### Supplying Exogenous Information
#### mRNA Vaccines 
Most famously, the Pfizer _tozinameran_ (Comirnaty) vaccine against COVID-19 is an mRNA vaccine, which caused the antigenic spike protein to be translated and thus recognised by the immune system.

#### mRNA Cancer Vaccines
Can be targeted to several cancer specific antigens, similar to CAR-T or BiTE therapy in the sense that they prime the immune system itself to attack the cancer.

#### mRNA Therapies
Aim to induce the expression of proteins that themselves are therapeutic in effect, e.g. inhaled mRNA for CFTR.
