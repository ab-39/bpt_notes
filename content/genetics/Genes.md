### Overview
There are over 20,000 human genes, with thousands per chromosome. Most genes, but not all, encode for the production of a protein, and depending on the complexity of that protein, the underlying gene can range from a few hundred to many thousand base pairs long.

**Exons** = sections of a gene that are transcribed into mature mRNA, which can go on to encode amino acids making up a protein. Not _all_ parts of the exon encode proteins, there are 3’ and 5’ non-coding/untranslated regions which do have clinical relevance (e.g. triplet expansion regions in DMD and FXTAS). 

**Introns** = sections of DNA in genes that arae spliced out of the final RNA transcript. May not encode amino acids, but can affect gene expression.

**Promoters** = sections of gene that direct transcription.
