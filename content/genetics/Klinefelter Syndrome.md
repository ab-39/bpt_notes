# Klinefelter Syndrome
#bpt/endocrinology #bpt/genetics

### Overview
Klinefelter (47;XXY) syndrome is characterised by supernumerary X chromosomes, leading to reduced testosterone production and secondary loss of inhibition of FH and LSH. 

In adults, this is characterised by infertility and signs of androgen deficiency (e.g. gynecomastia, sexual dysfunction, or osteoporosis), as well as the cardinal finding of small, firm testes. Semen analysis reveals azospermia (and consequent infertility). Commonly associated conditoins include:
- Type 2 diabetes
- Osteoporosis
- Thyroid dysfunction
- Restrictive pulmonary disease
- Breast cancer

Definitive diagnosis is with karyotyping (where the cell cycle is arrested with colchicine in metaphase). Management is typically with TRT. If sperm are present, ICSI can be used to facilitate fertility, although the risk of chromosomal abnormalities in offspring is elevated.
