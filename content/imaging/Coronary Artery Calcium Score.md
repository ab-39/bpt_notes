### Overview
Fundamentally, there are three types of atherosclerotic plaques at play in coronary arteries:
- Calcified ‘hard’ plaques
- Mixed plaques
- Fibrotic ‘soft’ plaques

Unlike CTCA, CACS is a non-contrast CT of the heart that is looking for ‘hard’ plaques. The total amount of plaque visible is used to generate a calcium score that has significant diagnostic and prognostic value. 

#### Cui Bono?
In general, CACS is used for _asymptomatic_ screening:
- Patients aged 45-75y with intermediate cardiovascular risk
- Individuals aged 40-75y with lower cardiovascular risk but specific risk factors:
  - Diabetes
  - Family history of premature CHD
  - Indigeneity 

CACS is _not_ recommended for patients who are:
- At very low risk (<5% absolute 10y risk)
- High risk (>20%) as testing is unlikely to alter the recommended management 9e.g. T2DM, CKD, FH, etc.)
- Symptomatic or previously documented CAD 

#### Interpreting and Acting on the CACS
| Score                         | Interpretation                           | Recommended Action                                      |
|-------------------------------|------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| 0                             | Very low (<1%) risk of MACE/death at 10y | Diet and lifestyle                                      |
| 1-100                         | Low (<10%) risk                          | Diet and lifestyle                                      |
| 101-400                       | Intermediate (10-20%) risk               | Aspirin + statin                                        |
| 101-400 **and** >75th centile | Moderately high (15-20%) risk            | Aspirin + statin                                        |
| >400                          | High (>20%) risk                         | Aspirin + statin to target LDL <2.0mmol/L + stress test |
