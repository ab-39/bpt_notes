### Overview
Malaria is an ancient disease caused by parasitic amoebae of the genus _Plasmodium_ that infects erythrocytes. These can belong to many species, with the most common including: 
- _Plasmodium falciparum_
- _Plasmodium vivax_
- _Plasmodium malariae_
- _Plasmodium knowlesi_ - a macaque malarial parasite typically only encountered in forested parts of South East Asia 
- _Plasmodium ovale_

While efforts at control have substantially reduced cases in the last twenty years, malaria still causes over 200 million infections each year, predominantly in subsaharan Africa, but also in Mesoamerica, South Asia, and Indochina.

### Lifecycle 
![](Malaria/image.png)
1. _Sporozoites_ - the female mosquito (genus _Aenophilus_) injects the parasite into the bloodstream of a human
2. Human liver stage - the sporozoites invade liver cells where they replicate and mature into _schizonts_; this is typically asymptomatic
   - _P. vivax_ and _ovale_ are the most prone to lying dormant in the liver -> require additional treatment to eradicate hypnozoites 
3. Human blood cycle - the symptomatic phase; schizonts rupture after 6-30 days, releasing hundreds of _merozoites_ into the bloodstream where they infect red blood cells, and:
   - Mature into schizonts
   - Rupture, destroying the red cells
   - Release hundreds of merozoites
   - Repeat the cycle
4. Sexual stage - a small proportion of merozoites mature into gametocytes which are then taken up by a female mosquito while feeding
5. Mosquito early and late stages - maturation of malarial parasites ultimately resulting in their being injected as sporozoites back into a human

#### Dormant Phase
The life cycles of _P. vivax_ and _P. ovale_ contain dorman hypnozoites, which can (and should) be eradicated as they can reactivate and cause relapse. Traditionally, this has been done with a 7-14 day course of primaquine, although this is contraindicated in patients deficient in G6PD. A newer treatment, tafenoquine, is emerging as a single dose treatment for dormant malaria. 

> [!TIP] Mnemonic
> *F*alciparum *f*ucks you up (most severe), but *v*ery *o*ld (*v*ivax, *o*vale) malarias fall asleep (hypnozoite stage)

### Clinical Features
The hallmark clinical feature of malaria is _fever_, which can occur alongside almost any other clinical feature.

Severe malaria is a medical emergency and can progress to end-organ damage, high parasitaemia (>10%), anaemia, jaundice, seizures, kidney injury, shock, and altered conscious state. 
> Typically, severe malaria is caused by _P. falciparum_
_P. vivax_ and _P. knowlesi_ can also cause severe disease.

### Diagnostics
Malaria requires specific diagnostic testing, although their may be non-specific clues (e.g. thrombocytopenia makes malaria less likely). Testing can be with:
- Microscopy - thick (screening) and thin (density and speciation) films
  - Should do these at least three times to exclude malaria
  - _P. knowlesi_, in particular, can be complex to diagnose as it can cause severe disease at a relatively lower parasite density 
- Rapid antigen test - can be genus and species specific, but unable to determine parasite density 

> [!TIP] Mnemonic
> _Thick to pick, thin to pin_
> Thick film = highly sensitive screening test
> Thin film = highly specific diagnostic test

### Management
Management varies by species and severity:
- Severe malaria -> IV artesunate 
- Uncomplicated malaria:
  - _P. falciparum_ -> artesunate based combination therapy (e.g. artemether-lumefantrine) + single dose primaquine
  - _P. vivax/ovale_ -> chloroquine + primaquine (needs 2-3 week eradication course)

Artemisinins form the backbone of malaria treatment, paricularly when faced with severe disease. Supportive care is also important, particularly looking for hypoglycaemia and judicious use of fluids. 

Artemisinin resistance is increasingly common in SE Asia, particularly along the Thai-Cambodia border. This is likely due to artemether monotherapy driving mutations in the _KELCH13_ gene. Resistance leads to slower parasite clearance and requires prolonged courses of treatment. 

In 2021, the WHO approved the RTS,S/ASO1 vaccine for falciparum malaria, which utilises a recombinant fusion protein from the _P. falciparum_ sporozoite.
