# Amoebic Liver Abscess
#bpt/infection
- - -
### Overview
Generally caused by the amoeba _Entamoeba histolytica_. Key differential is bacterial liver abscess, usually with _Klebsiella pneumoniae_. The relevance of this differential is that bacterial liver abscesses are routinely drained, while amoebic liver abscesses are treated medically unless very large (>10cm) or displaying signs of impending rupture.

> [!NOTE] _E. dispar_
> _E. dispar_ is nonpathogenic and does not require treatment. 

_E. histolytica_ has two forms:
1. Cysts - ingested
2. Trophozoites - live form
![](Amoebic%20Liver%20Abscess/unknown.png)<!-- {"width":259} -->

Once invasive trophozoite/extraintestinal disease is established, it needs metronidazole to be eradicated: tissue-active -> trophozoites. 
Cysts are able to adhere to colonic mucosal cells via specific lectins and thus need elimination with an intraluminal agent (e.g paromomycin).
