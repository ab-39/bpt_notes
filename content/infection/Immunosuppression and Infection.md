# Immunosuppression and Infection
#bpt/infection
- - -
### Overview
Treatment with immunosuppressive agents in the management of malignant and autoimmune conditions is associated with significantly increased risk of severe infections. Particular therapies have particular risks:
- Cytotoxic agents -> neutropenia -> bacterial and fungal infections
- Purine analogues (e.g. fludarabine, cladribine)/IL2 antagonism/ATG -> T-cell depletion -> viral infections, PJP
- CD19/CD20 antagonists -> profound B-cell depletion -> loss of vaccine response, viruses
- CD52 antagonists -> profound B- and T-cell depletion -> multiple infections

Management is quite algorithmic. In general, febrile neutropenia warrants management with high-dose piperacillin-tazobactam +/- vancomycin +/- gentamicin if needed.

### Neutropenia 
Prolonged (>10d), profound neutropenia (<0.5x10^9/L) is associated with a significant risk of severe bacterial and fungal infection. In general, infections can be divided based on _when_ in the neutropenic period they occur:
- Early (<10 days) - endogenous bacteria
- Mid (10-20 days) - yeasts, exogenous bacteria
- Late (>20 days) - moulds 

#### Neutropenic Syndromes:
- Viridans streptococci -> strongly associated with neutropenia, mucositis and cytarabine treatment. Organisms of the mouth. Classic presentation of fevers, rash, and stomatitis, with a high risk of shock syndrome. High mortality. 
- Typhilitis -> neutropenic enterocolitis. Generally a mixed infection which may or may not be associated with a bacteraemia. Medical management. 
- Ecthyma gangrenosum -> necrotising lesions due to _P. aeruginosa_. 

####  Fungal Infections and Neutropenia
Generally, prolonged severe neutropenia warrants antifungal prophylaxis until the neutropenia resolves:
- High risk = severe (<0.1) and prolonged (>3/52) neutropenia -> posaconazole
- (Relatively) low risk = moderate (0.1-0.5) neutropenia for intermediate duration -> fluconazole

GvHD is also considered high risk -> posaconazole.

As counts recover, there is a risk of an inflammatory response to fungi that have invaded the portal vasculature -> _hepatosplenic candidiasis syndrome_. This is most commonly due to _C. albicans_.
