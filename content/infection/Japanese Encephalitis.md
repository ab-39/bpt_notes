# Japanese Encephalitis
#bpt/infection #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Japanese encephalitis is caused by an RNA virus of the genus _Flaviviridae_, one of the [[arboviruses]]. Geographically, it is primarily seen in southeast Asia, the western Pacific, and northern Australia (tends to be the rare G4 genotype). It predominantly affects children and non-immune adults, with severe encephalitis mostly affecting the very young and very old.
- - -
### Natural History
The incubation period is 5-15 days, with the majority of patients remaining asymptomatic or else going on to develop an acute febrile illness with or without headache, myalgias, or rash. 

Encephalitic features include:
- Focal neurological disease
- Seziures
- Parkinsonian features
- Altered consciousness
- Motor weakness (uncommon)

Investigation findings:
- Elevated CSF opening pressure
- Serology - IgM in CSF or serum (NB. _may_ crossreact with other flaviviruses)

Treatment is supportive - there is no established antiviral treatment.
