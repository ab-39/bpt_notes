# Varicella Zoster Virus 
#bpt/infection #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Varicella zoster virus, or human alphaherpesvirus 3, is a double-stranded DNA virus that causes chickenpox and herpes zoster. It is an _extremely_ infectious virus that spreads by airborne transmission, and is considered infectious from 48h prior to the development of rash, until 4-5 days after crusting of the lesions. 

Varicella is vaccinated against in childhood, which has significantly reduced incidence, with two important caveats:
- There is a cohort of non-immune adults from _prior_ to the rollout of vaccines
- The lack of community circulation of VZV may impair control in patients with latent VZV, leading to more frequent episodes of herpes zoster

#### Natural History
Following primary infection, the VZV will lie dormant within dorsal root ganglia. Intermittently, immune control can fail, leading to the development of zoster rash in the associated dermatome. In 15% of patients, this will be associated with postherpetic neuralgia.

### Chickenpox
#### Uncomplicated Varicella
Prodrome:
- Fever
- Malaise
- Pharyngitis

Cardinal Features:
- Generalised rash, that is:
  - Vesicular
  - Intensely itchy
  - Eventually crusts over (~4d)
  - Occurs in crops in various stages of development

#### Complications
Include:
- Bacterial skin infection
- Encephalitis - diffuse with acute onset of cerebellar ataxia
- Pneumonia - 1/400 with CFR ~30%

In the immunosuppressed, disseminated varicella can occur. 

#### Treatment
Most adults with chickenpox require treatment due to the higher risk of complications. Treatment is generally effective if commenced within 72h of onset of rash. Treatment depends on patient and disease factors:
- Uncomplicated disease in an immunocompetent host -> PO aciclovir or valaciclovir
- Complicated disease _or_ an immunocompromised host -> IV aciclovir
- Pregnant women = highest risk of fetal varicella syndrome during 12-28w gestation -> post-exposure prophlyaxis:
  - <4d -> VZV Ig
  - ≥4d -> PO aciclovir
  - Do **not** give the VZV vaccine (live) due to risk of inducing congenital varicella syndrome

### Herpes Zoster
Herpes zoster (or, shingles) is characterised by a painful, unilateral, dermatomal vesicular rash:
![](Varicella%20Zoster%20Virus/image%202.png)
This is usually treated with oral aciclovir or valaciclovir, which has slightly superior PO bioavailability. 

Although prevention of shingles has historically been with the live attenuated Zostavax, but this has recently changed to the adjuvanted recombinanted _Shingrix_ vaccine, that is administered as a two-dose course. Vaccine is administered irrespective of the history of chickenpox or shingles, and there is good evidence for reduction in disease rates (_ZOE-50_, _ZOE-70_). Caution is advised if ocular disease has occurred in the preceding year. 

Of all immunosuppressants, JAK inhibitors appear to carry the highest risk of VZV reactivation.
