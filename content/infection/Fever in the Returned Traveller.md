# Fever in the Returned Traveller
#bpt/infection #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Fever in the returned traveller is a common problem, which the _Geosentinel_ study estimated occurs with a prevalence of ~25% in unwell travellers. While fever in the returned traveller is often due to ‘exotic’ or ‘tropical’ causes, many cases can be due to mundane causes such as streptococcal pneumonia or COVID-19.

Note that patients visiting friends and relatives (VFRs) tend to have higher rates of malaria, enteric fever, TB, STIs, parasitic infections, strongyloidiasis and influenza. VFRs are also less likely to seek health advice on return. 
- - -
### Epidemiology 
Geography often determines aetiology, with the most common cause of fever in the returned traveller varying significantly based on the area in question:
- Subsaharan Africa -> malaria 
- Southeast Asia and Latin America -> dengue
- South Asia -> enteric fever 

In addition to the usual things, such as freshwater, cave, animal, and sexual exposures, it is important to ask if a patient has been to any hospitals or other medical facilities.

### Key Physical Findings
While these are often nonspecific, there can be some signs with higher positive likelihood ratios:
- Eschar -> plague, rickettsial infection (e.g. African tick bite fever), trypanosomiasis
  ![](Fever%20in%20the%20Returned%20Traveller/image.png)
- Jaundice -> hepatitis, malaria, yellow fever, leptospirosis, enteric fever, fascioliasis, 
- Lymphadenopathy -> dengue, rickettsial infections, brucellosis, HIV, syphilis, kala-azar, TB, Chagas disease, filariasis, monkeypox
  - Good negative LR for malaria
- Conjunctivitis -> zika, measles
- Conjunctival suffusion/subconjunctival haemorrhage -> leptospirosis 
- Hepatosplenomegaly -> malaria, enteric fever
- Rash is fairly nonspecific, although some rashes can be characteristic - e.g. measles on the face, monkeypox, etc.

### Investigations
Do:
- Respiratory PCR
- Malaria thick and thin films
- FBE, LFT, EUC, CRP
- Blood +/- urine and stool cultures 

Additionally, consider:
- PCR for arthropod borne viruses - need to have an idea of what you’re looking for!
- NS1 Ag for dengue
- Blood/urine PCR for leptospirosis
- Relevant serology
- Blood smear -> Babesia, Borrelia, Filaria
- LP
- Biopsy
- etc.
