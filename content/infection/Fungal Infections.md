### (Very) Basic Mycology
Fungi come in three classes:
- Yeasts = single celled organisms that reproduce by budding, can be further divided on the basis of having a capsule:
  - Encapsulated -> _Cryptococcus_
  - Unencapsulated -> _Candida_
    - _C. auris_ is resistant to amphotericin
- Moulds = multicellular organisms that have hyphae, can be further divided on the basis of whether the hyphae are septated:
  - Aseptated hyphae -> mucorales
  - Septated hyphae -> _Aspergillus_, _Fusarium_, many others 
  - Some moulds, such as *Scedosporium*, are resistant to LAMB and require treatment with voriconazole (which itself does not cover *Mucomycosis*)
- Dimorphic fungi = organisms that can do both depending on where they are (‘mould in the cold, yeast in the beast’). Typically the organisms that cause the endemic mycoses (e.g. _Histoplasma_, _Talaromyces_, _Coccidioides_, _Blastomyces_)

In general, fungi are eukaryotes that have a complex cell wall with two components:
- Cell wall proper - composed of chitin, glucans, and mannans
- Cell membrane - key element is ergosterol

### Antifungal Medications
By far, the molecule that is the target for the vast majority of antifungal treatments is the cell wall component _ergosterol_:
- Polyenes - e.g. amphotericin B - bind ergosterol and weaken the cell membrane -> pore formation -> electrolyte leakage -> cell death (hence, also highly toxic to mammals)
  - Broad spectrum
  - _C. auris_ and _lusitaniae_ can be intrinsically resistant to amphotericin
- Azoles - inhibit fungal CYP450 -> reduced synthesis of ergosterol from lanosterol -> membrane instability -> cell death 
  - ’Early’ azoles - e.g. fluconazole -> strong activity against yeasts but less so against moulds
  - ’Late’ azoles - e.g. posaconazole, voriconazole -> strong activity against moulds but less so against yeasts
    - Voriconazole is the treatment of choice for invasive aspergillosis 
  - Side effects include hallucinations, flashing lights, and renal/hepatic duration 
  - Drug interactions +++ due to CYP induction 
- Echinocandins - e.g. caspofungin - inhibit the synthesis of β-glucan by 1,3-β-glucan synthase -> disruption of cell wall synthesis -> inability to withstand osmotic forces -> cell death
  - Excellent activity against yeasts (often first-line for candidaemia)
  - Not active against _Cryptococcus_ (which lacks the relevant enzyme) or dimorphic fungi
- Antimetabolites - e.g. flucytosine - activated to 5-FU by cytosine deaminase within the fungal cell -> interference with DNA synthesis -> cell death (hence, also myelosuppressive)

### Fungal Testing
Blood cultures have most utility for _Candida_ spp. Other fungi require more specific tests:
- Galactomannan = polysaccharide in cell walls of:
  - Aspergillus
  - Histoplasma
  - Fusarium
  - Cryptococcus
- Β-glucan = found in cell walls of:
  - Candida
  - Aspergillus
  - Pneumocystis
  - Fusarium
- Cryptococcal antigen
- Histoplasma antigen
- PCR for: PJP, Aspergillus
- Staining with methenamine silver stain (e.g. Grocott, Gomori)
- Evidence of filaments on histology
