### Overview
Syphilis, the great imitator, is a multisystem bacterial infection caused by the human-specific obligate spirochaete _Treponema pallidum pallidum_ (the subspecies is relevant as other subsp. of _T. pallidum_ cannot be distinguished serologically). Like all STIs, syphilis increases the risk of [[infection/HIV|HIV]] acquisition and transmission.
- - -
### Pathogenesis
Sexual contact is the main mode of transmission. The spirochaete enters the body via microscopic and macroscopic membranes in the skin and mucous membranes. From here, it can spread through the blood stream and lymphatic system. The second mode of transmission is vertical from mother to fetus.

Once entrenched, syphilis causes disease through the mechanism of endarteritis.
- - -
### Natural History
Syphilis is broadly divided into early (<2y from aqcuisition) and late (>2y from acquisition) phases.

Of patients who have an early infection with syphilis, one third will clear the disease spontaneously, one third will go on to develop asymptomatic (‘latent’) disease, and one third will proceed to tertiary syphilis. 

#### Primary
The average incubation time of syphilis is three weeks, but can range from 9-90 days.

The classic presentation of early syphilis is the _chancre_, a single macule or papule that eventually erodes to form a smooth, well-defined, painless ulcer. This last for up to five weeks, and is _highly_ infectious. 

#### Secondary
Secondary syphilis corresponds to the bacteraemic phase of the disease, and occurs two weeks to two months after the chancre. 

In addition to generalised symptoms such as fever and lymphadenopathy, other symptoms can include:
- Maculopapular rash - particualrly on the palms and soles
- Alopecia
- Mucosal patches
- Condyloma lata (highly infectious)
- Splenomegaly
- Dernaged LFTs
- Periostitic
- Aseptic meningitis
- Iritis/uveitis 

#### Latent
Latent syphilis refers to the stage when a patient has serological evidence of syphilis without clinical signs or symptoms. Early latent syphilis (<2y) tends to be infectious, while late latent syphilis is not. Latent syphilis can persist for decades, with only about one third of patients progressing to clinical tertiary syphilis.

#### Tertiary
Tertiary syphilis refers to the systematic evidence of late syphilis infection, characterised by:
- Gumma - granulomatous lesions of the skin
- Syphilitic aortitis -> aortic root dilatation; aneurysm formation
- Neurosyphilis:
  - Meningovascular syphilis - infectious arteritis causing stroke-like symptoms
  - Parenchymatous syphilis:
    - General paralysis of the insane - progressive dementia
    - Tabes dorsalis - posterior column deterioration
  - Argyll-Robertson pupil
  - Optic atrophy
  - Hearing loss

### Syphilis in Pregnancy
Spirochaetes cross the placenta freely, and can cause severe complications in pregnancy. Congenital syphilis is a leading cause of preventable stillbirth worldwide, second only to malaria.

Transmission risk fundamentally relates to the stage of syphilis in pregnanct:
- Primary/secondary -> up to 100% risk
- Early latent -> 40%
- Late latent -> 10%

### Diagnosis of Syphilis
#### Direct Tests
Require a swab from a lesion, e.g.:
- Dark ground microscopy
- Direct fluorescent antigen testing
- Treponemal NAAT/PCR

#### Serology
Screening test:
- Chemiluminescence immunoassay 
- Enzyme immunoassay

If the screening test is positive, we usually move on to a combination of treponemal and non-treponemal serological tests:
- Treponemal -> TPPA, TPHA 
  - Positive for life after exposure
- Non-treponemal -> RPR, VDRL
  - These are measures of disease activity
  - RPR is used to determine cure and reinfection:
    - 2-titre/fourfold reduction in RPR -> cure
    - 2-titre/fourfold increase in RPR post-cire -> reinfection
- - -
### Treatment
Penicillin is the mainstay of treatment for syphilis:
- Early -> single dose of 2.4MiU of IM benzathinepenicillin
- Late -> x3 weekly doses of 2.4MiU of IM benzathinepenicillin
- Neurosyphilis -> usually two weeks of IV benzylpenicillin

Doxycycline is a common alternative
