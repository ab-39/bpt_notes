# Herpes Simplex Virus
#bpt/infection #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
The herpes simplex viruses 1 and 2 (also human herpesviruses 1 and 2) are very common viruses belonging to the family of [[human herpesviruses]]. 

> [!NOTE] Resistance
> Resistance is uncommon among patients co-infected with HIV.

HSV produces a protein known as ICP47 (infected cell protein 47) that specifically inhibits the TAP transporter. By binding to and inhibiting TAP, ICP47 prevents the transport of viral peptides from the cytosol into the ER. This inhibition means that MHC Class I molecules have fewer viral peptides to present on the cell surface, significantly reducing the ability of CD8+ T cells to recognize and target HSV-infected cells.

### Herpes Encephalitis
Herpetic encephalitis is almost always caused by HSV-1, and has a bimodal distribution, affecting those in early childhood and those above fifty. 

#### Distribution
Tends to affect:
- Anterior and medial temporal lobes
- Inferior frontal lobe
- Thalamus
- Insular cortex

#### Symptoms
- Fever
- Headache
- Confusion
- Altered behaviour

#### Investigations
- CSF pleocytosis
- Positive HSV PCR in the CSF - peak at d3-7
- Characteristic MRI findings:
  - Asymmetric abnormalities in the mesiotemporal, orbitofrontal lobes and insular cortex
  - Oedema
  - Diffusion restriction
  - Haemorrhage

#### Treatment
IV aciclovir - reduced mortality, but with prolonged survival there is now emergence of more sequelae. Risk of crystal nephropathy with aciclovir. Resistant can emerge due to a thymidine kinase mutation within the virus, in which case foscarnet is usually a reasonable choice of treatment.
