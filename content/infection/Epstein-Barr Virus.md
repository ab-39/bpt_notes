### Overview
The Epstein-Barr virus, or human gammaherpesvirus 4, is one of the most prevalent viruses worldwide, affecting an estimated 90% of the worldwide population. It is spread via oromucosal contact, and results in lytic infection of the oropharynx, causing shedding of high levels of the virus. From there, the virus can remain dormant in the B-cells in the local lymphoid tissue. In the immunocompetent host, both the lytic and latent stages are often asymptomatic due to immune control.
- - -
### Diagnosis
The _heterophile antibody_ or “monospot” has high specificity for EBV. 

Both stages of the disease have specific viral protein markers:
- Lytic:
  - Immediate early -> BZLF1, BRLF1
  - Early -> DNA amplification
  - Late -> viral capsid
- Latent = no viral particles produced:
  - Nuclear latent proteins (EBNA)
  - Latent membrane proteins (LMP)
  - EBER -> non-coding highly-expressed RNAs

### EBV in Human Disease
#### Oncogenic Potential and the _LMP-1_ Oncogene 
The _LMP-1_ oncogene is an EBV viral antigen with low immunogenicity that activates signalling pathway by acting as though CD40 is bound, thus promoting cell survival and proliferation.
![](Epstein-Barr%20Virus/image.png)
The oncogenic potential of EBV was first noted by Denis Burkitt in cases of endemic lymphoma in Africa that now bears his name. Since then, multiple malignancies have been linked to EBV, including:
- Post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder
- Nasopharyngeal carcinoma
- Hodgkin lymphoma
- Gastric carcinoma
- Oral hairy leukoplakia 

#### Post-Transplant Lymphoproliferative Disorder
PTLD is a feared early complication of transplantation that is often caused by early primary infection with EBV in the post-transplant period. The key risk factor is serodiscordance, with D+/R- carrying a relative risk of up to 75 times. Among patients undergoing [[allogeneic stem cell transplant]], rates appear to be higher among those with haploidentical transplants rather than matched unrelated donors, administration of ATG or anti-CD3 antibodies (RR = 43!) for treatment of GvHD, and chronic GvHD. Importantly, the degree of T-cell depletion plays an important role. Interestingly, trials of belatacept (a recombinant CTLA-4 receptor) show a signal to _increasing_ the risk of PTLD despite being relatively poor at preventing GvHD. 

The goals of treatment are to _reduce_ immunosuppression and to increase the T-cell response. Other treatments such as rituximab and conventional chemotherapy can also be used.

Note that the minority of cases of PTLD (<10%) occur late and are _not_ due to EBV.

#### EBV and [[Multiple Sclerosis]] 
A recent study by Bjornevik et al. in Science ([viz.](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abj8222)) followed a cohort of 801 individuals in the US MIlitary over a period of twenty years. It found that of the 801 cases of MS over that time, 35 were initially EBV seronegative and all but one became EBV seropositive prior to the development of MS. The remainder of cases were _all_ EBV seropositive prior to the onset of MS.
