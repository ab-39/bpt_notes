### Overview
Von Willebrand Factor, the Jedi Knight of the bloodstream, plays a critical role as both a mediator of platelet function and a chaperone protein for factor VIII. 

VWF is synthesised in endothelial cells, where it is stored in Weibel-Palade bodies and constitutively released into the matrix. When stimulated, VWF is released into the blood, where ADAMST13 cleaves the ultra large multimers of VWF to prevent activation of the clotting cascade. 

#### Von Willebrand Disease
The most common bleeding disorder (~1% population prevalence), caused by a congenital reduction in levels or function of vWF;
- Type 1 = partial quantitative deficiency of vWF (levels <300iu/L) = ~80% of cases 
  - Autosomal dominant inheritance 
  - Note that levels will be lower in patients with blood group O due to a shorter half life of vWL
  - In patients with borderline vWF levels (e.g. 300-500iu/L) and a bleeding phenotype, the diagnosis can also be made

- Type 2 = qualitative abnormality (defined as activity:vWF Ag <0.7) of vWF due to mutations in specific binding sites:
  - Autosomal dominant inheritance
  - Type 2A - increased susceptibility to clceavage by ADAMTS13 -> fewer HMW multimers
  - Type 2B - increased binding to platelets (GPIb)
    - Note that analogous deficiency of GpIb on platelets also leads to a bleeding phenotype - _Bernard Soulier syndrome_ 
  - Type 2M - decreased binding to platelets and/or collagen
  - Type 2N - decreased binding to FVIII
- Type 3 = absence of vWF - most severe phenotype, comparable to severe haemophilia A
  - Autosomal recessive inheritance

#### Acquired von Willebrand Syndrome
Refers to any condition where the large multimers of vWF are broken down leading to a loss of the HWM forms with reduced function. Key examples include:
- Valvular diease: shear stress leading to unravelling of vWF and subsequent degradation by ADAMTS13 (in combination with angiodysplasia of the GIT = Heyde syndrome)
- ET/PCV: excess binding to platelets
- Antibody-mediated

#### Functional Tests for vWF
- vWF activity assay - measure of binding between vWF and GpIb on platelets
- Ristocetin cofactor assay - ‘mimics’ shear force -> causes vWF unfolding and platelet binding
  - Largely superseded by the vWF activity assay 
- Collagen binding assay
- Factor VIII levels - vWF is a chaperone for FVIII in the bloodstream -> vWD -> reduced levels of factor VIII

#### Treatment
Principles of Management:
- tranexamic acid for mild bleeding
- desmopressin (DDAVP): raises levels of vWF by inducing release of vWF from Weibel-Palade bodies inendothelial cells
- vWF/factor VIII concentrate
