#bpt/rheumatology #bpt/racp_cls

### How are the different types of [[vasculitis]] classified?
The Chapel Hill system classes the vasculitides according to the size of the vessels affected:
| Vessel Size    | Specific Points            | Granuloma | Conditions                                                   |
|----------------|----------------------------|-----------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Large Vessels  |                            | Yes       | [[Takayasu Arteritis]]<br>[[Giant Cell Arteritis]] (‘Temporal’) |
| Medium Vessels |                            | No        | [[Polyarteritis Nodosa]]<br>[[Kawasaki Disease]]<br>[[Isolated CNS Vasculitis]]* |
| Small Vessels  | Immune-Complex<br>Mediated | No        | Cryoglobulinemic Vasculitis<br>IgA Vasculitis (Henoch-Schönlein)<br>Hypocomplementemic Urticarial Vasculitis<br>(anti-C1q) |
| Small Vessels  | [[ANCA Vasculitis]]        | No        | [[Microscopic Polyangiitis]] - p-ANCA/MPO<br>                |
| Small Vessels  | [[ANCA Vasculitis]]        | Yes       | [[Granulomatous polyangiitis]] - c-ANCA (Wegener Disease)<br>[[Eosinophilic Granulomatosis with Polyangiitis]] (Churg-Strauss Disease) |
| Capillarys     |                            |           | Anti-GBM Disease (Goodpasture Disease)                       |

Additionally, the revision to the Chapel Hill criteria gives us a few more conditions:
- Variable vessel disease: [[Behçet Disease]], Cogan disease 
- Single organ disease: leucocytoclastic vasculitis, isolated CNS vasculitis
- Vasculitis associated with probable aetiology: HCV-associated cryoglobulinemic vasculitis, vasculitis associated with systemic disease, rheumatoid vasculitis
