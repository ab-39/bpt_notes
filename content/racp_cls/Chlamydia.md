### Overview
Chlamydia is a _very_ common, notifiable sexually transmissible infection caused by the obligate intracellular bacterium _Chlamydia trachomatis_, of which there are multiple serovars:
- A-C -> ocular trachoma
- D-K -> genitourinary disease, proctitis
  - Serious sequelae include pelvic inflammatory disease, perihepatitis, ectopic pregnancy, epididymoorchitis, and infertility
  
- L1-3 -> lymphogranuloma venereum

Chlamydia is transmitted primarily by sexual intercourse, after which it has an incubation period of 7-21 days. The most frequently affected anatomic sites are the urethra, endocervix, rectum, pharynx, and conjunctivae.

Clinical signs and symptoms of chlamydia include icnreased vaginal discharge, postcoital and intermenstrual bleeding, dysuria, lower abdominal pain, deep dyspareunia, and urethral discharge. 

Diagnosis is usually by NAAT from the relevant anatomical site based on history - e.g. first-pass urine (better in men), high cervical swab (better in women), etc.

The first-line treatment for chlamydia in doxycycline 100mg PO BD for seven days, which has superior evidence for symptomatic urethritis and rectal chlamydia when compared to the second-line treatment, 1g of azithromycin PO as a single dose. Contact tracing is important, as is testing for other STIs. Re-testing is advised after three months due to the high reinfection rate.
