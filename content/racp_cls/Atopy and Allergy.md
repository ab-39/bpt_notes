### Definition
_Atopy_ refers to immunological reactivity in which IgE is readily produced in response to ordinary exposure to common allergens in a subjects’ environment. In itself, atopy does not imply the presence of disease, but is a reflection of the immunologic state of the individual. 

While many allergic diseases are atopic, there are also IgE mediated non-atopic diseases (e.g. drug allergies) and non-IgE mediated allergic diseases (e.g. T-cell mediated drug reactions). 

The four key atopic diseases are:
- Asthma
- Allergic rhinitis
- Atopic eczema
- Food allergy

### Overview of the Allergic Inflammatory Response
1. Sensitisation
   - Allergen presentation to Th-cells via MHCII on antigen presenting cells
     - Costimulatory signalling between CD28 (on T-cells) and CD80/86 (=B7) on APCs is critical to this process, as the absence of costimulation leads to anergy
   - IgE-class switching and production by B-cells/plasma cells
   - IgE binding to receptors on tissue mast cells
2. Challenge
   - Re-exposure to the allergen with IgE mediated activation of mast cells
     - This involves cross-linking of two or more adjacent IgE molecules on mast cells, stimulating the release of intracellular calcium and via that mechanism a combination of preformed and newly-generated inflammatory mediators 
     - Preformed mediators include histamine, heparin, tryptase, and chymase
     - Newly-synthesised mediators are generally eicosanoids, including prostaglandins and leukotrienes 
     - Cytokines include:
       - IL-4 and 13 -> IgE class switching
       - [[Interleukin 5]] and GM-CSF -> eosinophil recruitment
       - [[Tissue Necrosis Factors]] (TNFα) -> primes leukocytes for mediator secretion
   - Release of immediate vasoactive amines and lipid mediators = early response of _wheal_ and _flare_
   - Recruitment of chronic inflammatory cells = late response of _induration_ and _inflammation_

### Eosinophils
The main effector cell type in allergic inflammation is the _eosinophil_. These are derived from the bone marrow, and require:
- Interleukin 3 -> initial commitment
- [[Interleukin 5]] and GM-CSF -> maturing and cell priming
Once activated, they secrete:
- Granule-derived basic proteins - e.g. MBP, ECP, EPO
- Newly synthesised lipid mediators - e.g. leukotrienes, PAF
- Cytokines - e.g. IL1, IL2, IL3, IL4, IL5, IL6, IL8, GM-CSF

### The Hygiene Hypothesis
Early life exposure to micro-organisms -> Th1 predominant immunophenotype -> to microbial pathogens

_Lack_ of early life exposure to micro-organisms -> Th2 predominant immunophenotype -> to environmental allergens
