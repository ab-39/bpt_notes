# Anti-LGI1 Encephalitis
#bpt/neurology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Anti-LGI1 encephalitis is caused by antibodies against the leucine-rich glioma-inactivated 1 protein, that is associated with voltage-gated potassium channels.

This is characterised by the gradual onset of illness, typically in older adults. Clinical signs include temporal lobe epilepsy and an amnestic syndrome. 

The most characteristic seizures are faciobrachial dystonic seizures - dystonic contractions of the face and ipsilateral arm and/or leg, occurring up to 100x/d - in combination with hyponatraemia. 

Initial MRI may show hippocampal hyperintensities in T2-weighting, which may involve into mesial temporal sclerosis.

Treatment is with immunotherapy.
