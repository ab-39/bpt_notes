### Epidemiology
Common - leads to an estimated 40,000 admissions annually in Australia. There is typically a 90-10 split of non-variceal to variceal bleeding.

### Clinical Presentation 
The presentation of UGIB is diverse, typically consisting of some combination of haematemesis, melaena, and anaemia. It is important to note that in large, rapid UGIB, there can be frank PR bleeding and often haemodynamic instability. 

The history can help to elicit the aetiology:
- Liver disease -> variceal bleeding
- NSAID use
- Past [[peptic ulcer disease]]
- Upper GI surgery 
- B-type symptoms and/or dysphagia
- Bleeding diatheses 

It is important to assess volume and haemodynamic status on presentation.

### Management 
Large bleeding requires resuscitation to begin with. 

#### Transfusion
General aim is Hb 70-90g/L, with the higher end of the range being for high-risk patients e.g. with heart disease. The evidence for a restrictive strategy (rather than >80g/L) comes from a paper by [Villanueva et al. from 2013](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa1211801). 

#### Endoscopy
Guidelines recommend endoscopy within 24h, but evidence is mixed for urgent endoscopy within shorter timeframes. 

Risk stratification is with some specific scores:
- Pre-scope -> Blatchford score
  - Score of 0-23
  - Score of 0 -> outpatient management
  - Higher the score -> higher the risk of requiring endoscopy 
- Post-scope -> Forrest score
  - Calculated on basis of visualisation to determine risk of rebleeding:
    - Clean base -> lowest risk/~10%
    - Clot -> indeterminate -> should dislodge clot
    - Spurting vessel ->  highest risk/~50%
  - Score of Ia -> III
  - Scores of II and III can be discharged early on PO PPI

The goal in endoscopy is **ICC** -> inject (with adrenaline), clip, and cauterise. Best practice is to carry out two of these interventions for a bleeding ulcer. An emerging therapy is endoscopic spray of haemostatic powder. 

If endoscopic strategies fail, or the patient has a second bleed, then escalation of treatment is typically either to IR embolisation or surgery. 

#### PPI Therapy 
Unequivocal that high- is better than standard-dose, but the optimal strategy is unclear (i.e. IV infusion vs bolus BD). PPI therapy reduces need for endoscopy, but not mortality or rebleeding. 
- Pre-procedure -> IV/PO depending on local guidelines 
- Post-procedure -> generally suggest IV bolus + 72h infusion, but emerging evidence for bolus dosing

#### TXA should _not_ be used
TXA should not be used except in the context of trauma. 

#### Antiplatelets and GI Bleeding
- Monotherapy:
  - Withhold on presentation with bleed
  - Monotherapy can be recommenced on D+3 after endoscopy
- DAPT:
  - Aspirin can be continued until endoscopy, the other agent should be withheld on presentation with bleed
  - Best to discuss with cardiologist if recent coronary intervention
  - In general - the second agent can be recommenced when the endoscopist is satisified with the degree of control  or if the lesion is low risk
  - If high risk, the antiplatelet should ideally be recommenced within 5 days
Viz. [Endoscopic diagnosis and management of nonvariceal upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage \(NVUGIH\): European Society of Gastrointestinal Endoscopy \(ESGE\) Guideline - Update 2021 - PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33567467/)

#### Anticoagulants and GI Bleeding
- Warfarin:
  - Safe to proceed with endoscopy for a UGIB if INR ≤2.5
  - If INR >2.5 -> reverse _unless_ the bleed is non-severe and there would be significant risk associated with reversal
  - Generally recommenced 102 weeks after haemostasis 
Viz. [Effectiveness and safety of oral anticoagulant therapy in patients with atrial fibrillation with prior gastrointestinal bleeding: A systematic review and meta-analysis - PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35966547/)

- DOACs:
  - Of the DOACs, dabigatran and divaroxaban appear to have a slightly higher risk of GI bleeding than warfarin
  - In contrast, apixaban appears to be slightly safer
  - Reversal agents:
    - Dabigatran -> idarucizumab
    - FXa inhibitors:
      - Andexanet alpha 
      - Ciraparantag
  - Resumption rules are generally the same as warfarinc
Viz. [Risk of Gastrointestinal Bleeding in Patients Taking Non-Vitamin K Antagonist Oral Anticoagulants: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis - PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28458008/)

#### Repeat Assessment
While repeat endoscopy is not routinely recommended for duodenal ulcers, it is suggested for gastric ulcers 8-12 weeks after index endoscopy if any of the following are present:
- Ongoing symptoms despite medical therapy
- Unclear aetiology
- Ulcer >2cm
- Inadequate biopsy
- Suspicion for malignancy
- Evidence of ongoing bleeding
- Risk factors for gastric cancer - e.g. age >50y, _H. pylori_, epidemiologic risk, etc.
