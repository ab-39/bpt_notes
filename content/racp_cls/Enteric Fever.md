# Enteric Fever
#bpt/infection #bpt/racp_cls

### Overview
Enteric, or typhoid, fever is caused by _Salmonella enteria_ serotypes Typhi and Paratyphi A/B/C. High incidence areas include south and southeast Asia, southern Africa, and parts of Oceania including Samoa and Fiji. Humans are the only reservoir and trasmission is by direct contact with an infected person, or through contaminated food or water.

In the distant past, enteric fever could be treated with amoxicillin, TMP-SMX, or chloramphenicol. Increasingly, however, MDR is spreading. Most typhoid strains in South Asia are now resistant to fluoroquinolones, and there have been outbreaks of ESBL _S. enterica_ Typhi in Pakistan.
- - -
### Clinical Features 
After an incubation period of 5-21 days, enteric fever is characterised by a three week ‘classic’ presentation:
- Week 1 -> rising, stepwise fever with relative bradycardia
- Week 2 -> abdominal pain with rose spots
- Week 3 -> septic shock, intestinal perforation, and hepatosplenomegaly (this led to a CFR of 15% prior to antibiotics)

Now, since the development of antibiotics, most patients present with:
- Persistent fever
- GI symptoms - diarrhoea or constipation
- Neurologic and respiratory symptoms

1-6% of patients can progress to chronic carriage. This is associated with biliary tract disease and female sex. 
- - -
### Diagnosis
Most sensitive test is a bone marrow culture, and is considered the gold standard! By comparison, blood cultures have a sensitivity of only ~50%. Due to the extremely invasive nature of bone marrow testing, however, most patients should at minimum get blood an stool cultures done. The Widal test should not be used routinely due to poor sensitivity. 
- - -
### Treatment 
Treatment of enteric fever depends on the severity of the illness and the susceptibility of the isolate:
- Empiric -> ceftriaxone or azithromycin
- Pakistani -> carbapenem

Adjunctive dexamethasone can be used in severe infection.

Chronic carriage is treated with prolonged eradication therapy - e.g. ciprofloxacin 500mg PO BD for four weeks. 
- - -
### Prevention 
The mainstay of prevention is the availability of clean water, functional sanitation systems, and education. Additionally, injectable and oral vaccines (all three of conjugated, polysaccharide, and live) are available. These only provide limited protection against the Typhi serovar.
