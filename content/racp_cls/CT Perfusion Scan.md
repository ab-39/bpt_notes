### Overview
The CT perfusion is a contrast scan used in [[ischaemic stroke]]. It gives us three key pieces of information:
1. Cerebral blood flow
   - This will drop in both the core (precipitously) and the penumbra (to a lesser degree)
   ![](CT%20Perfusion%20Scan/image.png)<!-- {"width":257} -->
2. Time to perfusion - the time to peak contrast density 
   - Increased in core and penumbra 
   ![](CT%20Perfusion%20Scan/image%202.png)<!-- {"width":257} -->
3. Cerebral blood volume - the AUC of contrast density over time
   - Reduced in the core
   - Note that the CBV only mildly reduces, and can even actually _increase_ in the penumbra due to vasodilatation
   ![](CT%20Perfusion%20Scan/image%203.png)<!-- {"width":701} -->

Fundamentally:
> $CBF = CBV/TTP$ 

The two key pieces we aim to glean from this are:
1. How _much_ blood supply? 
   - i.e. will the tissue die?
2. How _delayed_ is the blood supply?
   - i.e. at how much risk is the tissue?
