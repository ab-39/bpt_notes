### What is pneumonia?
Pneumonia is an _acute infection_ of the pulmonary parenchyma resulting in _alveolar occlusion_ with _inflammatory infiltrates_. 

It can be bacterial, viral, or inflammatory in aetiology. 

Acquisition:
- Community
- Hospital: >48h into an hospital admission
- Ventilator: >48h on invasive ventilation
- - -
### Diagnosis of pneumonia
Diagnosis of pneumonia is typically clinical, with adjunctive use of radiologic findings (usually CXR or CTC).

Differentials:
- (IE)COPD
- Reactive process to subdiaphragmatic pathology
- Empyema (particularly for HAP)
- PE
- Atelectasis
- - -
### Epidemiology
_Very_ common - prevalence of ~2.4/1000 adults in Australia. 
Rates rising in the very elderly and very young. 
Mortality of 6.5% in hospital globally, up to 23% at 6/12. 
- - -
### High Risk Groups
- Elderly
- Chronic comorbidities
- Viral coinfection
- Impaired airway protection
- Specific pathogens
- - -
### Aetiology
Microbiological diagnosis is typically not achieved, and in fact, most bacterial pneumonia is polymicrobial anyway. In viral pneumonia, there is typically a degree of bacterial co-infection, particularly for influenza and RSV.

The most common bacterial causes of pneumonia are:
- _Streptococcus pneumoniae_ (~30%) - that being said, the proportion of cases caused by _S. pneumoniae_ in North America has declined dramatically with vaccination and in the antibiotic era 
- _Mycoplasma pneumoniae_
- _Haemophilus influenzae_ b
- _Legionella pneumophila_ 
- _Staphylococcus aureus_
  - This was previously hypothesised to be more dangerous and more prone to forming abscesses/necrosis due to the presence of the pore-forming, necrotising exotoxin known as _Panton-Valentine leucocidin_, but this is now thought to be simply the marker of more virulent MRSA clones
- _Pseudomonas aeruginosa_ and _Chlamydia pneumoniae_ are quite rare

The most common viral causes of pneumonia are human rhinovirus, influenza, RSV, and metapneumovirus. 

There are also a range of fungal and other exotic pathogens that can cause pneumonia.

### Clinical Presentation 
Findings:
- Bronchial breathing
- Vocal fremitus
- Mucopurulent sputum
- Fevers
- Hypoxia
- Atypical presentations: confusion, diarrhoea, hepatitis, hyponatraemia (typically Legionella)

CXR findings:
- New focal infiltrates
- Dense lobar consolidations
- Loss of heart border

Can also consider CT-chest (new infiltrates, consolidation) or USS thorax (“lung hepatisation”, air bronchograms) 

Microbiology: _new_ pathogenic flora with clinical correlation. 

Diagnostic criteria for HAP:
- New CXR changes with at least **one** of:
  - Fever
  - Lymphocytosis or lymphopenia
  - New/worsening purulent secretions
  - Worsening gas exchange 
- - -
### Investigations
Routine:
- Hx + Ex
- CXR 
- Markers of severity - e.g. CRP
- Nasopharyngeal swab for respiratory PCR

Consider:
- Sputum Gram stain and culture
- Blood cultures
- Pneumococcal and Legionella urinary antigen
  - Note that the Legionella antigen only looks for _L. pneumophila_, not _L. longbeachae_ which is more common in Australia and NZ 
- Biomarkers 
- Influenza swab

There is a low threshold for additional tests (e.g. cross-sectional imaging) in immunosuppressed patients. 
- - -
### Severity and Severity Scores 
The **most important** thing to look for when considering severity is the presence of **sepsis or shock**, typified by signs such as:
- tachypnoea (RR > 22)
- tachycardia (HR > 100)
- hypotension (SBP < 90)
- confusion
- hypoxia (SpO2 < 90)
- multipolar involvement on CXR
- lactate > 2.0mmol/L

The **CURB-65** score predicts mortality, and is often used to decide who needs to be admitted to hospital:
- Confusion
- Uraemia (>7mmol/L)
- Respiratory rate (>30brpm)
- BP < 90mmHg systolic or 60mmHg diastolic
- Age > 65y

It is specific but not sensitive. A score of 0 or 1 suggests that a patient can be managed as an outpatient. A variant of this (which makes it non-invasive) is CORB, substituting ‘oxygenation’ or ‘uraemia’. 

Another is SMART-COP, which stratifies the requirement for admission to ICU. The most complex score is the PSI, which requires _a lot_ of information and is very sensitive but not specific.

### Treatment
| Low                     | Moderate                     | Severe                                       |
|-------------------------|------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Amoxicillin 1g PO q8h   | Benzylpenicillin 1.2g IV q6h | Ceftriaxone 2g IV daily                      |
| Doxycycline 100mg PO BD | Doxycycline 100mg PO BD      | Azithromycin 500mg IV daily for three doses  |

Special considerations:
- Non-severe penicillin hypersensitivity -> cefuroxime or ceftriaxone
- Severe penicillin hypersensitivity -> moxifloxacin 400mg PO/IV daily 
- Non-severe disease in the tropics -> ceftriaxone + gentamicin + doxycycline 
- Severe disease in the tropics -> meropenem 1g IV q8h + azithromycin 
  - Adds cover for _Burkholderia pseudomallei_ and _Acinetobacter baumanii_ 
- Suspected _Staphylococcus aureus_ -> add vancomycin
- Suspected _Pseudomonas aeruginosa_ -> switch to piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV q6h (+ azithromycin) 

Adjunctive steroids have been found to improve  mortality in moderate severity CAP requiring ICU admission ([_CAPE-COD_](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2215145)), with attendant risks of hyperglycaemia and readmission. The usual duration of antibiotic treatment is 5-7 days.

### Sequelae
Strong association between pneumonia and new onset heart failure
