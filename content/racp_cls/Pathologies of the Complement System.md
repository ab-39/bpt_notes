### Overview of the Complement Cascade
From AMBOSS:
![](Pathologies%20of%20the%20Complement%20System/big_5fb3d7853a1c0.jpg)
Basically, there is:
- Classical pathway - initiated by C1s in response to antibody binding to pathogenic antigen 
- Lectin pathway - initiated by MBL binding to pathogenic carbohydrates
- Alternative pathway - an ‘ancient’ pathway activated in response to specific pathogen factors

> [!NOTE] Simplified Overview
> The two main molecules of the complement system are activated by enzymes in response to local toxins:
> - C3 -> C3a + C5b
>   - C3a = chemotaxin - attracts neutrophils + triggers mast cells -> release vasoactive amines
>   - C3b = opsonin -> triggers phagocytosis 
> - C5 -> C5a + C5b
>   - C5a = chemotaxin - attracts neutrophils + triggers mast cells -> release vasoactive amines
>   - C5b = triggers binding: C6 -> C7 -> C8 -> C9 = MAC formation -> osmotic lysis 

These all ultimately result in the activation of the final common pathway: 
- C3-convertase cleaves C3 into C3a and C3b
- C3b complexes with C3 convertase to form C5-convertase
- C5-convertase cleaves C5 into C5a and C5b
- C5b complexes with C6-9 to form the membrane attack complex

### Host Defences Against Complement
Activation of complement against self is prevented by membrane-bound complement inhibitors on cell surfaces, such as:
- Decay accelerating factor = CD55 -> breaks down C3 convertase
- Membrane cofactor protein -> breaks down C3 convertase
- Homologous restriction factor = C8-binding protein and CD59 -> prevents the formation of the MAC on host cells
  - Deficiencies of CD59 -> [[paroxysmal nocturnal haemoglobinuria]] 
- - -
### Classical Pathway Deficiencies
Deficiencies in C1, C4, and C2 lead to a syndrome of SLE-like autoimmunity due to a failure to clear self-antigen. That being said, recurrent sinopulmonary infections are a common feature, particularly in C2 deficiency.

Management typically involves appropriate vaccinations for meningococcus, haemophilus, and pneumococcus, as well as prophylactic antibiotics. 

Is c1 the most common deficiency -> hereditary angioedema.
- - -
### Common Pathway Deficiencies
C3 deficiencies are clinically indistinguishable from severe antibody deficiencies, presenting with recurrent or severe infections.
- - -
### Terminal Complement Deficiencies 
Deficiencies of the membrane attack complex lead to recurrent infections with _Neisseria_ spp. - e.g. meningitis, meningococcal sepsis, gonococcal arthritis, etc.
- - -
### Alternative Pathway Deficiencies 
Deficiencies in the proteins of the alternative pathway - e.g. Factor B, properdin, Factor D - lead to recurrent Neisserial and bacterial infections.
