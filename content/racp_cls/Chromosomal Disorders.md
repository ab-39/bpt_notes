### Overview
There are numerous chromosomal abnormalities - from changed numbers of whole chromosomes (aneuploidy) to deletions of specific sections of chromosomes. Germ cell chromosomal abnormalities increase with advancing maternal age. (In contrast, advancing paternal age is associated with increased de novo DNA mutation in sperm cells). 
- - -
### Down Syndrome
Trisomy of chromosome 21 typically due to a sporadic mutation arising due to either nondisjunction in meiosis (95%) or a Robertsonian translocation (4%). Occurs in 1/800 live births, with advancing maternal age being the key risk factor. The risk at maternal age 37 is 1/100.

Although the majority of cases are due to sporadic mutations and have a low risk of recurrence, there are cases of germ cells from the parent having unbalanced translocation, leading to three copies of the q arm of chromsome 21, which is associated with a ~10% chance of recurrence. An alternative aetiology is mosaicism.

Clinical features of Down syndrome include:
- Developmental delay
- Intellectual disability
- Characteristic facies
- Muscle hypotonia
- Congenital heart disease
- Increased risk of: CORD, coeliac disease, hypothyroidism, leukaemia, Alzheimer disease (typically early onset), OSA, diabetes, some cancers, seizures

We now screen for T21 using a nuchal translucency scan in the first trimester. There is also non-invasive prenatal testing, however this is not publicly subsidised.

### Turner Syndrome
Monosomy of the sex chromosomes, with partial or complete loss of one X chromosome (hence also called monosomy X). Leads to phenotypically female individuals. Affecting 1/2,500 live births.

Clinical features of Turner syndrome include:
- Growth delay (can be treated with GH)
- Congenital heart abnormalities - bicuspid aortic valve 
- Hypogonadotrophic hypogonadism
- Neurocognitive difficulty 

The karyotypes of Turner syndrome can be more complex, with alternatives including:
- 45,X (monosomy X)
- 45,X/46,XX or 47,XXX (mosaicisim with 46,XX/47,XXX)
- 45,X/46,XY (mosaicism with 46,XY)

### Klinefelter Syndrome
Supernumerary X chromosomes, usually in the form 47,XXY (but also 48,XXXY, etc.) leading to phenotypically male individuals with  hypergonadotrophic hypogonadism. More X chromosomes -> more severe disease. Affects 1/450 live births. Tends to be sporadic.

Clinical features:
- Androgen deficiency
- Small, firm testicles
- Low muscle tone
- Delayed milestones/learning problems
- Low libido/infertility
- Comorbidities include: metabolic syndrome, hypothyroidism, osteoporosis, increased risk of cancer, etc. 

Management involves monitoring of comorbidities and TRT if needed.

### DiGeorge Syndrome
Deletion at chromosome 22, q arm, band 11.2 (22q11.2), typically diagnosed on microarray or FISH. Classically one of the [[well-defined syndromes with immunodeficiency]], characterised by features including:
- Congenital cardiac disease
- Abnormal facies
- Thymic hypoplasia and T-cell deficiency
- Cleft palate
- Hypoparathyroidism
(Hence the mnemonic, _CATCH-22_). Transmitted in an autosomal dominant fashion.
