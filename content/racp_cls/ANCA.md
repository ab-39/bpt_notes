# ANCA
#bpt/rheumatology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
Anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antigens antibodies, two types (referring to indirect immunofluorescence pattern):
1. Central 
   1. ANTI PR3
   2. GPA
2. Peripheral 
   1. MPO
   2. Less specific than cANCA
   3. Can be affected in other diseases 
![](ANCA/image.png)
| Central                                                      | Peripheral                                                   |
|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Anti-proteinase 3                                            | Anti-myeloperoxidase                                         |
| = c-ANCA = PR3-ANCA                                          | = p-ANCA = MPO-ANCA                                          |
| Pattern of cytoplasmic granular fluorescence with central accentuation | Pattern of perinuclear fluorescence with artefactual nuclear extension |
| Disease associations:<br>[[Granulomatous Polyangiitis]] - 90% of cases<br>[[Microscopic Polyangiitis]] - 30% of cases | Disease associations:<br>Microscopic Polyangiitis - 70% of cases<br>Granulomatous Polyangiitis - 10% of cases |
| Atypical variant active against bactericidal permeability-increasing protein (found in IBD, central accentuation absent) | Atypical variants in IBD, active against elastase, cathepsin G, lactoferrin, and HMG proteins |
| Predictive of high relapse rate                              | Predictive of low relapse rate                               |
| Associated with ENT and upper respiratory tract disease      | Associated with haematuria and glomerulonephritis            |
| Levels tend to be high at presentation and fall with treatment, although can persist  | Levels tend to be high at presentation and fall with treatment, typically disappearing |
| Likely that the antibodies themselves are pathogenic         | Very likely that the antibodies themselves are pathogenic; often low levels of p-ANCA in other autoimmune disease  |

i.e. 
> c-ANCA = PR3-ANCA
> p-ANCA = MPO-ANCA 

Can be falsely positive if ANA positive. Atypical ANCA typically not significant. 
- - -
Testing for ANCA is typically carried out to either diagnose or exclude a small-vessel vasculitis, as well as to monitor disease progression after a diagnosis is established (although this is not recommended by the current guidelines). 

Testing for ANCA can also be used in a number of other autoimmune diseases, including IBD, drug-induced vasculitis, rheumatoid vasculitis, PSC, and [[Interstitial Lung Disease]] (although again, the current guidelines do not recommend this). 
- - -
### ANCA in gastrointestinal disease
- Ulcerative colitis - p-ANCA positive in 70% of patients
  - No correlation with disease severity or anatomic extent
- Crohn disease - p-ANCA positive in 10% of patients, typically in UC-like disease
  - Also test for anti-saccharomyces antibodies
- Variably positive antigens: 
  - Primary sclerosing cholangitis - 90% positive
  - Chronic active hepatitis - 70%
- - -
### ANCA and arthritis 
- ANCA is positive in ~20% of patients with rheumatoid arthritis
  - Typically p-ANCA or atypical ANCA
  - Various antigenic specificities
  - May suggest rheumatoid vasculitis
- - -
### ANCA and lung disease 
- [[Cystic fibrosis]] - 90% of patients
  - Correlates with vasculitis, presence of pseudomonas, and worse outcomes
- [[Interstitial lung disease]] - 10% of patients, typically p-ANCA often low level
- Often positive in suppurative lung disease (cf. [[bronchiectasis]])
