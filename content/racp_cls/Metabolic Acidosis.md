### What is metabolic acidosis?
Metabolic acidosis is a process driving a reduction in blood pH, typically either due to:
- An increase in acidic substances
  - Endogenous - e.g. lactate, ketones
  - Exogenous - e.g. toxic alcohols, salicylates 
- A decrease in bicarbonate rich buffer solution
  - e.g. prolonged diarrhoea 
- - -
### Compensation for a metabolic alkalosis
The respiratory system is able to compensate rapidly for a metabolic acidosis through hyperventilation, aiming to decrease CO2 and thereby increase blood pH. Typically:
- PaCO2 decreases by 1.2mmHg for each 1mmol/L decrease in [HCO3-]

Winter’s Formula is used to determine if there is an appropriate degree of respiratory compensation:
> `pCO2 ≈ [1.5*HCO3]+ 8 +/-2`

 pCO2 (actual) > pCO2 (predicted) -> concurrent [[respiratory acidosis]]
 pCO2 (actual) < pCO2 (predicted) ->  concurrent [[respiratory alkalosis]]

Can either by associated with a high or normal [[anion gap]]. 

### HAGMA
Due to increased acid generation - due to KULTs:
1. Ketoacidosis 
2. Uraemia - advanced renal failure,
3. Lactic acidosis 
4. Toxins - methanol, paracetamol, inhalants, etc. 

### NAGMA
Due to either:
* Loss of bicarbonate
* Diminished renal acid excretion 

In NAGMAs, the loss of HCO3 is compensated by an increase in Cl (i.e. “hyperchloremic”).
