### What is ALS/MND?
ALS is a complex neurological disorder that affects both the central and peripheral nervous system, associated with mutations in the superoxide dysmutase 1 gene on chromosome 21. This leads to an increase in glutamate toxicity mediated via redox reactions. 
- - -
### Natural History
ALS typically begins in one, discrete body region, most commonly (~60% of the time) with focal weakness. Other common first presentations include bulbar signs (~20%) or muscle wasting (~10%). Fasciculations, paraesthesiae, and weight loss are relatively uncommon first presentations. 
> Re: muscle wasting - the ‘split hand’ phenomenon, where abductor pollicis brevis and dorsal interossei tend to be more strongly involved than abductor digiti minimi, which in theory is also supplied by the ulnar nerve (DI) and C8/T1 nerve roots (APB), is a relatively common presentation

#### Is there a premorbid ALS phenotype?
Typically, ALS patients:
- Have no previous medical history
- Are usually fit/athletic
- Have a normal/low BMI
- Tend to have a lower family history of CAD/IHD

#### Associations 
It may appear that when the Vikings came out of Scandinavia (particularly Finland) to the United Kingdom, and thence to Australia, they may have brought with them a gene (specifically a hexanucleotide (G4C2) expansion C9ORF72) that carries with _it_ a predisposition to ALS and frontotemporal dementia. 

As such, it appears that ALS may be part of a spectrum of disorders encompassing FTD, and even [[Parkinson’s Disease]] and [[Multiple Sclerosis]]. Most patients with ALS (~50%) have some degree of cognitive impairment (typically higher/executive functions), and are currently classed as follows:
- ’Pure’ ALS - motor symptoms only
- ’ALS plus’ - motor symptoms with subtle (i.e. sub-threshold) executive of behavioural changes
- ALS-FTD - motor symptoms and overt FTD clinical syndrome. Associated with TDP-43 inclusions. 

There is also an association between ALS and metabolic syndrome. Although most patients will present with low BMI due to eating changes, high BMI may be a harbinger of disordered eating associated with FTD.

### Diagnosis of ALS
Imaging: MRI typically does not have any clear signs of A LS, but there may be signal change in the corticospinal tract. There is emerging evidence for iron deposition in the cortex of the pre central gyrus (i.e. Ms1) as a sign of MND. 

Neurophysiological studies play a very important role, with evidence of polyphasic, prolonged motor units, fasciculations, and denervation (fibrillation). 

#### Diagnostic Criteria
Current consensus guidelines: 
- Progressive motor impairment documented by history or repeated clinical assessment, and preceded by normal motor development; and, 
- Presence of **_upper and lower_** motor neurone dysfunction in at ≥1 body region, or lower motor neurone dysfunction in ≥2 body regions; and,
- Investigations excluding other diagnoses

### Variants of ALS
#### Kennedy Disease
Spinobulbar muscular atrophy is an X-linked disorder caused by unstable CAG repeats in the androgen receptor gene on the X-chromosome. It is characterised by adult-onset of progressive weakness due to degeneration of lower motor neurons in the **spinal** cord and **brainstem** (hence spinobulbar). Symptoms include weakness in an LMN distribution, gynaecomastia, defective spermatogenesis, and androgen resistance.

### Treatment of ALS
Treatment of ALS is centred on the role of glutamate and glutamate toxicity. 

The most classic drug used in the treatment of ALS is **riluzole**, which has demonstrated 9-month benefit (i.e. disease remaining milder for longer), but with a similar prognosis at ~5-6y. The dose is 100mg/day. 

Emerging evidence for SOD1 inhibitors. 

#### Role of NIV
NIV has benefit for symptoms - e.g. headaches, fatigue, etc. - but is typically organised in coordination with a respiratory physician. Used in combination with inspiratory muscle training.
