# Inflammatory Bowel Disease 
#bpt/gastroenterology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
There are two major recognised forms of IBD recognised:
- [[Ulcerative Colitis]] - which affects the colon 
- [[Crohn Disease]] - which affects any part of the GIT (hence its old name, regional ileitis)

In addition to this, there is also _microscopic colitis_, which is a closely related condition, and IBD-U (undetermined). 
- - -
### Histopathology
Histopathological findings in IBD can overlap across the two phenotypes, but commonly involve:
- Crypt architectural disruption
- Basal lymphoplasmocytosis 
- Paneth cell metaplasia
- Non-caseating granulomas are pathognomic of Crohn disease.
- Microscopic colitis:
  - Intraepithelial lymphocytosis -> lymphocytic variant
  - Subepithelial collagen deposition -> collagenous variant
- - -
### Presentation
Presentation can vary, but some combination of abdominal pain, diarrhoea (often bloody), and fevers is common.
- - -
### Diagnosis
Direct visualisation of the lumen of the gut with endoscopy or capsule endoscopy is the best method by which to diagnose and assess the extent of the disease. Failing this, enterography can be used, with MR having superior sensitivity and specificity as compared to CT. 
- - -
### Management
#### Biologics
In general, infliximab is the first-line biologic, particularly if there is fistulising Crohn disease. Except for fistulising CD, adalimumab is generally another reasonable choice for first-line. 

Second line therapy is typically with ustekinumab or vedolizumab. Anti-TNF drugs are generally considered to be safer than vedolizumab and ustekinumab in pregnancy. Vedolizumab is generally preferred in the elderly.

### Extra-Intestinal Manifestations
Both CD and UC can have extra-intestinal manifestations, typically affecting women in middle age with colonic disease of either type:
- Skin:
  - Erythema nodosum - tender erythematous nodules on extensor surfaces
  - Pyoderma gangrenosum - papules/pustules/nodules that break down into ulcers with a violaceous, undermined border 
    - Usually traumatic in origin -> avoid triggers
    - Typically on the legs or peristomal
    - Does _not_ correlate with luminal disease 
    - Treatment is with infliximab induction
  - Sweet syndrome - raised tender nodules on the face, arms, and trunk associated with fever and an intense neutrophilic infiltrate on histology (note this is _not_ a vasculitic rash)
    - Unlike erythema nodosum, this is associated with systemic signs - fever, CRP, etc.
    - Correlates with luminal disease -> induce with steroids 
  - Psoriasis
    - If not on an anti-TNF -> treat luminal disease with ustekinumab
    - If on an anti-TNF -> psoriasis is a side effect -> topical treatment if mild; or switch to another anti-TNF if severe
- Joint:
  - Inflammatory arthropathies -> pain at rest improving with movement, morning stiffness
  - Peripheral:
    - Classic -> oligoarthropathy favouring the large joints in the lower limbs, correlates with disease activity
    - Atypical -> small, polyarticular, rheumatoid arthritis-like presentation, does not correlate with disease activity 
  - Axial arthropathy -> rule out ankylosing spondylitis (MRI + HLA-B27)
- Eye:
  - Episcleritis -> red, painful eye, but no impairment of vision; most common ocular manifestation (parallels disease activity)
  - Scleritis -> red eye with impairment of vision (does not parallel disease activity)
  - Anterior uveitis -> red, painful eye with photophobia; can precede the diagnosis of IBD 
- [[Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis]]:
  - Associated primarily with ulcerative colitis, but can be associated with colonic CD
  - Key laboratory finding: elevated ALP
  - Key investigation finding: MRCP (failing that, a liver biopsy)
  - Does _not_ correlate with disease activity
  - No effective treatment 
  - Manage with annual colonoscopies for risk of colon cancer 

> [!NOTE] Correlation with Disease Activity
> The extra-intestinal manifestations of IBD that correlate strongly with luminal disease activity are:
> - Sweet syndrome
> - Type I enteropathic arthritis - large joints in the lower limbs 
> - Erythema nodosum
> - Episcleritis

### Cancer Surveillance in IBD
Both UC and CD carry an increased risk of colorectal cancer, and require screening with colonoscopy that is stratified according to various factors:
- High risk -> annual colonoscopy 
  - Extensive UC or CD >50% of colon, and one or more of:
    - PSC
    - 1st degree relative with CRC <50yo
    - Dysplastic polyp in colitic area in past 5y
    - Colonic stricture
- Medium risk -> colonoscopy every 2-3y
  - Extensive UC or CD >50% of colon, and one or more of:
    - Inflammatory polyps
    - Family history of CRC >50yo
- Low risk -> colonoscopy every 5y
  - Segmental colitis
