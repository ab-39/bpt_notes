### Overview
Gonorrhoea - formerly, ‘the clap’ - is an STI caused by _Neisseria gonorrhoeae_, a Gram-negative bacterium that classically appears as _diplococci_ due to their division by binary fission. 

Transmission is my direct inoculation of infected secretion from one mucous membrane to another (most commonly from male to female, but also during kissing and vaginal delivery), after which there is an incubation period of 1-14 days. The primary sites of infection are the columnar epithelial surfaces of the urethra, endocervic, rectum, pharynx, and conjunctivae. Secondary infection to other sites is also possible.

While gonorrhoea can often be asymptomatic, clinical features can include urethritis, cervicitis, proctitis, and less commonly pharyngitis and conjunctivitis. Complications include epididymo-orchitis, pelvic inflammatory disaese, abscesses, perihepatitis, prostatitis, and disseminated infections (associated with a characteristic rash). Like all STIs, there is an icnreased risk of HIV transmission and acquisition. 

The gold standard for diagnosis is identification of Gram-negative intracellular diplococci with numerous polymorphs on microscopy. The usual method of diagnosis, however, is PCR from swabs from anatomical sites based on sexual history. Culture is important for antimicrobial susceptibility testing due to increased rates of treatment failure and resistance. Resistant strains fo gonorrhoea are on the rise, particulary in North America and Western Europe. 

Treatment is based on local guidelines, but usually involves ceftriaxone with azithromycin. Patients are advised to avoid sexual contact for seven days, and should undergo testing for other diseases and contact tracing.
