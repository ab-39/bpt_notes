### Overview
Hyperemesis is a disease of early pregnancy, running a spectrum from mild nausea and vomiting to full blown hyperemesis gravidarum. The typical course is of nausea and vomiting beginning at 5-6 weeks, peaking at 9 weeks, and then subsiding by 20 weeks. The most severe form of this is HG, which is often defined by:
- Persistent vomiting 
- Weight loss ≥5%
- Mild ketonuria
- LFT derangement - typically a mild transaminitis 

Although there are no “significant” maternal or fetal effects, HG is nonetheless a condition with the potential to cause significant distress.

Management is complex, and centres around:
- Antiemetics
- Thiamine
- Acid suppression
- Laxatives
- IV fluids
- Corticosteroids
