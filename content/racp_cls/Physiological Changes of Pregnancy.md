# Physiological Changes of Pregnancy
#bpt/obstetrics #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Cardiovascular Changes
- Reduced total peripheral resistance (~25%)
- Reduced pulmonary vascular resistance (~25%)
- Increased plasma volume (~40%)
- Increased cardiac output (~30-50%)
- Increased heart rate (~30-50%)
  -> increased stroke volume and RVOT velocity

### Renal Changes
- Increased CrCl/Reduced sCr
- Increased protein excretion **but** <300mg/d 
  - Proteinuria in pregnancy is thus defined as:
    - ≥300mg/d protein in the urine
    - uPCR >30mg/mmol
    - uACR >8mg/mmol
  - In the first trimester, this suggests underlying renal disease
  - After 20/40, new proteinuria is associated with pre-eclampsia 
- Decreased serum urea 
- Increased GFR
- Increased renal plasma flow
- Increased glycosuria 
- Lower threshold for ADH release 
![](Physiological%20Changes%20of%20Pregnancy/image%202.png)
eGFR (measured by the CKD-EPI formula) is not validated for pregnant women!
- - -
### Haematologic Changes
- Reduced platelets, usu. 120-180 (~1% of normal pregnancies will be associated with plt < 100)
- Mildly increased WCC (up to 12)
- Normal coagulation studies 
- Prothrombotic state: increased fibrinogen and vWF, decreased protein S
- Increased red cell turnover -> decreased HbA1c
- - -
### Respiratory Changes 
| **Parameter** | **Change in pregnancy** | **Mechanism** |
|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| **FRC** | ↓ ~20% by third trimester | Diaphragm pushed up by gravid uterus → reduced **ERV + RV → FRC decrease** |
| **DLCO** | ↑ slightly | Increased **pulmonary blood volume** improves gas exchange |
| **FEV1** | ↔ | Airway resistance and lung volumes mostly unchanged → FEV1 stable |
| **FVC** | ↔ | Total lung capacity only slightly reduced, FVC preserved |
- - -
### Hepatic Changes
- Normal bilirubin
- Low transaminases
- ALP ~2-4x ULN
- Reduced albumin (~30-35g/L)
-> any abnormality in transaminases or bilirubin requires evaluation
- - -
### Thyroid Axis Changes 
Increased thyroid hormone synthesis is required to provide for fetal needs, as the fetus is reliant on transplacental transfer of maternal thyroxine (T4), which is then deiodinated in the fetal circulation. Maternal T3 does not cross the placenta. Other changes include:
- Increased TBG
- Increased urinary iodine excretion
- Increased thyroid hormone metabolism
- Reduced TSH in early pregnancy
  - Note that hCG shares a common ⍺-subunit with TSH and has independent thyrotropic activity, causing a reciprocal reduction in TSH
  - This means that the reference range for TSH varies throughout pregnancy, from 0.5miu/L in T1 to normal in T3
