# Sickle Cell Disease
#bpt/haematology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Sickle cell disease is one of the [[haemoglobinopathies]] that is characterised by the production of a defective β-globin molecule that is prone to ‘sickling’ when exposed to hypoxia, and then lodging into the microcirculation and causing the infarctions which are a hallmark of the disease. 

Associated with salmonella infections.
- - -
### Pathophysiology
Caused by a single point mutation substituting valine for glutamate at position six in the _HBB_ gene (E6V) that is inherited in an autosomal recessive fashion.

The resultant molecule, HbS (ααββ) is less soluble in the deoxygenated form than HbA (ααββ), and tends to form networkds of fibrous polymers. This leads to the characteristic sickling of red cells at sites of low PaO2, blocking the microvascular circulation and leading to occlusive crises particularly affecting the spleen, marrow, brain, kidney, lung, and bone.
