### Scleroderma
Scleroderma is the cardinal manifestation of systemic sclerosis, sufficient for diagnosis, and at one time its synecdochical name. 

It is characterised by puffy fingers, followed by proximal progression of skin thickening, which are associated with irreversible joint contractures, which may diminish over time (particularly >5y). 

This is associated with reduced hand function, and the attendant effects of body image, mood, and social isolation.

### [[Interstitial Lung Disease]] 
ILD represents the leading cause of death in SSc. Pathogenesis is likely dependent on autoimmunity, endothelial dysfunction, and fibrosis. Implicated cytokines include TGFβ, IL8, and IL6, as well as TNF⍺. 

Diagnosis is based on clinical features. The most common pattern on HRCT is nonspecific interstitial pneumonitis (80%), while usual interstitial pneumonitis is far less common (10%). Lung biopsy is rarely necessary to make the diagnosis. 

The disease course of SSc-ILD is highly variable, with clinical significance in approximately 40% of patients. The worst prognostic marker is PAH. 

There is a subgroup of patients with SSc-ILD who present with progressive pulmonary fibrosis, defined as at least two of:
1. Worsening respiratory symptoms
2. Physiological evidence of disaese progression (decrease in FVC >5% or decline in DLCO > 10%)
3. Radiological evidence of disease progression

Patients with SSc should be screened at least annually for ILD with PFTs, with an HRCT at diagnosis and then again if there is decline on the PFT. Stable lung function in year one predicts a better prognosis.

### [[Pulmonary Hypertension]]
The most common phenotype of pulmonary hypertension in SSc is _type 1_ ‘pulmonary arterial’ hypertension (i.e. pre-capillary). This has an approximate prevalence of 10% in the SSc cohort. That being said, group 2 PH (due to heart disease) and group 3 PH (due to ILD) are also common.

Risk factors for SSc-PAH are varied. Current recommendations are for annual screening for PAH with echocardiogram.
> Peak tricuspid regurgitation velocity (>2.8m/s) is the echocardiographic screening metric for PAH

### Scleroderma Renal Crisis
Life threatening, hyper-reninaemic, rapidly progressive renal impairment, usually occurring within five years of onset of diffuse SSc in about 10% of patients. Assocaited with RNA-polymerase III antibodies. Triggers include corticosteroids.

Pathophysiology is related to vascular narrowing, and so SRC is often associated with MAHA and thrombocytoopenia. 

Clinically characterised by abrupt onset of moderate to severe hypertension, normal urine sediment/mild proteinuria, and progressive renal failure. We may also see flash APO/heart failure, and signs of malignant hypertension.

### Gut Involvement
[[Gastro-oesophageal Reflux Disease]] is _very_ common, affecting up to 90% of patients with SSc. This is complicated by the tendency of limited SSc to involve oesophageal dysmotility. 

In addition to usual management, there is evidence for prokinetics in SSc - e.g. mosapride or buspirone (5HT1A agonist). 

Faecal incontinence is present in ~30% of patients. Often due to weakning of the internal anal sphincter. First line is to bulk the stool.
