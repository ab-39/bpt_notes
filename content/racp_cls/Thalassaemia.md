# Thalassaemia 
#bpt/haematology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
[[Haemoglobinopathies]] characterised by deficiencies in one or more of the four ⍺-globin chains on chromosome 16 (⍺-Thalassaemia) or the β-globin chains on chromosome 11 (β-Thalassaemia).
- - -
### ⍺-Thalassaemia
⍺-Thalassaemia is defined by reduced synthesis of ⍺-globin chains, inversely proportional in severity to the number of normal alleles present:
- One gene deletion -> silent carrier (⍺⍺/⍺-)
  - Common in Māori, SE Asian, and African population
  - Hb tends to be borderline low (~120g/L), with borderline low MCV (~80fL)
  - No reproductive issues
  - Diagnosis usually made by blood count parameters in the presence of a normal ferritin (to rule out iron deficiency)
- Two gene deletion -> ⍺ trait (⍺-/⍺- = ⍺+/⍺+ **or** ⍺⍺/—)
  - Leads to a mild microcyctic anaemia (MCV 65-75fL)
  - Deletion _in cis_ (SE Asian variant) has reproductive implications
- Three gene deletion -> HbH disease (⍺-/—)
  - Multiple HbH inclusions leading to cell damage and chronic haemolysis
  - Leads to a moderate anaemia (70-100g/L) with significantly reduced MCV (~50-60fL)
  - Generally requires chronic transfusions
- Four gene deletion -> ‘Hb Barts’ = hydrops fetalis (—/—)
  - Significant accumulation of γ tetramers
  - Leads to fetal or early postnatal death _unless_ in utero transfusion
  - Significant maternal complications also

This means that ⍺-thalassaemia can be diagnosed through the presence of excess β and γ chains:
- Excess β-tetramers -> HbH bodies (round intracellular inclusions, pictured)
- Excess γ-tetramers -> Hb Barts (detected via an immunochromatographic strip assay)

### Genetics of ⍺-Thalassaemia
Each copy of chromosome 16 has _two_ copies of the gene encoding ⍺-globin. This means that there are six possible states an individual can be in, genetically:
- Wild type - all four copies of the gene are preserved
- Carrier - asymptomatic - only one copy of the gene on a single chromosome is missing or mutated
- α-Thalassaemia minor - asymptomatic - two copies of the gene are missing or mutated, either:
  - In _trans_ = on different chromosomes -> pass on a single normal copy of the gene to offspring
  - In _cis_ = on the same chromosome -> pass on _no normal copies_ of the gene to offspring; often has a hypochromic microcytic anaemia (common in SE Asia)
- HbH disease - symptomatic - no functional copies of the gene on one chromosome, and only a single functional copy of the gene on the other 
- Haemoglobin Barts/Hydrops Fetalis - no functional copies of the gene

### β-Thalassaemia
β-Thalassaemia is defined by reduced synthesis of β-globin chains. Usually caused by mutations that lead to either reduced (β+) or absent (β0) expression of that gene. There are numerous (~250) genes with variable severity, ranging from mild (β+/β) to severe (β0/β0). The diagnostic test for β-thalassaemia is an increased HbA2 (⍺2/δ2). The genotype corresponds to the phenotype:
- Heterozygous/trait -> β-thalassaemia minor 
- Homozygeous **or** compound heterozygous:
  - Transfusion-dependent β-thalassaemia (_major_), clinical features include:
    - Severe anaemia (Hb 30-70gL)
    - Microcytosis (50-70fL)
    - Predominantly HbF (>90%)
    - Transfusion-dependence
    - Expanded bone marrow -> bony deformities
    - Hypersplenism
    - Hypercoagulable
    - Iron overload (2’ to transfusions)
  - Non-transfusion-dependent β-thalassaemia (_intermedia_)

### Clinical Spectrum
Patients with thalassaemias or other haemoglobinopathies run a spectrum from minimally affected, requiring few transfusions, to those who are transfusion dependent for survival. 

Transfusion-dependent patients usually require 3-4 pRBC every 3-4 weeks, which runs the risk of iatrogenic iron overload, for which chelation is the usual treatment:
- Deferasirox
- Deferipone
- Desferrioxamine

As patients progress, luspatercept is another option to help with maturing erythrocytes. HSCT is another option.

Emerging therapies are targeting hepcidin, erythropoietic pathways, induction of HbF synthesis, and reduction of ⍺-globin synthesis.

### Combinations
#### More Favourable
One allele each for ⍺ and β thalassaemia can actually ameliorate the condition by correcting the globin chain imbalance, thus leading to fewer precipitates of excess chains.

#### Less Favourable
- β-thalassaemia + HbE ->  generally more transfusion-dependence 
- β-thalassaemia + HbS -> worsens sickle trait as relatively more HbS
