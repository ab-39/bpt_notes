# Lambert-Eaton Myasthenic Syndrome
#bpt/neurology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
LEMS is a typically paraneoplastic [[neuromuscular junction disorder]] that is defined by pathological antibodies against P and Q type voltage-gated calcium channel (anti-VGCC). This leads to a deficiency of presynaptic acetylcholine release, and as such, a failure of neuromuscular transmission leading to skeletal muscle weakness, areflexia/hyporeflexia, and autonomic dysfunction. The ‘classic’ triad is:
- Proximal weakness
- Autonomic features - dry mouth, erectile dysfunction, constipation, etc.
- Areflexia - can improve after exercise

Sensory symptoms are typically mild. It is most common in older males, and is strongly associated with [[small cell lung cancer]]. Investigations include anti-VGCC antibodies, NCS/EMG (repetitive stimulation shows decrementing with slow rates and incrementing with fast rates), and screening for malignancy. Treatment is typically symptomatic with 3,4-diaminopyridine (blocks K+ channels to increase action potential duration) +/- pyridostigmine; as well as IVIG/PLEX/immunosuppression. 

> [!NOTE] DDx
> Unlike MG, where muscles are fatiguable, LEMS classically shows a temporary improvement after exercise.
