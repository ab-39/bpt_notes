# Cytomegalovirus Infection
#bpt/renal #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Cytomegalovirus is a common cause of viral infection that is associated with rejection following [[renal transplantation]]. Patients at highest risk are those for whom the _donor is CMV positive while the recipient is negative_, which is also associated with disease severity. 

Transplant patients should either receive primary prophylaxis or weekly screening with prompt treatment depending on their serology status:
- D+/R- -> 6m prophylaxis
- D+ or D- and R+ -> either strategy for 3m
- D-/R- -> no prophylaxis unless on ATG

The only factor limiting prophylaxis is neutropenia, and dose must be adjusted according to GFR, with the caveat that reduced dose can lead to viral resistance. There is a risk of infection post-cessation of prophylaxis, so ongoing monitoring is indicated. 

Treatment is typically with high-dose PO valganciclovir, although IV ganciclovir can be used if the enteral route presents difficulty or in severe cases. If there is resistance to these drugs, the next line is foscarnet or cidofovir, although these can be nephrotoxic. Antimetabolites are particularly associated with CMV infection, so immunosuppression must be carried out carefully. Ongoing monitoring is recommended. Secondary prophylaxis is not recommended.
