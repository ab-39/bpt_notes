### What is ARDS?
ARDS is lung injury that affects both the endothelium and the epithelium of the lung:
- Endothelial Side:
  - Disruption of tight junctions and fluid leakage
  - Loss of endothelial glycocalyx
  - RBC injury
  - Neutrophil transmigration
  - Platelet microthrombi
- Epithelial Side:
  - Severe oedema formation
  - Severe disruption of tight junctions
  - Epithelial necrosis
  - Hyaline membrane formation
  - Absent sodium and chlorine transport
- - -
### What causes ARDS?
ARDS can arise secondary to direct or indirect lung injury:
- Direct
  - Aspiration
  - Smoke/vapour 
  - Near drowning
  - Pneumonia
  - Pulmonary contusion
  - VILI
- Indirect
  - Neurogenic
  - Reperfusion injury (e.g. post-transplant or endarterectomy)
  - Drug toxicity
  - TRALI
  - Pancreatitis
  - Sepsis
  - Severe trauma
  - Fat emboli

### ARDS Phenotypes
There are two main phenotypes of ARDS:
| Feature       | Hyperinflammatory                                            | Hypoinflammatory               |
|---------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Biological    | Generally more systemically unwell                           |                                |
| CT            | Diffuse and patchy loss of aeration                          | Dorsal-inferior consolidations |
| Responsive to | PEEP                                                         | Proning                        |
| Physiologic   | Recruitable                                                  | Non-recruitable                |
| Anatomic      | Greater dead space, greater non-inflated lung, lower compliance, lower PF ratio |                                |
| Outcome       | Higher risk of death                                         |                                |
