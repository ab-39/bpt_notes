### What is coeliac disease?
Coeliac disease is an CD8T-cell-mediated systemic disorder elicited by gluten in genetically susceptible people, characterised by a small-intestine enteropathy. 

Coeliac disease is intimately associated with the HLA-DQ2/8 gene. The environmental antigen is dietary gluten, a  protein found in wheat, rye, and barley. Other risk factors include infectious episodes (particularly rotavirus), high gluten intake in early life, female sex, and family history.
- - -
### Epidemiology
Coeliac disease is common, affecting about 1% of Australians. Its global prevalence varies significantly from country to country, apparently higher in areas with high wheat intake. 

Coeliac disease is strongly associated with T1DM and autoimmune thyroid disease, likely due to involvement of the underlying genetic locus.

Strong association with microscopic colitis.

### Coeliac serology
In Australia, we primarily use two antibodies for serological testing in coeliac disease:
- Anti-tissue transglutaminase
- Anti-deamidated gliadin peptide 

There are two additional antibodies, anti-endomysial antibody and anti-gliadin antibody, but these are outdated and rarely used in Australia. 

To perform serology testing, we use anti-tTG in combination with either anti-DGP or total IgA.

### Diagnosis of coeliac disease 
Definitive diagnosis of coeliac disease is based on biopsy, which is characterised by a triad of:
- Villous atrophy
- Crypt cell hyperplasia
- Intraepithelial lymphocytes

Although classic in coeliac disease, these features alone are not enough for a diagnosis, and serology must also be positive. Another important aspect is an improvement on subsequent biopsy with gluten avoidance. 

In children, if anti-tTG antibodies are ≥10x the upper level of normal, it can be considered diagnostic of coeliac disease. 

The coeliac HLA gene has very poor sensitivity but excellent sensitivity and NPV -> it can be used as a rule out test in people with an equivocally positive serology.

### Management
Avoid gluten - lots of secondary stuff (bloods, family screening, vaccination due to the higher risk of hyposplenism, etc.)

There is a risk of disease being ‘refractory’ to at least twelve months of a strict gluten free diet with ongoing symptoms of malabsorption.
- Consider differentials:
  - Drug-related enteropathies (e.g. ARBs)
  - Tropical sprue
  - Autoimmune enteropathy
  - Giardia
  - Crohn’s disease 
Most common in people with coeliac who have undergone extended gluten exposure. This is considered a pre-cancerous state due to the high-risk of EATLs. There are two forms:
- T1 - normal IEL phenotype -> treated with immunosuppression 
- T2 - abnormal IEL phenotype -> treated with chemotherapy +/- ASCT

### Long Term Risk and Complications
Fatty liver disease - due to higher fat % in coeliac disease.
There is a risk of development of enteropathy-associated T-cell lymphoma (EATLs), which is increased with ongoing epithelial lymphocytosis.

Generally see deficiencies in:
- Iron
- Fat soluble vitamins
- Vitamin B12 in 25-50% of cases, typically if there is terminal ileal involvement or autoimmune gastritis
- Folate
- Copper
- Vitamin D
