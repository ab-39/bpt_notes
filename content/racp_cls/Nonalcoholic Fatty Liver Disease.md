# Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease 
#bpt/gastroenterology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
NAFLD, properly “metabolic dysfunction associated steatoic liver disease”, is an umbrella term covering the entire spectrum of fatty liver disease:
- Nonalcoholic fatty liver -> ≥5% hepatic steatosis without injury
- Nonalcoholic steatohepatitis -> ≥5% hepatic steatosis with inflammation and hepatocyte ballooning, but without fibrosis
- NASH cirrhosis -> cirrhosis with evidence of current or previous NASH

In general, NAFLD refers to fatty liver arising from a metabolic aetiology, and its relationship to the metabolic syndrome is understood to be bidirectional. Pathogenesis is related to the development of insulin resistance in response to lifestyle factors, leading to an inflammatory cascade driving the deposition of lipids in and ultimately fibrosis of the liver. Fibrosis is ultimately the most significant predictor of progressive disease. 
- - -
### Epidemiology
NAFLD is _extremely_ common, with an estimated prevalence of 25%, that is only on the up in parallel with the increase of obesity. Although <10% go on to develop cirrhosis or HCC, this obviously corresponds to a massive number, and, indeed, NAFLD is the second leading cause of end stage liver disease and HCC. 

The most common cause of death in patients with NAFLD is cardiovascular disease, likely due to the bidirectional relationship with the [[Metabolic Syndrome]].

### Risk Factors
- Obesity 
- Type II Diabetes 
- Metabolic syndrome/dyslipidaemia
- PCOS
- _PNPLA3_ genotype

### Investigations
MRI is highly useful for identifying low levels of steatosis, but once it is advanced, ultrasound is the most commonly used test.

Laboratory tests are often non-specific:
- AST and ALT -> typically x2 ULN
  - AST:ALT <1 (unlike alcoholic liver disease), this increases with disease 
- The degree of transaminitis does not reflect the degree of liver injury
- Ferritin >1.5x ULN is associated with NASH and fibrosis 
- Often normal 

The gold-standard test is liver biopsy, but diagnosis can be made on the basis of imaging.

Transient elastography, known commercially as FibroScan, is a method of measuring the ’stiffness’ of the liver by measuring the pressure generated by an ultrasonic wave as it travels through the liver. The stiffer the liver, the higher the pressure, and thus the higher the chance of fibrosis. This test has a strong NPV.

### Grading
The _NAFLD Activity Score_ (NAS) is used to grade disease severity based on histopathological appearance:
- Steatosis
- Lobular inflammation
- Hepatocellular ballooning
Each of which is graded from 0-3. A score ≥5 is considered diagnostic of NASH. 

The NAFLD fibrosis score has a good [[negative predictive value]] for excluding cirrhosis.

### Management
Lifestyle:
- Weight loss -> histological resolution of NASH occurred in 90% of individuals in one study with 10% weight reduction
- Mediterranean diet
- Exercise 

Lipid modification:
- Evidence-free zone
- General consensus is that statins do not have a specific additional role in NAFLD

Pharmacotherapy:
- Limited to patients with biopsy-proven fibrosis
- Metformin helps with insulin sensitivity, but does not change liver histology 
- Pioglitazone has been shown to improve histologic parameters including fibrosis
- Vitamin E
- Both of these are niche treatments 

Surgical:
- Bariatric surgery
- NAFLD is the third most common indication for liver transplantation
  - May recur 

Future therapies:
- GLP1RA - e.g. semaglutide
  - +GIPRA -> tirzepatide
  - +glucagon RA -> survodutide 
- FXR (i.e. bile receptor) antagonists (obeticholic acid)
- CCR5a -> antifibrotics 
- PPAR agonists -> elafibrinor, lanifibrinor 
- Liver-directed thyroid hormone receptor agonist -> resmetirom
