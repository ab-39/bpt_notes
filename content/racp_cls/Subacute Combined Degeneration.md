# Subacute Combined Degeneration
#bpt/neurology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview 
Subacute combined degeneration is a manifestation of vitamin B12 deficiency as an [[Acquired Peripheral Neuropathy]]. It has a well recognised constellation of clinical symptoms, involving sudden onset, painless sensory symptoms, typically in the both upper and lower limbs, and hyperpigmentation of the knuckles. There is typically a hyperintensity in the dorsal column of the spinal cord on MRI. 

It is caused by vitamin B12 deficiency, either due to autoimmune (e.g. pernicious anaemia), dietary (e.g. veganism), or recreational drug use (e.g. nangs). The presentation is with a diffuse, symmetric myelopathy, often with subacute paraesthesiae in the hands and feet, loss of vibration sense and proprioception, and a progressive spastic and ataxic weakness. Diagnosis is confirmed by findings of macrocytosis, low serum B12, and elevated levels of homocysteine and methylmalonic acid. Treatment is with high-dose IM vitamin B12 replacement. Key differentials include folate deficiency myelopathy and hypocupric myelopathy.
