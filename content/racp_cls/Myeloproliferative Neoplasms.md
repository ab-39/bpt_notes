# Myeloproliferative Neoplasms
#bpt/haematology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
The myeloproliferative neoplasms are characterised by a proliferation of haematopoietic cell lineages in the bone marrow, spleen, and liver that manifest as terminal myeloid expansion in the peripheral blood. These syndromes are characterised by somatic driver mutations affecting the JAK-STAT pathway. The WHO 2022 classification recognises several MPNs, of which the term often is used to refer to [[polycythaemia vera]], [[essential thrombocytosis]], and [[primary myelofibrosis]]. That said, other conditions such as [[chronic myeloid leukaemia]],  chronic neutrophilic leukaemia, and chronic eosinophilic leukaemia also fall into this category.
- - -
### Aetiology
Although family clustering of MPNs is well-described, the pathognomic driver mutations are all somatic. ~1/10 people with an MPN will have a first-degree relative who also has an MPN. Other factors include risk factors such as radiation, occupation (poultry workers, petroleum workers), chemical exposure (benzene, petroleum), comorbidities ([[Crohn disease]], [[giant cell arteritis]]), smoking, and BMI. 

#### Driver Mutations
Major diagnostic criteria for the myeloproliferative neoplasms include:
- _JAK2_ V617F (exon 14) - occurs in all MPN subtypes; positive in 97% of cases of PRV, most common in ET	
  - This is leads to a loss of inhibition by the regulatory JAK2 subunit
- _CALR_
  - Only in ET and PMF
  - Typically deletions or insertions in exon 9
  - Deletion = type 1 -> myelofibrosis phenotype with favourable prognosis 
  - Insertion = type 2 -> ET phenotype albeit with low risk of thrombosis 
- _MPL_ - only in ET and PMF
- _JAK2_ exon 12/13 mutations - exclusive to PRV; positive in the remaining 3% of patients 

The mechanism by which the JAK2V617F mutation contributes to the pathogenesis of myeloproliferative neoplasms is constitutive activation of the JAK2 tyrosine kinase. This mutation occurs in the pseudokinase domain (JH2) of JAK2, disrupting its normal autoinhibitory function and leading to persistent kinase activity independent of cytokine stimulation. Constitutively active JAK2V617F phosphorylates downstream targets, most notably STAT transcription factors, resulting in continuous activation of the JAK-STAT signaling pathway. This drives uncontrolled proliferation and survival of myeloid progenitor cells, leading to clonal expansion and the clinical features of polycythemia vera, essential thrombocythemia, and primary myelofibrosis.

There is also a role for clonal marker testing in patients who are ‘triple negative’.

### Investigations
- FBE + blood film
- Biochemistry including LDH
- Iron studies (note: iron deficiency may ‘mask’ PRV)
- Driver mutation testing - _JAK2, CALR, MPL_ 
- Spleen assessment
- BMAT + cytogenetics
  - Important for:
    - Diagnosing PRV
    - Differentiating between prefibrotic PMF and ET
    - Exclude CML that presents with thromboytosis
    - Prognostic information
  - Much of this comes down to the megakaryocyte morphology:
    - Normal megakaryocytes -> reactive thrombocytosis
    - MKCs + ring sideroblasts -> MDS/MPN overlap
    - Small megakaryocytes -> CML
    - Large megakaryocytes in loose clusters -> ET
    - Hyperchromatic, irregularly folded MKCs -> early PMF
- - -
### Prognosis
ET has the best sutvival, followed by PRV, with the mortality of PMF being significantly higher due to the risk of leukaemic transformation being ~10%. 

> [!NOTE] MPNs and Budd-Chiari Syndrome
> MPNs are the single most common cause of the Budd-Chiari syndrome (thrombosis of the hepatic vein), accounting for approximately 50% of all cases.
- - -
### Leukaemic Transformation
Any MPN can transform to myeloid leukaemia, but the risk is highest with PMF (2%/y), then PRV (0.2%/y), and minimal in ET. After the blast phase (= >20% blasts in BM) has developed, survival is very poor, typically under 1y even with chemotherapy. ASCT can prolong life expectancy to a few years.
