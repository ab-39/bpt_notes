### Management = [[Diuretics]] 
Intravenous loop diuretics are recommended in patients with acute heart failure associated with congestion in order to improve symptoms of fluid overload, and form the mainstay of treatment for ADHF. It is worth noting that although this is a strong recommendation in the current guidelines, it is supported by relatively poor evidence from trials.

In general:
1. Loop diuretics - e.g. frusemide, bumetanide
2. Thiazides and thiazide-like diuretics - augment Na+ excretion by ~5%, but can help a) if frusemide resistance develops (sequential nephron blockade) or b) if the patient has comorbid hypertension
3. Acetazolamide - the [_ADVOR_ trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2203094) found that IV acetazolamide increases the success of decongestion in ADHF in combination with loop diuretics, but did not improve mortality

#### Other Treatments:
Vasodilators:
- May be considered in patients with ADHF where the systolic blood pressure is >90mmHg to relieve symptoms of congestion

Inotropes:
- May be considered in patients with ADHF **and** evidence of peripheral hypoperfusion (should be avoided otherwise)
