# Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome
#bpt/respiratory #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### What is obesity hypoventilation syndrome?
OHS is a triad of:
1. Awake hypercapnia (PaCO2 > 45mmHg)
2. BMI >30kg/sqm
3. Sleep disordered breathing (when other causes of hypoventilation have been excluded)
- - -
### Epidemiology
Population prevalence is low (~0.5%), but increases with BMI (50% in BMI >50). 
- - -
### Presentation
Common to present with chronic [[type 2 respiratory failure]] or signs of right heart disease.  This is associated with a reduction in expiratory reserve volume. 
- - -
### Pathophysiology
Leptin is implicated in the pathophysiology of OHS:
- Obesity -> leptin resistance -> high circulating leptin -> reduced respiratory responsiveness

This is in addition to the increased mechanical load implicit in obesity. This is complicated by skeletal muscle atony in REM, which makes overcoming this mechanical load more difficult. 
- - -
### Complications of OHS
OHS is strongly associated with cardiovascular disease, including HTN (in up to 80% of patients), [[pulmonary hypertension]], and right heart failure. 

All cause mortality is ~25% at 2 years for untreated OHS. 
- - -
### Treatment of OHS
[[Continuous positive airway pressure]] improves hypercapnia and long term mortality. If there is a significant burden of sleep hypoventilation (as opposed to OSA), then there may need to be a consideration of [[non-invasive ventilation]] (i.e. BPAP). For OHS alone, however, there is no difference in outcomes between CPAP and BPAP, so CPAP is usually preferred in the first instance.
