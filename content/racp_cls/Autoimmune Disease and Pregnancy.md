# Autoimmune Disease and Pregnancy
#bpt/obstetrics #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Almost _all_ autoimmune diseases have a risk of flare or deterioration during pregnancy and the postpartum period. The two exceptions to this are:
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Grave disease 

Pregnant women exist in an immunomodulated state:
- The TH2 cell response is _increased_, with predominantly anti-inflammatory cytokines such as [[interleukin 10]] and TGF-β being expressed
- The TH1 cell response is _decreased_, with inflammatory cytokines such as IL-12, IL-2, and IFN-γ being suppressed

While this increases the success rate of pregnancy and reduces susceptibility to _TH2-mediated_ inflammatory conditions, it can also increase susceptibility to infectious diseases.

### Transplacental Autoantibodies
Fetal and neonatal effects tend to be rare, but there are some important considerations:
- Sjögren Syndrome/[[Systemic Lupus Erythematosus]] - anti-Ro/anti-La (SSA/SSB) antibodies are associated with an increased risk of congenital heart block
- Grave disease - TRABs may cause fetal thyrotoxicosis 
- [[Immune Thrombocytopenia]] - antibodies may cause fetal thrombocytopenia
- [[Myasthenia Gravis]] - transient neonatal MG in 10-20% of infants born to mothers with ACHR+ve MG
