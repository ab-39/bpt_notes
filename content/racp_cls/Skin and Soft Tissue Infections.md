### Overview
An extremely common, frequently benign group of conditions that are leading causes of avoidable hospitalisation. Associated conditions include:
- [[Cellulitis]]
- Erysipelas
- Superficial pyoderma 
- Necrotising infections
- Diabetic foot infections
- - -
### Microbiology
Common organisms causing SSTIs include:
- Group A _Streptococci_
- Group C and G _Streptococci_
- _Staphylococcus_ spp. incl. _aureus_
- - -
### Necrotising Fasciitis 
_Necrotising fasciitis_ is a medical emergency which ‘only steel can heal’. While rare, they cannot be missed and require urgent surgical debridement. Some clincial signs that can point in the direction of NSTI include toxic shock, pain or a clinical syndrome out of proportion with the extent of the cellulitis, purplish discolouration, violaceous bullae, crepitus. Although MRI can be useful, it can be difficult to obtain urgently. While NSTI is frequently polymicrobial, _S. pyogenes_ is the most common organism. Part of the sepsis response in NSTI is driven by pyrogenic exotoxins (SpeA, SpeB, SpeC) that _Streptococcus_ spp. Antibiotics such as _clindamycin_ and _linezolid_ can directly reduce toxin production through ribosomal interference.
