### Overview
Aortic aneurysm refers to pathologic dilatation of the aorta, anywhere along its course. When thinking about an aneurysm, it is useful to consider the [[Physics for Medicine/Law of Laplace|law of Laplace]], that states that wall tension varies as pressure and radius -> controlling blood pressure is critically important to lowering the risk of dissection!

Risk factors include the traditional cardiac risk factors, as well as infection, inflammation, and trauma. Genes implicated in aortic aneurysm include *FBN, COL3A1, ACTA2, MLCK, and MYH11*. Bicuspid aortic valve (BAV) is another important risk factor, associated with ascending aortic aneurysm, as well as complex [[congenital heart disease]] (i.e. TOF, TA, TGA). Fluoroquinolones can weaken aortic aneurysms and increase the risk of rupture.

Proper assessment of the size of an aneurysm is limited by instrument error, typically to a range of about 3mm. The best modality is ECG-gated CT scan, but serial imaging is often carried out with echocardiography. Normal aortas grow at a rate of about 1mm every 10y, and as such, rapid growth typically reflects either measurement error or aortic dissection. 

The mainstay of medical management for aortic aneurysm is blood pressure management, typically with β-blockade. For Marfan syndrome, there is good evidence that losartan is able to reduce the rate of growth of the aneursym, and this is assumed to be a class-related effect generalising to other ARBs. Failing that, surgery can be carried out for specific indications:
- Ascending aorta: >55mm
- BAV + risk factors: >50mm
- Marfan syndrome: >50mm
- Loeys-Dietz syndrome: >42mm

There may be an increased risk of aneurysm expansion with CCBs. In the case of dissection:
- Type A: surgery is key, with IV β-blockade or CCB to temporise 
- Type B: management typically is with IV β-blockade first, followed by nitrates. Nitrates should not be used alone as their vasodilatory properties can increase aortic shear stress.

### Familial Aortopathies
- Marfan syndrome -> _FBN1_
- Loeys-Dietz syndrome -> _TGFBR1_, _TGFBR2_, _TGFB2_, _TGFB3_, _SMAD2_
  - Similar to Marfan syndrome with the absence of ectopia lentis
- Vascular Ehler-Danlos syndrome -> _COL3A1_
- Shprintzen-Goldberg syndrome -> _SKI_
- Congenital contractural aneurysm syndrome -> _FBN2_
- BGN- associated X-linked aortic aneurysm syndrome -> _BGN_
- Turner syndrome
- Arterial tortuosity syndrome -> _SLC2A10_
- Cutis laxa -> polygenic 
- Biscuspid aortic valve
- HTAD genes
- FTAAD genes
