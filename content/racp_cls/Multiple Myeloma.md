# Multiple Myeloma
#bpt/haematology #bpt/racp_cls

### Overview
_Multiple myeloma_ refers to the neoplastic proliferation of plasma cells that is characterised by the production of a monoclonal immunoglobulin. 

The plasma cell dyscrasias exist on a spectrum with ‘monoclonal gammopathy of unknown significance’ in the middle, with disease states existing at either end: on one hand, direct toxicity from the M-proteins, and on the other, conversion into cancer. 
![](Multiple%20Myeloma/image.png)
In addition to multiple myeloma, there are numerous other plasma cell dyscrasias:
- Monoclonal gammopathies - e.g. cold agglutinin disease, MGUS, MGRS, etc.
- Diseases with monoclonal Ig deposition - e.g. AL [[amyloidosis]], etc.
- Heavy chain diseases 
- Plasma cell neoplasms

### Progression
The Mayo Clinic criteria gives a straightforward approach to assessing the risk of progression of MGUS:
- M-protein >15g/L
- FLC ratio <0.26 or >1.65
- Non-IgG MGUS

The 20y risk of progression is as follows:
- One risk factor -> 20% (i.e. 1%/y)
- Two risk factors -> 40% (i.e. 2%/y)
- Three risk factors -> 60% (i.e. 3%/y)

A solitary plasmacytoma of bone has a ~50% 10y risk of progressing to multiple myeloma (cf. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5771205/).

### Pathobiology
The genetic variants that drive multiple myeloma begin to accumulate long before disease, or even MGUS, is evident. Examples of such abnormalities include:
- IgH translocatoins
- Cyclin D dysregulation
- Deletion of chromosome 13
- _NRAS_
- _KRAS_ and _FGFR3_
- _MYC_ 
- _TP53_

Another important concept is that of _clonal waves_: i.e. that selective pressure causes subsequent generations of clones of disease, one of which may eventually become treatment resistant.

### Epidemiology
MM accounts for ~1% of all cancer cases, and has a slight male predilection.

### Diagnostic Criteria
#### MGUS
All three of:
- M-protein <30g/L
- Clonal plasma cells <10% on BMAT
- No myeloma-defining events (SLiM-CRAB)

#### Smoldering or Asymptomatic Myeloma 
All three of:
- M-protein ≥30g/L **or** ≥500mg/d in urine
  - Note _urinary_ light chains = Bence-Jones proteins 
- Clonal plasma cells 10-60% on BMAT
- No myeloma-defining events (SLiM-CRAB)

If M-protein is greater than 20g/L, or clonal plasmacyte percentage is greater than 20%, or the FLC ratio is greater than 20, this is considered _high-risk_ smouldering myeloma and can be considered for treatment.

#### Multiple Myeloma
- Clonal plasma cells ≥10% on BMAT **or** ≥1 biopsy-proven plasmacytoma **and** one or more MM-defining events:
  - ≥CRAB feature:
    - Hypercalcaemia
    - Renal insufficiency - mechanisms: cast nephropathy, light chain deposition disease, AL amyloidosis, Fanconi syndrome, hypercalcaemia, hyperuricaemia
    - Anaemia
    - Bone lytic lesions - by two mechanisms:
      - MM directly inhibits RANKL -> unopposed osteoclastic activity
      - Direct plasmacytic lesions in the bone 
  - ≥1 biomarker of malignancy (SLiM):
    - Sixty: clonal plasma cells ≥60% on BMAT
    - Light chains: serum FLC ratio ≥100
    - MRI lesions: ≥2 lesions on MRI ≥5mm in size 

#### Hyperviscosity Syndromes
While not diagnostic of MM, hyperviscosity syndromes are a rare but significant presentation of multiple myeloma, and indicate plasmapheresis to reduce overall blood viscosity. This is a complication particularly of IgM (due to its pentameric structure) and IgA. Symptoms include confusion, visual changes, mucosal haemorrhage, retinal flame haemorrhages, and high output CCF. In general, we worry about the following Ig thresholds:
- IgM >50g/L
- IgA >70g/L
- IgG >100g/L

### Staging
Staging is largely based on albumin, β2-microglobulin, LDH, and certain high risk genetic variants:
| Stage | Criteria                                                     | 5y mOS |
|-------|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------|
| I     | Albumin >35g/L<br>**and**<br>β2-MG <3.5mg/L<br>**and** <br>Normal LDH<br>**and** <br>No high risk cytogenetics | 80%    |
| II    | Intermediate                                                 | 60%    |
| III   | β2-MG >5.5mg/L<br>**and** <br>Elevated LDH **or** <br>High risk cytogenetics  | 40%    |
Relevant high risk cytogenetic variants are:
- t4;14
- t14;16
- del17p 

#### Investigation Findings:
- FBE -> normocytic anaemia, cytopenias
- Film -> rouleaux (due to high circulating globulins), circulating plasma cells
- UEC -> as above
- Serum/Urine EPG -> M-protein spike (‘church steeple’) 
- Free light chain assay -> sFLC ratio (note that levels may be abnormal in benign conditoins, but the ratio being off indicates a monoclonal plasma cell disorder)
- BMAT -> Dutcher bodies, flame cells, Russell bodies, grape cells, CD138-positivity

- Cytogenetic techniques (i.e. [[genetic testing]]):
  - FiSH is best
  - DNA array runs the risk of missing balanced translocations
  - Conventional cytogenetics is limited by low mitotic index of myeloma cells
  - Next generation sequencing is emerging overseas - the only way to detect _TP53_ mutations

### Treatment
Key classes:
- Proteasome inhibitors - e.g. bortezomib, carfilzomib
  - Inhibition of the proteasome leads to protein accumulation in the cell and eventual cell death
  - Side effects: periphereal neuropathy, nausea, vomiting
- Immunomodulators/cereblon E3 ligase modulators - e.g. lenalidomide, pmalidomide, thalidomide
- Monoclonal antibodies - e.g. daratumumab (anti-CD38), elotuzumab (anti-CD319)
  - Note that daratumumab can interfere with group and hold assays 
- SINE inhibitors/XPOR1 antagonists - e.g. selinexor
- [[Autologous stem cell transplantation]] 

In general, management depends on if patients are fit for a transplant or not:
- Transplant fit:
  1. Bortezomib + cyclophosphamide + dex (VCD)
  2. Bortezomib + lenalidomide + DX (VRD) 
  3. Melphalan conditioning + autoSCT (associated with improved PFS but not mOS)
  4. Lenalidomide maintenance prolongs remission
- Transplant unfit:
  1. VRD (or else doublets)
  2. Lenalidomide maintenance
  3. Relapse #1 -> consider carfilzomib-based therapy
  4. Relapse #2 -> consider pomalidomide or daratumumab based therapy 

Patients who are ‘MRD negative’ (i.e. undetectable plasma cells) seem to have good survival irrespective of transplant status. 

As of 2024, MDAC has recommended CAR-T therapy for funding in Australia to treat myeloma. 

#### Immunotherapy in Myeloma
There are three main classes of immunotherapy available to treat myeloma:
1. Antibody-drug conjugates
2. [[Bispecific T-cell engagers]]
3. [[Chimeric antigen receptor T-cells]] 

The emerging evidence for CAR-T therapy is based on the _CARTITUDE_ studies, which found improvements in PFS and OS in patients refractory to three prior lines of therapy when treated with ciltacabtagene. 

There are multiple BiTEs, which tend to target plasma cell speficic antigens (e.g. BCMA, FcRH5, etc.) as well as CD3, essentially yoking cytotoxic T-cells to plasma cells and B-cells, enabling destruction of the tumour. The _MAGNETISM_ study found improvements in PFS in patients who had failed five prior lines of therapy upon treatment with elranatamab. The main side effects tend to be hypogammaglobulinaemia, and there is some concern about BCMA expression in the brain.
