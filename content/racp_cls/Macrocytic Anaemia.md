# Macrocytic Anaemia
#bpt/haematology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
The macrocytic anaemias are characterised by red blood cells with an MCV >100fL, and often as high as 130fL. These can either be megaloblastic or non-megaloblastic in nature:
- Megaloblastic = large erythroid precursors -> DNA problem arising due to B12/folate/homocysteine problems e.g. pernicious anaemia, vegan diet, folate deficiency, etc.
  - Note that B12 deficiency is associated with elevated levels of homocysteine and methylmalonic acid, and reduced levels of holotranscobalamin (the active form of B12 taken up into cells) 
    - Bactrim!
  - Antibodies against intrinsic factor are the most specific test for pernicious anaemia 
- Non-megaloblastic -> liver disease, alcohol, hypothyroidism, or myelodysplastic syndrome
  - Drugs: phenytoin, cytotoxics (think of hydroxycarbamide!!), antivirals, etc.
  - Myeloma
  - Haemochromatosis
  - Smoking
  - Aplastic anaemia -> pancytopenia + macrocytosis (similar to MDS)
