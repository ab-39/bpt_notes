# Thyroid Disease in Pregnancy
#bpt/obstetrics #bpt/endocrinology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Women who are pregnant, planning a pregnancy, or breastfeeding are recommended to take an iodoine supplement of 150microg/d. Targeted testing to hypothyroidism is only recommended if:
- Personal history of thyroid disease
- T1DM
- Symptoms of thyroid disease 

For patients with known hypothyroidism, levothyroxine should be adjusted to target a TSH level in the lower half of the trimester specific reference range (e.g. 2-3 extra doses/w). This should be monitored with 4 weekly TSH levels. Thyroid hormone requirements increase as pregnancy is associated with increased levels of TBG. 
- - -
### Subclinical Hypothyroidism
Defined as:
> TSH above the pregnancy-related reference range with a normal serum fT4

Inconsistent relationship between SCH and adverse outcomes. No indication for management or screening for TPO antibodies. There does appear to be a higher risk of pre-eclampsia. 
- - -
### [[Grave Disease]]
Patients with known Grave disease should have TRABs checked in early pregnancy and then again at around 18-22/40 if initially elevated. Elevated TRABs should be followed up with serial fetal USS and postnatal TFTs due to the risk of crossplacental transfer and fetal hyperthyroidism. 

Treatment varies depending on timing:
- Preconception -> PTU
- T1 -> PTU
  - Hepatotoxic
- ≥16/40 -> carbimazole (risk of embryopathy - specifically, _aplasia cutis_ - in T1)
  - Note that carbimazole is relatively more potent than PTU, and thus associated with hypothyroidism -> close monitoring of TFTs

> [!TIP] Mnemonic
> **P** **T** **U**  in **pre**-conception and **t**rimester **u**no

### Gestational Transient Thyrotoxicosis
There is considerable homology between the beta-subunits of hCG and TSH. As a result, hCG has weak thyroid-stimulating activity and may cause hyperthyroidism during the period of highest serum hCG concentrations. Note that oestrogen also stimulates increased production of TBG. Gestational/hCG mediated thyrotoxicosis is usually less severe than Grave disease, and self-limited, occurring in the first trimester. Treatment is generally not indicated, with the exception being if the thyrotoxicosis is being driven by a molar pregnancy.
