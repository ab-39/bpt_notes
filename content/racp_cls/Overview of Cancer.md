### Overview
![](Overview%20of%20Cancer/image.png)
The hallmarks of cancer are:
1. Sustaining proliferative signalling
   - Replicative immortality
   - Angiogenesis
2. Evading growth suppressors 
3. Avoiding immune destruction
4. Enabling replicative immortality
5. Tumour-promoting inflammation
6. Inducing or accessing vasculature
7. Genome instability and mutation
8. Resisting cell death
9. Deregulating cellular metabolism 

Comprehensive genomic profiling enables us to investigate which genetic changes and which of these cancer hallmarks have occurred in a given cancer, which can aid the physician with:
- Diagnosis
- Prognosis
- Prediction/treatment 

Cancers arise from an aberrant clone, and evolve in response to selective pressure in their microenvironment. This means that there can be multiple lineages within a tumour, affecting the choice of therapy. The first step in the development of cancer is the development of [[Genetic Variants in Cancer|genetic variants]], e.g.:
- Point mutations
- Copy number variants
- Translocations
- Alterations to expression - e.g. methylation
- Chromothripsis - a catastrophic event that leads to major rearrangements of the chromosome with _high_ oncogenic potential

Mutations can occur in many fashions:
- Mutual exclusivity:
  - Functional redundancy - e.g. _BRAF_ and _KRAS_ mutations rarely co-occur as they are in the same pathway 
  - Synthetic lethality (to the cancer cell) - e.g. _CCNE1_ and _BRCA1_ in ovarian cancer
- Randomly
- Co-occurence:
  - Synergistic effects - e.g. _KRAS_ and _APC_ in the intestinal epithelium
  - Amelioration of deleterious effects 
  - Mutational processes

| _C-MYC_  | Transcription factor     | Amplification                 | Breast, colon, gastric, lung          |
| _PI3KA_  | Phosphoinositol-3-kinase | Point mutatoin                | Many                                  |
| _HRAS_   | GTPase                   | Point mutation                | Colon, lung, pancreas                 |

| _NRAS_   | GTPase                   | Point mutation                | Many incl. melanoma                   |
| _RET_    | RTK                      | Point mutation, rearrangement | MEN2a, MEN2b, thyroid                 |
