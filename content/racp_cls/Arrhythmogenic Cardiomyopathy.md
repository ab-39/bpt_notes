### Overview
ACM is a class of the [[inherited cardiomyopathies]] that is characterised by the _replacement of ventricular tissue with fibrous or fibro-fatty scar tissue_. This leads to a propensity for arrhythmia and increased risk of sudden cardiac death. In the past, this condition was thought to be limited to the right ventricle, although more recent evidence suggests this isn’t likely to be true. 

cMRI is the most useful diagnostic test, as it can characterise myocardial scar. ECG is also useful for detecting repolarisation abnormalities, and may also reveal an LBBB pattern (as the focus is often within the right ventricle). 

European criteria for ACM:
- Morphological and functional centricular abnormalities
- Strucutral myocardial tissue findings - histology or MRI
- Depolarisation and repolarisation abnormalities
- Ventricular arrhythmias 
- Familial/genetic background

Various genes are implicated in ACM, including:
- Desmosomal genes - e.g. _PKP2, DSP, DSC2_
- Desmosomal genes associated with cardiocutaneous syndromes - e.g. _JUP_-plakoglobin, _DSP_-desmoplakin
- Non-desmosomal genes - e.g. _TMEM43, PLN, DES_
- Non-desmosomal genes associated wiht neuromuscular disorders - e.g. _DMD_, _DMPK_
