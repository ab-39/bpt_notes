# Iron Deficiency Anaemia
#bpt/haematology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Iron deficiency anaemia is characterised by a hypochromic, [[microcytic anaemia]].
![](Iron%20Deficiency%20Anaemia/image.png)
Note that even without progressing to anaemia, iron deficiency is associated with delayed development, cognitive and physical performance, and pregnancy complications. 
- - -
### Iron Metabolism
Iron is absorbed from the diet in the duodenum, on average 2-3mg/d. Dietary iron is absorbed into the gut epithelium by the haem transporter (haem iron) and divalent metal transporter 1 (free ferrous iron), and then released into the bloodstream by ferroportin. Free ferrous iron is reoxidised by hephaestin, before binding to plasma transferrin in its ferric state. Ferroportin is inhibited by hepcidin. 
![](Iron%20Deficiency%20Anaemia/image%202.png)
Once in the bloodstrea, ferric iron is either:
- Used - in the marrow to produce haemoglobin or in the muscle to produce myoglobin
- Stored - as _ferritin_ in the liver

Note that there is no mechanism to reduce excess iron - only about 1-2mg is lost via blood or sloughing of skin in the day. The only regulator of iron homoeostasis is hepcidin. Hepcidin is upregulated  by cytokines in response to inflammation. 

Additionally, ferritin is a _positive_ acute phase reactant while transferrin is a _negative_ acute phase reactant. 
- - -
### Diagnosis
Iron deficiency is diagnosed on the basis of serum ferritin:
- Adults: <30µg/L
- Children<20 µg/L

With the caveat that if the patient is in a pro-inflammatory state, the cutoff is much higher at 100µg/L. 

The transferrin saturation should be used to guide diagnosis, with a level <20% supporting the diagnosis of iron defiency. Note that in contrast, the serum levels of hepcidin and transferrin will be elevated. 
- - -
### Causes
- Reduced intake:
  - Inadequate dietary intake
  - Malabsorption states
- Increased GI loss:
  - Blood loss/occult bleeding
  - Pregnancy
  - Menstrual loss
  - GI parasites

Note that for asymptomatic, young patients with straightforward iron deficiency (without anaemia) in whom there is minimal susipcion for complex causes of iron deficiency or gastrointestinal symptoms, there is typically no indication for a referral to a haematologist or gastroenterologist.

### Treatment
IV Iron.
