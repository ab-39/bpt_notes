### Why perform an ABG?
Arterial blood gasses are indispensable to the diagnosis of causes of dyspnoea and hypoxia, and also for determination of causes of [[acid-base disorders]]. 

When interpreting an ABG, we need two key pieces of information:
1. The clinical scenario
2. The FiO2
- - -
### Validating ABGs
The [H+] can be manually calculated using a derivation from the Henderson-Hasselbach equation:
> `[H+] = 24 * (PaCO2 / [HCO3-])`

If this differs significantly from the [H+] corresponding with the ABG pH, consider resampling. 
- - -
### Stepwise interpretation
1. Look at the pH:
   - pH <7.35 -> acidaemia
   - pH >7.45 -> alkalaemia 
2. Calculate the [[alveolar-arterial gradient]]
   - `A-a = (150 — 1.25 * PaCO2) — PaO2`
