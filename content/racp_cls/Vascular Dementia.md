### Overview
Vascular dementia refers to significant parenchymal injury of the brain arising from cerebrovascular disease, with a temporal relationship between vascular events and cognitive deficits. The most prominent domain affect is information processing, complex attention, and frontal-executive functioning. Note that vascular disease preferentially affects the arterioles in the white matter, hence the most common imaging finding is white matter hyperintensities.

Possible signs suggesting a vascular or microvascular aetiology include:
- Deficits in attention, information processing, and executive function with relatively preserved memory
  - Predominantly a dysexecutive pattern of memory loss associated with cognitive slowing 
- Apathy
- Pseudobulubar affect = inappropriate involuntary laughing and crying
- Dyspraxic gait

The mainstay of cognitive impiarment arising from microvascular disease is blood pressure control.
