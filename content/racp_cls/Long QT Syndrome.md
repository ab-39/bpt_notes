### Overview
LQTS is associated with QT-prolongation and T-wave abnormalities on ECG, and confers an increased risk of arrhytmia, cardiac arrest, and sudden cardiac death (which is the presenting feature in as many as 15% of cases). Cardiac events most commonly occur in the second or third decade, but can occur throughout the lifespan. 

The mode of inheritance tends to be autosomal dominant, and just three genes are implicated in >90% of cases:
- _KCNQ1_:
  - Male predominance
  - Often associated with exercise as a trigger
  - Disorder of hypofunctional potassium channels
- _KCQN2_:
  - Female predominance
  - Associated with arousal/stress as a trigger
  - Particularly high risk in peripartum period 
  - Disorder of hypofunctional potassium channels
  - Bifid T-wave on ECG
- _SCN5A_:
  - Female predominance
  - Associated with rest/sleep as a trigger 
  - Disorder of hyperactive sodium channels

> [!TIP] Mnemonic
> Boys swim, girls stress, but salty girls sleep
> _Boys swim_ - LQTS1 is more prevalent in males and triggered by exercise
> _Girls stress_ - LQTS2 is more prevalent in females and triggered by stress 
> - Also LQTS **2** = **bifid** T-waves
> _Salty girls sleep_ - LQTS3 is a sodium channel pathology more prevalent in females and triggered by sleep 

The mainstay of treatment is β-blockade with ICD implantation for primary prevention. Additional therapies include left cardiac sympathetic denervation and (for _SCN5A_ mutations) sodium channel blockade. Drugs that prolong the QT-interval (i.e. potassium-efflux blockers) need to be avoided.

Associated syndromes include:
- Anderson-Tawil syndrome
- Jervell syndrome
- Lange-Nielsen syndrome
- TImothy syndrome

> [!NOTE] Acquired LQTS
> _Many_ drugs can prolong the QTc - among antidepressants, citalopram is the worst offender.
