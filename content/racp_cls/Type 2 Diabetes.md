### Overview
Type 2 diabetes is a complex metabolic condition that essentially consists of a triad of:
- Impaired insulin secretion
- Decreased muscular glucose uptake
- Increased hepatic glucose production
Culminating in a state of hyperglycaemia. This is underpinned by the ‘ominous octet’ of pathogenetic factors:
- Decreased pancreatic insulin secretoin
- Increased lipolysis
- Increased renal glucose reabsorption
- Decreased muscular glucose uptake
- Neurotransmitter dysfunction
- Increased hepatic glucose production
- Increased glucagon secretion from pancreatic ⍺-cells
- Decreased incretin effect in the GIT
- - -
### Ketosis-Prone Type 2 Diabetes
Also _Flatbush Diabetes_, a type of T2DM more common in African-origin individuals that is characterised by initial kketosis with insulin deficiency that later goes into remission and _does not require_ insulin treatment (i.e. DKA presentation followed by clinical T2DM). The majority of patients go onto experience further ketotic episodes. There are no clear autoimmune or genetic markers, and the exact pathogenesis remains unknown. 
- - -
### Pathophysiology
Recall that insulin (among its other action) suppresses lipoolysis and stimulates glucose uptake into the adipocytes. As weight gain occurs, adipocytes undergo _hypertrophy_ (amd some hyperplasia). This eventually leads to localised tissue hypoxia and adipocyte death, with secondary inflammation and release of free fatty acids into the circulation _in spite of the action of insulin_.

Once released into the circulation, free fatty acids interfere with the usual process of insulin signalling:
![](Type%202%20Diabetes/image%202.png)
This in turn causes organ-based dysfunction:
- Liver -> increased gluconeogenesis and reduced glycogen synthesis
- Muscle -> reduced glucose uptake and glycogen synthesis
- Pancreas -> direct β-cell toxicity
![](Type%202%20Diabetes/image%203.png)
This is not an _inevitable_ outcome - some individuals tend towards hyperplasia of adipocytes that tend to remain in subcutaneous stores, which remain insulin-sensitive and are less likely to release free fatty acids.

### Screening
Universal screening is not recommended, but targeted screening with HbA1c is recommended for high-risk individuals:
- Overweight or obese
- Features of the metabolic syndrome
- Ethnicities at risk of T2DM
- Women with a history of GDM
- Family history of T2DM

### Prevention
Is possible!
- [Finnish Diabetes Prevention Study](https://diabetesjournals.org/care/article/26/12/3230/21849/The-Finnish-Diabetes-Prevention-Study-DPS) -> lifestyle interventions halved risk of diabetes (ARR 12% at four years)
- Diabetes Prevention Programme -> lifestyle or metformin associated with reduced progression to diabetes at 2.8y
- ACTNOW study -> poglitazone reduces risk of diabetes at 2.4y (ARR 5.5%) when compared to placebo
- [Scale Obesity Prediabetes Study](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2817%2930069-7/abstract) -> liraglutide reduces risk of diabetes by 2/3rd when compared to placebo at 3y (ARR 4%)

### Complications
The complications of diabetes occur in three groups:
- Microvascular -> eyes, kidneys, and peripheral nerves
- Macrovascular -> brain, heart, and extremities
- Autonomic -> cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, sudomotor, and genitourinary

Complications tend to be more severe in those that develop T2DM before tha age of 40y. 
- - -
### Glycaemic Control
The _UKPDS_ study established the importance of glucose-lowering in preventing microvascular complications, **but** further studies (_ACCORD_ and _ADVANCE_) found that aggressive control (HbA1c <6.5%) was actualy associated with _increased_ mortality.

### Treatment Options 
Various classes of oral antihyperglycaemics include:
- Biguanides (practically only metformin)
- Sulfonylureas (-zide)
- DPP-IV inhibitors (-gliptin)
- ⍺-Glucosidase inhibitors (e.g. acarbose) 
- Thiazolidinediones
- SGLT2-inhibitors 
  - Work in the proximal tubule
  - Renoprotective - increase sodium at the macula densa leading to reflex reductions in  systemic and renal BP
  - Empagliflozin requires eGFR >30
  - Dapagliflozin requires eGFR >25
  - Risk of euglycaemic ketacidosis - particularly while fasting or if unwell 
    - Mechanism relates to lower insulin secretion in response to glycosuria 
    - Highest risk with canagliflozin, then empagliflozin, then dapagliflozin

Aside from insulin, the main injectable drugs for T2DM belong to the GLP-1RA class. Side effects include:
- Acute pancreatitis
- Hypoglycaemia when used in combination treatment
- Tachycardia
- Limitations: dulaglutide -> eGFR >15; semaglutide -> eGFR >30

The newer incretins include:
- Orforlipron - a daily, oral, non-peptide GLP1RA
- Cagrilintide-semaglutide - Ozempic plus an amylin/calcitonin agonist -> superior weight loss than either alone
- Retatrutide - triple therapy (agonist of GLP1, GIP, and glucagon receptors)
