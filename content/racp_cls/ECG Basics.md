### LVH Criteria
Most commonly a sequela of longstanding hypertension.

Solokov Criteria = sum greater than 35mm of:
- S in V1/V2
- R in V6

Cornell Criteria = sum greater than 22mm of:
- S in V3
- R in aVL

Associated with:
- ST depression in V5-6
- Asymmetric TWI 
- - -
### Bundle Branch Blocks
Definition = QRS >120ms (=three small squares)

Type of bundle branch block depends on V1 and V6 morphology:
- LBBB:
  - QS pattern in V1
  - R-wave predominant in V6
- RBBB:
  - RSR’ in V1
  - S-wave persistence in V6

Also possible to have a mixed pattern = ‘intraventricular conduction delay’.

Associated findings:
- LBBB:
  - 2-3mm STE in V1-3 -> note new LBBB is a STEMI equivalent
  - STD in I and aVL
  - Negative T-waves in the lateral leads -> positive _concordant_ T-waves in the lateral leads could indicate ischaemia
- RBBB:
  - TWI in V1-3

### T-Waves
High peaked T-waves can portend hyperkalaemia. While elevated potassium can cause cardiac arrest, it is _not_ a cause of myocardial ischaemia. 
- - -
### Supraventricular Tachycardias
Once the two commonest SVTs, sinus tachycardia and AF are excluded, the majority leftover are:
- AVNRT
- AVRT
- - -
### Atrial Flutter
Characterised by a ‘sawtooth’ appearance of regular P-waves at 300/m in the baseline. Common in heart failure. Anticoagulated as per atrial fibrillation.
- - -
### Heart Block
In any heart block greater than first degree, there will be _more_ P waves than QRS complices.
