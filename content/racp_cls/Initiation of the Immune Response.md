### Overview 
Within almost every tissue in the human body there is a population of tissue resident macrophages that constantly interact with and sample their microenvironment. Upon encountering a non-self or abnormal protein, macrophages begin to secrete key initiating cytokines: 
- IL-8 
- TNFα
- IL-1
In addition to this, local injury also activates the [[complement]] system and triggers mast cell degranulation.
- - -
### Key Cytokines
The initiating cytokines are the type I interferons, IL-1, TNF⍺, and IL-6: 
| Cytokine         | Source                                                       | Major Target                             | Function                                                     |
|------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Type I IFN (⍺/β) | Virally infected cells                                       | Cells surrounding virally infected cells | Induce antiviral state, activate NK cells, enhance cell mediated immunity |
| IL—1β            | Macrophages, dendritic cells, fibroblasts, epithelium, endothelium | T and B cells, neutrophils, CNS, Liver   | Promote inflammatory/acute phase response, cause fever (hypothalamus) |
| TNF⍺             | Macrophages, dendritic cells, fibroblasts, epithelium, endothelium | Macrophages, T cells, NK cells           | Acute phase response (liver), inflammation, fever, cachexia  |
| IL-8             | Macrophages                                                  |                                          | Recruit immune cells                                         |
| IL—6             | Dendritic cells, macrophages, T and B cells, fibroblasts, epithelium, endothelium | T cells, B cells, hepatocytes            | Acute phase reactants, Fever, induction of T and B cell growth |

### Cytokines in Innate Host Defence 
Although cytokines are predominantly produced by leucocytes, they may also be produced by parenchymal cells, and often act locally. The major acute innate cytokines are:
- IL-1
- TNF⍺
- IL-6
- IL-12
- CXCL8
- GCSF
- GMCSF

Which enact a diverse range of functions, including:
- Activation of endothelial cells and local tissue leucocytes
- Triggering of cytokine mediated amplification loops
- Generation of endothelial cell adhesion molecule expression
  - This in turn triggers neutrophil margination and diapedesis
- Slowing of blood flow
- Increasing of vascular permeability
- Recruitment and activation of leucocytes
