### Overview
Orthoptic liver transplantation (OLT) was pioneered by Thomas Starzl at the University of Colorado in the 1960s, and then in Australia by Russell Strong in Queensland in the 1980s. For patients with advanced liver disease, OLT provides a staggering 85% survival at 5y. 

OLT can be indicated for a range of reasons, but typically fall into one of a few categories:
- [[Cirrhosis]] with a MELD score >15
- [[Hepatocellular carcinoma]]
- [[Acute liver failure]] 
- In some cases - steroid-resistant alcoholic hepatitis can be an indication in some cases
- Multiple other indications, many of which relate to the complications of [[portal hypertension]] - diuretic-resistant ascites, recurrent haemorrhage, [[respiratory consequences of liver failure]], etc.

In acute liver failure, transplant is typically offered for patients meeting Kings College Hospital Criteria. Transplant in oncology patients is typically based on the Milan or UCSF criteria, which relate to the number of lesions and their size:
- Milan: single lesion <5cm or 3 lesions all <3cm
- UCSF: single lesion <6.5cm or 3 lesions cumulatively <8cm with the largest <4.5cm
The newer system is the Metroticket criteria. Patients can also be ‘downstaged’ with TACE to allow transplantation. AFP > 1000ng/mL is a contraindication for transplantation, as a surrogate for microvascular invasion. 

Infection is the leading cause of mortality after transplant, with especially high risk in the first three months. 

OLT can also be considered for cholangiocarcinoma and extensive metastases of colorectal cancer.
