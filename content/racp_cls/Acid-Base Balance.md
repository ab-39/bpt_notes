### What is happening?
Tissue metabolism releases CO2 into the bloodstream, which is typically buffered to HCO3 using the mechanism of carbonic anhydrase via the intermediate form of carbonic acid. In balance, tissue production of CO2 is balanced by respiratory excretion of CO2. When ventilation is insufficient to remove CO2, this causes a [[respiratory acidosis]].

In contrast, when hyperventilation leads to excessive removal of CO2, this is a [[respiratory alkalosis]].

### The Role of Potassium
During a metabolic acidosis, cells attempt to mitigate this by absorbing protons from the blood stream. To maintain electroneutrality, a cation must also be moved into the blood: potassium. 

As such, excess potassium can also cause or potentiate metabolic acidosis through the same mechanism:
- Excess K+ enters the cell, with H+ being released in return
- K+ competes with H+ for secretion by the collecting duct
- Hyperkalaemia decreases renal ammonia production -> inhibiting H+ excretion (as ammonia is the chief urinary buffer for H+ through its conversion to ammonium)

Similarly, hypokalaemia causes metabolic alkalosis through the reverse process by augmenting the H+/K+/ATP-ase which secrete H + and reabsorb K+ in the type A intercalated cells in the collecting duct.
