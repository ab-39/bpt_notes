# Polymyalgia Rheumatica
#bpt/rheumatology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
PMR is one of the most common systemic rheumatic conditions, with a lifetime risk of 2.5% in women and 1.5% in men. It has a very strong association with [[giant cell arteritis]]. 

It is characterised by inflammation in the peritendineum and perimysium, which can now be diagnosed with imaging using FDG-PET.

Clinically, the typical symptoms include:
- Aching and stiffness around the neck, shoulders, and hips
- Prolonged morning stiffness 
- Normal muscle strength but pain limiting testing
- Associated with depression, fatigue, and weight loss
- Usually presents in patients >50y
- The synovitis associated with PMR tends to be non-erosive 

Investigations should include:
- Inflammatory markers
- RF/CCP - exclude seropositive [[Rheumatoid Arthritis]]
- SPEP - exclude MM/MGUS
- CK - exclude myositis 
- Serum BAFF and IL-6 correlate with disease activity

Differentials include seronegative RA, regional soft tissue rheumatism, myeloma, myositis, and fibromyalgia. 

Treatment usually involves a long course of prednisolone, starting at 15mg/day for four weeks, with a slow taper over 1-2 years. More recently, tocilizumab (and a newer IL6-inhibitor: _sarilumab_) has also been investigated for PMR, with a very favourable rate of remission at 16w compared to steroids alone. Methotrexate can also be introduced if the patient is at a high risk of relapse or prolonged therapy. Strength training should also be introduced.
