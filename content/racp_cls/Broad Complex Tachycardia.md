# Broad Complex Tachycardia
#bpt/cardiology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Defined in opposition to a narrow complex tachycardia, i.e.:
> QRS complex >110ms, with activation of the ventricles occurring in some way _other than_ via a normally functioning AV conduction system

Never use IV verapamil to treat broad complex tachycardias, due to its negative inotropic and simultaneously vasodilatory effects.
- - -
### Aetiopathogenesis
Recall that normal conduction begins in the sinus node, travels through the atria to the AV node, and then causes rapid depolarisation (~5x faster than endplate conduction) of the ventricles by travelling down the bundle of His, the left and right bundle branches, and the Purkinje fibres. At a basic level, broad complex tachycardias can occur via three mechanisms:
1. _Ventricular rhythms_ - rhythms being generated below the level of the bundle of His, that are broad complex by nature 
2. _Aberrance_ - some problem in the conduction system leading to normal impulses generated above the AV node not being conducted properly, leading to a broad complex rhythm
   - Note that this requires the morphology of the QRS complex to match one of the bundle branch blocks for the diagnosis to be made
3. _Pre-excitation_ - the existence of an accessory pathway causing activation of the ventricles prior to the normal depolarisation of the atria, leading to a broad complex tachycardia (as is the case in [[Wolff-Parkinson-White syndrome]])

This mechanism-based approach also gives us the main differential list for a broad complex tachycardia:
1. Ventricular tachycardia 
2. SVT with functional or fixed bundle branch block
3. Pre-excited tachycardias
4. Paced tachycardias 
5. (Artefact) - look for high frequency, non physiologic activity

### Differentiation of VT from SVT with BBB
In general, VT is far more likely to display (negative) concordance than SVT with BBB. The other hallmark of VT is AV dissociation, which can be diagnosed clinically by the presence of cannon a waves.

Left VT:
- V1: R wave >40ms, notched S wave
- V6: Q waves

LBBB vs VT:
- V1 in LBBB: small R wave
- V6 in LBBB: no Q waves

Right VT:
- V1: monophasic R wave or R wave predominance
- V6: S wave predominance

RBBB vs VT:
- V1 in RBBB: rSR’ configuration
- V6 in RBBB: R wave predominance

### Treatment
If there is any whiff of urgency -> electrical cardioversion.

Pharmacological cardioversion is generally a once-only affair, as adding multiple agents can be risky and cause haemodynamic compromise. Choice of agents include:
- Sotalol
- Procainamide
- Lignocaine
- Amiodarone
Generally, these patients have advanced structural heart disease with poor LVEF, so drugs such as flecainide, verapamil, metoprolol etc. are contraindicated. 

Note that ICDs are not a treatment for the VT itself, but rather a prophylactic against their risk of death. Drugs and ablation treat the VT.
