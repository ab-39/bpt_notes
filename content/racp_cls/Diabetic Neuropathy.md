### Overview
Diabetes, and even impaired glucose tolerance, can cause an [[Acquired Peripheral Neuropathy]]. The most common pattern is a symmetric, distal, axonal neuropathy that leads to the classic ‘glove and stocking’ pattern of sensory loss, which can be compounded by small-fibre neuropathy leading to pain. This occurs via the pathway of Wallerian degeneration secondary to microvascular ischaemia and oxidative stress. In addition to this, patients with diabetes can develop cranial neuropathy, mononeuritis multiplex, diabetic amyotrophy (caused by a radicular plexopathy), and autonomic dysfunction. 

This is the most common cause of neuropathy, and ~50% of patients with diabetes will eventually develop some form of neuropathy.
