### Overview
Dysphagia refers to difficulty swallowing, that can arise due to either oropharyngeal or oesophageal problems.
- - -
### Oesophageal Dysphagia
Oesophageal dysphagia is characterised by a triad of dysphagia, regurgitation, and chest pain, and can result in the long term in malnutrition or aspiration. The causes of dysphagia are myriad, and can relate to local or systemic disease:
- Narrowing/compression of the oesophagus
  - Called a ‘Schatzki Ring’ at the GOJ - presents with sudden onset dysphagiapreg
- Inflammation of the mucosa
- Changes in neuromuscular function
  - Lack of relaxation of the lower oesophageal sphincter -> [[achalasia]]
  - Ineffective peristalsis
  - Hypercontractile states
  - Functional

Investigation of dysphagia usually begins with gastroscopy, which can also be a therapeutic procedure if strictures are identified. Oesophageal manometry and barium swallows are also useful tests. 
- - -
### Malignant vs Benign
Some clinical features may point in the direction of either diagnosis:
|                 | Malignant                | Benign                                                       |
|-----------------|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| *Time Course*   | Shorter duration         | Often a long history                                         |
| *Type of Food*  | Solids > liquids         | Solids and liquids                                           |
| *Progression*   | Constant and progressive | Intermittent/unpredictable                                   |
| *Cohort*        | Older age                | Younger age                                                  |
| *Key diagnoses* | Adenocarcinoma, <br>SCC  | Eosinophilic oesophagitis,<br>Achalasia,<br>Benign stricture,<br>Globus hystericus |
