### What is mitral regurgitation?
Mitral regurgitation is a form of [[valvular heart disease]] characterised by abnormal regurgitant flow across the mitral valve during systole. 

Note that unlike the other valves, the mitral valve has an additional component (beyond the leaflets and the annulus) - namely the papillary muscles and the chordae tendineae. 

Mitral regurgitation is typically primary, due to degeneration of the valve itself, or secondary, due to dysfunction of the left ventricle.

### Clinical Presentation
Murmur:
- High-pitched
- Holosystolic
- Exact location is determined by the direction of the jet:
  - Anterior leaflet -> posterior jet -> axilla
  - Posterior leaflet -> anterior jet -> parasternal
- May be an associated diastolic flow murmur

Small volume pulse, soft S1, [[pulmonary hypertension]], and absent S4 are signs of severe MR. 

Note that LVEF may be normal!
- - -
### Management 
Treatment of primary MR is with transcatheter or surgical valve _repair_, which has improved durability when compared to replacement. 
- Current ESC guidelines recommend intervention for severe primary MR with LVEF <60% (or LVESD >40mm)

Secondary MR requires treatment of the underlying problem, which is typically heart failure. If this fails, surgery may be indicated.
