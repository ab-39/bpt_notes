# Cytokines
#bpt/immunology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Cytokines are a class of small glycoproteins that regulate the function of the immune system by binding to specific receptors on target cells to alter gene expression and protein function, either in an enhancing or inhibitory way. They are soluble, low-molecular weight proteins that can be produced by a diverse range of cells, and which are effective at very low concentrations. In general, these tend to be pro-inflammatory, with the exception of IL-10 and TGFβ. 
- - -
### Mechanism of Action
Cytokines (and [[chemokines]]) have three main mechanisms of action. After being produced by a cytokine-producing cell in response to an inducing stimulus, cytokines are released to either:
- Act in an _autocrine_ fashion
- Act in a _paracrine_ fashion
- Act in an _endocrine_ fashion 
To induce some biological effect. 
- - -
### Classifying Cytokines
Cytokines are classified based on families with similar effects:
- Interferons
- Tumour Necrosis Factors
- Interleukins
- Transforming Growth Factors
- Colony Stimulating Factors

Alternatively, cytokines can be classed based on their function:
- Mediators and regulators of innate immunity -> act on endothelial cells and leucocytes to stimulate early innate responses
  - e.g. IL-1, IL-10, IL-12, TNF⍺, IL-6, IFN⍺/β, IFNγ
- Mediators and regulators of adaptive immunity -> act on lymphocytes to stimulate and regulate adaptive responses to specific antigens
  - e.g. IL-2, IL-4, IL-5, TGFβ
- Stimulators of haematopoiesis -> act on the bone marrow to stimulate growth and differentiation of leucocytes and lymphocytes 
  - e.g. stem cell factor, IL-3, IL-7, GM-CSF
