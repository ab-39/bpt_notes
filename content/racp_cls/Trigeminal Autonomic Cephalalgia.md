# Trigeminal Autonomic Cephalalgia
#bpt/neurology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
The trigeminal autonomic cephalalgias are a class of [[primary headache disorders]] typified by unilateral autonomic symptoms during attacks:
- Redness/tearing of eyes
- Miosis 
- Ptosis
- Rhinorrhoea/nasal stuffiness
- Fullness/tinnitus of ears 
- - -
### Cluster Headache 
Cluster headaches predominantly affect men, and are characterised by severe headaches and restless patients. These often manifest as 1-8 attacks of 15m-3h a day, in clusters lasting up to 12w. Onset is often nocturnal. In addition to the autonomic symptoms, there may be photophobia, _but it will be unilateral_. 

Management can involve injectable triptans, verapamil, greater occipital nerve injections, corticosteroids (for short clusters), and in some cases, mood-stabilisers. 
- - -
### Paroxysmal Hemicrania and Hemicrania Continua
These are rare TACs that are, by definition, highly responsive to indomethacin. It is characterised by shorter attacks, and is often more common in women. Topiramate is second line. Tends to manifest as _multiple_ attacks a day. 
- - -
### SUNCT/SUNA
These are short-acting unilateral neuralgic headaches, with either conjunctival injection and tearing, or cranial autonomic symptoms. These are characterised by brief, stabbing headaches.
