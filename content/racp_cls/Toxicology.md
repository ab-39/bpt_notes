### Overview
> _Sola dosis facit veninum_ - Paracelsus

Acute poisoning is a _dynamic_ condition - toxic effects can set on rapidly, and deterioration can be swift.

Acute poisoning should be managed like any critical care issue, with a few additions to the standard algorithm:
- Airway + antidotes
- Breathing
- Circulation
- Disability + decontamination
  - Typically done with _activated charcoal_, which is useful for most medications but **not** acids, alkali, alcohols, or metals/ions
  - Other strategies such as bowel irrigation, gastric lavage, or forced emesis are rarely to never used
- Exposure + enhanced elimination
  - Usually dialysis or multidose activated charcoal; urinary alkalisation is rare 
  - Generally used if there is no antidote, poisoning is life threatening, or if the poison is actually dialysable 

Any assessment of a poisoning case requires a patient-centred risk assessment, considering:
- History of exposure
- Type of poison and amount
- Time since ingestion
- Patient factors - e.g. age, comorbidities
- Available medical facilities 
- Collateral history

An assessment of pharmacokinetics is also important to understand how far the patient is from the peak effect of the drug. Concentration often relates to toxicity, but not always in a strictly proportional or directly temporal fashion.
