# Facioscapulohumeral Muscular Dystrophy
#bpt/neurology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview 
FSHD is caused by deletion (type 1) or hypomethylation (type 2) of the subtelomeric region of chromosome 4q, which leads to abnormal expression of a normally silent transcription factor (DUX4). The pattern of heredity can be autosomal dominant (type 1, 95% of cases), or digenic (type 2, 5%). The patient typically presents in childhood to young adulthood with facial weakness (orbicularis oculi and oris), scapular winging (rhomboids, serratus), and arm weakness (biceps, triceps with relative sparing of deltoid). The pattern of weakness is typically asymmetric initially. There are associations with retinal vasculopathy and sensorineural hearing loss (Coat’s syndrome). The CK is normal to mildly elevated. Treatment is typically supportive (e.g. AFO to help with foot drop).
