### Overview
Tumours of the oesophagus and stomach are a group of histologically and genetically diverse cancers that make up the fourth-leading cause of cancer death worldwide. In the cranial portion oesophagus, we tend to see squamous cell cancers associated with mutations in genes such as TP53, while as we progress more caudally, we begin to see more adenocarcinomas often attributable to other factors such as EBV or microsatellite instability.

### Clinical Presentation
Key risk factors:
- Environmental:
  - _Helicobacter pylori_
    - Risk factor for gastric adenocarcinoma **but** appears to be protective for oesophageal cancer 
  - EBV infection
  - Salt intake
  - Cigarette smoking
- Host factors:
  - Obesity
  - [[Barrett oesophagus]]
  - _CDH1_/e-cadherin mutations (hereditary diffuse gastric cancer)
  - Lynch syndrome (familial mismatch repair deficiency)
  - Polyposis syndromes - e.g. FAP, Peutz-Jeghers 
  - _ALDH2_ E487K mutation associated with increased risk of oesophageal cancer

Symptoms:
- Dysphagia
- Dyspepsia/abdominal pain
- Weight loss
- Loss of appetite 
- Abdominal pain
- Iron deficiency 
- Acanthosis nigricans 
- Tripe palms
- Leser-Trélat’s sign

### Diagnostic Approach
The mainstay of diagnosis for gastro-oesophageal tumours is OGD, which is often used to make the primary diagnosis. 

Once this is achieved, endoscopic ultrasound is useful for T-staging (i.e. depth), while CTCAP is useful for distant spread. PET/CT and laparoscopy also have a role, in the case of the latter, ruling out peritoneal disease (particularly if there is a possibility of surgery). 
- - -
### Survival
Depends on stage. Outcomes for metastatic disease is poor, and surgery is the only possibility of cure. 
- - -
### Treatment
Treatment for upper GI cancers is multidisciplinary, involving medical oncologists, surgeons, radiologists, radiation oncologists, etc.
![](Gastro-oesophageal%20Cancers/image%202.png)A recent advance in this space has been the emergence of [[immunotherapy]]. In particular, squamous cell cancers of the oesophagus tend to respond well to PD-1 inhibitors such as nivolumab. The Checkmate 649 and Keynote 590 studies found that PD-1 inhibition have clear benefit in the treatment of patients with metastatic squamous cell carcinoma of the oesophagus, and these treatments are now becoming standard of care in combination with chemotherapy. 

Gastric or GOJ cancers with surgical intent typically receive perioperative FLOT (= 5-FU + oxaliplatin + taxane), which improves survival.

Another emerging treatment is the use of monoclonal antibodies targeted at claudin 18.2, a tight junction protein that is exclusively found in the gastric mucosa and as such in gastric cancers. _Zolbetuximab_ has been found to have benefit in this setting.

#### Early Stage Gastric Cancer
Early stage gastric cancers are typically treated surgically, and those with local invasion can have neoadjuvant and adjuvant chemotherapy added.

### Palliative Management
Patients who relapse or who have widespread disease at diagnosis are often treated with palliative-intent chemotherapy.

#### First-Line
= platinum + fluoropyrimidine doublet + anti-PD1
e.g. cisplatin + 5FU/capecitabine

#### Second-Line
= irinotecan 
OR
= paclitaxel +/- anti-VEGFR2 (e.g. ramucirumab)

#### Third-Line
= trifluradine/tipiracil 

In addition to this, patients need dietitian input, consideration of PEG, EOL discussion, psychological support, pain management, etc. 
- - -
### _HER2_ in Gastric Cancer 
Tumours with _HER2_ amplification, as diagnosed on immunohistochemistry or FiSH, are treated with:
> chemotherapy +/- trastuzumab 

More recently, there has been the development of an antibody-drug conjugate of trastuzumab with deruxtecan, which demonstrates survival benefit in _HER2_+ve cancers as compared to chemotherapy alone.
