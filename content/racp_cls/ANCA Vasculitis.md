# ANCA Vasculitis
#bpt/rheumatology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview 
The anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antigen antibodies, or [[ANCA]], are autoantibodies that characterise several of the small vessel vasculitides, particularly:
- [[Granulomatous polyangiitis]] - a.k.a Wegener granulomatosis
- [[Microscopic polyangiitis]]
- [[Eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis]] - a.k.a Churg-Strauss disease (in 50% of cases)
- Some cases of drug-induced vasculitis
- Some cases of rheumatoid vasculitis

Importantly, PAN, Kawasaki, IgA vasculitis, isolated CNS vasculitis, cryoglobulinemic vasculitis, leucocytoclastic vasculitis, and viral vasculitides are _not_ associated with ANCA. 
- - -
### Pathogenesis
The pathogenesis of ANCA-associated disease typically involves:
1. Infection induces neutrophils to express the [[ANCA]] antigens (i.e. PR3 and MPO) on the cell surface
2. ANCA-FAb binds to the antigen, while ANCA-Fc binds to the FcγR _on the same neutrophil_ (this is termed “the Kurlander phenomenon”), thereby activating the neutrophil
3. Neutrophil activation -> degranulation -> binding of PR3/MPO to vascular endothelium -> endothelial activation -> vascular injury
   - Additionally: ANCA activate monocytes to release chemoattractants, promoting the formation of a granuloma 
   - This also triggers the activation of the alternative complement pathway (from C5a)
   - ANCA binds to ⍺1-antitrypsin, preventing it from degrading PR3, worsening this cycle

### Other autoantibodies in ANCA-vasculitides
- ANA - 15% of patients
- Lupus anticoagulant - 10% of patients
- Anticardiolipin - 10% of patients
- Anti-GBM - 5% of patients
