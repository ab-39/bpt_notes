# Hereditary Cancer Syndromes
#bpt/oncology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
There are over 200 hereditary syndromes associated with an increased cancer risk, that together account for ~10% of the total burden of cancer. 

These are usually associated with mutations in genes with an active role in tumorigenesis - e.g. _TP53_, _APC_, _BRCA_. Importantly, specific genes may be associated with specific histological subtypes for a given cancer. 
- - -
### Factors Suggestive of a Familial Cancer Syndrome
- Two or more relatives with the _same type_ of cancer on the _same side_ of the family
- Several generations affected
- _Earlier ages_ at diagnosis than generally expected for that cancer types
- Occurrence of cancers in one family that are known to be _genetically related_ (e.g. breast and ovarian, colorectal and uterine) 
- Occurrence of _associated nonmalignant conditions_ and cancer in the same person and/or family (e.g. Marfan and medullary thyroid cancer in MEN2B)
- - -
### Screening for Cancer Syndromes:
- BRCA1/2, PALB2
  - Breast screening from 25yo
  - BSO to reduce risk from 40yo (or 5y below the age of the youngest family member affected)
- Lynch syndrome
  - MHL1/MSH2 -> screening colonoscopy from 25yo
  - PMS2/MSH6 -> screening colonoscopy from 30yo
  - TAHBSO to reduce risk from 40yo (or 5y below the age of the youngest family member affected)
- FAP
  - Screening colonoscopies from 12yo (18yo in AFAP) until colectomy 
- VHL
  - Annual exam with BP from 2yo
  - Annual plasma/urine metanephrine screen from 2yo
  - Annual abdominal screening (alternating MRI/USS) from 10yo
- - -
### Surgery in the Management of Hereditary Cancer Syndromes
- VHL -> nephron sparing surgery due to risk of multiple or bilateral tumours
- Lynch syndrome -> subtotal vs hemicolectomy, may require risk reduction surgery for other cancers
- FAP -> colectomy vs protocolectomy, AFAP status needs to be considered too
- High genetic risk breast cancer -> consider prophylactic mastectomy of contralateral breast, may not be needed if relatives were diagnosed late in life; also consider adjuvant PARP inhibition
