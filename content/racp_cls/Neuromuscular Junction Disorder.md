### Overview
NMJ disorders are characterised by a fluctuating pattern of muscular weakness, a temporal course which can help to differentiate them from the [[myopathies]]:
| NMJ Disorders                                                | Myopathies                                                   |
|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Ocular symptoms: ptosis, diplopia, ophthalmoplegia           | Negative symptoms: weakness, fatigue, muscle atrophy         |
| Other symptoms: dysarthria, dyspnoea, limb weakness, fatigue | Positive symptoms: cramps, stiffness/myotonia, contracture, muscle hypertrophy, myalgia, myoglobinuria |
| Paraesthesiae (in LEMS)                                      |                                                              |
| Tend to have an asymmetric pattern                           | Tend to have a symmetric, proximal (most) or distal pattern  |

Key investigations include:
- MG: AChR, muscle-specific Y-kinase, LDL-R related protein 4
- LEMS: anti-VGCC antibodies
- Molecular genetic studies

NCS/EMG can be useful in differentiating between NMJ disorders and the myopathies. Muscle biopsy can also be helpful, generally of the biceps, quadriceps, or deltoid.
