# Transient Ischaemic Attack
#bpt/neurology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### What is a transient ischaemic attack?
A transient ischaemic attack, or TIA, is _the same thing_ as an [[ischaemic stroke]], but with the proviso that the clot dissolves _before_ causing permanent injury to the brain. This means that:
> there is no persistent lesion on neuroimaging (i.e. MRI) 

Typically, these have an average duration of about 10 minutes. 

> [!NOTE] Limb-Shaking TIA
> A ‘classic’ TIA syndrome is unilateral limb shaking - indicative of severe contralateral carotid stenosis leading to hypoperfusion. 

The old definition used to be of symptoms resolving within 24h, but in reality, many of these patients had changes on neuroimaging without persistent symptoms. 
- - -
### TIA is an emergency
The risk of stroke with untreated TIA is _10% in the first week_, and continues to remain elevated thereafter. 

Every patient who has a TIA should get:
- CT stroke protocol
- ECG
- Carotid imaging
- DAPT
- Antihypertensive
- High-dose statin
