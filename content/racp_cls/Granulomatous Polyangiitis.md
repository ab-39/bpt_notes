### Eponym
Wegener disease

### Overview
Granulomatous polyangiitis, or Wegener’s granulomatosis, is a small-vessel, [[ANCA vasculitis]] that typically presents as a rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis with multi-system involvement. It is associated with c-/PR3-ANCA. 

There is an association with ⍺1-antitrypsin, in that individuals with abnormal genotypes (MS, MZ, SS, ZZ) are more likely to develop GPA. This is likely due to the fact that ⍺1-antitrypsin inhibits PR3, and uninhibited PR3 is more immunogenic for the development of anti-PR3 [[ANCA]]. Importantly, once PR3-related disease begins, there is also less ⍺1-antitrypsin to mitigate the damage. The worst disease is associated with the ZZ/null genotype.

The current KDIGO guidelines suggest that glomerulonephritis that is suspicious for small vessel vasculitis should be treated empirically without waiting for the results of the kidney biopsy. 
> Early treatment -> superior outcomes
New onset ANCA-vasculitis should be treated with steroids with either rituximab or cyclophosphamide initially. If eGFR is rapidly declining, there is better evidence for steroids and cylophosphamide or cyclophosphamide and rituximab. 
> Consider plasma exchange if there is raised or increasing serum creatinine, or alveolar haemorrhage

Rituximab vs Cyclophosphamide:
| Rituximab                                                    | Cyclophosphamide                                             |
|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Patient groups:<br>Children/adolescents<br>WOCA<br>Frail older adults  | Patient groups (IV > PO):<br>Low WBC counts<br>Moderate cumulative dose of cyclophosphamide |
| Considerations:<br>Important if glucocorticoid-sparing is a consideration | Considerations:<br>Oral form is cheap and easy for patients<br>Easier to access than rituximab |
| Disease considerations:<br>c-ANCA/PR3-ANCA positive<br>Relapsing disease  | Disease considerations:<br>Can be used in combination with rituximab if severe GN (i.e. [Cr] > 350umol/L) |
|                                                              | **Has a risk of bladder cancer in the long run**             |

Glucocorticoids (?prednisolone) are usually initiated at a dose of 50-75mg/day, aiming to taper to 7.5-12.5mg/day at six months, and 5mg/day at one year.

Anti-C5a-R agents (e.g. avacopan) can be used as a steroid-sparing agent. 

Maintenance therapy is typically with rituximab or azathioprine and low-dose steroids:
| Rituximab         | Azathioprine + glucocorticoids     |
|-------------------|------------------------------------|
| Relapsing disease | Low baseline IgG                   |
| PR3-ANCA disease  | Note: azathioprine > mycophenolate |
| Steroid-sparing   |                                    |
Optimal duration of maintenance therapy is 18 months to four years.

Transplantation should be delayed until there is complete remission for at least six months, but persistence of ANCA titres should not delay it.

Response to treatment is usually rapid, with 90% of patients feeling better within days:
- Urinary RBC counts stabilise and fall within seven days
- Creatinine stabilises and begins to fall within seven days, with maximal improvement at one month
- Lung haemorrhage ceases within days, and nodules begin to clear within one month

90% of patients will experience some long term morbidity, typically nasal deformities, renal impairment, and respiratory insufficiency. Treatment itself is not without risk, with cyclophosphamide causing increased risk of infections in ~50% of patients and bladder cancer in 16% of patients at 15 years. Side-effects of treatment can be minimised by minimising the course of cyclophosphamides, minimising sun exposure (for SCC risk) if azathioprine is used, and using prophylactic Bactrim to prevent infection. Standard precautions for long-term steroid use are also required, noting that alternate day steroid dosing is less effective.

### Limited GPA
GPA without the characteristic renal disease is referred to as ‘limited’ GPA, which may lead to the symptoms being overlooked initially. This is [[ANCA]]-positive in ~70% of cases. Almost 90% of patients will go on to develop renal disease, although the risk factors for this are unknown. 

Unlike for generalised GPA, steroids alone are not sufficient for induction treatment of limited GPA. Patients typically require combination therapy of rituximab and azathioprine and methotrexate.
