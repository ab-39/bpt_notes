# Reactive Arthritis
#bpt/rheumatology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Reactive arthritis is a form of sterile arthritis following a remote, often genitourinary (oft. *Chlamydia*) or gastrointestinal (oft. *Yersinia* or *Shigella*), infection. 

> [!NOTE] Differential
> This phenomenon of _reactive_ arthritis is distinct from the polyarthritis associated with other bacterial infections, commonly gonohorrhoea and mycobacterial infections.

The symptoms usually begin 1-4 weeks after infection:
- Asymmetric, oligoarticular arthritis of the lower limb
  - +/- enthesitis
  - +/- dactylitis
  - +/- sacro-iliitis
- Classic triad (Reiter syndrome): arthritis + urethritis + conjunctivitis
  - Uncommon

One should try and identify the preceding infection if possible (e.g. FP urine NAAT for chlamydia). Other useful tests include inflammatory markers, synovial fluid analysis, and imaging. HLA-B27 does not aid with diagnosis, but portends more severe, chronic disease. 

Treatment involves eradication of the underlying infection if needed. NSAIDs are useful, as are intraarticular steroids, with systemic steroids if unwell. Conventional DMARDs may be used for chronic disease, to which ~20% of patients may progress..
