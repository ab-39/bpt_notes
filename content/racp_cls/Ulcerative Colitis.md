# Ulcerative Colitis 
#bpt/gastroenterology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### What is ulcerative colitis?
Ulcerative colitis is the subtype of [[inflammatory bowel disease]] that is characterised by continuous inflammation limited to the colon.
- - -
### Epidemiology
The prevalence of UC is typically 80-150/100,000.

Smoking appears to play a protective role in UC. 

Around ~5% of patients with UC will also develop [[Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis|PSC]]. 
- - -
### Clinical Presentation
Almost invariably, patients with ulcerative colitis present with bloody diarrhoea, as this is typical of inflammation of the colon. 

UC is characterised by ascending inflammation of the colon, which can occasionally affect the terminal ileum due to backwash ileitis (in 15% of cases). 

Severity of acute ulcerative colitis is graded using the Truelove and Witt Criteria:
|                          | Mild   | Moderate | Severe  |
|--------------------------|--------|----------|---------|
| Frequency of defaecation | <4     | 4-6      | >6      |
| Bloody stool             | +/-    |          | +++     |
| Fever                    | Absent |          | Present |
| Tachycardia              | Absent |          | Present |
| Anaemia                  | Absent |          | <100g/L |
| ESR                      | Normal |          | >30mm/h |

#### Fulminant Colitis
About 1/5 cases first present as _fulminant colitis_, defined with Truelove and Witt’s criteria as bloody diarrhoea more than _six_ times a day along with one of:
- Tachycardia
- Fever
- Anaemia
- Elevated inflammatory markers (ESR >30mm/h)

This used to be a _highly_ fatal presentation of IBD, with ~3/4 of patients dying within the first year of presentation, but this mortality rate has dropped to <1% in the current era. Presentation with fulminant colitis is a _strong_ predictor of progression to colectomy (~30%), particularly if the patient continues to have >8 stools/d on day three of presentation.

#### Toxic Megacolon
A rare, life-threatening complication of colitis with acute dilation of the colon that presents with bloody diarrhea, severe abdominal pain and distention, and sepsis, increasing the risk of perforation. Typically more common in UC due to the fact that inflammation is not transmural (i.e. CD will just perforate).

### Treatment 
#### Acute 
Acute treatment of a UC flare is with anti-inflammatories, daily AXR to assess for toxic megacolon, and VTEp:
- Mild -> 5-ASA
- Severe -> IV steroids 

After three days of treatment for acute treatment, response is graded with the Travis criteria:
- 8+ bloody diarrhoea/day **or** >3 bloody diarrhoea/day and CRP >45mg/L -> progress to salvage therapy
- If not -> continue steroids 

Salvage therapy in the acute phase is typically either infliximab or surgery. Less commonly, IV cyclosporine can be used. Methotrexate does not appear to be effective in UC. 

#### Maintenance 
Conventional maintenance therapies include [[steroid-sparing immunosuppressants]], such as azathioprine and methotraxate, as well as newer medications:
- Vedolizumab = anti-integrin -> safest (e.g. elderly, cancer, TB)
- Infliximab = anti-TNF -> quickest (also used in acute UC), used in steroid-refractory disease 
  - Can be used in TB after one month of treatment
  - Cannot be used if there is a history of melanoma 
- Adalimumab/golimumab -> subcutaneous injections 
- Ustekinumab -> generally well tolerated, increased risk of malignancy and serious infection
