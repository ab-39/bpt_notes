### What is cirrhosis?
Cirrhosis is an advanced state of hepatic fibrosis and scarring that can be the endpoint of almost any form of chronic liver disease (importantly, conditions that cause severe acute hepatitis - e.g. HAV, EBV, DILI - typically do not cause cirrhosis). The classic description of cirrhosis is of nodularity and scarring with significant architectural distortion.
- - -
### Pathophysiology
Cirrhosis primarily arises due to the activation of hepatic stellate and Kupfer cells in the space of Disse (i.e. tissue resident macrophages and pericytes), that normally play an important role in healing in the liver, but with chronic activation can cause increased collagen and ECM. 
- - -
### Clinical Presentation 
Suspicion that a patient has cirrhosis can begin when there is:
- Thrombocytopenia 
- Nodularity on ultrasound
- Impaired synthetic function - low albumin, [[Haemostatic Abnormalities in Liver Disease|haemostatic abnormalities]]
- High bilirubin
- Stigmata of chronic liver disease 
- Evidence on fibroscan

Many of the clincial stigmata are driven by an increase in circulating oestradiol due to alterations in hepatic metabolism and increased SHBG levels binding:
- Spider angiomata
- Gynaecomastia
- Feminisation of males
- Palmar erythema
- Testicular atrophy

Other key signs include:
- Jaundice
- Terry nails
- Clubbing
- Hypertrophic osteoarthropathy
- Dupuytren contracture

### Imaging 
Classic imaging findings of cirrhosis include:
- Surface nodularity (caused by alternating areas of necrosis and regeneration)
- Irregular surface
- Coarse echo texture 
- Blunt edges 
- Decreased right:caudate lobe ratio
- Indirect evidence of portal HTN

Various modalities can be utilised, including USS, CT, and MRI, which are all limited by relatively poor sensitivity and specificity. 

The most useful form of imaging is elastography - either FibroScan (transient liver elastography) or ARFI (acoustic radiation force impulse). These send an ultrasonic wave through the liver and measure its ‘stiffness’ as a proxy for fibrosis. 

> [!TIP] Fun Fact
> Elastography was first developed to test the ripeness of soft cheeses

### Grading
The severity of cirrhosis is measured with the _Child-Pugh score_:
- Encephalopathy - none/mild/severe
- Ascites - none/mild/severe
- INR - <1.7/1/7-2.2/2.2<
- Albumin - >35/35-28/28>
- Bilirubin - <34/34-50/50<

Each metric is graded on a scale from 1-3 points, to give a letter based score:
- A -> 5-6 points = 100% survival at 1yr, 85% survival at 2yrs
- B -> 7-9 points = 81% survival at 1yr, 57% survival at 2yrs
- C -> 10-15 points = 45% survival at 1yr, 35% survival at 2yrs

A similar score is the MELD score, which was used to grade suitability for TIPS procedures. It is often used in transplant waitlist selection. In addition to the metrics on the CP scale, it includes creatinine and sodium.

Another handy score that can be used in cirrhosis is the APRI (**A**ST to **p**latelet **r**atio **i**ndex):
> ` APRI = (AST / 40 ÷ plt) * 100`

Where 40iu/L represents the ULN for AST. Any score ≤1.0 suggests that the patient is unlikely to have cirrhosis.

### Management 
#### Lifestyle:
- Vaccinations
- HEHP diet
- BMD screening
- HCC surveillance

#### Medications:
- Rifaximin + lactulose to minimise risk of encephalopathy
- SBP prophylaxis
