### Overview
Hypoglycaemia is a common complication of antihyperglycaemic treatment for type I and type II diabetes. Its symptoms can be divided into the autonomic and the neuroglycopenic:
- Autonomic (generally BSL <3.1mmol/L):
  - Adrenergic -> palpitations, tremor, anxiety, pallor
  - Cholinergic -> sweating, hunger, paraesthesiae
- Neuroglycopenic (usu. BSL <2.8mmol/L) -> cognitive impairment, behavioural changes, psychomotor abnormalities -> seizures -> coma 

Divided into three levels:
- Level 1 = symptomatic hypoglycaemia = BSL <4.0mmol/L
- Level 2 = neuroglycopenic hypoglycaemia = BSL <3.0mmol/L
- Level 3 = severe hypoglycaemia requiring assistance/resuscitation

### Hypoglycaemia Unawareness
Hypoglycaemia unawareness is a syndrome characterised by deficient counterregulatory hormone release in the context of hypoglycaemia. Risk factors include:
- Increasing age and long diabetes duration
- Aggressive glycaemic control
- Frequent hypoglycaemia
- Autonomic neuropathy
- Medications - esp. β-blockade

Can affect critical tasks (e.g. driving) and the decision on how aggressively to control sugars. It can be improved if hypoglycaemic episodes are minimised. Can utilise the _Clarke survey_ to assess awareness of hypoglycaemia:
