### Overview
The key difference between OSA and CSA is that central sleep apnoea is characterised by a _lack_ of respiratory effort.
![](Central%20Sleep%20Apnea/image%202.png)
Treatment is theoretically with adaptive servo ventilation, but there is compelling evidence that this _worsens_ outcomes in patients with HFrEF ([Adaptive Servo-Ventilation for Central Sleep Apnea in Systolic Heart Failure](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1506459)), which is one of the commonest causes of CSA. There remains a lack of evidence for BPAP and CPAP.
