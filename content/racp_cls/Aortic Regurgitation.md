# Aortic Regurgitation 
#bpt/cardiology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### What is aortic regurgitation?
Aortic regurgitation is a [[valvular heart disease]] characterised by regurgitant flow across the aortic valve during diastole, due to some problem with the aortic valve, aortic root, or both. 
- - -
### Aetiology and Epidemiology 
Aortic valve:
- Congenital valve abnormalities -> bicuspid valve, VSD
- Acquired valve abnormalities -> age-related degeneration, [[infective endocarditis]], rheumatic heart disease, etc.

Aortic root:
- Congenital/connective tissue -> Ehler-Danlos, Marfan, Loeys-Deitz
- Acquired -> syphilis, vasculitis, SLE, aortic root abscess, etc.
- - -
### Clinical Presentations
Murmur:
- Early diastolic decrescendo murmur
  - Loudest on expiration
  - Loudest at left sternal edge
- Concomitant systolic flow murmur
- Austin Flint murmur - low-pitched mid-diastolic rumble due to regurgitant jet impinging on the mitral valve 

Acute, severe AR is a clinical emergency, typically due to aortic dissection or endocarditis, although trauma is an important differential. The usual clinical and echocardiographic clues are typically absent due to the rapidity of onset. 
- - -
### Echocardiography 
Key findings by severity:
- Mild:
  - Structural: LV size normal
  - Flow: small central jet; brief, early diastolic reversal 
- Severe:
  - Structural: wide coaptation or flail, dilated LV
  - Flow: large central jet (eccentric can be variable); prominent holodiastolic reversal 

- - -
### Treatment 
Symptomatic AR, or AR that is affecting LV function, is typically considered for AVR.
