### Overview
Lp(a) is a form of low-density lipoprotein that is bonded to apolipoprotein A that is associated with increased atherogenicity, thrombogenicity, and proinflammatory properties. ASsociated with aortic stenosis. 

Lp(a) should be tested in:
- Patients with premature CVD or stroke without evident risk factors
- Patients at intermediate risk to further risk-stratify
- Patients with recurrent or rapidly progressive vascular disease 
- In cases of [[familial hypercholesterolaemia]], genetic dyslipidaemia, or low HDL-c
- In cases of genetic defects of haemostasis, homocysteine metabolism, diabetes, or autoimmune disease 
- Elevated CVD risk 

There are emerging treatments (e.g. pelacarsen) that are antisense oligomeric [[nucleic acid therapies]] against hepatic Apo(a) synthesis. Paradoxically, statins can _increase_ Lp(a).
