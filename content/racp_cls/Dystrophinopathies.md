# Dystrophinopathies

### Overview
The dystrophinopathies are muscular dystrophies caused by an absence (Duchenne) or reduction in function (Becker) of the dystrophin protein due to deletions of one or more exons. These are both X-linked conditions caused by a variety of mutations in the gene encoding dystrophin. DMD affects ~1/3000 live births, while BMD is much rarer, affecting ~5/100,000. Both are associated with dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM).

|                       | DMD                                                    | BMD                    |
|-----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|------------------------|
| *Age of Onset*        | Early childhood                                        | Later onset            |
| *Pattern of Weakness* | Proximal > distal                                      | Proximal > distal      |
| _Clinical picture_    | Waddling gait, difficulty climbing stairs, Gowers sign | Calf hypertrophy       |
| *CK elevation*        | >10 ULN                                                | >5 ULN                 |
| *Progression*         | Rapidly progressive                                    | Moderately progressive |
| _Age of Wheelchair_   | 12y                                                    | 16y                    |
| *Age of DCM*          | ~18y                                                   | ~20s                   |
| *Age of Death*        | <30y                                                   | 40s                    |

The presentation is of a progressive, symmetric muscle weakness, first affecting the proximal lower limbs (hence the famous Gower sign), and then the proximal upper limbs and diaphragm. There is a strong association with cardiac involvement and cardiomyopathy, particularly in BMD. Additionally, female carriers of abnormal dystrophin genes may present with cardiomyopathy. 

DMD-associated DCM is characterised by left ventricular dilatation and congestive heart failure. It typically presents in males with BMD in the 20s-40s, and progressly rapidly, being the main driver of morbidity and mortality in this group. In females, onset tends to be later and progress slower. 

Genetic testing and muscle biopsy are important in establishing the diagnosis, as the abnormal or absent dystrophin proteins can be identified on Western blot. 

The mainstay of treatment is glucocorticoids, which are commenced between the ages of 4-7yo in DMD, and have been found to prolong ambulation and preserve upper limb strength. There are also dystrophin restoration [[nucleic acid therapies]] used overseas, including:
- Antisense oligonucleotides - e.g. eteplirsen - restore reading frame, allowing for a shortened but functional protein to be produced in DMD
- Ataluren - only applicable to nonsense variants of DMD; promotes stop codon read through
- [[Gene Therapy]]:
  - CAS9-CRISPR
  - Gene transfer therapy using AAV vectors 

Life expectancy is reduced for both conditions.
