### What is microscopic polyangiitis?
Microscopic polyangiitis is a condition characterised by the presence of palpable purpura and systemic features (note how this contrasts with [[leucocytoclastic vasculitis]], where there is palpable purpura without visceral involvement). It is associated with p-/MPO-ANCA. 
- - -
### How does microscopic polyangiitis present?
This condition typically presents with signs such as:
- glomerular haematuria
- renal failure
- abnormal liver function

The laboratory findings characteristic of microscopic polyangiitis are:
- P-ANCA with MPO-specificity 
  - **m**icroscopic **p**olyangiitis -> **p**-ANCA -> **MP**O
- - -
### Presentations
There are typically two main presentations of MPO:
| Primary Pauciimmune Glomerulonephritis   | MPA                                                        |
|------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| Very common, particularly in the elderly | Range of vessels, although typically not arteries involved |
| Glomerulonephritis only                  | Usually involves renal disease at presentation             |
| Kidney only involved                     | Pulmonary haemorrhage common (~40%)                        |
| p-ANCA/MPO-ANCA positive                 | p-ANCA/MPO-ANCA positive                                   |

Despite MPO itself being artery-sparing, about half of all patients with MPO present with an overlap syndrome with [[polyarteritis nodosa]]. This leads to arterial involvement, typically of the bowel and spleen, that unlike ‘pure’ PAN is ANCA positive.
