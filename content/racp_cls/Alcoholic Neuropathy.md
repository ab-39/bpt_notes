# Alcoholic Neuropathy
#bpt/neurology #bpt/addiction #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
This is a highly common complication of long-term alcohol use, with up to 66% of patients eventually developing it. The pathophysiologic mechanism is uncertain, but is thought to involve the toxic effect of alcohol itself (and its key metabolite: acetaldehyde), as well as the consequences of thiamine deficiency. This is typically a length-dependent, sensory neuropathy, that is quite painful. It is treated with cessation of alcohol intake, ensuring adequate nutrition, and symptomatic treatment. There may be some role for PKC inhibitors in reducing pain.
