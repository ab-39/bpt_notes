# Primary Myelofibrosis
#bpt/haematology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview 
Characterised by fibrosis of the bone marrow (caused by hyperplasia of megakaryocytes and fibroblasts) leading to extramedullary haematopoiesis and splenomegaly. Often has anaemia with thrombocytosis, and less commonly thrombocytopenia. Characterised by teardrop cells in the peripheral blood as well as a _leukoerythroblastic_ picture.
- - -
### Pre-Fibrotic Myelofibrosis
Tends to progress to overt PMF at a rate of about 1-2%/y. Bone marrow features include:
- Low-grade fibrosis (slightly increased reticulin)
- MKC morphology - less similar to ET
- Minor diagnostic criteria:
  - LDH
  - Leucocytosis
  - Thrombocytosis
  - Anaemia
  - Splenomegaly

Diagnostic criteria include:
- Major criteria:
  - MKC proliferation and atypica without reticulin fibrosis (>grade 1), often with decreased erythropoiesis
  - Not meeting criteria for CML, PV, ET, or MDS
  - Presence of driver mutations or other clonal markers or absence of reactive bone marrow fibrosis
- Minor criteria:
  - Anaemia not due to a comorbid condition
  - WCC >11
  - Palpable splenomegaly
  - Increased LDH 

Treatment is generally quite individualised, and may involve aspirinor cytoreduction.

### Definition
Primary myelofibrosis is characterised by:
- Reticulin and collagen scarring in the bone marrow
- Large numbers of megakaryocytes
- Myeloid metaplasia - movement of haematopoiesis to the spleen
- Peripheral blood:
  - Leukoerythroblastosis
  - Teardrop red cells

PMF is a _BCR-ABL negative_ clonal MPN, in which the proliferation of clonal haematopoietic stem cells leads to cytokine release, myeloid hyperproliferation, bone marrow fibrosis, and osteosclerosis. This is associated with extramedullary haematopoiesis and a risk of transformation to AML. This is associated with constitutional activation of the JAK-STAT pathway in the megakaryocytes, most commonly due to _JAK2_ mutations (~60%), _CALR_ (~25%), or _MPL_ (~5%). ~10% of patients are ‘triple negative’. This leads to increased signalling via this pathway, and attendant uncontrolled activation of aberrant megakayocytes and release of proinflammatory cytokiens. 
- - -
### Clinical Manifestations
While 20% of patients are asymptomatic, the rest may display:
- Abnormal blood counts
- Splenomegaly
- Bone pain
- Constitutional symptoms - anorexia, weight loss, night sweats, etc.
- Gout
- - -
### Treatment and Prognosis
Useful prognostic scores in PMF include the IPSS, dIPSS, and MIPSS70+. _CALR_ deletion mutations (type I) carry a favourable prognosis, while severe anaemia portends a worse prognosis.

Low and very low risk patients can generally be managed with observation alone, unless tehre are significant symptoms, anaemia, splenomegaly, significant leukocytosis (>25) or marked thrombocytosis (>1000). In this case, patients should receive cytoreduction with HC or IFN, or second lin ruxolitinib. Anaemia is treated with androgens, prednisolone, or ESA. 

For patients with symptomatic PMF, ruxolitinib is the cornerstone of therapy, decreasing splenomegaly, improving sympoms, and improving survival (albeit modestly). Adverse effects include anaemia and thrombocytopenia. 

Newer therapies include combined _JAK1/2_ and _ACVR1_ inhibitors such as _**momelotinib**_ that has been found to be non-inferior to ruxolitinib in reducing spleen volume and improving symptoms. It works by suppressing hepcidin production and enhancing erythropoiesis. Unlike ruxolitinib, this drug can _improve_ anaemia, which is a major advance in therapy. 

_**Fedratinib**_ is a small molecule inhibitor of _JAK2_ that has been foind to reduce spleen size and improve symptoms in patients with PMF who are refractory to ruxolitinib (_JAKARTA_ study).  There is a small risk of Wernicke’s encephalopathy. 

Despite these advances in therapy, no treatment has yet been proven to reduce the risk of progression to blast phase disease except an _**[[allogeneic stem cell transplant]]**_. In general, this treatment is reserved for young patients with a good performance status and few comorbidities. Disease should typically be in advanced phase or high risk, albeit prior to the development of the blast phase. RUX improves transplant specific risk factors, and clinical improvement on a _JAK2_ inhibitor is associated with a favourable outcome.
