### Treatment Approaches for NSCLC
Several options - surgical, radiotherapy, ablation therapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, molecular targeted therapy + combinations thereof
	-> needs MDT discussion 

Early stage patients (I-II, ~20%) -> gold standard is surgical resection with curative intent
- This encompasses tumours with nodal spread up to the ipsilateral hilum
- Good evidence for 5% reduction of death with adjuvant platinum based chemotherapy 
- ’Classic’ exam finding is a thoracotomy scar following the rib line, although VATS is increasingly common today 
- Nonsurgical options (if cannot tolerate surgery) can be curative but with worse outcomes:
  - Stage I -> stereotactic radiotherapy
  - Stage II -> radiotherapy +/- additional chemotherapy 

Stage IIIa disease (e.g. involving subcarinal nodes) is typically treated with chemoradiotherapy, followed by immunotherapy.
- There are specific Y-kinase inhibitors for *EGFR, ALK*, and *ROS1* positive cancer, which can be used instead of standard immunotherapy 
- For cancers without a driver mutation (either SCC or adenocarcinoma), the next step is to test for PDL1 expression:
  - PDL1 high -> pembrolizumab alone
  - PDL1 low -> pembrolizumab + chemotherapy

Stage IV disease unfortunately has no curative treatment, options typically include some combination of chemotherapy and immunotherapy, with supportive care and palliative radiotherapy as needed. 

NSCLC (particularly SCC) is more dstrongly associated with PTHrP-related paraneoplastic syndromes.

### Small Molecule Inhibitors in NSCLC
| Mutation | Epidemiology                          | Drug                                            |
|----------|---------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| *EGFR*   | 10-30% of tumours<br>Common in Asians | gefitinib, erlotinib<br>afatinib, osimeritinib  |
| *ALK*    | 5% of tumours                         | crizotinib, alectinib,<br>ceritinib, brigatinib |
| *ROS1*   | 1-2% of tumours                       | crizotinib, entrectinib                         |

Associated with ADRs such as hypercholesterolaemia, weight gain, and CNS side effects.
