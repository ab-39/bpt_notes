# Inflammatory Myopathies
#bpt/neurology #bpt/rheumatology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
The inflammatory [[myopathies]] are a diverse group of conditions characterised by weakness and myalgia, encompassing dermatomyositis, antisynthetase syndrome, immune-mediated necrotising myopathy, overlap myositis, and polymyositis. Much of the classification of these diseases has been driven by the emergence of specific antibodies.

The pattern of muscle weakness in these conditions tends to be symmetric and proximal, often involving the proximal shoulder and hip girdle limb muscles, with some subtle involvement of the face and neck flexors. With time, there may be progression to the truncal muscles. This may be accompanied by a diffuse, tender myalgia. 

The general approach to investigation of myopathies involves:
- CK (at least >500iu/L, often expect to see >10,000iu/L)
- Myositis antibodies
- MRI muscle
- +/- biopsy
- Investigation of [[interstitial lung disease]]
- Cancer screening 

In general, the myositis antibodies can be thought of as falling into five main groups:
| Group                        | Antibodies              |
|------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Dermatomyositis-related      | P155, MJ, Mi-2, MDA-5   |
| Antisynthetase-related       | Jo-1, PL-7, PL-12       |
| Necrotising myositis-related | HMGCR, SRP              |
| Malignancy-related           | TIF-1γ, NXP-2           |
| Scleroderma-spectrum         | U1RNP, Ku, Ro52, PM-Scl |

Treatment of the inflammatory myopathies typically involves immunosuppressive treatments:
- Steroids - typically high dose prednisolone initially
- Steroid-sparing treatment -> e.g. azathioprine, tacrolimus, methotrexate, etc. 
- Specific treatments:
  - IVIg -> refractory dermatomyositis, immune-mediated necrotising myopathies
  - Rituximab -> refractory myopathies

#### Dermatomyositis 
Dermatomyositis is an inflammatory myopathy characterised by the subacute onset of proximal muscle weakness accompanied or preceded by a distinctive rash often occurring in photosensitive areas:
> **Heliotropic** rash: violet discolouration affecting the eyelids 
> Shawl sign: anterior chest
> Gottron’s sign: scaly, erythematous lesions over the knuckles and other bony prominences 
> Mechanic hands: cracked, dry fingertips 
![](Inflammatory%20Myopathies/image%203.png)
There is often involvement of the heart, lungs, GIT, and joints. CK is often increased. There is a strong association with malignancy.

Specific antibodies in dermatomyositis are associated with specific features of the disease: 
![](Inflammatory%20Myopathies/image.png)<!-- {"width":487} -->

Histopathology classically shows _perifascicular muscle fibre atrophy_, and MRI muscle (STIR sequence) often shows muscle hyperintensity and oedema. 

Often associated with malignancy:
- Most commonly in association with adenocarcinoma at any site
- Risk factors:
  - Age >65y
  - Severe cutaneous disease
  - Absent ILD
  - NXP2 or TIF-1γ antibodies
- Some antibodies appear to be associated with lower risk

Anti-MDA5 positivity is associated with rapidly progressive interstitial lung disease.

#### Antisynthetase syndrome 
The antisynthetase syndrome is associated with autoantibodies to aminoacyl transfer RNA synthetase proteins. The clinical picture typically involves an inflammatory myopathy, interstitial lung disease (UIP or BOOP), arthritis, Raynaud phenomenon, and mechanic hands. There are a few ‘classic’ antibody associations:
- Anti-Jo1 (histidyl-tRNAsynthetase) -> the most common, highly associated with myositis and ILD
- Anti-PL12 (alanyl-tRNA) -> often (~50%) of the time have no myopathy, but severe lung involvement

#### Immune-mediated necrotising myopathies 
The immune-mediated necrotising myopathies typically present with an acute to subacute severe, proximal weakness without extramuscular involvement, with a serum CK level typically in the high thousands. There are three types, based on the antibody involved:
- Anti-3HMG-CoA-reductase -> associated with statin use (70%); severe muscle involvement, no skin or lung involvement (can treat with IVIg)
  - Prognosis is worse in statin-naïve patients 
- Anti-signal recognition particle -> severe muscle involvement, rare lung involvement, no skin involvement (can treat with rituximab)
- Antibody-negative immune-mediated necrotising myopathy -> increased risk of malignancy
Treatment is generally with steroids + IVIG or rituximab. 
- - -
#### Overlap myositis 
Autoimmune myopathy that is associated with other, well-defined autoimmune connective tissue disorders, e.g. SLE or Sjögren’s. There are several associated antibodies:
- Anti-Ro52 -> most common, nonspecific 
- Anti-PMScl -> overlap of myositis and systemic sclerosis; associated with lung and oesophageal involvement
- Anti-Ku -> overlap of myositis and systemic sclerosis; common; associated with joint involvement, Raynaud phenomenon, and ILD
- Anti-U1 RNP -> overlap of myositis and scleroderma/SLE; associated with glomerulonephritis 
- - -
#### Polymyositis 
A rare entity, typically a diagnosis of exclusion, that presents with a subacute onset of proximal muscle weakness. Investigations typically show an elevated CK, myopathic EMG, and CD8+ infiltrates on biopsy. 
- - -
#### Sporadic Inclusion Body Myositis
This is the most common acquired muscle disease in over-50y, with a prevalence of 5-70/1,000,000. It presents as a slowly progressive weakness that preferentially affects the quadriceps (i.e. knee extension), finger flexors, and ankle dorsiflexors. Neck, facial, and bulbar weakness can also occur. 

> [!NOTE] Key Signs
> sIBM _spares_ the oculomotor muscles, so diplopia is very rare. By contrast, dysphagia is very common, and affects up to 50% of patients with sIBM.

The ‘classic’ early sign is a diminished knee jerk reflex. There may be mild sensory changes. CK is typically mildly (~1000U/L) elevated. The anti-NT5C1A antibody is often elevated, but is typically not sensitive or specific. 

> [!NOTE] DDx: Polymyositis
> Involvement of the long finger flexors can help differentiate sIBM from PM.

Electrodiagnostic studies typically show a mixed myopathic and neuropathic picture, and imaging shows a selective involvement of the anterior thigh. The histopathological definition is definitive, and involves the presence of endomysial inflammation. Rimmed vacuoles and proteinaceous inclusions may also be present. 

There is no proven efficacious treatment to slow or reverse the disease. The mainstay of treatment is supportive care. 
- - -
### Treatment
First choice = MTX, AZA used less commonly
Second-line = MMF, particularly useful in ILD
Third-line = TAC/CsA, particularly in combination
- TAC cream useful for DM rash

IVIg has particular utility in necrotising myositis.

Rituximab has mixed evidence as salvage therapy.
