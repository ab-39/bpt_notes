# Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy
#bpt/obstetrics #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
AFLP is somewhat poorly named, as the condition truly represents acute liver failure in pregnancy rather than simply steatosis. Unlike garden-variety [[nonalcoholic fatty liver disease]], AFLP is associated with _microvesicular_ steatosis. AFLP most commonly presents late in the third trimester or in the early postpartum period, often with vague symptoms such as fatigue and jaundice.

The biochemical picture is of severe liver failure, with:
- Severe transaminitis
- Elevated bilirubin
- Elevated INR
- Increased WCC (20+)

Effects on the mother include hepatic failure and hypoglycaemia, and there is increased risk of stillbirth. 

> [!NOTE] DDx
> Hypoglycaemia is a key clinical feature that helps to differentiate AFLP from other causes of deranged LFTs in pregnancy - namely HELLP syndrome. 

Treatment is largely supportive, and can involve NAC as well as delivery.
