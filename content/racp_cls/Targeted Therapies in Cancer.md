### Overview
Cancer cells tend to express some patterns that make them vulnerable to _targeted therapies_:
- Oncogene addiction - the phenomenon of cancer cells become _highly_ dependent on one or more oncogenes for their survival and proliferation, e.g.:
  - _HER2_
  - _EGFR_
  - _BRAF_ 
- Non-oncogene addiction - the dependence of cancer cells on genes that do not directly drive the cancer, but which are essential for the tumour’s survival and progression, e.g.:
  - _VEGF_ 
- Synthetic lethality - the phenomenon wherein the disruption of two specific genes leads to cell death, while disrupting either gene alone would not

Targeted therapies tend to come in a few varieties:
- Monoclonal antibodies:
  - Bind to the extracellular domain of cell surface receptors to activate/block them
  - Highly specific, and can play a role in engaging the immune system
  - Endings specify origins:
    - *u*-mab -> human - e.g. golimumab, ofatumumab
    - *xi*-mab -> chimeric (i.e. human-nonhuman fusion) - e.g. basiliximab, abciximab
    - *zu*-mab -> humanised (i.e. nonhuman altered) - e.g. trastuzumab, alemtuzumab
- Small molecule inhibitors:
  - Interfere with intracellular signalling, usually by inhibiting one of the tyrosine kinase pathways 
  - Somewhat less specific than MABs, and tend to have more side effects
  - Typically, end in ‘-nib’ - e.g. erlotinib
- Antibody-drug conjugates:
  - Antibody linked to a chemotherapeutic, allowing for the drug to be delivered _directly_ to a tumour cell, minimising side effects compared to conventional systemic treatment
  - Antibodies can be linked to different drugs! e.g.:
    - Trastuzumab-emtansine (microtubule inhibitor)
    - Trastuzumab-deruxtecan (topoisomerase I inhibitor)

### Side Effects
Targeted therapies can have general side effects, most commonly rash or GI toxicity, as well as those directly related to the mechanism of action:
- ILD -> trastuzumab-deruxtecan, mTOR inhibitors (e.g. sirolimus/rapamycin) 
- QTc prolongation -> several drugs
- HTN -> VEGF inhibitors (e.g. bevacizumab)
- Cardiac failure -> HER2 inhibitors (e.g. trastuzumab)
- Ocular -> MEK-inhibitors 
- Hyperglycaemia -> PIK3CA-inhibitors, AKT-inhibitors 
- Haematological -> CDK4/6, PARP inhibitors
