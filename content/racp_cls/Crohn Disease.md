### What is Crohn disease? 
Crohn disease is the subtype of [[inflammatory bowel disease]] that is characterised by transmural, (non-caseating) granulomatous inflammation that can affect any part of the gastrointestinal tract in a segmental fashion. Fistulising disease in the context of other signs of IBD is pathognomic of Crohn disease. 
- - -
### Epidemiology
The incidence of CD varies from country to country, but prevalence is typically 25-100/100,000. Smoking appears to be a risk factor/exacerbating factor for CD. 

The _NOD2/CARD15_ gene is associated with CD, particularly early stricturing disease and an early need for surgery. 
- - -
### Clinical Presentation 
Although bloody diarrhoea is ‘classic’ in IBD, this is in fact not _always_ present in CD, unless the colon is involved. 

Most extra-intestinal manifestations of CD correlate with disease activity, with the exceptions being:
- Skin - pyoderma gangrenosum
- GIT - primary sclerosing cholangitis
- MSK - small joint and axial arthropathies

### Treatment 
Unlike in ulcerative colitis, 5-ASA is _not_ typically used in CD, because it requires cleavage by colonic bacteria to be activated. That being said, it can be trialled in patients with mild disease to avoid the adverse effects of steroids and heavy immunosuppression.

Acute treatment is with steroid induction, and then maintenance is with azathioprine or methotrexate (which can be PO or SC).

Failing the above, treatment is with biologics: infliximab, adalimumab, ustekinumab, or vedolizumab.

If there is perianal fistulising disease, infliximab is the first line drug of choice. 
- - -
### Complications of Treatment
#### Diarrhoea
Resection of the terminal ileum can lead to fat malabsorption, as this is the site where bile acids undergo reabsorption to be cycled back to the liver from the gut. This in turn leads to diarrhoea, which can be managed with bile sequestrants such as _cholestyramine_. 
- - -
### Pregnancy
Disease activity during pregnancy is predicted by disease activity at the time of conception -> i.e. plan to conceive during a period of time when the disease is in remission.
