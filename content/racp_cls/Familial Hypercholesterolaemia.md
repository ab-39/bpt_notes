# Familial Hypercholesterolaemia
#bpt/cardiology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
A relatively rare condition, arising due to mutations in the genes encoding the LDL-receptor, apolipoprotein B, or PCSK9 that lead to fewer functional copies of the LDL-receptor on the liver, elevating serum LDL-C levels, and leading to a significantly accelerated progression of atherosclerotic disease.

The condition is most prevalent in the northern reaches of Europe, particularly the Netherlands, who gave thier name to the Lipid Clinic Network Score to stratify the risk of FH. It includes such features as:
- Family history of premature CAD
- Family history of tendon xanthomata or arcus cornealis
- Personal history of premature CAD
- Presence of tendon xanthomata or arcus cornealis
- Pre-treatment LDL-C
- Presence of relevant mutations

Treat to a target of LDL-C <1.4mmol/L (very high risk patients), evidence for which comes from the CTT, IMPROVE-iT, FOURIER, and ODYSSEY studies. 

An emerging treatment is _evinacumab_, an antibody against the angiopoietin-like protein 3.
