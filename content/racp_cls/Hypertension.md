### Definition
While somewhat arbitrary, the Australian guidelines define blood pressure ranges as:
| Category      | SBP (mmHg) | DBP (mmHg) |
|---------------|------------|------------|
| Optimal       | <120       | <80        |
| Normal        | 120-129    | 80-84      |
| High-Normal   | 130-139    | 85-89      |
| Grade I HTN   | 140-159    | 90-99      |
| Grade II HTN  | 160-179    | 100-109    |
| Grade III HTN | ≥180       | ≥110       |
| Isolated sHTN | >140       | <90        |
The usual caveats about this diagnosis being made across multiple outpatient readings apply (unless the reading falls into grade III or stage 2). Ambulatory BP monitoring can be useful for measuring diurnal variation, as the loss of nighttime ‘dipping’ is an independent predictor of elevated risk. 

Staging of hypertension can also be carried out depending on the presence of end-organ damage related to HTN:
1. Stage 1 = uncomplicated HTN
2. Stage 2 = end-organ damage **or** CKD3 **or** T2DM
3. Stage 3 = established CVD **or** CKD4/5 

_Resistant_ hypertension is defined as uncontrolled BP despite three medications including a thiazide-diuretic.
- Controlled resistant hypertension refers to BP being at target with the addition of a fourth medication
- Appears to be related to sodium retention and volume overload
- Associated with higher aldosterone levels, suppressed rening levels, higher ANP/BNP levels, and visceral adiposity
- The PATHWAY-2 study found that for patients with resistant hypertension, when compared with ⍺ or β blockade, spironolactone was associated with the most significant reduction in blood pressure 

_Refractory_ hypertension is defined as uncontrolled BP despite five medications including a thiazide-like diuretic and an MRA.

### Epidemiology
Hypertension is _the_ most common cardiovascular condition, affecting around one third of adults: generally younger men and older women. The usual SES factors apply.

European and US guideliens recommend opportunistic screening for all adults, and annual screening for those aged >40y or at high risk (ethnicity, comorbidities).

HTN confers linearly increased risk of stroke, CAD, HF, and the development of CKD. Every 20/10mmHg increase in SBP/DBP is associated with a _doubling_ in risk of fatal CAD/stroke. Happily, however, treatment can almost reverse this increased risk.

The ASCVD score can be calculated for patients to guide their management (<https://cvdcheck.org.au>), but tends to underestimate risk for First Nations people by around 5%.

### Management
Australian and international guidelines generally recommend initiating treatment once a patient has developed grade I hypertension, or normal-high BP if there is comorbid CAD or proteinuric CKD. 

#### Non-Pharmacological Measures
- Weight loss
- Healthy diet and <1g sodium/d (_DASH_)
- Increased potassium intake
- Physical activity - aerobic and resistance
- Reduced alcohol consumption

#### Pharmacological Measures
Equal first/second/third-line:
- ACEi/ARB
- CCB
- Thiazide(-like) diuretic

Fourth-line = MRA (should add _patiromer_/potassium binders if used in CKD)
- Spironolactone
- Finerenone (non-steroidal MRA) - lower BP reduction but lower risk of hyperkalaemia, new evidence for mortality benefit in HFpEF (_FINEARTS_) 

Equal fifth/sixth-line:
- Bisoprolol
- Doxazosin

Seventh-line:
- Clonidine (associated with rebound hypertension if ceased abruptly)

Other options:
- Peripheral vasodilators - e.g. hydralazine, minoxidil
  - Can cause reflex tachycardia and fluid retention
- Renal denervation - if eGFR >40

### Secondary Hypertension
The diagnosis should be suspected in young patients, those with sudden onset of hypertension, if there is acute worsening of previously well-controlled HTN, if there is truly resistant HTN, or if there are clinical features out of keeping with the severity of the measured HTN.

#### Renovascular Hypertension
- The most common cause of secondary HTN
- Population prevalence of 6-14%
- Signs/symptoms:
  - Resistant HTN
  - Flash APO
  - Rapidly declining renal function
  - AKI on ACE/A2RB
  - Generalised atherosclerosis
- Investigations:
  - Renal artery Doppler (good for >60% stenosis)
  - CT/MRA
  - ACE-inhibitor scintigraphy
  - DSA
- Treatment:
  - Antihypertensive treatment - ACE/A2RB are first line unless disease is bilateral or there is stenosis in a single kidney
  - Control CV risk factors
  - Revascularisation - benefit not demonstrated by any clinical trials, but is considered in certain cases:
    - Recurrent flash APO
    - Rapidly progressive renal failure
- Poor prognosis

#### Fibromuscular Dysplasia
- Population prevalence of <1-6%, mainly younger women
- Can multifocal (80%) with a ‘string of beads’ appearance of angiography, or focal (resembling atherosclerosis
- Most commonly affects the renal and cervical artery, but can affect any medium vessel
- Signs/symptoms:
  - Early-onset/severe HTN
  - Migraine
  - Pulsatile tinnitus
  - SCAD
  - Arterial dissection/aneurysm
- Treatment:
  - Antihypertensive treatment
  - Angioplasty - _without_ stenting due to risk of kinking and stent fracture
  - Smoking cessation
- Follow up:
  - Whole body CT/MRA at diagnosis
  - Indefinite follow up 

#### Primary Aldosteronism (Conn Syndrome)
- Prevalence of up to 20% among those with resistant hypertension
- Signs/symptoms:
  - Resistant HTN
  - Hypokalaemia
  - Metabolic alkalosis
  - AF
  - OSA
  - Adrenaloma
  - Family history of early stroke
- Screening test = plasma aldosterone-to-renin ratio
- Confirmatory tests:
  - Sodium loading test
  - Adrenal vein sampling
- Additional tests:
  - CT abdomen
  - Genetic testing (~5% of disease is familial)
  - Captopril challenge test 
- Treatment:
  - Unilateral -> adrenalectomy
  - Bilateral -> MRA 

#### Phaeochromocytoma 
- Rare - prevalence <1%
- Signs/symptoms:
  - Paroxysmal headaches, sweating, and palpitations
  - Large BP variations
  - CV manifestations (MI, arrhythmia, TCM)
- Screening test = plasma + urinary free metanephrines
- Additional tests:
  - Contrast CT/MRI
  - Genetic testing
- Treatment:
  - ⍺-Adrenoceptor blockade
    - **Avoid** first treating with β-blockade as this can precipitate a hypertensive crisis by blocking vasodilatory β2 receptors while allowing unopposed vasoconstrictory activity at ⍺1 receptors 
  - Laparoscopic removal of tumour 

#### [[Cushing Syndrome]]
- Prevalence - 2-5%
- Signs/symptoms:
  - Resistant HTN
  - Easy bruising
  - Moon facies
  - Skin thinning
  - Proximal myopathy
  - Weight gain with central adiposity
  - Diabetes mellitus
- Screening test = 24h urinary free cortisol, late night salivary cortisol, low-dose dexamethasone suppression test
- Additional tests:
  - Morning plasma ACTH
  - ACTH stimulation test
  - Imaging
  - IPSS 
- Treatment:
  - Normalisation of cortisol level
  - Surgery

#### Other causes:
- [[Obstructive sleep apnea]]
- Coarctation of the aorta 
- Thyroid disease
- [[Acromegaly]]
- Medications (e.g. NSAIDs, glucocorticoids, etc.)
