### Overview
All aspects of pharmacokinetics can potentially be affected by pregnancy. It is important to remember that the risk of teratogenesis is highest in the first ten weeks of pregnancy. Drugs that are known to have an unacceptably high risk of teratogenesis include:
- Retinoins
- Thalidomide 
- Sodium valproate -> neural tube defects, autism, intellectual disability
- Mycophenolate
- Warfarin
  - In general should be substituted for LMWH during pregnancy, except in high risk valves (e.g. mechanical MV, mechanical TV, multiple mechanical valves, or valvular AF)
- Methotrexate
- Statins
- ACE inhibitors:
  - t1 -> teratogenic (renal agenesis)
  - t2/3 -> skull hypoplasia, 

Several drugs can be problematic later in pregnancy, notably ACE-inhibitors and NSAIDs.

### Biologics 
The biologics can be challenging to manage in pregnancy, as they can can readily cross the placenta, particularly those with an IgG1 or IgG4 configuration. The exception to this is pegylated monoclonal antibodies, such as _certolizumab_. Risk of cross-placental transfer is lower in the first trimester, and tends to increase as pregnancy progresses. Importantly, serum drug levels can be as much as twice as high in the fetal as the maternal blood.
- - -
### Immunosuppressants and Pregnancy
SSZ and HCQ are generally safe in pregnancy.
