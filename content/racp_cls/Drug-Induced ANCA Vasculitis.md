# Drug-Induced ANCA Vasculitis
#bpt/rheumatology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Commonly caused by:
- Propylthiouracil and related drugs
- Drugs known to cause SLE - e.g. hydralazine
- Allopurinol
- Sulfasalazine
- Minocycline
- Cefotaxime
- Ciprofloxacin
- Thioridazine
- Clozapine

Drug-induced ANCA vasculitis is typically MPO-ANCA positive (and rarely elastase or lactoferrin), and occurs ~18 months into treatment for Grave disease in 30% of patients as PTU can accumulate intracellularly and bind to MPO. Disease severity can range from none or a mild rash to full blown crescentic glomerulonephritis. 

Mild disease typically only requires withdrawal of the offending agent and a short course of steroids if needed for symptomatic management, while more severe disease may warrant a course of steroids +/- immunosuppressants, as well as close monitoring for emergence of an underlying vasculitis.
