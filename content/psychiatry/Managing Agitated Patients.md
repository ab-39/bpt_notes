### Non-Pharmacological Factors
Environmental:
- First contain the environment
- Clear the area
- Ensure sufficient staff and security are around
- Activate a code grey

Patient:
- Introduce yourself
- Use their name a lot - it humanises them
- Try and ascertain if they are oriented or delirious
- Try to validate them
- Ask what is wrong and how you can help
- Familiar faces/family helps
- Never block their exit or let them block yours 
- - -
### Pharmacological Factors
Patients who are severely agitated or violent, IV haloperidol and lorazepam are good options.
- Pros: low risk of EPSEs
- Cons: high risk of torsades de pointes

Benzodiazepines **should not** be used alone. Haloperidol, on the other hand, can be:
- 3mg IM or PO -> 30 min -> 5mg -> 30 min -> 10mg -> 30 min -> etc. until desired effect is seen
  - IM/PO haloperidol is associated with an increased risk of EPSEs, but not IV
  - PO bioavailability of haloperidol is ~50%
