# Major Depressive Disorder
#bpt/psychiatry #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
A condition characterised by changes in affect, cognition, and neurovegetative function that affect normal functioning, lasting at least two weeks. Peak age of onset is ages 25-45y, with a mean episode length of eight months. Common symptoms include:
- (Disrupted) sleep
- (Lack of) interest = anhedonia
- Guilt and shame
- (Lack of) energy
- (Inability to) concentrate
- (Changes in) appetite and weight
- Psychomotor retardation
- Suicidal ideation

This yields the fun, nonsense mnemonic “**_SIG E CAPS_**”.

Diagnosis requires at least one of dysphoria or anhedonia, and four other symptoms.
- - -
### Epidemiology
Approximate 7% annual prevalence with a 20% lifetime risk. Female predominance. _The_ leading cause of disability and morbidity.

Mood disorder is common in the perimenopausal cohort. 
- - -
### Screening
High risk populations - e.g. pregnancy/postpartum, chronic illness, recent loss, etc.

Question:
- ”During the past month, have you often been bothered by feeling down, depressed, or hopeless?”
- “Have you often been bothered by little interest or pleasure in doing things?”
- ”Is this something with which you would like help?”

Can also utilise pro-formas such as the PHQ9, DASS, or GDS.

Also important to screen for comorbid [[substance use disorder]]. 

Also important to consider the so-called ‘organic causes’ of depression:
- Hypothyroidism
- Anaemia
- Brain injury
- Medications
- Hypoactive delirium
- Steroids
- Etc. 
- - -
### Suicide 
> “He who does not accept and respect those who want to reject life does not truly accept and respect life itself” - _Thomas Szasz_ 

Ask the patient - you won’t give them the idea to do it. Risk factors for suicide completion include:
- Male sex (but females are more likely to attempt suicide)
- Age - young adolescence and elderly
- Depression/hopelessness
- Previous attempts
- EtOH/Drug use 
- (Lack of) rationality
- Social isolation
- Organised plan
- No spouse
- Sickness
  -> _SAD PERSONS_ 
- - -
### Management
Physicians can manage major depression - just don’t use paroxetine. A good SSRI is sertraline.
