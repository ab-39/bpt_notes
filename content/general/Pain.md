### Overview
Pain is an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with, or described in terms of, actual or potential tissue damage.

Pain can be:
- Nociceptive and somatic:
  - Skin, muscles, bones, soft tissue
  - Aching, throbbing, cramping, gnawing
  - Localised
- Nociceptive and visceral:
  - Due to activation of nociceptors in the thoracoabdominal viscera
  - e.g. due to stretch, ischaemia, or inflammation
  - Diffuse, difficult to localise 
  - Associated symptoms of nausea and vomiting
  - Deep, dull squeezing
- Neuropathic:
  - Centralised - brain and spinal cord -> peripheral manifestations - e.g. burning, itching
  - Closely linked with phenomena such as wind-up -> hyperalgesia and allodynia
  - Related: referred pain

Management:
- Ensure non-pharmacologic measures have been taken, e.g.:
  - IDC
  - Pressure care
  - Bowel care
  - Mouth care
  - etc.

### Opioids in Renal Failure
Some particular opioids such as morphine, diamorphine and codeine derivatives produces toxic metabolites which accumulate in renal failure.

Studies report profound respiratory depression and narcosis when renal patients are given these opioids. Buprenorphine has not been adequately studied in humans with renal failure and therefore it cannot be recommended as first choice for mild to moderate pain in renal failure.

Literature also suggests that both Hydromorphone and Oxycodone are safer than morphine or diamorphine.

Preferred opioids in patients with renal impairment:
- Fentanyl
- Methadone - this is an agonist at the µ-opioid receptr and an NDMA-antagonist 
- Tramadol (caution if on SSRI,TCA)
- Oxycodone (use with caution-Prolonged elimination of oxycodone and its metabolites in renal impairment)

NOT recommended:
- Morphine
- Codeine
- Dihydrocodeine
- Dextropropoxyphene
