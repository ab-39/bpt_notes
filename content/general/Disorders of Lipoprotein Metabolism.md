# Disorders of Lipoprotein Metabolism  
#bpt/general

### Overview 
There are multiple phenotypes of dysregulated lipid metabolism that can be classed according to their Frederickson phenotype:
| Phenotype | Elevated Lipoprotein          | Elevated Lipid |
|-----------|-------------------------------|----------------|
| I         | Chylomicrons                  | TG             |
| IIa       | LDL                           | Cholesterol    |
| IIb       | LDL and VLDL                  | Both           |
| III       | VLDL and chylomicron remnants | Both           |
| IV        | VLDL                          | TG             |
| V         | Chylomicrons and VLDL         | Both           |

Lipoproteins are complexes of lipids and proteins that are repsonsible for the transport of cholestrol, triglycerides, and the fat-soluble vitamins. They consist of a core of cholesterol esters and triglycerides surrounded by strucutral and peripheral apolipoproteins.

Different apolipoproteins are associated with the different forms of lipoproteins.

### Chylomicron Metabolism
![](Disorders%20of%20Lipoprotein%20Metabolism/image%202.png)
Chylomicrons are the lipoproteins that carry dietary fats from the intestines to the tissues. TAGs are bound to chylomicrons by the microsomal transfer protein, after which they are transported into the lymphatics and then the vasculature. ApoCii and ApoE are added to the chylomicron by HDL. In the tissues (usually adipose, muscle, and heart), lipoprotein lipase degrade the TAGs into fatty acids and glycerol. The remnant chylomicron is then returned to the liver.

~Lipoprotein lipase~ is bound to the endothelium by GP1HBP1. Congenital deficiencies of LPL lead to _familial chylomicronaemia_ (Frederickson type 1), a rare, autosomal-recessive condition that is associated with extremely high levels of triglycerides, leading to recurrent pancreatitis, hepatosplenomegaly, retinal lipaemia, and xanthomata _without_ an elevated risk of cardiovascular disease. 

The ~chylomicron remnant~ returns to the liver, still holding onto the apolipoproteins (ApoE and ApoCii) and cholesterol. ApoE, in particular, is critical as it reacts with receptors such as syndecan-4 and LRP to allow the remnant to be taken up into the liver. Homozygosity for ApoE2 leads to a failure of this mechanism, which manifests as elevated cholesterol and triglycerides - a condition known as _familial dysbetalipoproteinaemia_ (Frederickson type 3). This confers a significantly elevated risk of cardiovascular disease, although typically only in combination with other risk factors.

### Lipoprotein Metabolism
Very-low-density lipoproteins transport endogenous triglycerides, phospholipids, and cholesterol around the body. The key apolipoprotein for this function is _Apo-B100_, which also requires the action of the microsomal transfer protein to bind fats. 

![](Disorders%20of%20Lipoprotein%20Metabolism/image%203.png)

Loss of function of either of these elements leads to _familial hypobetalipoproteinaemia_ or _abetalipoproteinaemia_, which is marked by very low levels of plasma triglycerides and cholesterol, which presents clinically as fat malabsorption and neurological symptoms (e.g. ataxia). 

Overproduction of VLDL leads to a Frederickson type IIb phenotype, with elevated VLDL and LDL, in a condition known as _familial combined hypercholesterolaemia_. This is associated with a high risk of premature cardiovascular disease that requires aggressive management. 

Like chylomicrons, VLDLs have their fats removed by LPL, which results in the formation of remnants called _intermediate density lipoproteins_ (IDLs). IDLs can undergo two fates: return to the liver where they are absorbed in a process mediated by ApoB100 and ApoE, or become LDL. Unlike chylomicrons and VLDL, LDL has its TAGs removed by the liver, and so _only_ contains cholesterol and ApoB100. 

Once formed, LDL is either taken up into the liver or into peripheral tissues, where it can cause atherosclerosis. 

A loss of hepatic LDL-R,gain of function mutations in PCSK9 (the protein that inhibits LDL-R recycling), or loss of function mutations in ApoB leads to an autosomal dominant condition known as _familial hypercholesterolaemia_ (Frederickson type IIa). This is a common condition (~1/250) that is associated with significantly elevated cholesterol and a very high cardioavascular risk. Treatment is with statins and PCSK9 inhibitors. Diagnosis is based on the Simon Broome criteria:
- Total cholesterol >7.5mmol/L or LDL-C >4.9mmol/L, plus one of:
  - Tendon xanthomas in the patient or a close relative
  - Identified mutation of LDL-R, ApoB, or PCSK9
There are also the more complex Dutch Lipid Network criteria.

Only ~80% of individuals with LDL >4.9mmol/L, however, have monogenic FH. The remainder are likely due to polygenic forms of FH, which still confer significantly elevated risk and require high-intensity statin therapy. 

HDL is the smallest and densest of the lipoproteins, marked by ApoA1, which is responsible for transporting cholesterol in an esterified form (under the influence of LCAT) from peripheral tissues to the liver and steroidogenic tissues. Deficiencies of HDL are associated with increased cardiovascular risk:
- ApoA1 deficiency - characterised by corneal opacities
- Tangier disease - deficiency of ABCA1 -> rapid metabolism of HDL. Characterised by hepatosplenomegaly and orange tonsils
- Familial LCAT deficiency - characterised by cloudy corneas

The other major lipoprotein is lipoprotein(a), a form of LDL bound to apopliprotein a, which occurs under situations of oxidation. These oxidised phospholipids are more inflammatory, and high Lp(a) levels (>125mmol/L)  are associated with increased CVD risk and aortic stenosis. A new therapy, **_pelacarsen_** is an antisense oligonucleotide that has demonstrated the ability to reduce Lp(a) levels by up to 90%!

### PCSK9
PCSK9 is a molecule that binds to LDL-R and causes it to be degraded endosomally along with LDL-C. Inhibition of PCSK9 with monoclonal antibodies allows for increased recycling of LDL-R, up to 150 times in its lifecycle! 

*Evolocumab* is the sole PCSK9 inhibitor (although an RNA PCSK9-synthesis inhibitor, *inclisiran*, is also used) available on the PBS, as a 2-4 weekly SC injection that has minimal side effects. It reduces serum concentrations of VLDL and LDL, while increasing HDL.

PCSK9 inhibitors are associated with reduced Lp(a).

### Triglyceride Metabolism
Hypertriglyceridaemia can either be simple primary HTG, or familial. The latter is an autosomal dominant, polygenic condition that is caused by reduced lipolysis of triglycerides. This leads to a Frederickson type IV phenotype. This is distinguished from mixed hypertriglyceridaemia (type V) by the presence of fasting chylomicronaemia in the latter.
