# IgG4-Related Disease 
#bpt/general
- - -
### Overview
IgG4RD is a fibroinflammatory condition characterised by the tendency to form tumefactive lesions within affected organs. IgG4RD can affect virtually any organ system, leading to variegated clinical presentations. Most commonly affected are:
- Pancreas = type I autoimmune pancreatitis -> painless jaundice, difficult to distinguish radiographically from malignancy; exquisitely sensitive to glucocorticoid treatment
- Salivary glands = Mikulicz’s disease -> presentation similar to Sjögren syndrome
- Aorta -> aortitis or dissection
- Lung -> inflammatory pseudotumour
- Retroperitoneum -> periaortic inflammation
- Lymph nodes -> lymphadenopathy
- Kidneys -> tubulointerstitial nephritis 
- Thyroid -> Riedel thyroiditis
- CNS -> pachymeningitis 

Histopathologically, IgG4RD is characterised by a few consistent findings irrespective of the affected organ:
- Lymphoplasmacytic infiltration
  - High percentage of IgG4-positive plasma cells
- _Storiform_ fibrosis (‘storea’ = woven mat)
  - Tends to affect vasculature, particularly veins -> obliterative phlebitis
- Mild to moderate tissue eosinophilia

Other tests include:
- Serum IgG4 30-40x ULN
- Elevated serum IgE and eosinophils
- Organ specific biomarkers
- Masses in IgG4RD tend to enhance uniformly on CT

Treatment is generally with steroids, to which AIP is particularly sensitive.  Rituximab can be considered in refractory cases. Treatment is generally more successful at inducing remission in early disease.
