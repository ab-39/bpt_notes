### Pharmacokinetics 
> *Medicines are nothing in themselves if not properly used, but the very hands of the gods if employed with reason and prudence* — Herophilus 

_Pharmacokinetics_ is about how much drug the patient is exposed to when they take a medicine - in other words, it is about what the **body** does to the drug between administration and effect. Pharmacokinetics is important to understanding the exposure of a given drug - that is, the _concentration_ over _time_ of the _active_ component of the drug, that is dosed at certain intervals (NB. a dose is not just an amount, but an amount per unit time). Another way of thinking about pharmacokinetics is _ADME_:
- Absorption
- Distribution
- Metabolism
- Excretion

Or, _ABCD2_:
- **Administration** - the route (e.g. PO, IV, SL, etc.) and form (e.g. IR, SR, etc.) by which a medication is given, as well as how _adherent_ the patient is to a given medication
- **Bioavailability** - rate (more important in drugs that are being delivered as an IV infusion) and _extent_ (F), that is the proportion of an administered drug that reaches the systemic circulation as a fraction of the IV dose:
  - High F = low variability (e.g. ciprofloxacin, simvastatin)
  - Low F = high variability (e.g. frusemide, atorvastatin)
- **Clearance** - the _apparent drug clearance_ is the physiological capacity of the body to remove a given drug. Steady state exposure is mostly determined by clearance. There are many avenues by which a drug can be cleared:
  - Liver
  - Kidneys
  - Small molecules -> enzymes/metabolism (but also kidneys)
  - Monoclonal antibodies -> target mediated and catabolism
- **Distribution** - the _apparent volume of distribution_ relates to anatomical distribution (e.g. total body water). Peak concentration and loading dose are determined by the Vd.
- **Dosing** - guidelines give _starting_ doses - you need to _individualise_ the dose, and dose to a biomarker (e.g. symptom, sign, result, TDM, etc.).

#### Half-Life
_Half-life_ is the best known and most widely researched pharmacokinetic parameter. It varies as two physiological parameters:
> Size — i.e. _volume of distribution_ 
> Inverse elimination capacity — i.e. the inverse of _clearance_ 

Knowing the half-life enables us to know how long a medication will take to reach a steady state (usually 4-5 half lives) and how long it will take for the drug to be completely cleared (usually the same). 
> $t_{1/2} = 0.7*Vd/C$ 
> $C_{SS} = (Rate of Infusion) / Cl$
