### Overview
Primary mitochondrial diseases are caused by genetic mutations in genes necessary for mitochondrial structure or function, and typically cause multisystem disease. 

#### Myoclonic Epilepsy with Ragged Red Fibres (MERRF)
Characterised by a triad of childhood-onset epilepsy, myoclonus (independently of epilepsy), and ‘ragged red fibres’ - clumps of diseased mitochondria evident in muscle fibres stained with Gomori trichrome. Caused by mutations in the mitochondrial tRNAlys gene. Associated with a myopathy in 80% of cases. 

#### Mitochondrial Encephalomyopathy, Lactic Acidosis, and Stroke-Like Syndrome (MELAS)
MELAS is a disease with onset in childhood, following normal development for the first few years. It is caused by mutations in the mitochondrial tRNAleu gene. It is characterised by stroke-like episodes of transient hemiparesis or cortical blindness that eventually result in encephalomyopathy with cognitive impairment. Initial presentation is often with seizures, headaches, vomiting, and proximal weakness. Lactate is typically elevated in the blood and CSF. Some patients have a restricted phenotype, commonly with diabetes mellitus. 

#### Chronic Progressive External Ophthalmoplegia (CPEO)
CPEO is caused by sporadic single deletions in the mitochondrial DNA. It is characterised by a progressive external ophthalmoplegia with onset in adulthood, associated with skeletal myopathy. 

#### Kearns-Sayre Syndrome (KSS)
KSS is characterised by a triad of onset before age 20y, retinitis pigments, and progressive external ophthalmoplegia. It is typically caused by a large deletion of 4,977 base pairs from the mitochondrial DNA. There are multiple other associations, including sensorineural hearing loss, myopathy, and cardiac conduction defects.
