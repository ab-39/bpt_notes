# Gestational Diabetes

### Overview 
Diabetes mellitus is screened for in pregnant women with an OGTT at ~24-28/40, and is associated with increased risk of:
- Large-for-gestational-age birth weight
- Neonatal and pregnancy complications
- Increased risk of maternal development of T2DM
- Abnormal glucose metabolism of offspring

In terms of management, lifestyle changes have been found to produce adequate glycaemic control in ~75% of women with GDM. In the remainder, insulin can be considered in order to control hyperglycaemia as it does not cross the placenta. 

Medications that should not be used include:
- Metformin - cross the placenta, no evidence for benefit
  - May be used in patients with PCOS, but should be discontinued by the end of the first trimester
- Sulfonylureas - cross the placenta
- Other OAHGs - no long-term safety data
- Injectables other than insulin - no long-term safety data

### Pre-Existing Diabetes in Pregnancy
For women who already have diabetes, good glycaemic control at the time of conception is critical to avoid the development of diabetic embryopathy, the risk of which increases proportionately as HbA1c, and which is associated with:
- Anencephaly/microcephaly
- Congenital heart disease
- Renal anomalies
- Caudal regression

Once pregnant, targets are:
- Fastning plasma glucose <5.3mmol/L
- 1h post-prandial glucose <7.8mmol/L
- HbA1c will be reduced due to increased RBC turnover -> aim <6%

Women with diabetes in pregnancy should receive aspirin for primary prevention of [[pre-eclampsia]].
