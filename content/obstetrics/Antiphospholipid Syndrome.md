# Antiphospholipid Syndrome
#bpt/obstetrics #bpt/haematology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Diagnostic Criteria
At least one clinical and one laboratory criteria:
- Clinical:
  - Vascular thrombosis
  - Pregnancy morbidity:
    - ≥1 fetal loss after 10/40
    - ≥1 premature birth after 34/40 due to pre-eclampsia or placental insufficiency
    - ≥3 miscarriages before 10/40
- Laboratory tests on two or more occasions at least 12 weeks apart:
  - Positive anti-β2 glycoprotein (IgG or IgM) -> the major pathologic antibody
  - Positive anti-cardiolipin (IgG or IgM) present in medium-to-high titre 
  - Positive lupus anticoagulant -> strongest predictor of clotting

There are more complex criteria determined by the ACR/EULAR (2023), but the BSH suggests reserving these for clinical trials and not routine clinical use. 

The most common cutaneous manifestation is livedo reticularis. 
- - -
### Epidemiology
Rare - incidence 5/100,000 per year, **but** among those with early clots or recurrent pregnancy loss it is far more common.
- - -
### Laboratory Testing
Clotting occurs on cell membranes, which are made up of _phospholipids_. Antiphospholipid antibodies interfere with normal coagulation in two ways:
- _In vivo_ -> promote clot formation -> thrombosis/pregnancy loss
- _In vitro_ -> retard clot formation (hence ‘lupus _anticoagulant_’)

These antibodies are measured in two ways:
- Enzyme immunoassay -> β2-glycoprotein and anticardiolipin
  - Not affected by the presence of anticoagulants
- Two functional assays -> lupus antocoagulant phenomenon
  - This is based on the fact that antiphospholipids _tend_ to preferentially prolong the aPTT pathway and the addition of phospholipid should normalise the clotting time
  1. aPTT: addition of phospholipid -> normalise the clotting time (bring it down)
  2. dRVVT: Russell viper venom activates factor X and causes clot formation -> lupus anticoagulant interferes with this and prolongs the dRVVT -> addition of phospholipid -> normalise the dRVVT

#### Emerging Tests
- Anti domain-1 β2-glycoprotein 1
- Anti-complex phosphatidylserine prothrombin (PS/PT)

### Catastrophic APLS
Rare: 0.8 per 100 APLS patients. Tends to be precipitated by some insult, e.g. surgery, infection, stopping anticoagulation, etc. Characterised by widespread systemic thrombosis and multiple organ failure, with a mortality as high as 50%. Triple therapy is recommended:
- Full dose anticoagulation (usu. IV UFH)
- Steroids
- PLEX
- +/- IVIg if comorbid infection

Additional treatments:
- SLE -> cyclophosphamide
- MAHA -> rituximab
- Eculizumab
- - -
### Management
Triple-positive patients -> warfarin

~Primary Prophlyaxis~ = low-dose aspirin +/- VKA:
- Triple-positive asymptomatic patients with no history of VTE
- Women with pure obstetric APLS and positive antibodies
- Patients with SLE and one or more positive antibody (or HCQ)

~Secondary Prevention~:
- DOACs are _inferior_ to VKA ([Pengo et al., 2018](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/132/13/1365/105711/Rivaroxaban-vs-warfarin-in-high-risk-patients-with))

Note significant risk of bleeding following renal biopsy - avoid if possible. 

Catastrophic APLS:
- Anticoagulation
- Antiplatelet
- Glucocorticoids
- IVIG or PLEX

### APLS in Pregnancy
In pregnancy, APLS is associated with an increased risk of:
- [[Pre-eclampsia]] 
- IUGR
- Pre-term delivery

Interestingly, mothers with recurrent miscarriages as a manifestiation of APLS tend to have lower rates of progression to pre-eclampsia. 

#### Treatment 
Treatment varies with clinical manifestations:
| Clinical Manifestation                                 | Recommended Treatment                                |
|--------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| APLS without thrombosis or fetal loss                  | Low-dose aspirin                                     |
| Previous thrombosis without fetal loss                 | Low-dose aspirin + therapeutic LMWH<br>Consider HCQ  |
| Recurrent miscarriage or fetal loss without thrombosis | Low dose aspirin + prophylactic LMWH<br>Consider HCQ |
| Severe pre-eclampsia or fetal growth restriction       | Low-dose aspirin +/- prophylactic LMWH               |
| Non-criteria obstetric APS                             | Low-dose aspirin +/- prophylactic LMWH               |
