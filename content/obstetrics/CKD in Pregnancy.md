### Overview
[[Chronic kidney disease]] in pregnancy can lead to many medical and psychosocial issues, and needs to be approached with care. Risk factors for adverse pregnancy outomces include:
- Preconception creatinine/eGFR
- Proteinuria
- Hypertension
- Diabetes
- The underlying diagnosis
- SLE 
- Vesicoureteric reflux

The adverse outcomes associated with CKD include but are not limited to pre-eclampsia, small size for gestational age, preterm delivery, NICU admission, and worsening CKD postpartum.

In general, the risk throughout pregnancy can be stratified as follows:
- CKD 1-2:
  - Likely uncomplicated pregnancy
  - A minority will progress to worse CKD
  - ~One quarter will develop pre-eclampsia
  - Generally deliver at term
- CKD 3:
  - Likely complicated pregnancy
  - A minority will progress to worse CKD
  - ~One half will develop pre-eclampsia
  - Generally deliver mildly preterm
- CKD4-5:
  - Very likely complicated pregnancy
  - ~30% will progress to worse CKD
  - Majority will develop pre-eclampsia
  - Age at delivery generally ≤34/40

Important parameters for monitoring in pregnancy include:
- Deteriorating kidney function
- Hypertension
- Superimposed pre-eclampsia
- Disease flares
- Treatable comorbidities - e.g. anaemia, infection, diabetes
- Fetal health
- Time of delivery

### The RAAS System and Pregnancy
This poses a tricky situation in pregnancy, as both the medications to treat a deranged RAAS system (e.g. ACEis) and the consequences of a deranged RAAS system (e.g. HTN) can cause fetal issues. In patients who are able to self-manage their medications, RAAS blockade can be self-ceased upon conception. This allows us to maximise RAAS blockade until conception, and reduce the time in which proteinuria will worsen. Additionally, optimising proteinuria prior to conception can be protective in early pregnancy.
