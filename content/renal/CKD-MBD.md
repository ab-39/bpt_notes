### Overview
Metabolic bone disease (MBD) in [[chronic kidney disease]] is a complication that results in a complex interplay of electrolyte derangements, bone abnormalities, and vascular calcification, leading to a triad of:
- Cardiovascular disease 
- Fractures
- Mortality 

In addition to the biochemical derangements discussed as [[complications of chronic kidney disease]], there are other factors to consider when treating CKD-MBD:
- Acidosis
  - Bone has an optimal pH for maintenance, which is essentially close to physiological pH
  - Treating acidosis with sodium bicarbonate can help to prevent bone resorption 
- Bone turnover-mineralisation
  - Can be high or low in CKD-MBD
- Cardiovascular risk factors unique to CKD-MBD:
  - Phosphate/FGF-23
  - Vascular calcification
  - Inflammation
  - Anaemia
  - Proteinuria 

Monitor:
- Biochemistry: Ca2+, PO4+, PTH, ALP, Vitamin D
- Bone health: PTH, ALP, DXA
- Calcification: lateral abdominal XR, echocardiogram

Management:
- Aiming for normal range PO4+ (avoiding aluminium based binders_
- Avoid hypercalcaemia (and calcium-based PO4+ binders)
- Osteoporotic management needs to be individualised in terms of treatments such as bisphosphonates or RANKL-mAb (denosumab)
