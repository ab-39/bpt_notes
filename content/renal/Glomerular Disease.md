### The Spectra of Glomerular Disease
Glomerular disease is the third most common cause of end-stage kidney disease in the Western world. Glomerular diseases are typically characterised by:
- Immunological or structural damage to the kidney
- Renal interstitial damage
- Symmetrical kidney involvement
- Haemodynamic consequences

Glomerular diseases are described with several histopathological and clinical terms. One key differentiation is between _nephrotic_ and _nephritic_ syndrome:
- [[Nephrotic Syndrome]] = podocyte damage leading to proteinuria 
- [[Nephritic Syndrome]] = endothelial/mesangial damage leading to _microscopic_ haematuria 
  - Macroscopic haematuria almost always suggests a urological process
  
|                | Nephritic Syndrome                          | Nephrotic Syndrome                                |
|----------------|---------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| *Haematuria*   | Yes (by definition)                         | Unusual                                           |
| *Proteinuria*  | Depends: proteinuria -> severity            | Yes (by definition >3g/d)                         |
| *Hypertension* | Yes (if bad enough)                         | Variable                                          |
| *CKD*          | Yes (if bad enough)                         | Common                                            |
| *AKI*          | Yes (if bad enough)                         | Rarely                                            |
| *Histology*    | Proliferative response<br>Severe: crescents | Non-proliferative<br>Tend to see matrix expansion |

Multiple spectra:
1. Proteinuria <- -> Haematuria
2. Non-proliferative (i.e. BM/pods) <- -> Proliferative (i.e. endothelial)

#### Overlap Disorders
Although thought of as two discrete phenomena, overlap syndromes of nephritic and nephrotic syndrome can occur, and typically cause a _membranoproliferative_ pattern of injury. Potential causes are diverse, and include:
- Immune complexes - e.g. infections, autoimmune, monoclonal gammopathies
- Complement-mediated
- Secondary to any cause of acute glomerular injury
Management generally involves workup for the underlying cause and standard proteinuric measures. 

#### Imaging
Current guidelines recommend the _CT urogram_ as the imaging modality of choice when working up unexplained microscopic haematuria, due to its high sensitivity (96%) and specificity (99%) for these conditions.

### Proliferative Disease
‘PIGARMS’
- P = postinfectious
- I = IgA
- G = GBM
- A = Alport’s
- R = RPGN (ANCA vasculitis)
- M = MPGN
- S = SLE

### Non-Proliferative Disease 
Nephrotic syndrome:
- Proteinuria (Massive)
- Hypoalbuminaemia
- Oedema

### Case 1:
34M HTN
Cr 138µmol/L
Haematuria +++
Proteinuria: PCR = 174mg/mmol (note this roughly equates to 1740mg/day as people pass 10mmol/day of urine)

Nephritic disease -> proliferative disease 

BBV screen negative
ANA 1:1280
Paraproteins negative

-> biopsy

-> proliferative

Three classes of proliferative GN:
- In Bowman’s capsule -> crescentic GN
- In the mesangium -> mesangial GN (associated with IgA)
- Throughout the glomerulus -> endocapillary

Dx = IgA nephropathy
