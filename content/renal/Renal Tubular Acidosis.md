# Renal Tubular Acidosis
#bpt/renal #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
The renal tubular acidoses are a group of tubular disorders characterised by a normal [[physiology/Anion Gap|anion gap]]/hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis with (relatively) preserved eGFR and either hypokalaemia (type I and II) or hyperkalaemia (type IV).

| Type                | Defect                                                       | Characteristics                                              | Treatment                                                    |
|---------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| I (distal)          | Diminshed H-ATPase activity in the distal nephron -> inability to secrete H+<br>(Rarely reduced membrane permeability to H+)<br>Associated with autoimmune conditions: Sjögren, SLE, PBC, AIH | Hypokalaemic NAGMA<br>**Urine pH >5.5** as there is less H in the urine<br>Proximal tubules continue to reabsorb akali including citrate -> kidney stones | Treat with alkaliser and K+ replacement                      |
| II (proximal)       | Failure of proximal bicarbonate reabsorption:<br>Autosomal recessive mutations in carbonic anhydrase (or drug inhibition)<br>NHE3/NBCe1 mutations<br>[[Fanconi Syndrome]]<br>Nephrotoxicity - tenofovir in particular, ifosfamide<br>Myeloma | Hypokalaemic NAGMA<br>** ** as distal tubule continues to secrete H+, but there is now less HCO3- in the blood <br>No kidney stones<br><br> | Treat with high-dose alkaliser and K+ replacement            |
| IV (hyporeninaemic) | Decreased aldosterone production or diminished responsiveness of the cortial duct to aldosterone<br><br>Associated with diabetes, NSAIDs, ACEi, calcineurin inhibitors, MRAs, and heparin | Hyperkalaemia NAGMA<br><br>Most common - often occurs in diabetes | Synthetic mineralocorticoids (e.g. fludrocortisone) - provided the patient is not hypertensive or fluid overloaded<br><br>Otherwise consider diuretics to induce secondary hyperaldosteronism through volume contraction |
