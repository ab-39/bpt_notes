# Fluid Balance and Plasma Osmolality
#bpt/renal

## Body Composition
About 60% of the average adult body is water, which is a little higher in fat people. 
* About 33% of fluid is in the ECF, where the main cation is sodium, and the main anions are chloride and bicarbonate.
* The remaining 66% of fluid is in the ICF, where the main cation is potassium and the main anions are organic phosphates.

## Plasma Osmolality
The biochemical constitution of plasma:
> 2 x [Na+] + [Glu] + [Urea]

Typically the effects of glucose and urea are quite small, except in uncontrolled diabetes or renal failure.

Plasma osmolality is tightly controlled via osmoregulators that titrate the release of ADH from the neurohypophysis in the posterior hypothalamus.

ADH binds to [[renal/Vasopressin Receptor 2|vasopressin receptor 2]] (V2 receptors) in the nephron, located between the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle, DCT, and collecting ducts. Engagement of V2 leads to the upregulation of aquaporin 2 receptors, promoting the entry of water into cells. 
* Note vasopressin is usually found preformed in vesicles, and returns to these vesicles on removal of ADH
* With chronic ADH stimulation, these proteins become phosphorylated 

The role of ADH is to maintain normal plasma osmolality by engaging with V2 receptors.

## Fluid Balance

ADH also functions as a volume regulator in response to drops in systemic blood pressure. This leads to a sharp rise in ADH levels, which:
1. Bind to V2R to increase water retention
2. Bind to V1R to increase TPR
