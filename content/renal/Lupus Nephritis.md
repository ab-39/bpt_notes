# Lupus Nephritis

### Overview
Renal disease, lupus nephritis, is possibly the most significant manifestation of lupus. The pathological changes typically progress from minimal mesangial changes, to mesangial proliferation, through to membranous glomerulonephropathy and diffuse sclerosis. This is accompanied by a deterioration in renal function clinically ranging from minimal proteinuria, to full-blown nephrotic syndrome, often with additional haematuria.

Electron microscopy typically shows subendothelial immune complexes and tubuloreticular inclusions.

Biopsy-proven lupus nephritis makes the diagnosis of SLE. 
- - -
### Biopsy
Indications for biopsy include:
- Increased sCr without compelling alternative cause
- Confirmed proteinuria (≥1g/d)
- Combinations of proteinuria ≥0.5g/d and:
  - Haematuria
  - Cellular casts

Evidence of crescentic nephritis on the biopsy is inevitably a poor sign. 
- - -
### Prognostic Indicators
The following factors are poor prognostic indicators:
- Disease severity
- Presence of interstitial fibrosis and/or crescents
- Delay in diagnosis/treatment
- Poor response to treatment
- HTN
- Low haematocrit
- Black race
- Low SES

Improvement in proteinuria (<0.7g/d) at one year is a **good** prognostic indicator.

### Treatment
#### Low Grade Disease (and end stage disease)
Low-grade disease/I/II - that is, where there is no/microscopic haematuria and/or proteinuria - and end stage disease typically do not warrant immunosuppression.

Treatment is focussed on standard renoprotective measures such as RAAS-inhibition and BP control. If there is a MAHA, PLEX may be indicated.

#### High Grade Disease
High-grade disease/III/IV/V - that is, where this is evidence of decreased renal function, nephrotic syndrome, and significant haematuria - typically requires immunosuppression.

Induction:
Induction immunosuppression is usually with pulsed IV steroids and MMF/MPA or CYC (found to be equivalent in the _ALMS_ trial). Between these agents, MMF/MPA is preferred in black and Hispanic groups, while CYC is prefererred in crescentic disease. MPA may have fewer side effects than MMF, probably related to its lower dose.

Maintenance:
MMF and AZA are the mainstay of maintenance immunosuppression. CYC may be used if there is no improvement in proteinuria after induction.

#### Emerging Therapies:
- Belimumab - anti-BLYS/BAFF antibody; significant improvement in _BLISS-LN_ trial when in combination with MMF and not CYC/AZA (but CYC/AZA group had worse disease)
- Voclisporin - novel calcineurin inhibitor, improved rate and speed of renal response in combination with MMF and steroids when compared to MMF and steroids alone (_AURORA-I_ trial), allows steroid sparing
- Obinutuzumab - fully humanised anti-CD20 antibody, phase II _NOBILITY_ trial found imrpoved effect compared to MMF+GCS alone
- Anifrolumab - signal of improved outcomes in _TULIP-II_ study; IFN receptor blocker

### Lupus Nephritis and Pregnancy 
Lupus nephritis can be particularly dangerous in pregnancy, and pregnancy is often the first time that patients with lupus nephritis are detected. Although good outcomes can occur with quiescent disease, flares can occur in any trimester and even postpartum.
