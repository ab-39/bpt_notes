### What is IgA nephropathy?
IgA nephropathy is a focal and segmental proliferative form of glomerulonephritis that is a very common cause of the [[nephritic syndrome]] and RPGN. It is characterised by deposition of galactose-deficient IgA1 in the mesangium of the glomerulus, often with associated crescent formation.
- - -
### Epidemiology
IgA nephropathy is the most common cause of glomerular disease worldwide. There may be an association with HLA-B loci on chromosome 6. Children and young males are most commonly affected. 
- - -
### Clinical Presentation
IgA nephropathy typically presents with asymptomatic microscopic haematuria, or recurrent macroscopic haematuria following respiratory tract infection. Risk of progression to ESKD is highest in patients with proteinuria. 
- - -
### Management 
All patients should receive RAAS inhibition. Steroids are typically reserved for patients with heavy proteinuria (>1g/day) or progressive disease. Tonsillectomy can reduce proteinuria and haematuria in those with recurrent tonsillitis. 

#### Novel Treatments Under Investigation:
- APRIL/BAFF inhibitors - these ligands bind to B-cells and play a role in class switching and antibody secretion, possibly driving the pathogenesis of IgAN.
