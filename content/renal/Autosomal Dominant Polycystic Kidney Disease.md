# Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease 
#bpt/renal
- - -
### Epidemiology
- Community prevalence of 1/400-1000 people
- Fourth-leading cause of ESKD (after diabetes, glomerulonephritis, and hypertension)
- - -
### Genetics and Pathogenesis
Autosomal dominant mutation of two possible genes:
- _PKD1_
  - Encodes polycystin 1
  - Can be a truncating (severe) or non-truncating (less severe) mutation
  - Located on chromosome 16
  - Causes 80% of disease, generally more severe phenotype
  - Mean age at onset of ESKD of 54y
- _PKD2_
  - Encodes polycystin 2
  - Located on chromosome 4
  - Causes 15% of disease, generally less severe phenotype
  - Mean age at onset of ESKD of 74y
- Other - 5% of disease

The _polycystins_ are glycoproteins expressed in the kidney, bile ducts, vascular smooth muscle, and bronchial epithelium. Both polycycstin 1 and 2 are strongly associated with primary cilia.

Vasopressin plays a major role in the pathophysiology of ADPKD. Cyst growth appears to be driven by elevated cAMP, of which vasopressin is the major cause in the distal nephron (simplified +++). The polycystins tie in here as one of the key _negative_ regulators of cAMP is calcium, and the polycystin complex acts as a calcium influx channel. 

Once developed, cysts not only affect ‘their own’ nephron, but also impinge on and can possibly obstruct adjacent nephrons, with the highly concentrated nephrons and vasculature of the renal medulla being particularly vulnerable.

### Clinical Manifestations
~Renal~:
- HTN
- Deficit in concentrating urine
- Albuminuria
- Renal impairment
- Flank pain
- Haematuria (macroscopic haematuria in 35-50% of patients with ADPKD, generally 2’ to a ruptured cyst)
- UTI - note that ~75% of cysts do not communicate with the collecting system, and so the urine may be clean -> generally need to treat with ciprofloxacin empirically for tissue penetrance

~Extra-renal~:
- Intracranial aneurysm - prevalence as high as 20% in those with a family history; generally in the anterior circulation/MCA territory 
  - Patients with a personal or family history of aneurysm, high risk groups (e.g. occupation, on anticoagulation, prior to surgery etc.) can be screened with an MRA
  - Minimise risk of rupture - HTN and smoking cessation
- Hepatic cysts - most common extrarenal manigestation
- Vascular dilatation - e.g. coronary arteries, aorta
- Other cysts - e.g. pancreas, seminal vesicles (without infertility), arachnoid cysts
  - There does _not_ appear to be an association with ovarian cysts

### Diagnosis
Based on age:
| Age    | Imaging Findings        | Diagnosis                                                    |
|--------|-------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| 15-39y | ≥3 cysts                | Cannot exclude the diagnosis even if imaging criteria are not met |
| 40-59y | ≥2 cysts in each kidney | Definite diagnosis<br>Patients _not_ meeting these criteria can have the diagnosis definitively excluded |
| ≥60y   | ≥4 cysts each kidney    | Definite diagnosis                                           |

Genetic testing is typically only indicated in equivocal cases _or_ when testing a donor for a potential transplant. The sequence of testing in the latter case is laid out by [KDIGO](https://kdigo.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/2017-KDIGO-LD-GL.pdf):
> *The first-degree relative with ADPKD should undergo PKD1 and PKD2 mutation screening (using an acceptable technique), and if a pathogenic mutation is successfully identified, the donor candidate can be tested for this same mutation.*

#### Markers of Severity
- Total kidney volume
  - Relates inversely to eGFR
- Mayo classification - generally based on TKV based on MRI 
- PRO-PKD, based on:
  - Genetics: worst = PKD1 truncating > PKD1 non truncating > PKD2
  - Male sex
  - HTN before age 35y
  - Urological evenet before age 35y

#### Differentials
Bilateral solid lesions -> consider:
- Tuberous sclerosis complex - mutations of hamartin or tuberlin -> angiomyolipomas of the kidney, elevated risk of renal cell carcinoma
  - Frequently associated with ADPKD as the loci are closely located on Ch16
- Von Hippel Lindau syndrome - inactivation of _VHL_ tumour suppressor gene -> multiple tumours - paragangliomas, vascular tumours, renal cell carcinoma

Bilateral cysts:
- Medullary cysts only:
  - Nephronophthisis: autosomal recessive mutation of the _NPHP_ genes, most commonly in children - associated with polydactyly and retinitis pigmentosa
  - Autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease: medullary cystic disease, difficult to visualise the cysts on ultrasound but tend to present with early renal failure
  - Medullary sponge kidney - cysts, stones, and UTIs, but very rarely CKD
- Cysts everywhere + enlarged kidneys:
  - ADPKD
  - Autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease -> tends to have a stronger association with severe liver disease
    - Due to mutations in the _PKHD1_ gene on Ch6 that encodes fibrocystin
    - Association with congenital hepatic fibrosis and dilatation of the intrahepatic ducts
    - Often diagnosed in infancy or in utero
    - Associated with ESKD by age 10y, and significantly reduced life expectancy

### Management
General measures:
- Avoid hormonal contraceptives in female patients
- Weight loss/avoid obesity
- Lipid lowering treatments 
- Hypertension control - ACEI/A2RB preferred 
- Consider screening for IC aneurysms in high-risk patients 

Suppress vasopressin levels:
- Low salt diet: <3g/d
- Hydrate well - aim for urine osmolarity <300mOsm/L

Screening:
- Children of PKD patients:
  - Screening for renal cystst not recommended until adulthood
  - Screening recommended for HTN

ESRF:
- HDx > PD due to organomegaly

~Disease Specific Management~:
- Phase III trials:
  - V2R antagonists - e.g. tolvaptan
    - Evidence for reduction in TKV (*[TEMPO 3:4](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1205511)*), although this effect oes not appear to be durable (*[TEMPO 4:4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6019005/)*)
    - Evidence for reduction in rate of decline of eGFR in advanced-stage PKD (*[REPRISE](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1710030)*) - _but_ may be a small reversible drop in eGFR on intiiation
    - ADRs include:
      - Polydipsia and polyuria
      - Deranged LFTs/hepatotoxicity 
      - Gout
      - Substrate for CYP3A4 (caution with inhibitors, e.g. azoles, amiodarone)
      - Diuretics - need to avoid dehydration
- Negative trials for somatostatin analogues and mTOR inhibiors 
- Phase II trials:
  - BTK inhibitors - e.g. bosutinib

Key mutations are PKD1 and PKD2. Autosomal dominant inheritance pattern.
