# Systemic Sclerosis
#bpt/rheumatology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### Overview
Systemic sclerosis is a rare fibrosing disorder of the skin (hence, _scleroderma_) and internal organs.
- - -
### Epidemiology
Rare: ~7/1,000,000
Female predominance 
Peak onset in ages 40-60y
Aetiology unknwon - there are genetic associations:
- HLA-DRB1
- STAT4
- IFN

And environmental ones:
- Silica
- Organic solvents
- Vinyl chloride 

Highest mortality and burden of disease among the rheumatic disaeses, with a life expectancy shortened by ~20y. Predominantly due to cardiorespiratory causes. 
- - -
### Pathophysiology
Likely a self-amplifying process consisting of:
- Initial microvascular/endothelial damage 
- Followed by an autoimmune response with inflammation
  - Leads to capillary pruning
- Finally characterised by diffuse fibrosis 
- - -
### [[Clinical Manifestations of Systemic Sclerosis]]
SSc is a heterogeneous, multiorgan disease, characterised by a broad triad of:
- Inflammation - of the skin, synovium, and muscles
- Vasculopathy - e.g. Raynaud phenomenon (often the first manifestation of SSc), telangiectasia, etc.
- Interstitial fibrosis - of the lung (association with pulmonary hypertension), kidneys, gut, heart, etc.
![](Systemic%20Sclerosis/image%202.png)
There are two main phenotypes of SSc:
1. _Diffuse_ cutaneous SSc:
   - 35% of cases
   - Rapid onset and progression to visceral fibrosis, particularly renal disease and interstitial lung disease
   - Involves skin of the trunk and proximal limbs 
   - Antibody associations:
     - Topoisomerase I/SCL-70 = ILD
     - RNA Polymerase III = renal crisis
     - ANA with a nucleolar pattern
2. _Limited_ cutaneous SSc:
   - 55% of cases
   - ’CREST’ syndrome:
     - Calcinosis
     - Raynaud phenomenon
     - Oesophageal dysmotility
     - Skin changes - typically only in upper limbs and face
     - Telangiectasia 
   - Weaker association with visceral fibrosis 
   - Strong association with pulmonary arterial hypertension
   - Antibodies tend to be anti-centromere antibodies 
3. Mixed connective tissue disorder
4. Morphoea
   - Focal areas of fibrosis

### Antibodies in SSc
#### Topoisomerase I = SCL-70
An extractable nuclear antigen with a speckled nucleolar ANA staining pattern. Positive in approximately 20% of cases, tends to be associated with diffuse SSc and ILD.

#### Centromere
An extractable nuclear antigen that (predictably) displays a centromeric pattern on ANA immunofluorescence. Positive in ~40% of cases, associated with limited SSc and tends to show a negative association with ILD and renal disease.

#### RNA Polymerase III
An extractable nuclear antigen that stains with a fine speckled nucleolar pattern. Positive in ~25% of cases, it is associated with diffuse SSc, scleroderma renal crisis, and a rapid progressoin to malignancy.

#### U1-RNP
Positive in ~10% of cases of either phenotype, tends to be associated with overlap syndromes, particularly mixed connective tissue disorder. 

#### Th/T0
Rare, tends to be associated with PAH and a poor prognosis.

#### PM/SCL
Rare, tends to be associated with myositis overlap syndromes.

### Diagnosis 
Diagnosis is with the ACR/EULAR criteria, and requires a score of at least nine.

Skin thickening of the fingers extending proximally to the MCP is considered a sufficient crtierion.
- - -
### Management 
Management is often targeted at the specific organs involved:
- e.g. Raynaud -> CCBs

There is _no_ overall disease-modifying therapy, although a range of immunosuppressive therapies, vasodilators, and antifibrotics are used.

It is important to assess for new organ involvement. ILD and renal crisis can occur, typically in the first five years, in diffuse SSc, while PAH can develop at anytime irrespective of the phenotype. Cancer screening is also important. 

#### Skin
Methotrexate + PT/OT/hand therapy

#### SSc-ILD 
Initially:
- Maximise therapy for GORD
- Smoking cessation
- Vaccinations
- Supplemental O2

First line:
- MMF up to 3g/d or MPA (better for diarrhoea)
- PO/IV CYC 
- AZA

Antifibrotics:
- Nintendanib (SENSCIS trial) -> reduced rate of decline in FVC (but there are very specific PBS criteria that demonstrate severe and worsening lung function)
- Pirfenidone

May be a role for B-cell depletion (rituximab) or IL-6 inhibition (tocilizumab).

#### SSC-PAH
Leading _single_ cause of death in SSc.
Treatment is complex.
CCBs are _not_ useful as monotherapy so vasodilator challenge is not routinely performed. Patients with extensive ILD may need to be enrolled in clinical trials. Treatments target:
1. Nitric oxide - e.g. sildenafil, riociguat
2. Add an endothelin receptor agonist - e.g. bosentan, ambrisentan - 
3. Prostacyclin - e.g. iloprost, selexipag 

#### SSC-SRC
Untreated, can progress to ESRF in 1-2 months.
Can be precipitated by steroids (esp. doses >10mg pred. equivalent/d).
Key aspect of treatment is to manage BP aggressively - e.g. with a rapid acting ACE-inhibitor such as _captopril_. Can rarely be normotensive. Progression to dialysis is a poor marker. Second-line therapy involves adding a CCB and then an ARB if required.

#### Joint Involvement
1. MTX
2. Add low dose glucocorticoid
3. Add HCQ
4. Add rituximab or tocilizumab

#### Raynaud Phenomenon
Investigation of choice: nailfold capillaroscopy -> looking for enlarged or tortuous capillaries with or without haemorrhage:
![](Systemic%20Sclerosis/image%203.png)

Investigate and treat secondary causes - e.g. cryoglobulinaemia.

Cold avoidance, etc.

First-line:
- Dihydropyridine CCBs

Second-line:
- A2RBs
- PDEV inhibitors
- SSRIs
- Antiplatelets

If severe/salvage therapy:
- IV prostacyclin (e.g. iloprost)

#### Digital Ulcers
Complication of RP. First line is cold avoidance and vasodilator therapy. Appropriate dressings are important. Treat infections if they arise. 

PIP ulcers can be deep and problematic. Need to screen for OM/deep swab. 

#### Calcinosis
Common problem. Monitor if asymptomatic. Usually discharge spontaneously. If progress to an ulcer, may need debridement. May need antibiotics if become infeceted. 

#### Gastric Involvement
Reflux/dysmotility -> high dose PPI +/- prokinetic agents
GAVE -> argon laser ablation

### Prognosis
Poor prognostic factors include:
- Increasing age
- Scleroderma renal crisis
- Pulmonary arterial hypertension
- Pulmonary fibrosis
- Rapid onset/progression - strong predictor of malignancy
- Associated malignancy

Life expectancy is significantly reduced.
