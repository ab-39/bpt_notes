### Overview
The two types of [[inflammatory bowel disease]] are associated with seronegative arthritis. This is assocaited with the HLA-B27 phenotype. Rheumatoid factor is typically negative.

While this is typically an axial arthopathy (with a relationship to ankylosing spondylitis), there can also be a non-erosive peripheral arthritis:
- Type I ->  oligoarticular and associated with flares
- Type II -> polyarticular and independent of flares 

Arthritis occurs in ~10-30% of patients with [[inflammatory bowel disease]], most comonly in those with other extra-intestinal features (e.g. uveitis), complicated IBD, or extensive bowel involvement (if CD). 

Like regular [[axial spondyloarthritis]], IBD-SpA can be axial or peripheral. 

Most commonly, IBD-SpA is acute, oligoarticular, and involving the lower limbs, in which case severity relates to disease activity/flares of the underlying IBD. In this case, the arthritis tends not to be erosive. In some cases, however, the SpA can be progressive independently of IBD disease activity. 

Mangement is usually centred around controlling the underlying IBD. NSAIDs should be used with caution, and cDMARDs are not effective in axial disease. IL-23 and TNF inhibition may be effective for both joints and bowel disease, while IL-17 inhibition has no utility in treating the bowel.
