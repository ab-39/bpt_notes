# Polyarteritis Nodosa
#bpt/rheumatology #bpt/racp_cls

### Overview
Polyarteritis nodosa is a [[vasculitis]] of the medium-sized vessels, that has a predisposition for visceral arteries at their bifurcations, leading to the formations of the ‘tangles’ of aneurysms from which it gets its name. 

PAN is fairly uncommon. Although PAN is less common than [[microscopic polyangiitis]], an overlap syndrome of both conditions actually occurs more commonly than ‘pure’ PAN. 

There is a milder, genetic form of PAN that is associated with missense *ADA2* mutations leading to a deficiency of adenosine deaminase 2 (DADA2). This phenotype typically affects the skin, CNS, and kidneys. It can be treated with anti-TNF agents and haematopoietic stem cell transplants. 

Clinically, this preponderance of aneurysms leads to presentations with organ ischaemia secondary to infarctions, typically of the spleen, bowel, and kidney, as well as peripheral neuropathy. 
> Unlike the ANCA vasculitides, however, there is typically no glomerulonephritis, lung haemorrhage, or systemic features such as fever

In terms of diagnostic tests, there is **no association** with ANCA, but there is a tenuous association between PAN and hepatitis B. 

Treatment for PAN usually consists of courses of steroids, and relapses are uncommon.
