### Overview
Unlike autoimmune disorders, which fundamentally relate to dysregulation of the adaptive immune system, autoinflammatory disorders are _antigen-independent_ processes that relate to dysfunction of the _innate_ immune system. 

The clinical hallmark of these syndromes is _fever_, due to the prolific release of proinflammatory cytokines, particularly IL-1 and IL-18, which are involved in the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway, IL-6, and TNF⍺. Attacks are often triggered by some physiological stressor. The importance of IL-1 and the inflammasome is highlighted by the key role that _anakinra_ and related therapies play in the. management of autoinflammatory disorders. CRP is generally elevated. 

Key medications:
- Anakinra - inhibitor of IL-1 (recombinant IL-1R)
- Colchicine - inhibitor of neutrophils (microtubule/β-tubule inhibitor)
- JAK inhibitors - target pathways that signal for pro-inflammation

### VEXAS Syndrome
VEXAS syndrome is an relatively common (est. prevalence 1/14,000) condition characterised by treatment-refractory NFκB-mediated inflammation and progressive haematologic abnormalities. It is strongly associated with [[myelodysplastic syndrome]], monoclonal gammopathies, and relapsing polychondritis. Its name is a handy acronym for its key features:
- _Vacuoles_ in the myeloid and erythroid precursor cells in the bone marrow, which arise due to impairment of the ubiquitin-proteasome-mediated degradation of proteins leading to protein aggregations
- _E1 enzyme_, a ubiquitin activator encoded by the _UBA1_ gene 
  - Specific mutations are on exon 3 of _UBA1_
    - M41V - most severe
    - M41L
    - M41T
- *X-linked* - exon 3 of _UBA1_ is on the X-chromosome so the condition is _far_ more common in men
- _Autoinflammation_ - relapsing polychondritis, neutrophilic dermatoses such as Sweet syndrome, polyarteritis nodosa, giant cell arteritis, but also haematologic abnormalities such as venous thromboembolism, myelodysplastic syndrome, and multiple myeloma
- _Somatic_

Treatment is both general (with steroids) and targeted at the cytokines involved:
- IL-6 -> tocilizumab
- IL-1 -> anakinra

Overall, however, _JAK/STAT_ inhibition, particularly with ruxolitinib appears to be the most effective therapy for inducing complete response in patients with VEXAS. Azacitidine is also an emerging therapy for this indication, achieving excellent results in one small study. AlloHSCT remains the only curative treatment, but is complicated as this is primarily a disease of older patients who have acquired comorbidities both due to the disease and the high dose of steroids often used in managing these conditions. 

Related NFκBopathies include OTULIN and LUBAC deficiency.

### Periodic Fever Syndromes
#### Monogenic Periodic Fever Syndromes
Periodic fever syndromes are a group of autoinflammatory disorders that present with recurrent attacks of fever in childhood and adult life. A subset of these conditions are caused by identifiable mutations in specific genes:
- Familial Mediterranean Fever:
  - The most common inherited autoinflammatory disease 
  - Autosomal recessive mutation in the _MEFV_ gene on chromosome 16
  - Gain of function mutations in _MEFV_ -> dysregulated pyrin -> increased activation of caspase 1 -> increased synthesis of IL-1β 
  - Triad of: recurrent attacks of fever, abdominal pain, and arthritis
  - Attacks triggered by stress, infections, or periods - between attacks patients are asymptomatic 
  - Treatment:
    - Lifelong colchicine - also reduced the risk of AA amyloidosis secondary to chronic inflammation 
    - IL-1 inhibitors if refractory - e.g. anakinra, canakinumab
- Hyperimmunoglobulin D syndrome: AR mutation of _MVK_ gene leading to reduced pyrin phosphorylation -> inflammation 
- Cryopyrin associated periodic syndromes:
  - Autosomal dominant gain of function mutations of _NLRP3_
  - Generally short episodes of fever with cutaneous manifestations (usually cold-induced urticaria)
  - Managed with IL-1 inhibition
  - Three specific phenotypes:
    - Familial cold autoinflammatory syndrome - urticaria, pain, and conjunctivitis exacerbated by cold (mild-moderate)
    - Muckle-Wells syndrome - urticaria, pain, and fevers + neurological symptoms and progressive sensorineural hearing loss (moderate-severe)
    - Neonatal onset multisystem inflammatory disease (NOMID) - most severe, presents at birth 
- TNF-receptor 1-associated periodic syndrome (TRAPS):
  - Né _familial Hibernian fever_
  - AD mutation of the TNF receptor (with incomplete penetrance) -> aberrant activation of NFκB 
  - Treatment of attacks with steroids and prevention with anakinra or etanercept 

#### Adult-Onset Stills Disease (Systemic Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis)
A rare condition characterised by a (loose) triad of:
- Quotidian fevers
- An evanescent, salmon rash
- Symmetrical arthritis/arthralgia

The exact aetiology is unknown, but appears to be related to an autonflammatory cascade secondary to innate immune cell activation which converges on a final common pathway of NLRP3 inflammasome activation and IL-18 production. Appears to be a (loose) HLA association and possible implicated polymorphisms in the IL-18 and macrophage migration inhibitory factor genes. 

> [!TIP] Mnemonic
> **Adult**-onset Stills disease is primarily driven by IL-**18** 

Key complication is macrophage activation syndrome, a subtype of [[haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis]]. 

Treatment is generally stratified according to severity and the presence of MAS:
- Mild + no MAS -> NSAIDs
- Moderate/severe + no MAS -> anakinra +/- adjunctive steroids 
- Concern for MAS -> anakinra + high dose steroids (e.g. pulsed methylprednisolone)
