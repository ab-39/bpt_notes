# Leucocytoclastic Vasculitis
#bpt/rheumatology #bpt/racp_cls
- - -
### What is leucocytoclastic vasculitis?
Also known as ‘hypersensitivity’ or ‘cutaneous’ vasculitis, leucocytoclastic vasculitis is a vasculitis typically caused by various drugs, that is defined by (ARA criteria) **three or more** of:
1. Age >16y
2. Temporal relationship with medication
3. Palpable purpura 
4. Maculopapular rash
5. Perivascular neutrophils on skin biopsy

Visceral involvement is typically minor. 
- - -
### What are the causes of leucocytoclastic vasculitis?
- Sulphonamides:
  - Frusemide
  - HCT
- Antibiotics:
  - Penicillins
  - Cephalosporins 
- Allopurinol
- Phenytoin
- - -
### What is the treatment for leucocytoclastic vasculitis?
No definitive treatment - need to cease the offending agent.
- Note that if the patient is presenting with heart failure, bumetanide can be used instead of frusemide
- The ‘classic’ picture of a leucocytoclastic vasculitis is of the heart failure patient who presents overloaded and is commenced on massively increased doses of frusemide
